# Why this concept follows the knowledge base

## Assumptions

Test Cream is a fictional everyday facial moisturizer. No formula, product performance, skin-type suitability, ingredient, certification, usage amount, price, or discount has been supplied. The ivory jar and sage lid are proposed prop styling. Maya, the synthetic presenter, is a fictional character in an illustrative evening scene, not a customer supplying a review.

Audience hypothesis: adults roughly 25–40 who recognize the feeling of an overcomplicated evening skincare routine. This has not been validated with interviews, reviews, ad-account results, or competitor research. The user asked for a fictional demonstration; a real campaign would replace these assumptions with product/customer evidence.

## What we are deliberately testing

Hypothesis: recognition of routine overload plus a calm, specific scene can earn attention without making a product-performance promise. Actual Self is the main identity target. Zone 1 relief is the dominant emotional direction. Everyday phrasing and matching visuals support processing fluency.

The script is an original adaptation, not a copy of the course's Maelove script. It does not borrow that brand's results or proof. Stronger claims can be added only when a real product supplies supporting evidence.

## Source tension: synthetic presenters

The KB includes [Sarah and Chase's skepticism about AI UGC](../../knowledge-base/wiki/tools/ai-ugc-tools.md), including trust concerns in the May 14 session **00:26:56–00:27:15**. That is the instructors' position, not independent proof that AI ads never work. This demo therefore uses an explicitly fictional presenter and no fake personal product results. A real campaign should test the format against appropriate real-creator or voiceover footage, rather than assume synthetic faces increase trust.
