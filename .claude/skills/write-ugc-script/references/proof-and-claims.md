# Proof and claims

The rule for both courses: **only say what is true and documented**. A script that invents proof gets blocked, however good it sounds. [T] = Tether Lab, [E] = Evolve Copywriting.

## Allowed vs never [T]
| Allowed (if true) | Never |
|---|---|
| An on-camera demo of a real claim | Faked or edited test results |
| A real customer quote, labelled as a quote, close to the original wording | Invented testimonials, composite "customers", a "real user quote" no customer wrote |
| Exact, typed numbers ("1,200+ reviews from dairy lovers") | Rounded-up or invented counts; untyped "thousands of women" |
| A real press or expert relationship | Press logos or "dermatologist-approved" you can't document |
| A real limited drop, deadline or occasion ("the Fourth of July sale") | "Only 9 left" that isn't true; "sale ends tonight" with no real reason |
| The creator's own honest experience, including doubt | "Overnight" transformations; instructor stats quoted as fact |
| A guarantee or trial you actually offer | Exaggerated fear or danger |

## Social-proof tiers [E]
Say only what your real numbers support. Defaults: use the exact documented number when you have it, use tier words only if the real count is inside the tier, and never round up.

| If you really have | You may say |
|---|---|
| 10,000+ customers/reviews | "Over 10,000 five-star reviews" |
| 1,000–10,000 | "Thousands of five-star reviews", "Trusted by thousands" |
| 100–1,000 | "Hundreds of five-star reviews"; true percentages ("94% would recommend") |
| 10–100 reviews | "Rated 4.8/5 stars"; percentages over volume |
| <10, or unknown | Skip volume claims. Use the mechanism, a real study, a demo, or the guarantee |

**Borrowed authority [E]:** say "we noticed X about Navy SEALs", never imply they endorse you. Name a study only if it exists and you can cite it.

## Honest first person [E]
- An AI voiceover or AI avatar makes no human-experience claims ("you're an AI. We know you didn't see it"). Keep it third person: "Scientists call it…", "Women report…" (only if that pattern is documented).
- A real creator says "I" only about what they actually experienced with the product. If you don't know, write the line in a form they can truthfully say, or bracket it for confirmation.
- No synthetic person gives a testimonial. [T]

## Product truth checks [E]
Before output, check every line:
1. Every number, ingredient and term matches the product information. The course's own final script had to correct "68%" to "56%" because of what the website said.
2. Scientific words are used correctly (e.g. DPP-4 is an enzyme, not a hormone).
3. Only benefits the product actually supports.
4. No unverifiable adjectives presented as fact ("rare", "clinically proven" without a study).
5. Numbers are identical across all hook variants.

## Regulated and health-adjacent categories
Supplements, skincare, sleep, posture, weight and pain products:
- No disease, cure or treatment claims ("fixes back pain", "cures insomnia") unless documented and allowed.
- No "clinically proven" without a real study, and no implied studies.
- Use experience and behaviour language ("my wind-down step", "I sit up before it buzzes") and the documented limits from the brief.
- Wording like "may", "could be" or "*not typical results" where the brief requires it. [T][E]
- Flag anything uncertain under "Verify before filming". Don't guess at compliance.

## Competitor and reference ads [T][E]
Copy the mechanism, not the asset. No lines (5+ consecutive words), footage, characters, catchphrases, claims or stats from a competitor or reference ad. If a competitor's hook works, find the desire it communicates and write your own version.
