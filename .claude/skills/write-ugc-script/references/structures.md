## 1. Story beat sheet (on-camera creator) [T]
Spend about **2/3 of the runtime on setup** (problem + lived experience) before the product appears. "The punchline is not the point." Timings are derived from that rule.

| Beat | 15 s | 30 s | 45 s | 60 s | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 | 0–3 | 0–3 | 0–3 | Core message in frame 1; stop the scroll |
| Problem / situation | 2–6 | 3–12 | 3–16 | 3–22 | One specific situation in customer words, "four levels deep" ("Every bowl that we gave him, he destroyed.") |
| Lived experience + failed attempts + realism | 6–10 | 12–20 | 16–30 | 22–40 | "Expensive ones. Cheap ones." Doubt, the product graveyard |
| Turn: product + mechanism | 10–12 | 20–24 | 30–36 | 40–47 | Why it works; show, don't tell |
| Proof | 12–13.5 | 24–27 | 36–41 | 47–55 | Demo, time sequence, or a real review |
| CTA (+ real offer) | 13.5–15 | 27–30 | 41–45 | 55–60 | Definitive CTA tied to a person or habit |

Rules [T]:
- Name the **main character** (narrator) and the **final CTA** before drafting. Narrator choices: the customer, or someone around them (vet, trainer, partner, even the pet). Each narrator is its own test.
- Add a **realism beat**: "cautiously at first", imperfect or gradual results. Never "it transformed everybody overnight".
- Write lines the way the creator would say them, and brief them to say it **in their own words** (lock hook lines and every claim).
- Slow down on emotional lines: "the faster you go through a story the more difficult it is for people to attach emotionally."

## 2. Story framework picker [T]
Name ONE framework and follow only its steps (LLMs blend similar frameworks if you don't).

| Angle / stage | Framework | Steps |
|---|---|---|
| Cold UGC, nothing is working | **Pixar** (Sarah's #1, "fantastic for injecting emotion") | Once upon a time… Every day… One day… Because of that… Because of that… Until finally… |
| UGC that needs a proof tail | **Story Spine** | Once upon a time… Every day… But one day… (found the product)… and **ever since then**, people have been asking me… |
| Switching from a competitor or habit | **SCQA** | Situation ("My <problem> was always…") → Complication ("I tried everything…") → Question ("Then <expert/friend> asked me: are you sure it's your <current product>?") → Answer (switched → result → CTA) |
| Fake podcast, retargeting, open loop | **In Medias Res** (her #2) | Start in the middle of the action (mid-rant) → flashback to explain how we got here → return to the present and resolve |
| Founder-led | **Founder's Post** (verbatim template) | "Before I started [brand], I was struggling with…" → "I tried [unsuccessful method]." → "Then I realized…" → "That's when I created [solution]." → "And since then, we've helped [others] do the same." |
| Hard close, bottom of funnel | PAS / AIDA | Use only when "really trying to sell hard" |
| Shame-risky topic | Put the shame on a character (skit) | Viewer recognises themselves without being accused |

## 3. Hook → Bridge → Hold → CTA (AI voiceover + B-roll) [E]
Total 30–45 s, hard cap 45. Problem-aware viewers 40–45 s (educate first); product-aware 30–40 s (differentiate).

| Section | Time | Job | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | Stop the scroll in 1–2 s | Don't explain. Two hook frameworks: "biggest thing happening now" or "quirky / counter-intuitive" |
| Bridge | 3–8 s | Intensify the desire or problem; start objection → claim | Exact customer frustration language; end on an open loop. One bridge per hook |
| Hold | 8–35 s | ONE objection → claim → proof → benefit cycle with ONE mechanism | Objection 8–11 · Claim 11–16 · Proof 16–25 · Benefit 25–32 · optional secondary benefit 32–35 |
| CTA | 35–45 s | Keep momentum; close the loop opened by the hook | Value-framed price (e.g. per day), risk reversal, real urgency only |

**Bridge patterns [E]** (pick one per bridge): explain the mechanism · escalate the problem · escalate the desire · reposition the problem ("it could be…") · allude to new information · set a timeline. Show the escalation as behaviour.

**Hold details [E]:**
- **Objection:** name the specific past attempts ("You've tried GLP-1 supplements, appetite suppressants, and natural alternatives"), never the generic "I know what you're thinking, you've tried supplements before" (the instructor calls that "a terrible line").
- **Reason-why:** why those attempts fail (the mechanism gap): "they only BOOST your fullness hormone. NONE of them PROTECT it."
- **Claim:** "the first and only [thing] that [mechanism]", only if true. Never categorise ("like Ozempic but natural", "best massage gun").
- **Proof:** a real study with exact numbers checked on the product page, or the real social-proof tier.
- **Benefit:** behaviour, tied back to the mechanism ("Imagine going hours without food crossing your mind").
- **Voice:** AI voiceover stays third person ("Scientists call it DPP-4").

**Packaging [E]:** 3 hooks, 3 bridges (one per hook), 1 shared hold, 1 shared CTA. The hold and CTA stay identical so the test isolates the opening.

**Emotional levers [E]:** hit 2–3 of the Big 4 value words per section (NEW/ONLY, EASY/ANYBODY, SAFE/PREDICTABLE, BIG/FAST). Forcing all four makes copy "clunky, listy". Every line should create curiosity for the next (slippery slope).

## 4. Skeptic format [T]
For a claim you can demonstrate on camera, aimed at buyers who've seen it all:
1. **Claim:** state the bold claim (or the ad you saw).
2. **Doubt:** the creator says out loud why they don't believe it.
3. **Test:** they test it on camera, one continuous shot where possible.
4. **Reluctant concession:** "Okay… I did not want to like this." Keep it partial and honest.

## 5. On-screen text, visuals and pace
- **Frame 1:** the hook overlay is the heaviest element (largest, highest contrast, in a calm area of the shot). ≤7 words, ≤3 lines. [T]
- One overlay line per beat; never two emotional claims in one frame. [T]
- Proof overlays (stars, counts) are small supporting elements, never the hook, and only with true numbers. [T]
- Use periods for deadpan and an ellipsis to pull the viewer on; avoid exclamation marks. [T]
- **Hook visual:** one salient object or an odd first frame; the face shows the hook's emotion. Don't open on skin with no context for women's products. [T]
- **Problem visuals:** props and settings that prime the right life stage; show the real mess (cluttered counter, the "product graveyard"). [T]
- **Mechanism visuals:** ingredients, materials, texture, pour, so people "smell it through the screen". [T]
- **Proof visuals:** one continuous demo shot, or a time sequence (week 1/2/3) for speed or durability claims. [T]
- **Show behaviour, not concepts** (have the creator walk past the pantry, not "animated enzymes"). [E]
- **AI-VO visual line format [E]:** `VISUAL: [source] - [avatar demographic] [specific action/emotion], [setting]. Text overlay: "[text]"`. Hook = one dramatic shot or fast cut; bridge = 2–3 quick problem cuts; hold = product, mechanism and solution scenes; CTA = clean product shot, price, guarantee.
- **Pace [T]:** cuts every 2–3 s, up to about 4 s; pause on key lines.

## 6. CTA rules
- Definitive: "See how it works", "Get yours", "Order yours now". Never "Shop now" or "Learn more". [T]
- Tie it to a person's approval or a habit: "Want your vet to be pleasantly surprised at your dog's next checkup?" [T]
- Close the loop to the hook, add risk reversal and value-framed price: "Try it risk free for less than $2/day". [E]
- The CTA promise must match the landing page. [T]
