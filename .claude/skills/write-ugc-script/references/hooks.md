# Hooks, bridges and CTA patterns

Patterns to fill with **your** product's facts and **your** customer's words. The examples show the move; never reuse their wording or claims. All examples are verbatim from the knowledge base:

## 1. Hook rules
- **Length.** On-screen hook text ≤7 words (≤2–3 lines on mobile). The spoken hook may run longer if hook + bridge ≤ about 8 s and the first sentence stands alone. [default, contradictions #21]
- **Self-selection.** Frame 1 must make the problem or payoff clear enough for the right viewer to self-select. The product or mechanism may be withheld until the bridge. [default, Evolve contradictions #28]
- **Gap + emotion + stakes.** Start in the action. Don't explain, don't list benefits, don't give the answer. [EV L23; L70 docx §A]
- **Two hook frameworks [EV]:** "This is the biggest thing happening now" (discovery: "revealed", "discovered", "scientists found") and "This is quirky" (counter-intuitive).
- **Promise vs open loop [EV L25]:** "Leave in the context → make a promise. Take out the context → open a loop."
- **One main loop.** One open loop runs from hook to payoff. Small line-to-line curiosity is fine if it closes within a line or two. [default, Evolve contradictions #29]
- **Three hooks, three levers.** Test three different levers, not three wordings of one idea. [TL, EV]

## 3. Bridge formulas [EV, L27 unless noted]
One bridge per hook, to about 8 s. Escalate; never reassure.

| Template | Example fill |
|---|---|
| "I thought I was being a [good AVATAR], but after [Y months] [PROBLEM] was still happening, and now [it's worse]." | "I thought I was being a good parent…" |
| "[Symptom]? Red flag. [Symptom]? Red flag. [Symptom]? Red flag." | "Only wanting chicken nuggets? Red flag." |
| "Are you one of the [N]% of [avatar] who don't [do thing]? Well, by [time], you could be setting up your [dependent] for [lifetime consequence]." | dog dental (real stat only) |
| "You're probably dreading [event] as much as [dependent] is, but what if I showed you [dream outcome]." | dogs falling asleep during nail trims |
| "My [authority] revealed something that [industry] doesn't want you to know." | tick preventatives |
| "But I'll teach it to you in about [time]." / "Give me the next [N] seconds." | laundry, dandruff |
| "It's not [mild name], it's [vivid, active, visual threat]." | "It's not dry scalp. That's a yeast problem on your head and it's feeding on your scalp like a buffet." (L52) |
| Hook "Watch how this [PRODUCT] stops [PROBLEM]" → Bridge "First, this [PRODUCT] has a [NEW MECHANISM]…" | |

## 4. Hold lines: right vs wrong [EV, L70 docx §C]
| Part | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Claim | "It's like Ozempic but natural" / "the best collagen on the market" / "Better than other massage guns" | "[Product] is the first and only [thing] that [mechanism]" (only if true) |
| Reason-why | "It reduces appetite" | "It blocks the enzyme that destroys your appetite hormone every 2 minutes, so your brain finally gets the 'stop eating' signal" |
| Proof | "It reduces calories by 24%. It works well." | "They ate 24% fewer calories in week one. And here's what happened next..." |
| Benefit | "You'll have better appetite control" | "Imagine eating breakfast and not thinking about lunch at 10am. Imagine walking past the vending machine at 3pm without even looking." |

Name the mechanism (Schwartz style) when you can: "The Alpine Ice Hack", "The 5-Second Water Method".

## 5. CTA patterns
- **Evolve structure:** close the loop to the hook → value-framed price → risk reversal → real urgency (never fake scarcity) → clear action.
- **Per-day price, only with the real price:** "$59/month = $1.97/day. Less than a bottle of kombucha." [EV L28 [34:09–35:13]]
- **Desire reframe:** "You're not buying a powder. You're buying the ability to wear the clothes in the back of your closet again." [EV L28]
- **Soft transition lines [EV L29]:** "Now here's the thing..." · "So if you're wondering where to start..." · "Here's what I did..." · "Now everyone in my comments asked about this".
- **Final line:** one definitive verb ("Get yours", "See how it works", "Order yours now"), never "Shop now" or "Learn more". [TL Roast #1 [0:26:52–0:28:09]; default X13]
- **Tie it to a person or habit:** "Want your vet to be pleasantly surprised at your dog's next checkup?" [TL May 14]

## 6. Line-level tricks
- **Story connectors [EV L40–41]:** replace every "and then" with "but" (change of direction) or "so" (progress).
- **Add conflict:** "The man worked at his desk" → "The man worked at the boss's desk". [EV T167]
- **Gradualization scaffold [EV T142]:** "[Accepted truth]. You also know [second accepted truth]. But here's what stops you: [real objections]. That's why [real stat]. We fixed [them]. [Mechanism]. [Specific proof]. Because [restated problem now solved]."
