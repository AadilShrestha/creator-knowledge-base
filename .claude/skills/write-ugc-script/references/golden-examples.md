# Golden examples

Real scripts from the courses, copied verbatim and checked against the raw transcripts or documents. Read the 2–3 closest to your structure **before drafting**. Learn the structure, the specificity and the pacing. **Never reuse their lines, characters, claims or numbers.**

**Read this before trusting any example:**
- No script here comes with performance data. "Crushing" or "winner" is the instructor's word, with no numbers shown.
- E5 and E6 are ChatGPT demo scripts Sarah used to teach frameworks. They never ran as ads.
- Machine-transcription slips are fixed in [brackets].

## E1. Metaglow collagen: 3 hooks + bridges, 1 shared hold, 1 CTA (Evolve)
**Use as a model for:** AI voiceover or mechanism-led ads, problem-aware audiences, a new-mechanism claim backed by a real study.
**Source:** Spencer's final client script, `raw/evolve/copywriting/_extracted/…/71. Final Script Written By Spencer.pdf.md`; annotated in `wiki/courses/evolve/copywriting/frameworks/video-ad-script-workflow.md` (L70–L72). No results shown. Study numbers are the client's claims, checked against the client's website.

**HOOK 1:** "After 40, your body produces 3x more of the enzyme that makes calorie deficits feel IMPOSSIBLE"
**BRIDGE 1:** "Scientists call it DPP-4. And it's literally breaking down your fullness hormone every two minutes. This one enzyme is why you have to avoid walking past the kitchen pantry, even after you just ate."
*Visual:* 45-year-old woman looking frustrated at bathroom scale, shaking her head. Quick cut to close-up of her face showing stress and exhaustion.

**HOOK 2:** "If 1,200 calories feels like torture, your fullness hormone is likely being destroyed..."
**BRIDGE 2:** "After 40, your body makes 3x more of one enzyme that makes calorie deficits IMPOSSIBLE. Scientists call it DPP-4. And it's literally breaking down your fullness hormone every two minutes. This one enzyme is why you have to avoid walking past the kitchen pantry, even after you just ate."
*Visual:* Woman's hands measuring tiny portion of food on plate with measuring cups and food scale visible, looks defeated and frustrated.

**HOOK 3:** "Here's why you can't stop thinking about food all day"
**BRIDGE 3:** "After 40, your body, starts making 3x more of an enzyme called DPP-4. And this one thing destroys your fullness hormone every two minutes. And it's exactly why food noise never stops And why staying below your calorie limit is literally impossible."
*Visual:* Close-up of woman's face looking distracted, and a thought bubble thinking of really delicious foods like chips or anything crunchy.

**HOLD (identical for all three)**
> You've tried GLP-1 supplements, appetite suppressants, and natural alternatives.
> But the problem is, none of those work long-term
> The reason they don't work is that they only fix 1/2 of the problem...
> They only BOOST your fullness hormone.
> NONE of them PROTECT it.
> So, your body may see some improvement for the first couple of days
> But as soon as it catches up, it produces more DPP-4, and next thing you know, you're elbow-deep in a bag of something crunchy
> NONE of these "fixes" actually fixes the real source of your cravings
> Except for Metaglow...
> Metaglow is the first and only collagen with Dyglomera.
> Dyglomera is a natural African fruit extract that is clinically proven to block DPP-4.
> Meaning, it tells your body to stop destroying your fullness hormone so you can STAY Full longer.
> In a 12-week study with 90 women, they saw an increase in GLP-1 by 56% and on average, they ate 24% fewer calories in the first 7 days.
> They didn't white-knuckling their way through or fill themselves with synthetic hormones
> They just protected the hormone that their body was destroying.
> Thanks to Metaglow, thousands of women are finally getting proper fullness signals again.
> Imagine going hours without food crossing your mind.
> Or actually staying full after you eat
> That's what happens when you protect your hormone, and not just boost it.

**CTA**
> This company is so confident you'll love Metaglow that you can try it for 60 days 100% risk-free
> If it doesn't work, they'll refund you.
> Your body has been fighting you.
> Metaglow helps you fight back.
> Try it risk free for less than $2/day

**Why it works (Spencer, L70–L71):**
- **Three entry points.** Hook 1 gives new information with an age and a number. Hook 2 is a deliberately lower-valence self-check that uses the customer's own word, "torture". Hook 3 is educational pain validation.
- **Specific objection.** The AI draft's "I know what you're thinking. You've tried supplements before" was "a terrible line". It was replaced with the three named past attempts.
- **Mechanism gap.** "they only BOOST… NONE of them PROTECT".
- **Details from research:** "kitchen pantry", "elbow-deep in a bag of something crunchy".
- **Third person for AI voiceover.** "My doctor told me" became "Scientists call it DPP-4".
- **Numbers corrected** against the product page (68% → 56%).
- **Tier word, not a fake count.** "Thousands" was used only after confirming the real count was 1,000–10,000.

**What to copy:** the shape (specific past attempts → why they fail → "first and only [X] with [mechanism]" → real study → benefit as behaviour → risk reversal and per-day price) and the level of specificity.

## E2. Aviorra translation earbuds: one-take UGC, story first, product late (Evolve teardown)
**Use as a model for:** on-camera UGC for a gadget, and drama that makes the product's use obvious without explaining it.
**Source:** a real ad played in Evolve L40 [28:52–31:31], from the Whisper transcript `raw/evolve/copywriting/_transcripts/…/40. ⭐️ What makes a great story….txt` [00:29:20–00:29:46]. The ad's first seconds may be missing from the transcript. Its on-screen hook was "I thought girls enjoyed going to the nail salon." Spencer: "likely crushing" (no data).

> "Absolutely insane. What happened in there? They were talking so bad about this girl getting the pedicure. I felt so bad for her because they were saying she had fungus under her nails. They smelled so bad that she needs to like have proper hygiene. Like they were making fun of her. You heard all that? Yeah, I heard it right here in my earbud. Oh my God. They thought I was just listening to music or something. The whole time it's translating what they're saying right into English in my ear. Wow, did they say anything about you? Yeah, when I went to pay at the end. Really? She goes... $10 tip, really? That's it? That's not even 20%. Oh, my gosh. And I was just like, smile and leave. You know what I mean? Like, I gave you $10 for a tip as you're, like, talking to your customers. So, yeah, it's insane. You have got to get a pair of these. That's crazy. How many languages do those translate? 134. Oh, my gosh. Yeah, so whatever they're speaking, you'll just hear right in English in your ear. That is crazy. They're also good because you can listen to music on them, like, take calls. They're, like, normal over-the-ear earbuds."

**Why it works (Spencer, L40 [30:13–31:31]):**
- **Withheld information:** the on-screen hook contradicts itself ("I thought girls enjoyed…"), so the viewer asks why.
- **Change:** "I thought those were just earbuds".
- **Stakes and drama:** strangers mocking someone.
- **Suspense:** "did they say anything about you?"
- **Cause and effect:** the product is what made hearing everything possible.
- **Pitch only after the story.**
- "Not as elegant… just a one take UGC video."

**What to copy:** dramatise one real use moment, let the friend's questions do the explaining, and give one spec ("134") only when it's asked for.

## E3. CarePod humidifier: stream of acceptances, then experience authority (Evolve teardown, partial)
**Use as a model for:** UGC voiceover for a household product, and disarming objections before the pitch.
**Source:** Evolve L28 [30:12–33:17]. The opening is verbatim from `raw/…/_transcripts/…/28. …txt` [00:30:12]; the rest is Spencer describing the ad. Spencer: "this chick just crushed it" (no data).

> "If you have a kid, you probably have a humidifier in their room. And if you have a humidifier, you probably hate cleaning it every few days. I've gone through about three different humidifier brands before I found CarePod and I could finally stop looking for a new one."
> `[gap: unboxing and demo lines not transcribed. Spencer's paraphrase: "we have one in Olive's nursery… because obviously she deserves the best… we finally upgraded to one in our room as well… I'm just so happy it's quick and easy to clean."]`

**Why it works (Spencer):**
- **The "yes" train.** Each line gets a silent "yes, I do" before any pitch.
- **Experience authority.** "I've gone through about three different humidifier brands" positions her as someone who knows.
- **Plain visuals:** "nothing crazy… literally just unboxing".
- **Real proof:** the unit sitting in the nursery.
- "She just disarmed all of them."

**Template (Evolve T143):** "If you have [identifier], you probably have [thing]. And if you have [thing], you probably [dislike an aspect]. I've gone through about [N] [products] before I found [brand]…" Use it only if the creator really did try those products.

## E4. Dr. Squatch cologne: 15 s outrageous claim, then defuse (Evolve teardown)
**Use as a model for:** short ads, sensory products you can't show (scent, taste), and claim discipline with humour.
**Source:** Evolve L32 [02:35–04:31]; `raw/…/_transcripts/…/32. …txt` [00:03:04]. The transcript reads "Dr. Squash".

> On-screen: "Packs of women are going feral for this new [Dr. Squatch] scent."
> Reaction clips: "If you smell like this, oh, I'm definitely coming over. I will hunt you down. No way. Ten out of [ten], guys."
> VO: "What if I told you there was a cologne scientifically proven to attract women? That would be an exaggeration, obviously. But this [Dr. Squatch] cologne smells so good, it should be illegal."

**Why it works (Spencer, L32 [03:33–04:31]):**
- It makes an outrageous claim, then defuses it ("obviously, that would be an exaggeration"), so it stays honest and gets the laugh.
- "They're writing the desire. They're writing the experience. They're not just saying you're going to smell better."
- The visuals (reactions) show the claim.

**What to copy:** a sensory benefit shown through other people's reactions, and an exaggeration the ad itself admits is one. Don't make a real "scientifically proven" claim.

## E5. Maelove "Pixar model" UGC script (Tether demo, ChatGPT-generated, never ran)
**Use as a model for:** story-led on-camera UGC with a named framework; the confession hook; the "product graveyard" image.
**Source:** Tether Lab Live May 14 [0:16:17–0:18:29], screen-only (keyframe `wiki/assets/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-14-maelove-pixar-script-01740.jpg`). Sarah read it aloud at [00:16:38–00:17:42].

1. **Hook** *(person scrolling on their phone, sighing dramatically)*: "You ever buy a skincare product just knowing it's not gonna work… but you do it anyway? Yeah. That was me with Maelove." *(Pauses, smirks)* "Unfortunately, now I have to admit I was wrong."
2. **Once upon a time…** *(bathroom mirror, frustrated)*: "Once upon a time, I was an optimist about skincare. A bright-eyed, dewy-skinned believer."
3. **And every day…** *(montage of a cluttered counter of half-used serums)*: "And every day, I got sucked into some new 'miracle' product. Vitamin C that oxidized in a week. Serums that felt like expensive water. 'Clinically proven'…to disappoint me."
4. **Until one day…** *(zoom on the serum in her hand)*: "Until one day, I found Maelove. Science-backed. Straightforward. No marketing B.S. Just results."
5. **Because of that…** *(applying serum, checking the mirror)*: "Because of that, I gave it a shot. Totally thought it was going to be just another bottle on my counter. But these guys were different. They really do obsess over their ingredients—plus dermatologists actually recommend it…and use it."
6. **Because of that…** *(before/after clips)*: "Because of that, my skin decided to have an 'I Told You So' moment, and now I can get rid of all those half-used bottles on my countertop because I finally found ONE thing that actually works."
7. **Until finally…** *(holding up the bottle, nodding)*: "I can stop treating my bathroom counter like a product graveyard. Maelove gets a permanent spot."
8. **CTA** *(pointing at screen, playfully serious)*: "If you've been burned by skincare before—trust me, I get it. But this? Worth it."

**Why it works (Sarah [0:17:42]):** "this has a whole lot more emotion in it than if [you] just say write me a ugc script". The framework forces a progression; the "every day" beat lists **three specific failures**.
**Fix before using this pattern:**
- **Setup too short.** Only about 3/8 of it is setup; Sarah's rule is about 2/3, so expand the problem scenes.
- **Unchecked claims.** "Dermatologists actually recommend it" and the before/after clips are claims that need real proof.
- **Banned phrase.** "Trust me" is on the skill's slop list.

## E6. Maelove fake podcast, In Medias Res (Tether demo, ChatGPT-generated, never ran)
**Use as a model for:** skeptical markets, retargeting, and opening mid-rant to validate the viewer's cynicism.
**Source:** Tether Lab Live May 14 [0:20:41–0:22:22], screen-only. The opening was read aloud at [00:20:47]. The ending is cut off on screen.

> **HOST 1** *(skeptical, exasperated)*: "No, because explain to me why every skincare brand thinks I'm gullible. 'This serum changes everything.' 'Clinically proven to do magic.' Like, do they think I was born yesterday?"
> **HOST 2** *(smug, amused)*: "Right, right. And remind me—where is your Maelove serum right now?"
> **HOST 1** *(muttering)*: "…on my bathroom counter."
> *(Rewind sound effect: flashback to skepticism)*
> **HOST 1** *(past self, mocking)*: "Wow, another 'dermatologist-approved' brand. Can't wait to pay $50 for scented disappointment."
> **HOST 2**: "And yet… you bought it."
> **HOST 1** *(defensive)*: "Because I was researching how bad it would be."
> **HOST 2** *(laughing)*: "And…?"
> *(Fast-forward sound effect: back to present)*
> **HOST 1** *(begrudgingly admitting)*: "And now my skin looks better than it has in years, and I'm annoyed about it."
> **HOST 2**: "So basically, you wanted it to fail so you could say 'I told you so', but instead, it told you so?"
> **HOST 1** *(sighs)*: "I hate this. I hate that I'm saying this, but… Maelove actually works. It's straight to the point, no BS, no overpriced nonsense—just solid, effective skincare…" *(cut off)*

**Why it works (Sarah [0:20:47]):** "those brands want to start at the top of the story by saying i found the perfect thing for me and it changed everything… this person flips it upside down because we're starting in the middle of the story". Host 2 validates the skepticism, and the reluctant, "annoyed" convert adds realism.

## E7. The Conscious Bar: unaware "did you know" educational story (Evolve teardown)
**Use as a model for:** food or wellness products with a "hidden ingredient" story, and shifting blame from the buyer to the industry.
**Source:** a real ad played in Evolve L40 [22:46–28:52]; Whisper transcript `raw/…/_transcripts/…/40. …txt` [00:22:46–00:24:05]. Spencer: "absolutely crushing" (no data). The health statistics are the brand's claims. **Don't reuse them.**

> "What is the number one ingredient in most chocolate bars? I'll give you a hint. It is not cacao. This is a cacao pod. This is where chocolate comes from. This fruit started off as medicine and it's one of the most powerful plants that ancient cultures had access to. Cacao contains 40 times more antioxidants than blueberries. It has some of the highest magnesium out of all plant foods and it naturally boosts your serotonin and dopamine. But then sugar entered the picture and everything changed. Chocolate was no longer chocolate. It became candy. Today, some bars are over 50% sugar by weight. One in two Americans now lives with a chronic illness. And it's been proven that sugar is to blame for a lot of that. And it's hidden in almost every single chocolate bar on the shelf. So we asked ourselves, What would happen if instead we paired this magical superfood with nature's most nutrient-dense sweetener organic dates? Would it taste any good? Would it still feel like chocolate? Or would it suck? Well, not only did it not suck, it turned out rich, fudgy, and unbelievably delicious. It was the most delicious chocolate we've ever had. We truly believe it is the future of chocolate altogether. This is the conscious bar. Just cacao and dates. That's it. And we really, really, really want you to try it. So we're hooking you up. Click below to get 20% off your first order."

**Why it works (Spencer, L40 [24:22–28:52]):**
- **The hook withholds the answer** ("if it's not cacao, like, what is it?").
- **Change:** medicine → candy.
- **Blame on the industry:** "They put the blame on the fact that these companies have hidden this amount of sugar". Spencer: "if you can make your customer feel good about themselves, they are more likely to trust you".
- **"But" and "so" carry the story** ("but then sugar entered the picture"; "so we asked ourselves").
