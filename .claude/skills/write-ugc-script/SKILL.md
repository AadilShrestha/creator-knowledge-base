# Write a UGC / video ad script

You turn real product facts and real customer language into a 15–60 s script with 3 testable hooks. Quality comes from three things, in this order: **specific inputs**, **one sharp angle**, and **ruthless line editing**. Frameworks only arrange those; they never replace them.

Work through the steps below in order. Keep a copy of this checklist in your notes and tick it as you go:

```
- [ ] 0 Intake: inputs gathered, gaps listed
- [ ] 1 Customer language collected (or marked missing)
- [ ] 2 One angle, one core emotion, one-sentence core message
- [ ] 3 Structure picked
- [ ] 4 Read 2–3 golden examples
- [ ] 5 Six+ hooks drafted, 3 kept
- [ ] 6 Body drafted
- [ ] 7 Line edit done
- [ ] 8 Checklist passed (fresh critic if possible), fixes applied
- [ ] 9 Output in the template
```

## Step 0: Intake

You need these. Pull them from the request, attached files or the product page:

| Input | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Product: what it is, how it's used, how it works (mechanism), price, offer, guarantee | Every claim must trace back here |
| What must NOT be claimed (regulated category, no studies, etc.) | Hard limits |
| Audience + their situation (when the problem bites) | The script lives in that moment |
| Customer language: verbatim reviews, comments, Reddit posts, interviews (5–20 lines) | The single biggest quality driver |
| Real proof that exists: review count, rating, studies, press, guarantee | Proof can only come from here |
| Execution: on-camera creator, AI voiceover + B-roll, or founder | Decides voice and structure |
| Length (default 30 s) and platform | Timing |

**If something is missing:** if you can ask the user, ask once, listing every gap in one short message. If you can't ask, or they say "just write it", carry on. Then:
- Put every unconfirmed product fact in [square brackets] and list it under "Verify before filming".
- Never invent customer quotes, reviews, counts, ratings, studies, endorsements or results to fill a gap.
- With no customer language, say the angle is a hypothesis and propose where to get real language.

## Step 1: Customer language

If you have web access and the user didn't supply reviews, mine them yourself: the product page, competitor reviews (Amazon, the brand site) and Reddit threads about the problem. Collect verbatim lines in five buckets: **pain moment**, **failed attempts**, **desired outcome**, **objections/doubts**, **labels** (the customer's own names for things). Keep the source of each line. Use only words people actually wrote. Never present a line as a customer quote unless a customer wrote it.

## Step 2: Angle, emotion, core message

1. Find the **moment the buyer actually hates**, not the category promise. "Better sleep" is the category; "tired body, brain starts a podcast at 11:45pm" is the moment.
2. Pick **one core emotion** (specific: "relief at finally switching off", not "happy") and **one angle** (the reason to buy).
3. Write the **core message** in one sentence. The first 3 seconds (spoken and/or on screen) must make the problem or payoff clear enough for the right viewer to recognise themselves. The product or mechanism can wait until the bridge.
4. **Swap test:** drop a competitor's name into the core message. If it still fits, it's generic; make it more specific (mechanism, detail, situation only this product owns).
5. Never sell "better than / like [brand]". Say the difference: "the first/only [thing] that [mechanism]", but only if it's true.

## Step 3: Pick the structure

Read [references/structures.md](references/structures.md) and pick ONE:

| Situation | Structure |
|---|---|
| On-camera creator, story-led, cold audience | **Story beat sheet**: about 2/3 of runtime on the problem and lived experience before the product (Tether). Name one story framework: Pixar, Story Spine, SCQA, In Medias Res, or Skeptic |
| AI voiceover + B-roll, mechanism-led, problem- or product-aware | **Hook → Bridge → Hold → CTA** (Evolve): one objection → claim → proof → benefit cycle, 30–45 s |
| Demonstrable claim, skeptical market | **Skeptic format**: claim → doubt → test on camera → reluctant concession |

## Step 4: Read golden examples

Open [references/golden-examples.md](references/golden-examples.md) and read the 2–3 examples closest to your structure and niche. Study **how specific they are**, how each line pulls the viewer to the next one, and how proof is handled. Copy the structure and the level of specificity. Never copy their words, claims or characters.

## Step 5: Hooks (write 6+, keep 3)

- Use [references/hooks.md](references/hooks.md) for patterns. Fill them with this product's facts and this customer's words.
- Each hook = **spoken line + on-screen text (≤7 words) + first visual**.
- A strong hook has a **gap** (a question the viewer needs answered), an **emotion**, and **stakes**. It starts in the middle of the action, with no "In this video…" and no backstory.
- The 3 kept hooks must test **3 different levers**, e.g. discovery (new mechanism/information), contrarian ("what they're not telling you"), pain validation (customer's words), reluctant convert, or a slightly lower-valence self-check ("is something wrong with me?").
- Each hook gets its own short **bridge** (to about 8 s) that escalates the problem or desire and leaves an open loop. No reassuring bridges ("it's extremely common").

## Step 6: Body

Draft the body for your structure. Whatever the structure:
- **Problem** in the customer's words, as a specific situation (time, place, object), not a general claim.
- **Failed attempts / realism:** name the specific things they already tried and why those fall short. Include doubt or imperfect results; never "overnight" transformations.
- **Turn:** introduce the product through the **mechanism** (how it works), shown on screen where possible.
- **Proof:** only from the proof that exists (see [references/proof-and-claims.md](references/proof-and-claims.md)). The creator's own honest experience counts only if it's true.
- **Benefit as behaviour:** what the viewer will do or notice ("I had breakfast and I'm still not hungry by dinner"), not a benefit list.
- **CTA:** definitive ("Get yours", "See how it works", "Try it tonight"), tied back to the hook, with the real offer/guarantee. Never "Shop now" or "Learn more".
- Pace: about 2.5 spoken words per second (30 s ≈ 70–80 words). Every beat gets a visual note and at most one on-screen text line.
- Demos take real time. A one-take demo needs its full real duration in the timing table; if it can't fit, shorten the action or mark a speed-up on screen.

## Step 7: Line edit (where the quality comes from)

Treat your first draft as 70% done. For every line:
1. **Generic → specific:** replace it with a detail from the inputs or customer language. ("the kitchen" → "the kitchen pantry"; "cravings" → "elbow-deep in a bag of something crunchy".)
2. **Spoken test:** would a person say this out loud to a friend? Use contractions and grade-6 words. Cut anything written-sounding.
3. **Fact test:** is every number, ingredient and claim in the inputs? If not, cut it or bracket it. Watch for the four slips that show up most in tests:
   - **Mechanism drift:** the "why it works" must be the mechanism in the product facts. Don't promote a reviewer's opinion ("it's heavy, so it doesn't slide") into the mechanism.
   - **Review → promise:** a customer's result stays theirs (a labelled quote, or a first-person line true for the creator). Never turn it into a general promise to the viewer ("then you sit up before it buzzes"), especially in health-adjacent categories.
   - **Assumed usage details** (where to apply, how many, how long it takes): bracket them **everywhere** they appear, including hooks and on-screen text, not just in the body.
   - **Offer wording:** state the offer exactly as given ("30-day returns", "60-day money-back guarantee"). Don't paraphrase it into something broader ("a full month", "money back if it's not for you").
4. **Delete test:** can the line go without losing meaning? Then delete it.
5. **Voice test:** AI voiceover speaks in the third person ("Scientists call it…"). A creator says "I" only for experiences they really had.
6. **Slop test:** remove AI tells: "game-changer", "say goodbye to", "unlock", "elevate", "revolutionize", "Are you tired of…?", "Introducing…", "trust me", stacked adjectives, rhetorical triplets. *(This list is our addition, not from the courses.)*

## Step 8: Critique against the checklist

Run [references/checklist.md](references/checklist.md) on the draft.
- **Best:** if you can start a fresh subagent, give it only the draft, the inputs and the checklist, and ask it to fail items with quoted evidence. A fresh reviewer catches what the author can't see.
- **Otherwise:** answer every item yourself with the line it refers to.
- Any ⛔ item failing → fix it before output. Three or more other failures → revise and re-check.

## Step 9: Output

Use this template. Keep it scannable. No essay before the script.

```markdown
## <Product>: <length> s <execution> script

**Angle:** <one line> · **Core emotion:** <specific> · **Core message (first 3 s):** "<…>"
**Audience moment:** <who, when the problem bites> · **Structure:** <name>

### Hooks (test all 3 with the same body)
| # | Spoken | On-screen (≤7 words) | First visual | Lever tested |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | | | | |
| H2 | | | | |
| H3 | | | | |
(Bridges per hook, if the structure uses them.)

### Script
| Time | Beat | Spoken | On-screen text | Visual / shot | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### Proof and claims used
| Line | Claim / proof | Source (brief, review #, study) |
|---|---|---|

### Verify before filming
- [bracketed facts], missing inputs, compliance notes

### Test plan
3 hooks × this body. When a hook wins, test a second body with exactly ONE change (proof order, narrator, or story framework).

### Checklist result
⛔ all pass · other fails: <none or list with fixes made>
```

For creator shoots, add **locked words** (hook lines plus every claim and number) and tell the creator to "say the rest in your own words".

## When the two courses disagree (defaults)

| Topic | Default |
|---|---|
| Test packaging | Launch 3 hooks (with bridges) × 1 body; after a hook wins, test a 2nd body with one change |
| Setup length | Story-led creator UGC: about 2/3 setup before the product. AI-VO mechanism ads: claim at about 11–16 s of 30–45 s |
| Hook length | On-screen text ≤7 words; the spoken hook may run longer if hook + bridge ≤ about 8 s |
| Social proof | Use the exact documented number; tier words ("thousands") only if the real count is in that tier; never round up |
| Emotion | One core emotion per ad; 2–3 value words (NEW/ONLY, EASY, SAFE, BIG/FAST) that serve it |
| Frame 1 vs withholding | Frame 1 makes the problem/payoff clear for the right viewer; withhold the product or mechanism until the bridge |
| Open loops | One main loop from hook to payoff; small line-to-line curiosity that closes within a line or two |
| CTA voice | Recommendation voice for the transition ("Here's what I did…"), then one definitive verb in the last line |
| AI presenters | AI voiceover and clearly-AI animation are OK; no synthetic person gives a testimonial or first-person result |
| Skeptical vs novelty buyers | Burned-before buyers: lead with SAFE + EASY and a modest, provable result. Novelty seekers: lead with NEW + BIG |

Full list: `knowledge-base/wiki/contradictions.md` (rows X1–X29, S1–S5, R1–R16) and `knowledge-base/wiki/courses/evolve/copywriting/contradictions.md`. Which course owns which topic: `knowledge-base/wiki/topic-map.md`.
