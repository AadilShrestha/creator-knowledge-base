## GripBoard: 40–45 s on-camera creator UGC script (Meta / TikTok)

**Angle:** The board stays put when you lean into a hard cut, because it has silicone feet on all four corners and weighs 6 lb. · **Core emotion:** relief that the board stays still under your knife, so you stop bracing it with your other hand · **Core message (first 3 s):** "Your cutting board shouldn't move when you cut. This one doesn't."
**Audience moment:** home cooks aged 28–55 who cook most nights. The bad moment is mid-cut on something hard (a squash, a sweet potato), when the cheap, scarred plastic board slides across the counter. · **Structure:** story beat sheet (Tether Lab), using the Pixar framework: "Once upon a time" (I cook most nights) → "Every day" (the board slides on every hard cut) → "One day" (this board) → "Because of that" (it stays put, shown in a demo) → "Because of that" (the meat side keeps juice off the counter) → "Until finally" (a board that stays put). Proof comes from a single-take demo.

### Hooks (test all 3 with the same body)
| # | Spoken | On-screen (≤7 words) | First visual | Lever tested |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | "Watch the board. Not the squash." | Watch the board, not the squash. | Tight overhead shot. The creator brings a chef's knife down into a butternut squash and leans their full weight on it. The GripBoard doesn't move. | Payoff first (a live demo creates the gap: *why* doesn't it move?) |
| H2 | "Cutting board sliding around? It's not your knife skills." | It's not your knife skills. | The creator's own plastic board slides sideways as the knife hits a squash. Their free hand grabs the board. Deadpan look into the lens. | Pain validation + reframe (takes the blame off the cook) |
| H3 | "A review of this board: 'Heavy. Like, really heavy.'" | "Heavy. Like, really heavy." | The creator lifts the GripBoard with both hands, with visible effort, then sets it on a kitchen scale that reads 6 lb. | Objection first (the downside is the reason it works) |

**Bridges (one per hook; hook + bridge is about 7–8 s):**
- **H1 → B1:** "[My old board] would've skated halfway to the sink by now." *(Quick cut: the old plastic board sliding.)*
- **H2 → B2:** "It's the board. Yours is too light to stay put." *(Cut: knife scars on the old board, then it slides again.)*
- **H3 → B3:** "Six pounds. That's exactly why it doesn't do *this*." *(Hard cut: the old plastic board sliding across the counter.)*

**Deliberately not used:**
- **"Knives stay sharper"** (review #5). It would read as a product performance claim, and the brief doesn't support it. Add it only if the brand can back it up.
- **Antibacterial or hygiene wording** ("cleaner than plastic", "bacteria"). Banned by the brief, including by implication.
- **Dishwasher safe.** It isn't.
- **Review counts, star ratings, "bestseller", "thousands of cooks".** None were supplied, so the script makes no volume claims.
- **Other sizes.** Review #6 asks for a bigger size; don't suggest one exists.

### Verify before filming
- **Feet on a double-sided board:** confirm where the silicone feet sit and whether the board stays put with **either** side up. If it only grips one way up, film the demo on that side only, and don't imply the meat side is non-slip.
- **The creator must really have the problem.** "[I cook most nights]", "[My old plastic board was all scarred up, and it skated…]" and "[My old board] would've skated" must be true for them. Cast someone who owns a sliding plastic board and film *their* board. They should use GripBoard for [at least 1–2 weeks] before the shoot.
- **[Wet paper towel] beat:** keep it only if the creator really tried this and the board really slid. Otherwise delete it (the script still fits the time).
- **Demo integrity:** test the squash cut and the palm-push off camera first, on the counter you'll film on. No mats, clamps or cuts inside the demo shot. If the board shifts at all, don't say "Nothing." Tell us what you saw and we'll rewrite the line. Note the counter surface (quartz, laminate, wood) in the shoot notes.
- **Return policy:** confirm the 30-day returns don't require the board to be unused. If they do, keep "30-day returns" but don't suggest customers can use it and send it back.
- **Oiling frequency:** the script doesn't say how often (review #6 says once a month). Add a frequency only if the brand's care instructions state one.
- **Review permission:** confirm the brand is allowed to show reviews #1, #3 and #4 in ads, word for word.
- **Landing page:** it must show $79, free shipping, 30-day returns, the silicone feet and hand-wash care, so the ad's promise matches the page.
- **Missing inputs (not blocking):** no review count or rating, no product-page URL and no competitor reviews. That's why the script has no volume proof and the angle rests on six reviews. Mining 20+ more reviews would confirm whether sliding is the #1 pain or whether knife scars or juice mess come first.

### Test plan
1. **Round 1:** H1, H2 and H3 × this body, with the same creator and the same CTA, so only the opening changes.
2. **After a hook wins:** test a second body with exactly ONE change. Recommended: **longer setup**. In this body the product appears at about 18 s (≈40% of runtime), earlier than Tether's "about 2/3 setup" rule for cold story-led UGC. I front-loaded it because the demo is the strongest proof. Body B drops the meat-side beat and spends that time on the problem (the old board, the scars, the hand bracing it), so the product arrives at about 26–28 s.
3. **Seasonal option (same body, new hook):** a Thanksgiving hook built on review #4: the creator carving a turkey on the groove side, with on-screen text "Turkey juice stayed ON the board." Run it in the weeks before Thanksgiving (Nov 26, 2026). The occasion is real; don't add fake deadlines.

Hooks drafted and cut: "If you cook most nights, look at your cutting board right now" (a self-check, but weaker than H2's reframe); "I didn't think a cutting board could make me excited to chop onions" (a customer's line, which the creator can't say as their own); "I've been putting a wet paper towel under my board for years" (depends on an unverified creator habit; kept as a bracketed body beat instead).
