## Upright Pro: 30 s on-camera creator UGC script (3 hooks + 1 shared body)

**Angle:** Not a brace, and no promise to fix anything. It's a tiny stick-on sensor that buzzes when you slouch, and we prove it on camera.
**Core emotion:** Wry relief: "finally, something that just reminds me, without strapping me in."
**Core message (first 3 s):** "Posture straps failed you. This is just a poke when you slouch."
**Audience moment:** A desk worker aged 25–45, mid-afternoon at the laptop, who notices they've folded forward over the keyboard. The strap from last time is in a drawer.
**Structure:** Skeptic format, live-test version: claim → doubt → test on camera → reluctant concession. It suits a burned-before market because the core claim ("buzzes after 10 s of slouching") can be shown in one shot. It leads with SAFE + EASY (skin-safe pad, no straps, money-back) and a modest, provable result.
**Execution:** One real creator talking to camera, 9:16, deadpan. A second angle or a mirror shows the sensor on the upper back.

### Verify before filming
- **Creator truth (blocking).** These lines are first-person: H1 hook and bridge ("my last posture strap", "it's in a drawer"), the H2 bridge ("I don't believe any of them"), the H3 bridge ("I don't notice till I'm folded over my laptop") and the concession. Cast a creator who **really** bought a posture strap and quit it, really hunches at a laptop, and has worn Upright Pro for at least [several days] with the "annoying, then you sit up before it buzzes" experience. If that isn't true for them:
  - H1: show the line on screen as a labelled customer quote (only with a real, consented source). Change the bridge to "Most of them end up in a drawer."
  - Concession: "People say the buzz is annoying for a couple of days, then they sit up before it buzzes." Use this only if that pattern is documented in more than one interview.
- **Demo integrity.** One continuous take; the only edit allowed is the labelled 3× speed-up. Confirm that 10 s is the default threshold a buyer gets out of the box [confirm; adjustable?]. The buzz must be visible or audible without added effects.
- **"Skin-safe" overlay.** Confirm what substantiates "skin-safe" (material spec or test) [confirm]. If it can't be substantiated, use "Sticks on. No straps."
- **Guarantee terms.** Confirm the exact 60-day conditions (full refund? who pays return shipping? used pads OK?) and match the landing page wording [confirm].
- **Pads.** How many adhesive pads come in the box, and what do refills cost [missing]? Nothing is claimed here, but skeptics will ask in the comments. Answer it on the landing page.
- **Battery (about 7 days).** Left out of the spoken lines to protect the 30 s. Optional swap for the mechanism line: "It sticks on, no straps, and charges about once a week." Keep the word "about".
- **H3 compliance read.** "This won't fix your back" is a disclaimer, not a claim, but have the person who signs off health claims approve it. Same-lever fallback: "It's not a brace. It buzzes after ten seconds of slouching."
- **App screen.** Show only the creator's real "upright time". No voiced or overlaid numbers or "% improvement".
- **Missing inputs.** Platform (assumed TikTok/Reels 9:16); real reviews, ratings or return data; the landing page the CTA points to. The angle rests on 3 interview lines, so treat it as a hypothesis. Before round 2, mine more language from 1–3★ posture-strap reviews (Amazon) and desk-worker Reddit threads.

### Test plan
Run 3 hooks × this body, with the body and CTA identical so the test isolates the opening. When a hook wins, test a second body with exactly ONE change. First change: **proof order** (run the 10-second test straight after the hook and move the mechanism line after it). Next candidates: the narrator (a coworker who catches the creator hunching) or the concession line.
