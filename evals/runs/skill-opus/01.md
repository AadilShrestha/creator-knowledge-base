## [Brand] Magnesium Sleep Spray: 30 s on-camera UGC script

> I wrote this without client input. Anything in [square brackets] is an assumption or a placeholder; confirm it before filming. The full list is under "Verify before filming."

**Angle:** Not a pill. A wind-down step for people whose body is tired but whose brain won't switch off.
**Core emotion:** relief at finally being "off duty" at night (not knocked out, just done)
**Core message (first 3 s):** "My body's ready for bed. My brain isn't."
**Audience moment:** adults [25–45, likely women, check against your buyer data], in bed at about midnight, phone in hand, replaying the day. They've tried melatonin or tea and want something that isn't one more thing to swallow.
**Structure:** Story beat sheet (Tether Lab), on-camera creator, with the Pixar story framework squeezed into 30 s: *every night…* (problem and failed attempts), then *now…* (the spray step), then *because of that…* (the honest result).

### Verify before filming
- **[Brand]** name, **[offer/price/guarantee]**. The CTA overlay must match the landing page exactly.
- **Application directions:** where on the body, how many sprays, and how long before bed. The script assumes the feet, after a shower (bracketed in H3 and in the turn). If the label says otherwise, change both lines and the visuals.
- **Tingle/itch:** does your formula tingle? If yes, keep the on-screen note. It builds trust and heads off "it burns" refunds, which is a top complaint in category reviews. If no, cut the overlay. If it really doesn't tingle, that's a stronger point of difference worth its own hook. Also add your label's skin cautions (sensitive or broken skin, freshly shaved areas) to the landing page.
- **Point of difference:** with no product details, the swap test only half passes. Another magnesium spray could run this body. Send the formula details (scent, form of magnesium, mg per spray, a non-sticky or no-residue claim, what else is in it) and I'll tighten the turn beat around what only you have.
- **Claims to avoid (compliance):** no "treats/cures insomnia", no "sleep aid" unless your regulatory category allows it, no "absorbs through the skin", "works better than pills" or "no morning grogginess" without substantiation, and no "clinically proven." Don't let the edit stage the spray *replacing* melatonin (e.g. throwing pills away). That implies a comparison. Independent evidence on topical magnesium for sleep is limited, and doubters in the category say so loudly. Keep to unwind and wind-down experience language.
- **Creator truth:** the melatonin, tea and phone lines, and the result line, must be the creator's real experience. AI avatars must not deliver this script in the first person.
- **Paid partnership disclosure** on the post (#ad or platform tag).
- **Customer language:** this angle is a **hypothesis** built from category reviews, not your customers. Before scaling, pull 10–20 lines from your own reviews, post-purchase surveys and comments. Look for their words for the bad moment and what they tried before.

**Locked words (creator says these exactly):** all three hook lines (with the confirmed application site); "Magnesium on my skin, not another pill"; "It doesn't knock me out"; "Try it tonight"; any offer wording. **Say everything else in your own words.** Don't add results, timeframes ("asleep in 10 minutes"), discount codes or health claims.

### Test plan
- Launch **3 hooks × 1 body** (H1/H2/H3 each with its own bridge, then the identical 8–30 s body and CTA), so the test isolates the opening.
- When a hook wins, test a second body with **exactly one change**. Pick one of: (a) narrator, with a partner who says "she used to scroll till 1 a.m." instead of the creator; (b) the Skeptic format (claim, then doubt, then the nightly routine over 7 nights, then a reluctant concession); or (c) leading the turn with the real point of difference once you've confirmed it.
- If H3 wins, skepticism is the main barrier, so lean on demos and the guarantee. If H1 wins, the moment is the hook, so build more "body tired, brain on" situations.

### Checklist result
⛔ all pass after a fresh-reviewer pass. The fixes made were: bracketing the application site in H3 and the turn; removing the implied "no grogginess vs melatonin" comparison (in both the line and the H2 visual); replacing the pseudo-physiological "tells my body the day's done" with "wind-down step"; and cutting from 82 to 71 spoken words to fit 30 s. No invented proof, urgency, quotes or numbers. Creator "I" lines are flagged as creator-true. No sleep-aid or disease claims.
Other open items:
- **#8 swap test, partial.** The body fits any magnesium spray until the product's point of difference is confirmed (flagged above).
- **#12 customer language:** none was supplied. The angle comes from category reviews ("switch off", melatonin and tea as failed attempts, placebo and itch doubts) and is labelled a hypothesis.
- **#14 mechanism:** shown as the step (on skin, rub in, phone away), not as a physiological "how it works". That's deliberate, because no substantiated mechanism claim is available.
