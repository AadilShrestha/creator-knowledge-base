# GripBoard: 30–45 s on-camera UGC ad (Meta / TikTok)

**Deliverable:** 3 hook options (each with its own bridge), 1 shared body ("hold") and CTA, a shot list, a proof ledger, and notes for the client.
**Format:** 9:16, on-camera creator in a real home kitchen, burned-in captions. Master length about 40 s.

---

## 1. Strategy in one block

| Field | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Core message (frame 1) | **Your board shouldn't move when you cut.** | Review 1 names the exact pain ("skate across the counter… does not move. At all.") and the silicone feet are a claim we can **demonstrate** on camera. |
| Core emotion (one only) | Relief: "finally, it stays put" | Starts as low-grade annoyance (old board slides, scarred) and ends in calm, steady cutting. Low intensity, no fear tactics. |
| Self | Actual self: someone who already cooks most nights, not an aspiring chef | The audience already cooks. We fix one annoying tool; we don't sell a lifestyle. |
| Awareness / stage | Problem-aware; evaluation (they own a board and are deciding whether an upgrade is worth $79) | |
| Story framework | **Skeptic / reluctant convert**: doubt → test on camera → concede | The product's main claim can be tested in one shot, and the price ($79 for a cutting board) is the obvious doubt to say out loud. |
| Narrator | A home cook (28–55) who has used GripBoard for at least a week before filming | First-person lines have to be true for the person saying them. |
| Proof | A live, unedited push-and-chop demo, the juice groove in use, and the real offer. **No counts, ratings or "best-selling" claims** (none were supplied). | |
| Offer | $79, free shipping, a month to return it. No discount, no urgency. | |

## 2. Three hooks (each tests a different lever)

The body and CTA stay identical across all three, so the test isolates the opening.

| # | Lever tested | Spoken hook (0–3 s) | On-screen text (≤7 words) | First visual | Bridge (3–8 s, spoken) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | **Pain validation**: "it's not you" | "If your cutting board slides every time you cut a squash… it's not your knife skills. It's the board." | **It's not you. It's the board.** | Tight shot: a plastic board skates sideways as the knife sinks into a butternut squash. | "Cheap plastic boards are light and slick underneath, so the second you lean in, they move." |
| **H2** | **Skeptic test**: price doubt + live demo (promise + open loop) | "I refused to pay seventy-nine dollars for a cutting board. So I tried to make it slide." | **I tried to make it slide.** | The creator braces both hands on GripBoard and shoves hard. It doesn't budge. Deadpan look to camera. | "Both hands. Full weight. Nothing. Okay, let me show you what my old board does." *(cut to the old board sliding)* |
| **H3** | **Self-check habit**: names a workaround | "Still putting a wet paper towel under your cutting board?" | **Still doing the wet towel trick?** | A hand smooths a damp paper towel under a scarred plastic board. | "I did that for years. It still slid on anything hard, like squash or sweet potato." |

**Backup hook (customer-language variant)**, if H3's towel habit can't be confirmed (see notes): put the real review on screen as the hook, labelled as a customer quote:
OST: *"My old board used to skate across the counter every time I cut a squash."* ("Real customer review"). Creator spoken: "This was literally me." Same first visual as H1.

## 3. Shared body (hold) + CTA, about 8–40 s

| Beat | Time | Spoken (creator: say it your way; **bold** = locked words) | On-screen text | Visual / b-roll | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | 8–14 s | "I cook almost every night, and my old board would skate across the counter the minute I leaned on it. And look at it." | **Scarred. Sliding. Every night.** | Close-up of the knife-scarred plastic board; it shifts as she cuts. | Tired, matter-of-fact. No drama. |
| Doubt (realism) | 14–17 s | "Honestly? I thought a board's a board." | **A board's a board… right?** | Creator shrugs, holding the old board. | Skeptical, a little dry. |
| Turn: product + mechanism | 17–23 s | "This is GripBoard. **Silicone feet on all four corners**, and it's **six pounds** of acacia." | **Silicone feet. All 4 corners.** | Flip the board to show the feet (macro on one foot), then set it down with an audible thud. | Showing, not selling. |
| Proof: the demo | 23–29 s | "Same squash. Watch the board." … "Doesn't move. At all." | **Same squash. Zero slide.** | **One continuous, uncut shot**, side-on at counter height: knife through the squash, board completely still. Then she pushes the board with her palm: nothing. | Slow down here and let the shot breathe. |
| Second benefit | 29–34 s | "Flip it over, there's a **juice groove** for meat, so the juice stays on the board, not all over my counter." | **Juice stays on the board.** | Flip to the groove side, carve a real roast chicken or steak, and show juice pooling in the groove with a clean counter edge. | Pleased, understated. |
| Objection: weight and care | 34–37 s | "Is it heavy? Yep. It's not going anywhere." | **Hand wash only. Board oil included.** | Lifts it with two hands; quick shot of the small oil bottle beside the board. | Light, honest. |
| CTA | 37–42 s | "It's **seventy-nine dollars**, **shipping's free**, and you've got **a full month to send it back**. Get yours, then try to make it slide." | **$79 · Free shipping · 1-month returns** → end card: **Get yours** | Product in hand, warm kitchen light; end card with the board on the counter. | Recommendation voice, then the action verb. |

**Spoken total:** about 115–125 words including hook and bridge, roughly 40 s at a natural pace. If a take runs long, cut the problem line "And look at it" first; the scars still show on screen.

**Setup share:** about 17 of 42 s (≈40%) before the product appears. That is deliberately short, because the demo *is* the proof. Body B below tests a longer setup.

### Body B: test only after a hook wins (one change: longer problem section)
Use the winning hook and bridge, the same creator and the same CTA. Change **one thing**: extend the problem and lived-experience section to about 2/3 of the runtime, with a real moment from the creator's kitchen (for example, chasing the board across the counter mid-chop or re-laying the towel). Then go straight to the uncut demo and the CTA. In Body B the juice groove appears only as on-screen text.

### 30 s cutdown
Drop the "Is it heavy?" line (keep its on-screen text over the CTA shot) and trim the problem beat to a single line. Never cut the uncut demo shot.

## 4. Shot list and creator brief

- **Casting:** a home cook aged 28–55 in their own lived-in kitchen. Not a chef, not a studio. Natural light, slightly warm grade.
- **Props:** the creator's own scarred plastic board, one butternut squash (plus a sweet potato as backup), a roast chicken or steak for the groove shot, a paper towel (H3), the board-oil bottle, and GripBoard.
- **Must-get shots:** (1) the old board sliding under a hard cut, real and not faked; (2) **the uncut push + chop demo on GripBoard**, side-on at counter height so viewers can see the board edge against the counter; (3) a macro of a silicone foot; (4) juice in the groove; (5) a two-hand lift.
- **Deliverables:** H1, H2 and H3, each with its own bridge, as separate takes; one hold take, plus a backup; raw files; captions burned in and kept out of the TikTok/Reels UI zones (bottom ~20%, right edge).
- **Locked words:** every price, offer and spec line in bold above. Everything else can be said in the creator's own words.
- **Pace:** cut every 2–4 s, except the demo shot, which plays uncut.
- **Don't say:** antibacterial, hygienic, germ-free, "safer than plastic", dishwasher safe, "knife-friendly", "keeps knives sharp", "never scars", "lasts forever", "sale" or "limited". Don't name competitor brands; say "plastic boards".

## 6. Notes for the client

1. **Claims I left out on purpose.**
   - Review 5 ("My knives stay sharper") is a performance claim that isn't in your product facts. Saying it in an ad implies the board protects knife edges. Don't use it until you can back that up.
   - I also avoided any antibacterial, hygiene or "safer" wording (per your note) and anything about dishwasher use.
2. **The towel trick in H3 is my assumption.** None of the six reviews mention it. Before you shoot H3, check that more reviews, comments or Reddit threads mention it; if not, run the backup review-quote hook instead.
3. **The creator must actually use the board before filming** (at least a week). The first-person lines ("I cook almost every night", "I thought a board's a board", the old board sliding) must be true for whoever says them. Never fake or speed-ramp the demo; the whole ad rests on it being real.
4. **Review usage:** confirm you have permission to show Review 1 on screen. Keep the wording verbatim and label it as a customer review.
5. **Returns wording:** the script says "a full month to send it back", which lands better than "30 days". Make sure your returns policy really allows a full month (30 days can fall short in a 31-day month). If it doesn't, say "30-day returns" instead. Either way, the landing page should repeat the same three things the ad promises: **doesn't slide (show the demo), $79 with free shipping, and the returns window.**
6. **Turn your objections into reasons.** The reviews' only complaints are weight (Review 3), size (Review 6) and monthly oiling (Review 6). The script states the weight openly and puts care on screen. If size comes up in comments, show "17 × 12 in, 1.25 in thick" as on-screen text over the turn beat rather than hiding it.
7. **Test plan:** launch H1/H2/H3 × the same hold. One learning goal: *which opening lever (validation, skeptic test or habit self-check) earns the most purchases, not just views?* After a winner emerges, test Body B (longer setup) against the winning cut. Suggested names: `EVAL_RELIEF_noslide_ugc_h1b1_v1`, `…h2b1_v1`, `…h3b1_v1`.
8. **If it underperforms,** fix in this order: pacing, then hook, then story, then creator. A low hook rate means rewriting the opening; good hook rate but weak sales means looking at the demo, the offer and the landing page.
