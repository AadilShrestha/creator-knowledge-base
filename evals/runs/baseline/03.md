## 2. Three hooks (each tests a different lever)

Each hook comes with a spoken line, on-screen text of 7 words or fewer, a first visual and its own bridge. The hold and CTA (section 3) stay **identical** for all three.

| # | Lever tested | Spoken hook (0–3 s) | On-screen text | First visual | Bridge (≈3–7 s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | **Skepticism / claim test** (the claim objection) | "I don't trust posture ads either. So I'm testing this one." | "I don't trust posture ads either." | Creator hunched at a laptop, holding the small sensor up to the lens between two fingers. Deadpan side-eye. | "My last one was a strap. Felt like a backpack I couldn't take off." *(pattern: escalate the problem)* |
| **H2** | **Mechanism in customer words** ("reminder, not a brace": product/market objection, reassigned) | "I don't need a brace. I need someone to poke me when I'm hunching." | "Not a brace. Just a poke." | Close shot of the creator slumped over the keyboard. An off-screen finger pokes their upper back and they jolt upright. | "Turns out that's basically what this thing does." *(holds up the sensor; pattern: introduce the mechanism)* |
| **H3** | **Honest admission / barrier buster** (a lever this category never uses) | "Fair warning: the buzz on this thing is annoying." | "Fair warning: it's annoying." | Mid-slouch, the creator flinches as the sensor buzzes and turns to the camera unimpressed. | "Which is the point. Straps just sat on me while I hunched anyway." *(pattern: reposition the problem)* |

**Why these three:** the KB asks for three different entry points so the test teaches something: contrarian/skeptic (H1), customer-language pain validation (H2) and honest admission (H3). See `playbooks/ugc-script-writing.md` Step 2 and `write-a-video-ad-script.md` Step 8. H2 uses the strongest interview line almost word for word. H3 moves the barrier buster up to the hook because skeptics trust whoever names the flaw first (`barrier-buster.md`).

**Reserve hooks** (spares from the "write 6, keep 3" step; use them in the next round):
- "It's not a medical device. It's basically a nag." / text: "Not medical. Just a nag." *(honesty plus humour)*
- "Day one I hated this. Day three I stopped needing it to buzz." *(reluctant convert. Only use this if the creator's real experience matches.)*
- "Catch your reflection in the laptop screen. That's the posture I'm fixing." ⚠ Don't use this until "fixing" is replaced. Suggested: "That's the hunch I wanted to catch."

## 3. Full script: H1 cut (≈78 spoken words, 30 s)

To make the H2 or H3 cut, swap rows 1–2 for that hook and bridge from the table above. Rows 3–7 never change.

| # | Time | Beat (skeptic format) | Spoken (key words are locked; say the rest your way) | On-screen text | Visual / shot | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–3 s | Hook: doubt | "I don't trust posture ads either. So I'm testing this one." | **I don't trust posture ads either.** | Hunched at the laptop, sensor held up to the lens | Flat, deadpan, eye contact |
| 2 | 3–7 s | Bridge: lived experience | "My last one was a strap. Felt like a backpack I couldn't take off." | "Like a backpack I couldn't take off" | Quick cut: creator tugging at a generic, unbranded strap over a T-shirt, then tossing it on the bed | Wry, a little exasperated |
| 3 | 7–10 s | Mechanism (show it) | "This one's just a sensor. It sticks to my upper back. No straps." | "Sticks on. No straps." | Mirror shot: peel the adhesive pad, stick the sensor between the shoulder blades, shirt back down | Matter-of-fact |
| 4 | 10–12 s | Set up the test | "Supposedly it buzzes if I slouch for ten seconds. Let's see." | "Claim: slouch 10 s → buzz" | Back at the desk. Phone timer visible on the desk | Skeptical, eyebrow up |
| 5 | 12–19 s | **The test**: one continuous shot | *(silent, slouching)* … buzz … "…okay." | On-screen counter 1 → 10, then **BZZ** | One unbroken take: the creator slouches, the timer runs, the sensor buzzes at about 10 s and the creator jolts upright. **Do not cut inside this shot.** | Real reaction; let the jolt land |
| 6 | 19–24 s | Reluctant concession + realism | "Honestly? The buzz bugged me for two days. Now I mostly sit up before it goes off." | "Day 1: annoying. Now: I sit up first." | Creator sitting upright at the laptop; cut to their **real** app screen showing daily "upright time" (their own data, nothing staged or edited) | Grudging half-smile: "fine, it works" |
| 7 | 24–30 s | CTA: loop closed + risk reversal | "It's fifty-nine bucks, and you get two months to send it back. Don't trust me. Test it yourself." | **$59 · 60-day money-back guarantee** / end card: "Test it yourself." Small text: *Habit reminder. Not a medical device.* | Product in hand, close-up, then the end card | Calm and direct, no hype |

**CTA button / end-card verb:** "Test it yourself" or "Get yours". Not "Shop now" (`concepts/active-cta-language.md`). It closes the loop opened by the hook ("I don't trust…" → "Test it yourself").

**Pace:** cut every 2–4 s *except* row 5, which stays one shot because the unbroken take is the proof. Pause briefly before "…okay."

## 4. Proof ledger (every claim → product fact)

| Line / visual | Claim type | truth_ref (brief) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| "just a sensor… sticks to my upper back" | Product description | C1: small sensor, skin-safe adhesive pad, upper back | ✅ |
| "No straps" | Product description | C1 | ✅ |
| "buzzes if I slouch for ten seconds" + live test | Function, **shown on camera** | C2: vibrates after slouching for more than 10 s | ✅ The demo must be real and uncut. Never fake the buzz in the edit. |
| App "upright time" screen | Feature | C3: app shows daily upright time | ✅ Creator's own data only; no invented numbers on screen |
| "bugged me for two days… now I sit up before it goes off" | **Creator's first-person experience** | Mirrors interview language Q3; true only if the creator lived it | ⚠ The creator must use the product for at least ~1 week before filming. If their experience differs, rewrite the line to match what really happened (`rubrics/copy-checklist-scorecard.md` G6: honest first person). |
| "My last one was a strap… backpack I couldn't take off" | Creator's first-person experience | Mirrors interview Q1 | ⚠ **Casting requirement:** the creator really has tried a strap. If not, use the H2 or H3 cut. |
| "$59", "two months to send it back" / "60-day money-back guarantee" | Offer | C4: $59, C5: 60-day money-back | ✅ The landing page must show both. "Two months" is the same promise phrased more simply (Sarah's "say one month, not 30 days", `risk-reduction-hooks.md`). |
| "Habit reminder. Not a medical device." | Disclaimer | C6 | ✅ Also works as an honesty signal for skeptics |

**Not used, and why:** star ratings, review counts, "thousands of happy customers", experts, studies and before/after posture photos. None of them exist in the brief, and inventing them is a blocking failure (`AGENT-GUIDE.md` rule 2; `rubrics/ugc-script-checklist.md` section F). The interview quotes shape the creator's lines but are **not** presented as customer reviews on screen.

## 5. Compliance guardrails for this script (health-adjacent)

**Banned words/claims (send to the creator as a don't-say list):** fixes / corrects / cures posture · back pain, neck pain, "relief" · scoliosis, spine health, alignment · "retrains your muscles" · "clinically proven", "doctor- / chiropractor-recommended" · "better posture in X days" · "permanent" · before/after body photos.

**Safe framing:** *reminder, nudge, buzz, poke, habit, notice, sit up, upright time.* The product reminds you; your results are only ever "I sit up more", spoken in first person and true for the creator.

Note: the KB doesn't document Meta's exact health-ad policy. The KB's product-truth check (`frameworks/static-ad-workflow.md` T6) says to check compliance wording per platform. Have legal review before launch. *(derived)*

## 6. Test plan

| Cut | Name | Learning goal |
|---|---|---|
| H1 + hold | `EVAL_RELIEF_reminder-not-brace_ugc_h1b1_v1` | Does openly voicing skepticism beat the others? |
| H2 + hold | `EVAL_RELIEF_reminder-not-brace_ugc_h2b1_v1` | Does the customer's own "poke, not a brace" line win? |
| H3 + hold | `EVAL_RELIEF_reminder-not-brace_ugc_h3b1_v1` | Does admitting the flaw up front win? |

- Keep creator, offer, CTA, landing page and length the same across all three cuts. One learning goal per test: **which entry lever stops skeptics**.
- **Next round (Body B, one change):** once a hook wins, test an "extended problem" hold: more strap and hunching lived experience before the sensor appears, moving towards Tether's ~2/3 setup share. The live-test format compresses setup to about 40% of the runtime. That's a deliberate trade-off logged under contradiction **X19**.
- **Reuse the concession:** "Day 1: annoying. Now: I sit up first." can become a static headline test (`skeptic-format.md` step 8).

## 7. Notes for the client

1. **Questions I need answered before we brief a creator.** These are likely purchase blockers, and I haven't made claims about any of them:
   - Is the sensor visible under a T-shirt?
   - How many adhesive pads come in the box, and what do refills cost?
   - Does removing the pad hurt or irritate skin?
   - Is the app free?
   - Does the 60-day guarantee cover used units?

   Once confirmed, the strongest answer can become a 4–5-word line or visual (`concepts/barrier-buster.md`).
2. **Ship the product to the creator 1–2 weeks before filming.** The script's believability depends on real first-person experience, especially the "annoying for two days" line.
3. **Cast a natural deadpan performer who has actually worn a posture strap.** The KB is explicit that skeptic comedy falls flat with non-performers (`skeptic-format.md`, HexClad example).
4. **The landing page must match the ad:** $59, the 60-day money-back guarantee and "habit reminder, not a medical device", with no stronger claims than the ad makes (`rubrics/ugc-script-checklist.md` G).
5. **Interview quotes:** they're used here as voice-of-customer language in the creator's mouth. To show them on screen *as customer quotes*, we need real, permissioned quotes. These came from test interviews.
6. **Don't call it a "posture corrector".** That's the category your audience already distrusts, and Evolve's rule is to never categorise the product (`video-ad-script-workflow.md`, Core Rule 2). Position it as "a reminder, not a brace."
