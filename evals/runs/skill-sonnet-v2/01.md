## Magnesium sleep spray: 30 s on-camera UGC script

**Angle:** the last five minutes of the night are the part nobody has a routine for. The spray gives you a physical "day's over" step. *(Hypothesis. No customer language was supplied.)*
**Core emotion:** the relief of finally slowing down (not "sleepy", not "happy").
**Core message (first 3 s):** tired body, busy brain, and a bedtime that's just you and your phone.
**Audience moment:** a tired adult in bed, lights off, phone in hand, 11:40pm, wanting to switch off and not managing it. *(Assumed. The brief gives no audience.)*
**Structure:** Story beat sheet (Tether), Pixar order: ordinary night, every night, the thing that didn't work, the change, how it went. About 2/3 of the runtime is setup. The product turn lands at 16 s.

**Assumptions I made because the brief is one line and the client isn't reachable.** Please confirm or correct each:
1. Execution is a real on-camera creator, vertical, for TikTok/Reels/Meta. If you want AI voiceover plus B-roll instead, the body needs restructuring (Hook, Bridge, Hold, CTA).
2. No sleep outcome, deficiency, absorption or "clinically proven" claims. The brief only says it's meant to help you unwind, so the script claims nothing more.
3. Every creator "I" line and every product detail I couldn't source is in [square brackets]. Nothing in brackets is a fact yet.

### Verify before filming
- **Product facts:** ingredient list and form (magnesium chloride?), concentration, scent, and the sprays-per-use and body-area instructions on the label. Replace "[two sprays]" and "[shoulders/calves]" with the label's instructions. Check what the packaging says about skin sensitivity. Topical magnesium sprays are often reported to tingle on skin, so ask the brand what to say so the creator isn't caught off guard on camera.
- **Offer:** price, any bundle, guarantee, return window. Quote it exactly. The CTA can't be finalised without it.
- **Claims compliance:** the script deliberately says only "meant to help you unwind". Do not add that it helps you fall asleep, treats insomnia, anxiety or magnesium deficiency, or that skin absorption is proven. Each of those needs documented support and legal sign-off. Check the product page and packaging against the final script.
- **Creator truth:** every creator "I" line (what they tried, how many nights, what they noticed) must be literally true for the creator. If it isn't, cut it or reword it so it is.
- **Disclosure:** add #ad / paid partnership / gifted-product labelling as the platform and local rules require.

### Missing inputs (what I'd have asked the client)
1. Real customer language: 5-20 verbatim review or comment lines, grouped as pain moment, failed attempts, desired outcome, doubts and the customer's own labels. **None was available, so the angle is a hypothesis.** Good sources: this product's reviews, competitor magnesium-spray reviews, and Reddit threads on trouble winding down. Rewrite the hooks and bridges using those exact words.
2. Audience: age, who, when. Is the buyer skeptical or novelty-seeking? If skeptical, keep H2 as the lead. If novelty-seeking, test a "new way to wind down" hook.
3. Price, offer, guarantee, and any real social proof (review count, rating) that could replace the experience beat.
4. Execution preference (creator, founder or AI voiceover) and platform.
5. Anything else the brand can't say.

### Test plan
- Run **3 hooks x this one body**, with the same CTA, so the test isolates the opening.
- When a hook wins, test a second body with exactly **one change**. Candidates are the narrator (a partner who notices, in place of the creator) or the story framework (swap Pixar for Skeptic: claim, doubt, on-camera test, reluctant concession).
- Replace the creator-experience brackets with real customer language as it comes in, then re-run.
