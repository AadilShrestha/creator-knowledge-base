## GripBoard: 40 s on-camera creator UGC script (Meta / TikTok, 9:16)

**Angle:** The board that stays put, shown on camera, from a buyer who first balked at the $79 price. · **Core emotion:** Relief at not chasing a sliding board while holding a knife. · **Core message (first 3-4 s):** "A cutting board that doesn't move."
**Audience moment:** Home cook, 28-55, cooking most nights, cutting something hard (a squash) on a cheap plastic board that skates and has gone scarred. · **Structure:** Skeptic-style, test-led demo (doubt → on-camera test → honest care caveats → offer). Three hooks, each with its own bridge, share one body and one CTA.

Why not the 2/3-setup story format: the KB's default for story-led UGC is about two-thirds setup before the product. The brief gives only a few real problem details (sliding, scarring, the squash, Thanksgiving turkey). Padding the setup would force invented specifics. A demonstrable claim plus a price objection suits the KB's Skeptic format instead. The product is in hand from frame 1.

- **B3** (after H3): "Then I turned it over. That's when it made sense." Visual: creator flips the board, bottom corners visible, but no mechanism explanation yet. Text: none.

### Verify before filming
No one was available to answer questions, so these are open assumptions to confirm:
1. **Creator truth:** the creator really cooks most nights and really owns or has used plastic boards that slid. The bracketed lines are H1 "[skated]", "[I've been through a few]" and H3 "[Yeah, I said that too.]". If any is untrue, swap H1 for a version that reads review 1 aloud on camera as a quote, and rephrase the others in third person ("Plastic boards slide. They scar up.").
2. **Reviews:** confirm the three quoted reviews are real, published reviews, that you have permission to show them, and that the quotes on the cards match the originals word for word. The brief gave no names, review count or rating, so none appear. Don't add "5-star", "thousands of reviews" or a count.
3. **"Doesn't slide" scope:** shoot the demo on the dry counter surface the product claim covers. The brief doesn't cover wet counters or other surfaces, so don't imply them (no wet-towel or soapy-counter test unless you have verified it).
4. **Care wording:** "once a month" is a customer's wording on the card. Confirm the brand's own oiling schedule before the creator says anything about frequency.
5. **Bigger size:** the brief's only size is 17 x 12 x 1.25 in. Review 6 wishes for a bigger one, which we don't use. If asked in comments, don't promise a future size.
6. **Squash on camera:** any hard vegetable works for the chop, but the shot must be real, with no speed-up, and continuous.
7. **Platform text:** keep the on-screen cards inside the TikTok / Reels safe zones. Review cards must be legible for at least 2 s each.

### Test plan
3 hooks × this one body and CTA. Judge on hook rate and hold rate first, then cost per purchase. When a hook wins, test a second body with exactly one change, such as moving the review cards earlier, using a different narrator, or opening the body with the care caveats ("hand wash only") instead of ending on them.

### Checklist result
All blocking items pass. Evidence:
- **Claims traceable and offer exact:** every product line maps to the table above. The offer is repeated word for word. The mechanism is the silicone feet, not "heavy".
- **No invented proof:** only the three supplied review excerpts are used, labelled as customer reviews. There is no count, rating or result.
- **Category limits:** nothing implies antibacterial, dishwasher-safe or any sharpness benefit.
- **Honest voice:** every creator "I" line is bracketed for confirmation.
- **Three levers:** pain validation, payoff-first demo, and price skeptic.
- **Other notes:** none failed. The two soft spots are the spoken pace in the first 8 s, at about 2.75 words per second, and the fact that the setup is shorter than the story-led default (explained above).
