# Upright Pro: 30-second UGC ad script (3 hooks + full script)

Prepared for: Upright Pro client
Format: 30 s, creator on camera, handheld/selfie, at a real desk
Angle in one line: a skeptic who is tired of posture-corrector ads tests one narrow, honest claim on camera: "it taps you when you slouch."

The client could not be reached, so I made the assumptions listed in section 6 and flagged anything that must be confirmed before filming. Nothing below goes beyond the facts in your brief.

---

## 1. Strategy in brief

| Item | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Audience state | Desk workers 25-45 who have seen many posture ads, tried straps, and don't believe the category | Your brief |
| Format | Skeptic / reluctant-convert: doubt, then a real on-camera test, then a grudging admission | The KB recommends this format when buyers distrust the category and the claim can be shown in one shot. Here, "buzz after 10 seconds of slouching" can be filmed in one take. |
| Core message (frame 1) | A different entry point per hook (skeptic call-out, strap failure, "a poke, not a brace") | The KB requires the core message in frame 1, and each of 3 hooks should test a different lever. |
| One core emotion | Dry, low-key recognition ("yes, that's me") that resolves into relief. No fear, no shame. | The KB flags shaming the viewer as off-limits, and the category is saturated with fear-based ads. Hunching is shown as the creator's own habit, not the viewer's flaw. |
| Journey stage / self (derived) | Evaluation stage. Speaks to the Actual self ("how I really sit"), not the Ought self ("you should sit better"). | The KB advises Actual/Ideal in cold ads and keeping Ought out of them. These tags are my application, not client-supplied. |
| Promise | Narrow: a habit reminder you can feel and see (the app's upright time). No outcome promise. | Your brief: not a medical device, no clinical studies. |

## 2. Three hooks (0-3 s, same body, different openings)

Each hook has a spoken line, an on-screen text overlay of 7 words or fewer, and a first visual. Each tests a different lever, so the result teaches something.

| # | Spoken (creator says it their way, but keep the bold words) | On-screen text | First visual | Lever being tested |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H1: Skeptic call-out** | "Another **posture thing**. Yeah. I rolled my eyes too." | "Another posture ad. I know." | Creator at laptop, mid-hunch, deadpan look to camera | Joining the viewer's skepticism first (barrier buster) |
| **H2: The strap failure (customer's own words)** | "The straps felt like a **backpack I couldn't take off**." | "A backpack I couldn't take off." | Creator holds a strap-style brace up to camera, then drops it on the desk | Shared failed solution / comfort objection |
| **H3: The poke, not a brace** | "I don't need a brace. I need **someone to poke me when I'm hunching**." | "Not a brace. A poke." | Creator at laptop, hunched, then pokes her own shoulder blade with a finger | The concept (a reminder, not a correction) |

Notes:
- H2 and H3 are adapted from your "known customer language" quotes. They come from your interviews and should not be captioned or labelled as customer reviews (see section 4).
- Do not use H2 unless the creator genuinely tried straps and found them uncomfortable (see section 6).

### Bridges (3-8 s, one per hook; everything after is identical)

| After hook | Bridge (spoken) | On-screen text | Visual |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | "But I do hunch at my laptop all day. I just never catch it." | "I never catch it." | Same desk shot; she straightens when she notices the camera, then slumps again |
| H2 | "So they went in a drawer. And I kept hunching at my laptop." | "In a drawer. Still hunching." | Tosses the strap in a drawer, closes it, back to the laptop slumped |
| H3 | "I hunch at my laptop all day. I just never catch it." | "I never catch it." | Same as H1 |

## 3. Full script (30 s)

Reading pace is about 150 words per minute. Hook, bridge and body total roughly 73 spoken words, which is tight. Read it aloud against a stopwatch before filming. If it runs long, cut from the "doubt" line, not from the demo.

| Time | Beat | Spoken | On-screen text | Visual / b-roll | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | **Hook** | H1, H2 or H3 (section 2) | Per hook | Per hook | Deadpan, a little tired. No hype voice. |
| 3-8 | **Bridge** | Per hook (section 2) | Per hook | Per hook | Quick, matter-of-fact |
| 8-16 | **Doubt (lived experience + realism)** | "A sensor that buzzes when I slouch? I figured it'd end up in the drawer with the straps." | "Figured it'd end up in the drawer." | Creator on a normal day: laptop, mug, ordinary clothes. No staged "glow-up" lighting. | Slight eye-roll, self-aware |
| 16-22 | **Turn: product + honest limits** | "It's Upright Pro. Not medical. Just a reminder: slouch for ten seconds, it buzzes." | "Not medical. A reminder." Then small overlay: "Daily upright time in the app. Battery: about 7 days." | Close-up of the small sensor stuck to the upper back (show the pad), then a clean shot of the app screen with her real daily upright time | Plain, no sales voice. Say "not medical" without apologising. |
| 22-27 | **Proof: on-camera test + realism** | "Annoying for about two days. Then I started sitting up before it buzzed." | "Annoying at first. Then: sit up first." | One continuous take: she slouches, counts to ten on camera or with a visible timer, the buzz is audible or visible (hand to back), she sits up. | Wry, a little reluctant. Grudging, not amazed. |
| 27-30 | **CTA** | "Try it for 60 days. Money back if it's not for you." | End card: "$59. 60-day money-back guarantee." plus a definitive button label such as "Try Upright Pro" | Product in hand, then end card | Direct, calm |

Setup share (hook, bridge and doubt before the product is named) is about 0.53 of runtime. The KB's default for story-led UGC is about two-thirds. I made this trade-off deliberately: the live buzz test needs 5-6 seconds and a skeptical audience needs to see it. If the 30-second cut underperforms, test a 45-second version that restores a longer setup.

### Read-through of H3 end to end (for sense-checking)

> "I don't need a brace. I need someone to poke me when I'm hunching." [pause] "I hunch at my laptop all day. I just never catch it." "A sensor that buzzes when I slouch? I figured it'd end up in the drawer with the straps." "It's Upright Pro. Not medical. Just a reminder: slouch for ten seconds, it buzzes." [demo] "Annoying for about two days. Then I started sitting up before it buzzed." "Try it for 60 days. Money back if it's not for you."

## 4. Claim rules (what this script may and may not say)

### Allowed claims, each tied to your brief

| ID | Claim in the script | Source in brief |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Small sensor stuck to the upper back with a skin-safe adhesive pad (shown visually, not spoken) | Product facts |
| C2 | Vibrates gently if you slouch for more than 10 seconds | Product facts |
| C3 | App shows daily upright time | Product facts |
| C4 | Battery lasts about 7 days | Product facts |
| C5 | $59 | Product facts |
| C6 | 60-day money-back guarantee | Product facts |
| C7 | "Not medical." It is a habit reminder, not a medical device | Product facts |
| C8 | "Annoying for about two days, then I sat up before it buzzed" as the creator's own experience | Customer language; usable only if the creator genuinely had that experience (section 6) |

### Never say or imply

- Anything about back pain, neck pain, scoliosis, spine health, "fixes", "corrects", "heals", "relieves" or any condition.
- That it "trains your muscles", "retrains your posture permanently" or has lasting effects. The brief says it is only a reminder, and there are no clinical studies.
- Any "doctors/chiropractors recommend it" line, invented stats, or before/after posture photo comparisons.
- Invented upright-time numbers ("went from 2 hours to 6 hours"). Show only the creator's real app data, unedited, and don't narrate it as a result.
- "Everyone stops slouching after two days." The two-day line is one person's experience, not a promise.
- Scarcity or urgency ("only a few left", "sale ends tonight"). None is in the brief.
- Customer-review framing for H2/H3. Don't label them "real customer quote" on screen unless a real, documentable customer wrote that exact line and gave permission.
- Mocking or shaming the viewer's posture.
- Saying straps "don't work" as a universal statement. Only say they were uncomfortable for the creator.

## 5. Production notes for the creator

- Casting: a real desk worker aged 25-45 who is comfortable with deadpan. The skeptic tone fails if it is acted too hard.
- Wear it for at least two weeks before filming so the "annoying for about two days" line is true. Do not film that line otherwise.
- Locked words: product name, "not medical", "ten seconds", "60 days", "$59". The rest is in the creator's own words.
- The buzz demo: one unbroken take. Don't cut away during the ten seconds.
- Cuts every 2-4 seconds are fine elsewhere, but use no filters or "glow-up" grading on the demo or the app shot.
- Captions: sentence case, periods rather than exclamation marks, text matching the creator's expression in each shot.
- Avoid skin close-ups with no context. Show the sensor on the upper back over clothing, or bare back only if the creator is comfortable.

## 6. Assumptions and items to confirm (the client was unavailable)

| # | Assumption or open item | Why it matters | Default I used |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The creator has actually used straps and found them uncomfortable | H2 and the "drawer" lines are first-person claims | If untrue, drop H2 and the "drawer" lines and rework the script around H1/H3 only |
| 2 | The creator has actually used Upright Pro for 2+ weeks | The two-day realism line is the strongest trust move and must be true | Required before filming |
| 3 | The "two days" figure comes from your fictional test interviews | It is one group's experience, not a guarantee | Phrased as personal experience only |
| 4 | Money-back terms (return shipping, condition of product, who pays) | The ad says "money back if it's not for you" | Landing page must state the full terms. The ad says only what the brief confirms. |
| 5 | "Skin-safe" adhesive: any test or certification behind it? | Shown visually only; not used as a spoken claim | Add to copy only if you can document it |
| 6 | Landing page content | The page must repeat the same honest framing, including "habit reminder, not a medical device" | Not seen. Please check the page. |
| 7 | Platform ad-policy review for health-adjacent wording | I did not verify current Meta/TikTok policy text. The KB flags compliance-sensitive wording only as a non-blocking note. | Script avoids all condition and pain language. Have compliance review before launch. |
| 8 | Word "trainer" in the product name | "Trainer" can read as a training effect | The script says "reminder" and never calls the product a trainer or corrector. H1 uses "posture thing" only as the skeptic's phrase. |

## 7. Test plan (suggestion)

- Learning goal: which entry point lets a skeptical audience stay past 3 seconds (skeptic call-out, strap failure, or the "poke" concept).
- Run H1, H2 and H3 with the identical body, creator, offer, CTA and landing page. Change nothing else.
- Suggested names: `EVAL_wry-recognition_poke-not-brace_ugc_h1b1_v1`, `..._h2b1_v1`, `..._h3b1_v1`.
- After a hook wins, change one thing in the body (for example a 45-second version with a longer setup, or a different narrator, but not both).
- I am not predicting which hook will win. The KB has no performance data for this product or category.
