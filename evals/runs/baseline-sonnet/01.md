# 30-second UGC script: magnesium sleep spray

**Deliverable:** 3 hook options + 1 full 30 s script (hook, bridge, shared body, CTA), shot notes, and a short creator note.
**Status:** ready to shoot once the 5 "confirm before filming" items below are answered. Nothing in the script depends on facts I had to guess. Where I needed a fact I didn't have, it is a `[bracket]`.

---

## 1. What I assumed (the brief was one line, and I couldn't ask)

| Assumption | Why | If it's wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer: an adult who is tired at bedtime but mentally "still on" (to-do list, phone, replaying the day). This is a hypothesis, not research. | The brief says "unwind", so the problem is winding down, not a sleep disorder. | Swap in real review or Reddit language. The hook lines are the easiest part to swap. |
| One core emotion: **relief / switching off** (calm, low intensity). The hooks open with a light "pain whisper" and resolve into calm. | One emotion per ad. A category that mostly says "get better sleep" leaves the real bedtime moment open. | |
| Creator is a peer who really uses the product, filming on a phone at home at night. | Native UGC. | If the creator hasn't used it, don't film this script. Brief them to tell only what they actually experienced. |
| Calm and deadpan beats hype. Warm lamp light, slow voice. | Matches the "unwind" promise. | |
| No brand name, price, offer, guarantee, reviews or ingredient amounts were supplied, so none appear. | I can't invent proof, offers or numbers. | Add them to the truth sheet (section 5). |

## 2. Hook options (pick 3 to test; each tests a different lever)

| # | Spoken (creator says it their way) | On-screen text (max 7 words) | First visual (frame 1) | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | "I'm not even *not* tired. My brain just won't clock out." | "Tired, but can't switch off?" | Creator on the edge of the bed, lamp on, phone face-down next to her, deadpan look to camera | **Pain validation** in the buyer's own words. Reframes "I need better sleep" as "I can't switch off". |
| **H2** | "This is the last thing I do before my phone goes down at night." | "My last step before bed" | Hand picks the bottle off the nightstand and holds it to camera | **Ritual / open loop.** The viewer wonders what it is. Product shown in frame 1, purpose withheld for a few seconds. |
| **H3** | "I thought a sleep spray you put on your *skin* was a gimmick. I'll be honest, I still raised an eyebrow." | "A sleep spray for your skin?" | Creator holds the bottle at arm's length, raised eyebrow | **Skeptic / reluctant convert.** Says the viewer's doubt out loud first. |

Each hook has its own two-line bridge, then everyone gets the identical body. That isolates the opening for the test.

| Hook | Bridge (about 3 s to 8 s) |
|---|---|
| H1 | "So I stopped lying there scrolling and made a tiny wind-down routine. Step one is a bit random." |
| H2 | "It's a magnesium spray. Yes, you spray it on your skin. Yes, I judged it too." |
| H3 | "So I'm not going to sell you on it. I'll just show you exactly what I do." |

## 3. Full script (30 s, calm pace)

Roughly two-thirds is setup and the product comes late, because the problem and the lived experience carry the emotion. Word count is about 75, spoken slowly. That is enough room for pauses.

| Time | Beat | Spoken (the creator's own words; **bold** = keep as written) | On-screen text | Visual / b-roll | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:03 | **Hook** (H1, H2 or H3) | see section 2 | see section 2 | see section 2 | Quiet, direct to camera. Don't oversell. |
| 0:03-0:08 | **Bridge** | see section 2 | none, or the bridge's key words only | Same shot, or a cut to a lamp and phone being put down | Wry, slightly tired |
| 0:08-0:14 | **Problem / lived experience** | "Lights off, and then my whole to-do list shows up. I've done [the tea, the podcast, the phone-in-another-room thing]." | "Lights off. To-do list on." | Creator in bed, staring at the ceiling. Phone glow, cluttered nightstand. Use a real mess. | Flat, relatable |
| 0:14-0:20 | **Realism beat** | "**It's not a magic switch.** [Some nights I still lie there for a bit.] But it gives my bedtime an actual end." | "Not magic. A cue." | Creator sits up, hand on the bottle, honest shrug | Slow. Pause before "an actual end". |
| 0:20-0:25 | **Turn: product and how it's used** | "It's a magnesium spray. I do [number of sprays per label] on my [shoulders / legs], rub it in, and that's the signal: day's over." | "Spray. Rub in. Done." | **Demo in one continuous shot.** Spray onto clothed shoulder or calf, then rub it in. Show the bottle label and the pour/mist texture. Don't open on bare skin with no context. | Unhurried. Let the mist be seen. |
| 0:25-0:27 | **Honest-friction line** (include only if true for this product) | "[It tingles a little at first.]" | none | Quick reaction face | Matter-of-fact, no hype |
| 0:27-0:30 | **CTA** | "If your brain's still at work at midnight, make this your last step tonight. **Link's right below.**" | "Make it your last step" | Product in hand, lamp light, phone in the other hand going down | Warm, direct |

**On-screen text rules used:** the hook overlay is the biggest and clearest element in frame 1, with one short line per beat and no second emotional claim. Cuts are every 2-4 s, with a slow voice and pauses on the key lines.

**Shot and light notes:** warm lamp light, low and soft, wider framing and a calm expression. Nothing saturated or fast. Hold the demo as one unbroken take so it reads as real.

## 4. Claim guardrails (what the script deliberately does NOT say)

The brief is for a supplement-like, body-care product in a regulated space, and I had no approved claims. So the script:

- states **no outcomes** ("fall asleep faster", "sleep through the night", "sleep better", "cures insomnia", "treats anxiety").
- makes **no absorption or science claims**. It doesn't say magnesium "absorbs through the skin", "works faster than pills", or that it's "clinically proven".
- cites **no study, doctor, expert or press mention**. If you want authority, the safe borrowed version is "experts say …", only if it is true and your compliance review clears it.
- invents **no review count, rating, testimonial, offer, scarcity or deadline**.
- keeps everything in the creator's **first-person feeling** ("gives my bedtime an end", "the signal: day's over"), which has to be true for the creator.

The lines that carry the most risk, and what to do about them:

| Line | Risk | Safer swap if the client's compliance says no |
|---|---|---|
| "It gives my bedtime an actual end." | Reads as a benefit claim | "It's become my cue that the day is over." |
| "that's the signal: day's over" | Implies an effect | "it's how I mark the end of my day" |
| "Tired, but can't switch off?" | Could read as describing a condition | "Is your brain still at work at bedtime?" |

Platform-policy specifics (for example Meta's rules on health claims and words like "treat" or "cure") are **not in the knowledge base** beyond a general "check compliance wording" rule. Have the client's compliance owner check the final copy before launch.

## 5. Confirm before filming (the client wasn't available, so these are open)

1. Brand and product name, and the exact spoken product name.
2. Label directions: how many sprays, where on the body, and when.
3. Approved claims and required disclaimers, plus any wording that must appear. Fill the `[brackets]` in the script from this.
4. Offer and CTA destination: price, bundle, trial or guarantee, and the landing page. Any urgency line must be real. The page must repeat the same promise.
5. Whether the spray tingles or stings on skin. If so, keep the friction line (it is the honest-admission pattern the KB shows working in supplements). If not, cut it.

## 6. Creator note (short)

- Cast a believable peer, someone who looks like the buyer's friend rather than a model, a nurse or an "expert". Don't dress them as an authority.
- **Film only what is true for you.** Replace every `[bracket]` with your real version. If you haven't tried it for a while, don't claim a time span. The bolded lines are the only locked words.
- Say the rest in your own words, calmly.
- Don't use an AI avatar or voice as a "real person" giving a personal experience.
- Deliver the three hook-plus-bridge openings as separate takes, then the shared body once, plus the demo as one unbroken take and a few seconds of b-roll (nightstand, lamp, phone put down).

## 7. Test plan (suggested)

Run 3 cuts: H1, H2 and H3 with the identical body and CTA. One learning goal: which opening makes the right viewer stay. If one hook wins, the next test changes only one thing in the body (proof order or narrator). Suggested names: `unwind_relief_ugc_h1b1_v1`, `…_h2b1_v1`, `…_h3b1_v1`.
