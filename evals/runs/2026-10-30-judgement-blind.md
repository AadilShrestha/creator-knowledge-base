# Blind judgement: UGC script pairs 1–6

Judged as ads, not as documents. Scores are 1–5 on Truthfulness (T), Specificity (S), Customer language (CL), Hooks (H), Spoken & filmable (SF) and Persuasion (P). CL is n.a. for pairs 1 and 4 because those briefs give no customer language, so their totals are out of 25. The rest are out of 30.

## Summary

| Pair | Brief | Winner | Margin | Total A vs B | Truth violations A vs B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Magnesium spray (minimal) | **A** | slight | 21 vs 19 (/25) | 1 vs 2 |
| 2 | GripBoard (rich) | **B** | slight | 24 vs 26 (/30) | 1 vs 1 |
| 3 | Upright Pro (medium) | **A** | slight | 28 vs 26 (/30) | 0 vs 1 |
| 4 | Magnesium spray (minimal) | **A** | clear | 20 vs 16 (/25) | 2 vs 0 |
| 5 | GripBoard (rich) | **B** | slight | 24 vs 25 (/30) | 0 vs 1 |
| 6 | Upright Pro (medium) | **B** | slight | 24 vs 25 (/30) | 1 vs 0 |

No pair contains invented proof: no fake reviews, ratings, studies or urgency. Every violation below is a small overreach, such as an assumed usage detail stated as fact or an offer reworded beyond its terms. A recurring craft failure is runtime: several scripts put an uncut 10-second demo into a 5–7 s slot, or pack 80+ words into 30 s while asking for a "slow" read.

## Pair 1: Magnesium sleep spray (minimal brief)

| | T | S | CL | H | SF | P | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 4 | 5 | n.a. | 4 | 4 | 4 | **21** |
| B | 4 | 4 | n.a. | 4 | 3 | 4 | **19** |

**Truthfulness**
- A (1): The body shows "a melatonin bottle on the nightstand" and then says "Now… Magnesium on my skin. Not another pill." That stages the spray as a replacement for melatonin. A's own compliance notes ban this ("Don't let the edit stage the spray *replacing* melatonin"). A bracketed "[feet]", "[after my shower]" and "[Tingles a little at first]" correctly.
- B (2): (1) "Few sprays on my legs" states the application site as fact without brackets, even though the brief doesn't give one. (2) The on-screen text "My 10-second wind-down" is unconditional. The spoken H3 claim "takes about ten seconds" is marked "only if true", but the overlay isn't.

**Specificity:** A's moments are filmable and odd in a good way: the phone clock at 11:58, "clock at 3:07 and her shuffling to the bathroom", and "Tea just had me up at three to pee." B has "No phone after nine? Lasted two days." and "12:04 on the lock screen", but all three bridges reuse "replaying every email from today", so the cuts feel samey.

**Hooks:** A tests pain moment, contrarian ("I didn't need something to knock me out") and skeptic. These are genuinely different. B tests an in-group question ("Anyone else's brain start a to-do list…", a familiar format), skeptic, and habit swap ("This replaced my midnight scroll"). The habit swap is the best idea in either doc, but its 10-second claim isn't verified.

**Spoken/filmable:** A has 71 words in 30 s, with room for its comic pause. B asks for delivery "a little slower and quieter than normal UGC" but runs about 85 words, roughly 2.8 words per second. Those two instructions conflict, and the read will be rushed.

**Persuasion:** Both use the same honest frame ("It doesn't knock me out" / "It's not a sleeping pill"). B's CTA, "If your brain works the night shift too, grab one for your nightstand", is better than A's "Try it tonight." A's turn, "Magnesium on my skin. Not another pill.", gives a cleaner reason to choose a spray.

**Money behind: A, slight.** A's body has the single most human beat in the pair ("up at three to pee") and fits 30 s. B has the better CTA and the habit-swap hook, but it is overlong for its own tone and states two unverified usage facts. If A's melatonin staging is softened, it's the safer shoot.
- Best line: A, "Tea just had me up at three to pee." B, "If your brain works the night shift too, grab one for your nightstand."
- Worst line: A, "Now [after my shower], [Brand] goes on my [feet]." (three placeholders make it unspeakable until confirmed). B, "Anyone else's brain start a to-do list the *second* you lie down?" (a stock "anyone else" opener).

## Pair 2: GripBoard (rich brief)

| | T | S | CL | H | SF | P | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 | **24** |
| B | 4 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | **26** |

**Truthfulness**
- A (1): "you've got **a full month to send it back**" and the overlay "1-month returns" restate "30-day returns". A flags the risk itself, but it's locked in the script.
- B (1): The H2 bridge "Yours is too light to stay put" makes weight the cause of sliding. The product facts credit the silicone feet; only review 3's opinion credits weight. B verified the Thanksgiving date (Nov 26, 2026) and puts 6 lb on a scale, which is good.

**Specificity:** B is stronger: "would've skated halfway to the sink by now", "Score the squash beforehand so the cut finishes in one take", and a kitchen scale reading 6 lb. A has the wet paper towel prop and the "audible thud".

**Customer language:** B uses reviews 1, 3 and 4 verbatim as labelled overlays ("Does not move. At all.", "Turkey juice stayed ON the board.") and builds a hook on review 3. A uses "skate" and "Doesn't move. At all." in the creator's mouth and keeps the review quote only for a backup hook.

**Hooks:** A H2, "I refused to pay seventy-nine dollars for a cutting board. So I tried to make it slide.", is the best hook in the pair. A H3 (wet towel) rests on an unverified habit. B H1, "Watch the board. Not the squash.", is a strong payoff-first hook. B H3, which opens on the "Heavy. Like, really heavy." review, is a smart objection-first idea.

**Spoken/filmable:** A runs 115–125 words in about 42 s and claims that's natural pace. With a 6-s uncut demo it will run past the 45 s cap. B budgets 90–95 words plus about 6 s of silent demo, which is realistic. Both protect the uncut demo.

**Persuasion:** A handles both objections out loud: price in H2, and weight with "Is it heavy? Yep. It's not going anywhere." Its CTA loops back to the hook: "Get yours, then try to make it slide." B's proof stack is stronger (demo plus three labelled reviews), but it never addresses $79 as an objection.

**Money behind: B, slight.** B's ad is more grounded: real review overlays on a live demo, a scale on screen, and a timing plan that will actually fit. A has the better hook and better objection handling, but it's overwritten for 45 s, and its returns wording needs fixing. I'd film B and steal A's H2 as a fourth hook.
- Best line: A, "I refused to pay seventy-nine dollars for a cutting board. So I tried to make it slide." B, "[My old board] would've skated halfway to the sink by now."
- Worst line: A, "And look at it." (filler; A says itself it's the first cut). B, "End-grain acacia on this side. Silicone feet on all four corners. And it's six pounds." (a spoken spec sheet).

## Pair 3: Upright Pro (skeptical market)

| | T | S | CL | H | SF | P | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 5 | **28** |
| B | 4 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | **26** |

**Truthfulness**
- A (0): "two months to send it back" is a fair restatement of 60 days. First-person lines ("My last one was a strap", "The buzz bugged me for two days") are flagged as creator-true and come with a casting requirement.
- B (1): The body line "Then you sit up before it buzzes" turns one interview comment into a general outcome for the viewer, in a category where the brief bans outcome claims. A keeps the same idea in the first person.

**Specificity:** A's H2 visual, "An off-screen finger pokes their upper back and they jolt upright", turns the customer quote into a shot. A also has the creator tossing the strap on the bed and a phone timer on the desk. B has the strap pulled from a desk drawer and "Okay. Rude."

**Customer language:** Both use all three interview lines. A's H2 is the verbatim "I don't need a brace. I need someone to poke me when I'm hunching." B's H1 is the verbatim backpack line.

**Hooks:** A tests skeptic, customer mechanism and flaw-admission ("Fair warning: the buzz on this thing is annoying."), and all three are distinct and native to the category. B tests pain, demo ("Watch what this does when I slouch.") and anti-hype. B's H3, "This won't fix your back", puts "back" in frame 1 of a health-adjacent ad, which invites compliance trouble and makes the viewer think about pain.

**Spoken/filmable:** A has a real bug. Row 5 is a 7-s slot (12–19 s) for a one-take, real-time "counter 1 → 10" plus the buzz and jolt. Add 78 words and the ad runs about 35–38 s. B solves the same problem honestly with a labelled 3× speed-up, and 64–66 words fit 30 s.

**Persuasion:** A's close, "Don't trust me. Test it yourself.", plus the small-print "Habit reminder. Not a medical device." is exactly what a burned-before buyer needs, and it pays off the hook. B ends on "Get yours", which is generic.

**Money behind: A, slight.** A has the best hooks and the best skeptic-closing CTA across all six pairs. The runtime bug is fixable by trimming two lines. B is the more shootable draft today, but it's flatter, and "This won't fix your back" is a risky opener.
- Best line: A, "Don't trust me. Test it yourself." B, "…Okay. Rude."
- Worst line: A, "Supposedly it buzzes if I slouch for ten seconds. Let's see." (fine as a line, but it sets up a 10-s take with no room for it). B, "This won't fix your back."

## Pair 4: Magnesium sleep spray (minimal brief)

| | T | S | CL | H | SF | P | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 3 | 5 | n.a. | 4 | 4 | 4 | **20** |
| B | 5 | 3 | n.a. | 3 | 2 | 3 | **16** |

**Truthfulness**
- A (2): (1) "a 10-second step" (core message) and the overlay "Last step. 10 seconds." state a duration the brief doesn't give, unbracketed. (2) H2, "Magnesium spray on my legs?", states the application site. Inconsistently, the body brackets the same fact as "[lower legs or feet]".
- B (0): The spray count, site and failed attempts are all bracketed. B makes no outcome claims.

**Specificity:** A has "Brain is replaying an email from 2 p.m.", "Now it's 12:20 and I'm doing sleep math", "wipes a hand on the blanket", and a list of failed attempts ("Tea. Rain-sounds app. Phone in the other room."). B's details are generic: "my whole to-do list shows up", "cluttered nightstand".

**Hooks:** A tests a vivid pain moment, skeptic and self-check ("Why am I exhausted all day and wired the second I lie down?"); all three are strong and different. B H1, "I'm not even *not* tired.", is a garbled double negative. B H3 is a 21-word hook with a weak second sentence ("I'll be honest, I still raised an eyebrow."). B H2 (ritual / open loop) is good.

**Spoken/filmable:** B's body is out of order. The realism beat "**It's not a magic switch.** …it gives my bedtime an actual end." plays at 14–20 s, before the product is introduced at 20–25 s, so "it" refers to nothing on the H1 and H3 paths. The H1 bridge promises "Step one is a bit random", and the body never pays it off. On the H2 path, "It's a magnesium spray" is said twice. A flows cleanly: graveyard → turn → "Fair warning" → behaviour → CTA.

**Persuasion:** A's "It doesn't knock me out. It's just how my day ends now." with the lamp going off is the emotional payoff, and the CTA "Body done, brain not?" mirrors the hook. B's "make this your last step tonight" is decent, but the misordered body loses the logic.

**Money behind: A, clear.** A is the better ad by a distance: specific, well-ordered, and with three hooks that each stop a scroll. Its two unbracketed facts ("10 seconds", "legs") are easy to fix before the shoot. B is the most compliant script in the set, but structurally broken and verbally clumsy.
- Best line: A, "Lights off. Body's done. Brain is replaying an email from 2 p.m." B, "This is the last thing I do before my phone goes down at night."
- Worst line: A, "Magnesium spray on my legs? I thought that was dumb." (an unverified site in a locked hook). B, "I'm not even *not* tired. My brain just won't clock out."

## Pair 5: GripBoard (rich brief)

| | T | S | CL | H | SF | P | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 5 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 4 | 3 | **24** |
| B | 4 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 4 | **25** |

**Truthfulness**
- A (0): A is careful, and explicitly avoids crediting weight for the grip. A minor process gap: "So I switched to GripBoard" is first person but not marked (C).
- B (1): The H3 bridge "My last one weighed almost nothing. That was the problem." and the angle "because of what it weighs" make weight the mechanism, which the product facts don't support (they credit the feet). Also note H2, "I never thought a cutting board could make me excited to chop onions.", which is review #2's enthusiasm put in the creator's mouth. B flags it as creator-true, but it reads as a borrowed testimonial.

**Specificity:** B's "so I'd pin it with one hand and chop with the other" and "And every cut left a scar." are the most concrete pain moments in either GripBoard pair. A has "Chop, shove it back, chop, shove it back." and "It's 17 by 12, so measure your counter."

**Customer language:** B uses reviews 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 (with labelled overlays during the demo and the groove shot). A deliberately keeps reviews off-screen as optional cards, so the ad loses its best social proof.

**Hooks:** A's three are sharp and different: pain ("kept skating"), demo ("I shoved this cutting board as hard as I could.") and price ("Seventy-nine dollars. For a cutting board."). B H1 is strong and H3 (the heavy board) is a good curiosity hook. But B H2 is a lifted review line, and its bridge "Weird thing to say. Let me back up." stalls.

**Spoken/filmable:** Both run about 103–107 words for about 42 s, a plausible fit. B's groove shot has the creator "Pour a little water or juice in so the groove is visible", which is a staged demo, not real meat juice.

**Persuasion:** A raises the $79 objection in H3 but the body never answers it beyond a spec list. Its CTA, "Tap to see GripBoard", is limp. B shows review proof over the demo and ends with "Go chop something that stays put." A's honest beat, "so measure your counter", is a nice trust move.

**Money behind: B, slight.** B's body sells harder: a vivid pain moment, the live demo with a labelled review on top, and a closing verb. A has the better hook set, and I'd port A's H2 and H3 into B's body. B's weight-as-mechanism line needs tightening.
- Best line: A, "Chop, shove it back, chop, shove it back." B, "Every squash, every onion, it slid, so I'd pin it with one hand and chop with the other."
- Worst line: A, "Tap to see GripBoard." B, "Weird thing to say. Let me back up."

## Pair 6: Upright Pro (skeptical market)

| | T | S | CL | H | SF | P | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 4 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 | **24** |
| B | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 | **25** |

**Truthfulness**
- A (1): The spoken CTA "Money back if it's not for you" implies an unconditional, no-questions refund. The brief says only "60-day money-back guarantee", and A's own assumptions table (item 4) admits the terms are unknown.
- B (0): "Slouch ten seconds, it buzzes. **Gently.**" is faithful to the brief, the concession is bracketed, and B deliberately drops "skin-safe" until it's substantiated.

**Specificity:** A has "she straightens when she notices the camera, then slumps again" (lovely and true to life) and "end up in the drawer with the straps". B has "shoulders rolled forward, chin out", "Creator on a different day (new top)" to show time passing honestly, and peeling the pad.

**Customer language:** Both use all three interview lines; A builds two hooks on them, and B uses one hook plus the body.

**Hooks:** A's levers are skeptic, strap quote and poke quote, and two of the three are quote-led, so they overlap. A H1, "Another posture thing. Yeah.", is soft. B's levers are poke quote, skeptic ("Yes, another posture corrector ad. I rolled my eyes too.") and live demo, so they're more distinct. But B H3 puts a 10-s count and buzz inside a 0–3 s hook, then repeats the demo in the body.

**Spoken/filmable:** Both break the demo clock. A puts a "one unbroken take… Don't cut away during the ten seconds" inside a 5-s slot (22–27 s). B puts pad application plus a continuous 10-s slouch inside 13–18 s. A's turn overlay ("Daily upright time in the app. Battery: about 7 days.") is too much text for 6 s. B's spoken body is tighter: about 55 words in 22 s.

**Persuasion:** A's CTA, "Try it for 60 days. Money back if it's not for you.", is the stronger risk reversal for skeptics, but it overpromises. A's doubt line is excellent skeptic voicing. B's H2 bridge, "This one doesn't hold you up. It just tells you when you slump.", explains the product in one breath. B also says "Not a medical device, just a reminder." out loud.

**Money behind: B, slight.** B's hooks are more distinct and its body is tighter and more truthful. "Doesn't hold you up, just tells you when you slump" is the clearest positioning in the pair. Both need the demo re-timed, and B's H3 must be rebuilt so the demo isn't shown twice. A's CTA is better, but it promises more than the guarantee does.
- Best line: A, "A sensor that buzzes when I slouch? I figured it'd end up in the drawer with the straps." B, "This one doesn't hold you up. It just tells you when you slump."
- Worst line: A, "Another posture thing. Yeah. I rolled my eyes too." (a soft hook that names the distrusted category without a twist). B, "Slouch. Wait. Buzz." as H3 on-screen text over a demo that can't fit in 3 s.

## Cross-pair observations
- **Truthfulness is consistently strong.** No writer invented proof. The violations are assumed usage facts that weren't bracketed (application site, "10 seconds"), offers paraphrased beyond their terms ("full month", "if it's not for you"), and mechanism drift (weight vs feet). Pair 4 A is the worst offender, with two unbracketed facts in locked lines.
- **The demo clock is the most common craft failure.** Pairs 3A, 6A and 6B write a "one unbroken 10-second take" into slots of 5–7 s. Only 3B (labelled 3× speed-up) and the GripBoard scripts budget it correctly.
- **The best ideas are spread across both sides.** The strongest single assets: 3A's "Don't trust me. Test it yourself.", 2A's "$79… So I tried to make it slide", 5B's "pin it with one hand", 4A's "email from 2 p.m." and 6B's "doesn't hold you up, it just tells you when you slump."
