## Upright Pro: 30 s UGC script (on-camera creator, skeptic format)

**Angle:** It's not a brace. It's the "poke" people said they wanted: a small sensor that buzzes when you slouch, with nothing strapped to you.
**Core emotion:** Wry, reluctant relief ("fine, a nudge I can live with") and not hype.
**Core message (first 3 s):** "I don't need a brace. I need someone to poke me."
**Audience moment:** Desk worker, 25-45, who opens the laptop and folds forward without noticing. They have already tried straps and hated wearing them.
**Structure:** Skeptic format (claim, doubt, on-camera test, reluctant concession), packaged as 3 hooks with 3 bridges feeding 1 shared body and CTA. Customer lines are used as supplied. No invented proof appears anywhere.

### Verify before filming
- **Creator history with straps.** H1 and the 8-13 s beat assume the creator really tried straps and found them uncomfortable. Cast someone who did. If she did not, cut "I tried the straps" and say "A lot of people who tried straps say they felt like a backpack they couldn't take off." (That only works if you can show the interviews are real and fairly representative.)
- **Two-day line.** [18-23 s] must be true for this creator. She needs to have worn it for 3 or more days. If not, use a shorter line she can say truthfully: "It buzzes, and yes, that's annoying at first." If she has not used it at all, cut the line. Do not film a "week later" scene that did not happen.
- **Shot continuity at 13-18 s.** The buzz needs to be heard after about 10 seconds of real slouching. Confirm the actual trigger timing on the final firmware. Do not show a buzz that was added in editing.
- **Sensor placement.** The upper back is hard to apply alone. Decide whether to use a mirror or a helper, and confirm the instructions allow this placement.
- **Guarantee terms.** Check that "60 days to get your money back" is the exact promise (full refund? return shipping? worn units?), and match the landing page.
- **Battery.** The "about 7 days" figure is on screen only, so the creator does not speak it. Check that the on-screen wording matches the packaging and landing page.
- **Adhesive and skin.** The script does not say "skin-safe", because it needs documented wording. Add it only if you have that substantiation.
- **Don't add:** spine or X-ray graphics, before/after posture photos, "fixes back pain / neck pain / scoliosis", "retrains your muscles", "clinically proven", "doctor approved". All of these imply medical effect or evidence that does not exist.
- **Missing inputs:** real reviews, return rate, creator identity, platform (assumed TikTok / Reels, 9:16), landing page, competitor ad examples. The 3 hooks are hypotheses. The customer language comes only from the 3 interview lines supplied. If you can send 10-20 more verbatim lines, I would rewrite H2 and the bridges.

### Test plan
- Run 3 hooks (H1, H2, H3) with their own bridges against this same body and CTA. Judge on hook-to-3-second hold rate first, then click-through.
- When one hook wins, test a second body that changes exactly one thing. Good options: put the demo (13-18 s) before the strap line, or swap the narrator (for example a partner who says "I got tired of telling him to sit up").
- If H2 wins, the audience wants honesty about the category. Lean further into that: the "not a medical device" line becomes the hook.
