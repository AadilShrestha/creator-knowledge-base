## GripBoard: 43 s on-camera creator UGC script (Meta / TikTok)

**Angle:** The board that finally stays put, because of what it weighs and what it stands on. · **Core emotion:** Quiet relief at not having to pin your cutting board down with one hand. · **Core message (first 3 s):** "Your cutting board shouldn't skate across the counter."
**Audience moment:** A home cook (28-55) who cooks most nights, leaning into a squash or an onion on a thin plastic board that slides and is covered in knife scars. · **Structure:** Story beat sheet (on-camera creator), using the Pixar story framework (every day, one day, because of that, until finally) without the literal "once upon a time". One shared body, 3 hooks with 3 short bridges.

**Inputs used.** All product facts, the offer and the six reviews come from your brief. Nothing in this script comes from outside it. See "Proof and claims used".

### Verify before filming
- **Creator's own experience.** H1, H2, B1 and B2 are first-person lines ("I", "my old board"). The creator must really have had a thin plastic board that slid and scarred, and must really feel that way about chopping onions. If not, rewrite them in "you" form. Do not film "I" lines that are untrue.
- **Reviews.** Your brief marks the reviews as test/fictional. Before this runs as an ad, replace them with real customer reviews you have permission to show, and keep the exact wording. Don't show "Customer review" overlays for text no customer wrote.
- **The demo must actually work.** Test the shove and lean on your real counter surface, dry. Don't film a claim the board doesn't deliver. If it moves slightly on a wet or smooth counter, say "stays put" only for the surface you filmed, or soften to "barely moves".
- **Juice groove shot.** Confirm the groove visibly holds a pour of liquid in the footage. Don't cut away early and imply more than it shows.
- **Weight and size.** Check against the physical unit: 6 lb, 17 x 12 inches, 1.25 inches thick. Say the numbers exactly as given.
- **Oil.** The script only says the oil comes in the box. Review #6 mentions oiling about once a month. That cadence is not in your product facts, so confirm it with the maker before putting it in any caption or comment reply.
- **Offer.** Free shipping and 30-day returns: confirm the return window and that the landing page states them the same way.
- **No size claim "bigger one coming".** Review #6 wishes for a bigger size. Don't hint at one unless it exists.

### Test plan
- Run H1, H2 and H3 with the same body, same offer and same CTA, so the test measures the opening only.
- When a hook wins, test a second body that changes exactly one thing. Good candidates: move the demo before the problem beat, change the narrator (a partner or a guest cooking at the board), or swap the story framework.
- Optional later test: a version that moves the product reveal later (about 2/3 of the way in) to see whether a longer setup holds viewers.

### Checklist result
⛔ all pass. Other fails and fixes:
- **Setup length (soft miss).** The story-led rule is about 2/3 setup before the product. Here the product name appears at about 20 s of 43 s (under half). I kept this because the real customer material supports only a short problem story, and H2/H3 already show the board in frame 1. If the first test shows weak retention, test a longer setup.
- **Swap test (partial).** "My board slides" is category-wide pain. The script becomes specific only from the turn onward (silicone feet on four corners, six pounds, two-sided acacia and groove), which is where the swap test passes.
- **Customer language used:** reviews #1, #2, #3 and #4 (hooks, demo overlays), #6 (the honest one-size/hand-wash beat).
- **AI-tell check:** no "game-changer", "say goodbye to", "introducing" or "are you tired of". The three hooks use different levers.
