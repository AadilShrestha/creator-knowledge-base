**<u>The 3 Testing Methods</u>**

Now, the biggest thing when it comes to “how you test angles” and actually turning the information and angles that you found from the avatar training into ADS.

**Is knowing that no matter what, you are NOW AWARE.**

So, if you are struggling to combine things, which hopefully you aren't, the main, main takeaway is that.

Now again, the point of doing the avatar work that we just did is so that you have Angles that are actually relatable for your Concepts.

But now the question is, how do we actually go about testing all of these angles that we have in an organized way?

Well, that's why I want to discuss the 3 types of ways you can “test angles” or, really, “test ads” in general.

1.  **Marksman Method - Test a lot of things at once to see what sticks, but in a controlled environment.**

<img src="_work/ecomtalent/evolve-start/media\81-the-3-testing-methods-docx/media/image3.png" style="width:5.43229in;height:3.96105in" />  
  
The reason why it's called the Marksman Method is that for some reason I refer to advertising strategies using “gun analogies” *(I’m an Call of duty player)  *
  
Most of you are probably aware of the two most common gun analogies, which are

“Shotgun” & “Sniper” → Which are things we're going to get into.

The Marksman Method is basically the middle ground of them both.

With this method, you are NOT as honed in as a sniper, but you're also not as careless as a shotgunner.

You are the best of both.  
  
**Pros Of The Marksman Method:** Much more fluid, less “we have to stay within these lines,” and you really are focused on making the best ads possible.

Mainly, you will learn which **<u>Direction</u>** you should go in FASTER when you lack significant data.  
  
🌟**<u>Direction</u> can be defined as: identifying the specific angle that you should be going “deeper on”** *(aka doing more research + iterating on)* **to create more scalable ads (*example coming below)***

**Cons Of The Marksman Method:** You aren’t always able to test things the best way possible, and it can be hard to confidently say “This is why something is working” *(although that’s hard regardless)*

Overall, you're not able to go as deep into a specific ad Angle with the Marksman Method to fully validate and test it. BUT, you can test more at once in order to find a direction that you can then go deeper on.

2.  **Sniper Method - Test within a strict environment to get closer to significant learnings as fast as possible.**

<img src="_work/ecomtalent/evolve-start/media\81-the-3-testing-methods-docx/media/image1.png" style="width:5.27604in;height:3.52211in" />

**  **
Now, unlike the Marksman method above, where you are essentially testing multiple angles at once,

The Sniper Method is ALL about testing different executions/styles of a specific angle.

Essentially, once you find an angle that you know is working, you then leverage the sniper method to go all in on that angle in order to create the best ads possible.

**Pros:** You will have a clearer understanding of what works, be more organized, learn what “works” faster, and have more confidence that you have validated specific angles.  
  
🌟**Sniper Method REALLY crushes once you gain a direction from the Marksman Method so you can get the most out of angle.**

**Cons:** Much less fluid, restricts creativity, can hold you back from making “the best ads”, most people are unable to stay this focused on 1 specific direction, and can put you into an iteration overload.

Overall, you're able to go MUCH deeper on a specific ad concept to fully validate and test it. But you can lack the ability to find breakthroughs on other angles because of how specific you are being.  
  
If it wasn’t obvious above, here’s how we leverage these 2 testing strategies at the agency: **  **
  
**→ The Marksman Method** is used 1st to find the direction of what angles can be winners

**→ The Sniper Method** is used 2nd to go deeper & find winners once a direction has been found

**→ The Marksman Method** is then used again when performance plateaus on an angle.

**When you have ONE Angle** *=* you're taking more of a “Sniper Approach” (same Angle, different ways to say it/show it)

**When you have MULTIPLE Angles** = you're taking more of a “Marksman Approach” (Different Angles AND usually only 1 way to say it)

**<u>The \#1 Main Takeaway:</u>  
  
Regardless of the approach, just make the BEST ADS you can.  
  
Then, when you’re processing feedback loops, be AWARE of the avatars you're targeting.  **

Nowadays, it’s nearly impossible to do perfect “single-variable” testing as a result of Meta’s newest updates. The swings are “too small” and not significant enough at times for Meta to care.

If the Marksman Method & Sniper Method aren’t making sense, here is another way to put it.

We currently test a lot of new angles early on for new ads that we make.

AND THEN

When we find angles that pick up traction *(aka get spend)* we go more into “single angle” testing, which also means making iterations & variations *(we will define these as well)*

*So,*

**Step 1:** Test multiple angles initially to find something that “gets sales” or “wins”

**Step 2:** Make hypotheses on why something is winning

**Step 3:** Test those hypotheses to narrow down why something is winning to optimize it more.  
  
The first hardest part is step one, and that's what we're going to get into in the next video.

But once you do that, steps 2 and 3 become the easy part because of the knowledge that you have as a result of going through this training.

And that's going to be your “super power”

Most people NEVER know why something is winning.

They take broad learnings like:

“Videos ads work,” or “This has a good hook rate,” or “We should reuse this clip”, or “We should test a UGC version.”

But when you process learnings, you're going to be able to identify who the sub-avatar is, what the angle is, and then be able to go and do more research on that so that you can write new ads that are focused on the customer rather than the structural components.

The faster you figure out why something IS NOT winning, the FASTER you can scale.

The faster you figure out why something IS winning, the FASTER you can scale.

**\#3 - The Shotgun Method - Test a bunch of random things at once to see what sticks**

<img src="_work/ecomtalent/evolve-start/media\81-the-3-testing-methods-docx/media/image2.png" style="width:6.21354in;height:3.44743in" />**  **

The shotgun method is when you just launch a bunch of ads and hope something hits.

The right way to do this is through TikTok Shop content or our Insense product seeding/UGC strategy.

You get a bunch of content coming in from a bunch of different creators, and you don't really have time to go through and strategize every single one. So, you just launch all of the content in your ad account & hope that one of them hits.

And if they do, then you can process learnings to identify the angle, and use the Marksman Method or Sniper Method to refine it to create more winning ads

This is actually something that we did recently to scale a brand to 40K per day in spend.

Unfortunately, I cannot share that exact ad, but I promise you this strategy crushes.

**The \#1 Mistake Brands Make:**

The **WRONG** way to do the shotgun strategy is WITH your creative strategy team.

DO NOT use shotguns in 2025 when it comes to creative strategy.

Don't just blast dozens of iterations or dozens of videos with small hook changes and just hope and pray that something hits.

Because that's how people who think “volume is the only answer” test ads, and it's not a strategy that meta is really allowing or rewarding, especially with its new algorithm.  
  
The shotgun method should only be used for content strategy, and content strategy alone.
