# How to write with labels & customer language - ✍️ Evolve Copywriting (NEW) · Evolve

_Source: `✍️ Evolve Copywriting (NEW)/34. How to write with labels & customer language_✍️ Evolve Copywriting (NEW)_Evolve.html` (Skool lesson page text)_

One of the biggest mistake copywriters make is writing in "copywriter language" instead of customer language.

You shouldn't use a thesaurus to sound smarter.

**You should use customer quotes to sound more like them.**

Your potential customers already have words for their problems, desires, and identities. 

When you use THEIR words instead of yours, two crucial things happen:

1. **Trust** \- You're speaking their dialect. You're one of them, not selling them
2. **Attention** \- Their brain lights up when it sees familiar language (my people)

There are 2 great ways you can leverage this idea for your copywriting.

  


### **\#1 \- Using Labels: Direct callouts in your hook**

**Labels: What are the “Names” prospects have given themselves/identify as. What terms do they “own”?**

When you're able to leverage labels and use the terms and nicknames that they use and identify with, you're able to grab their attention because they say, "oh, that is me". 

**It is relatable**

People LOVE to own terms and label themselves because:

1. It's their identity (ego)
2. It builds a sense of community (others are in my tribe)
3. The brain loves labels (makes it easy to communicate)

**You can either FIND labels:** 

"sohm" \-\-\> stay at home mom

"ecombro" \-\-\> you know this one

"night owl" \-\-\> person who stays up late

"grinder" \-\-\> Person who works really hard

"Wasband" \-\-\> an ex husband

**Or you can create your own** 

*(this requires adoption, but when done correctly, can crush)*

Peanut Butter Example from Sarah Levinger

"Creamunchy" (not creamy, not crunchy, creamunchy)

And now people are adopting this term

"Labubu"

Or if you're a knock off girly, you're a 

"Lafufu"

Look at your mechanisms and ask, how can I create a new label for something?

  


### **\#2 \- Word Swapping: Changing "copywriter" words to "customer" words**

Look for the generic \& polished copywriter words throughout your copy and replace them with the rough, specific language your customers actually use.

**Generic copywriter language:**

* "Stressed out"
* "Looking for a solution"
* "Lose weight"
* "Revolutionary product"

**Customer language (extracted from reviews/research):**

* "Completely fried"
* "Desperate to find something that actually works"
* "Get rid of this muffin top"
* "Lifesaver"

**When you swap words** copy feels native, not salesy. 

Make a bank of "customer words" or "customer sayings" and add them into your copywriting tool kit.

### **How To Find Labels \+ Customer Langauge:**

One of the most interesting places to find Labels is actually on [urban dictionary.](https://www.urbandictionary.com/?page=2 "https://www.urbandictionary.com/?page=2") In the prompt below, you will be able to find the instructions to tell Claude to go and find labels for you on there.

The second prompt that I have is for Claude to scour the internet for other customer language and labels that exist in reviews and on other websites like Reddit.

But remember, while doing manual research \& building avatars, you can ALSO be jotting done specific terms \& labels you see.

If you "don't recognize something" that probably means you should jot it down.
