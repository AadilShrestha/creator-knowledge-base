## **❌ Losing Ad — Iteration Playbook**

***Problem We’re Trying To Solve: The ad isn’t getting breakthrough levels of spend or hitting KPI.***

The majority of the time when you create a losing ad, it's due to the fact that the hook just simply did not grab people's attention.

Remember, a losing ad is an ad that doesn't pull significant spend and also doesn't hit KPI.

Which means nobody cared about this ad.

And if nobody cares, it means the hook is just not stopping them at all.

So…

  


## **Most Common Reasons Ads Are ❌ Losers:**

1. Hook did not grab people's attention (usually less than 30%)
2. The overall angle might have just been off (too niche, too broad, nobody cares)
3. Unaware → that was too much of a “wildcard” was a really big swing that just missed.
4. Problem/Solution aware → where the hook was not relatable or the problem wasn’t painful enough
5. Product/Most aware → Offer is just not competitive. Differentiation is not clear.

## **Ad Types That We’ll Do Iterations On ❌ Losers:**

1. 💡Ideation \- We may try the idea 2\-3 more times (must have evidence for new version)
2. 🔁 Iteration \- We identify what went wrong in execution (for winning ads)
3. ***🎭 Imitations \- We do not do iterations on losing imitation ads***

# **❌ Loser Iterations:**

  


### **\#1 \- Hook Issue \#1**

You need to make the hook more engaging (see the [EVOLVE Hook training above](https://www.skool.com/evolve-8484/classroom/9917539c?md=12282075479d484499b80b03e566e413&utm_campaign=skool_link_classroom&utm_content=99f29f7059f347559e88fdeddd3f5516 "https://www.skool.com/evolve-8484/classroom/9917539c?md=12282075479d484499b80b03e566e413&utm_campaign=skool_link_classroom&utm_content=99f29f7059f347559e88fdeddd3f5516")), and the visuals needs to work with the messaging or contrast from the messaging to create more curiosity. This is usually the most common issue in losing ads

**Example:**  
[https://canva.link/uv89ippva61wlzo](https://canva.link/uv89ippva61wlzo "https://canva.link/uv89ippva61wlzo")

Man looking in mirror **VS** Trying to inject a drawing with a needle \-\-\> *Whats more likely to stop your scroll?*

  


### **\#2 \- TAM Issue**

Your angle, messaging, or labels are too niche/specific. The ad resonates with some, but it’s too narrow of a group to scale OR it's too narrow in comparison to what you already have scaling in your account

*NOTE\* If you feel like it is a niche angle that can scale and add spend, just not as "far" as your current winners, then you can test these types of "smaller TAM" ads in a separate campaign or ABO so it's not competing with your "at scale/large TAM" angles. HOWEVER, if you are not spending at least $3k/day, this is likely cope.*

***Example:***  
Men on TRT not happy VS men considering TRT \-\-\> *Which TAM is bigger?*  
[https://canva.link/uv89ippva61wlzo](https://canva.link/uv89ippva61wlzo "https://canva.link/uv89ippva61wlzo")

  


### **\#2\.5 \- Broad Issue**

Your angle, messaging, or labels are too BROAD \& your copy is NOT strong enough to capture the attention of the masses. 

If you are not confident in your direct response copy skills, do not go this broad. You have to be capable of crafting extremely high convert direct response copy to grab \& maintain the attention of the masses AND get them to convert. 

So again, going "too broad" can definitely be a reason why the ad is a loser, especially if you are not a strong copywriter. 

**Examples of too broad/weak copy:**

*“For men who struggle with hair loss you need this!”*

*“Want to lose weight? This is the \#1 doctor recommended”*

### **\#3 \- Valence \& Intensity Issue**

Credit to Sarah Levinger. This is when your ad could be saying the right thing, but it just isn't converting because it doesn't FEEL right to the customer. 

So you may need to shift the the emotional tone of the ad. 

If the ad is giving a more positive feeling → try the inverse (fear/guilt)

If the ad is giving a more negative feeling → try the inverse (feel good/aspirational) 

If the ad is super intense → try dialing it down. 

 if the ad feels super boring (very common) → try cranking up the intensity

**Example:**  
[https://canva.link/uv89ippva61wlzo](https://canva.link/uv89ippva61wlzo "https://canva.link/uv89ippva61wlzo")

Negative problems (not recognizing self, acne, bloating) VS Positive outcomes (Stayed away from needle, double my test levels)

[https://canva.link/7wv0tkh46m1tjnb](https://canva.link/7wv0tkh46m1tjnb "https://canva.link/7wv0tkh46m1tjnb")

Valence shifted from negative (ugly) to positive (coolest)

### **\#5 \- Empty Claims In Sophisticated Market**

Another huge issue where so many marketers fall short... If you're making claims with 0 proof, 0 facts, and 0 anything in a sophisticated market... Nobody is going to believe you.

Even if it is exactly what people want, if you don't have anything to back your claims, prevent objections, or build belief in your ad, the ad, most of the time, will fail miserably.

**Example:** 

[https://canva.link/06nfgxp6zcmf09y](https://canva.link/06nfgxp6zcmf09y "https://canva.link/06nfgxp6zcmf09y")

Beat the dad bod VS No claim at all... just redirecting a previous belief.  
  


### **\#6 \- Bridge Issue**

The bridge is awkward/does NOT connect seamlessly between the hook to hold. 

A lot of times, ads do a great job of grabbing attention in the hook. However, the bridge fails to maintain that attention and keep the audience that just got hooked in, hooked. 

Sometimes the best hooks will do a lot of the heavy lifting for KEEPING people hooked in. But again, the bridge section is usually a make\-or\-break on how much of that attention stays on the ad.

**Example:**

[https://canva.link/1ku5jb8hu95mmtd](https://canva.link/1ku5jb8hu95mmtd "https://canva.link/1ku5jb8hu95mmtd")

Bridge ON losing is all opinion based → it’s super sweet, not satisfying, and feels like more sugar 

VS

Bridge on winner is all fact based → this is a cacao pod, it’s where chocolate comes from, it started off as medicine…

But from an iteration perspective, you do this by adding more authority and/or optimizing your script to prevent objections/earn trust.
