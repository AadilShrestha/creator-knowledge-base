**metaphors/similes/analogies strategy**

You use them to:

* Provide context for the brain to draw from
* Make complex science simple
* Reframe why old solutions failed

**Metaphors**:

Make a symbolic comparison saying one thing is another.

*"A glove for your foot"* 

**Similes:**

Make direct a comparison between two things using "like" or "as".

*"Fits your foot like a glove"*

**Analogies** 

Show a relationship between relationships. They follow the pattern: **A is to B as C is to D**

*"A sock is to a foot as a glove is to a hand"*

**^ The problem is we're not doing poetry.**

So my definition (and most non poet's definition) is when you combine metaphors and similes together to make a statement like the following:

*"This shoe fits your foot like a glove fits your hand"*

*Not a great example, so here is one more relatable*

*"Trying to write winning ads without copywriting fundamentals, is like trying to fly a plane without a license"*

  


***The metaphor version requires an extra mental step, but all versions allow our brain to compare to something it knows and is familiar with to create an association with your product.***
