## **💸 Spend Winner Ad — Iteration Playbook**

***Problem We’re Trying To Solve: The ad is hitting breakthrough levels of spend, but is not hitting KPI or is not scaling the ad account.***

The majority of the time when you create a 💸 Spend Winner it means you’ve done something right.

The algorithm sees the ad is scalable and begins pushing more and more spend to it. AKA pushing it towards colder and colder audiences.

Again Spend Winner means the ad is pulling breakthrough levels of spend. However, the KPI is below the account average and did NOT scale the account. 

Something to also keep in mind is that not all spend winners are equal. Some spend winners suck up a bunch of spend and get a 0\.2 or 0\.3x ROAS, while some other spend winners suck up a bunch of spend and get a 1\.8x ROAS when your target is a 2\.00x ROAS.

Either way, the ad is NOT scaling your account.

So, that means that either the CPM, CPC, CVR, or AOV (the metrics that make up ROAS) are lacking. So you need to look at how it is performing in comparison to the account and identify where specifically it is lacking.

This is also when you can start to analyze other metrics on the ad as well such as:

* Hook Rates
* Hold Rates
* Hook to hold (what % of people who got hooked watched 15seconds or more)
* Average play time
* Frequency
* % of New Visitors (Northbeam/triplewhale)

You can also start to dig into some deeper metrics using the breakdown tool to analyze:

* Age
* Gender
* Placement
* Platform

Most of the time, when you hit a spend winner and it does not scale, it means you are lacking **trust** (**Belief**) **→ fix by adding more authority.** You are missing **Urgency→ fix by escalating the problem.** Or you have a **Funnel Issue → Fixing by optimizing your landing page.**

You can usually tell an ad is lacking in those departments because it will have a lower conversion rate compared to the account. 

Again, what this means is that people are getting stopped by the ad and watching/looking at it (which is what Meta wants) → However it’s not convincing enough to convert those stopping.

^ Now, a big trap that a lot of marketers can fall into is going into "hold iteration mode," where the take this as:

“Oh my hook works, now I need to test it on a bunch of different formats and test different holds”

And they don't ever consider that the hook can actually be the ROOT CAUSE of their conversion and scaling problem.

Sometimes the hook itself is the reason why the ad can NOT scale/get conversions, because it becomes too hard to actually convince someone based on how you set them up.

Just because your hook is stopping people and getting attention, doesn't mean the hook works.

Yes, your goal is to get attention. But NOT just attention. Your goal is to get someones attention and get them to BUY.  
  


## **Most Common Reasons Ads Are 💸 Spend Winners**

1. The hook is misleading people → There is a disconnect from what people expect from the ad VS what's on the page
2. You’ve grabbed the users attention, they like your claims, but they do not believe them to buy
3. It’s going after a broader/colder audience but is lacking conversion factor → Can be a funnel or an ad hold issue.
4. The ad lacks urgency/strong CTA → Your ad lacks a reason for these COLD people to take action NOW. This can be due to poor copywriting execution around why this is a problem that needs to be solved today, and/or it is missing a clear CTA.
5. Do not see the value in your offer *(price to value is off for the audience) →  study price objections use techniques there to overcome / prevent*
6. You failed to close open loops from earlier in the ad \- viewers were intrigued but some felt unsatisfied / not fully sold after watching through

## **Ad Types That We’ll Do Iterations On 💸 Spend Winner**

1. **💡Ideation** \- If an new idea gets spend, we always try the idea 3\-5 more times. That said, you still MUST have a direction on how you plan to make the execution better
2. **🔁Iteration** \- We identify what went wrong in execution (for winning ads)
3. **🎭Imitation ads** \- If an imitation ad is working well, we will try the idea 2\-3 more times to test the execution of it.

## **\#2\.5 \- Broad Issue** *(spend winner explanation)*

just like how we mentioned in the losers and KPI winners, the ad likely has a angle, messaging, or labels that are too BROAD.

however, in this instance, for the spend winner outcome, your copy was likely strong enough to capture the attention of the masses, which is why Meta is forcing more spend on it... But it is not strong enough to ultimately convert the masses.

So in this instance, you definitely want to make sure that you apply some of the things that are missing below in regards to belief, urgency, and funnel congruence.

### **\#9 \- Urgency Issue 🆕**

Another big reason why spend winners never become breakthroughs is because they lack urgency. Not urgency in the way that you're thinking of: sale ends tonight or limited quantities left... You are missing urgency from a standpoint of: 

WHY DOES THE CONSUMER NEED TO BUY THIS NOW 

If you look at your ad and your ad doesn't have at least one reason why they need to take action on this quickly, or it doesn't explain what could potentially happen if they don't take action, you do not have true urgency in your ad.

### **\#11 \- Funnel/Congruence Issue** **🆕**

If you have found a new spend winner and it has a new angle from your other winning angles, there's a high likelihood that you have a congruence issue or funnel Issue.

What this means is that the ad is grabbing the attention and has people interested. It's going to a more generic page that doesn't complete the customer journey, or it is going to a page that is talking about a different angle or speaking to a different avatar, or has an offer that doesn't make sense for this specific person.

Which is why it's important to analyze your funnel and try to see if there is an opportunity to improve in order to turn spend winners into breakthroughs.

The majority of the time, the reason why spend winners aren't breakthrough is not because they lack CTRs, it's because they lack of conversion rate. 

So, by optimizing the post\-click experience, you increase the odds of improving conversion rate

### **\#12 \- Misleading Issue 🆕**

A lot of people believe that Meta prioritizes engagement over anything else, which, to an extent, is true. However, over the long term, it will always optimize for purchases.

So, ads that get high engagement will usually pull spend, however if they are not a breakthrough, they will not sustain spend and they will not convert.

The problem that most people have is that Meta doesn't get that signal fast enough so sometimes ads get a LOT of spend and have HORRENDOUS performance.

Whenever this happens, it usually means you've created an ad that does a better job of getting engagement rather than purchases.

AKA, the ad is misleading and your getting engagement for the wrong reasons.

This is EXTREMELY common with a native ads \& ads that attack controversial angles
