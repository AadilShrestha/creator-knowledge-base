## Page 7

If it’s about to be gone, we want it more.
Scarcity triggers survival instincts. It tells the brain: act fast
or miss out.
Use it by:
Highlighting limited stock
Time-sensitive offers ("Only 24 hours left!")
Purchase limits ("Max 2 per customer")
Pro Tip: Quantity limits often outperform time limits. People
trust a number they can see.

## Page 8

2. Social Proof
If others like it, it must be good.
Humans are pack animals. Following the crowd meant
survival.
Use it by:
Showing customer counts ("10,000+ customers served")
Using testimonials, reviews, UGC
Highlighting "Top Seller" badges
Pro Tip: Don’t just show 5 stars. Show the people behind
them. ("10,000 coffee lovers trust us.")

## Page 9

We trust perceived experts.
Not actual PhDs necessarily. Just anyone who seems to
know more than us.
Use it by:
Referencing expert recommendations
Using technical-sounding terms ("plant-based
biopolymer" = big authority energy)
Offering guarantees ("Dermatologist-recommended,"
"Taste guarantee")
Pro Tip: Choose your authority carefully. Not all audiences
trust traditional experts.

3. Authority Bias

## Page 10

9
4. Effect Heuristic
If it feels good, it must be good.
Humans make emotional snap judgments based on feelings.
Use it by:
Painting a picture of the desired experience ("Put up the
tent, brought the flask.")
Selling the feeling, not the feature
Pro Tip: The faster you make them feel something,
the faster they’ll buy.

## Page 11

If it's easy to understand, it feels more trustworthy.
Simple = safe. Complicated = risky.
Use it by:
Keeping layouts clean
Using short, clear headlines
Avoiding clutter and over-explaining
Pro Tip: If a 7-year-old can’t understand your ad in 3
seconds, simplify it.

5. Processing Fluency

## Page 12

11
6. Temporal Discounting
The brain craves instant rewards.
Later feels uncertain. Now feels good.
Use it by:
Highlighting immediate results ("Feel better today.")
Using fast-action language ("Start now," "First use.")
Pro Tip: Remind them what’s happening right now — not
"someday."

## Page 13

Whatever stands out gets the brain's attention.
Novelty and contrast force a pause.
Use it by:
Bright, bold colors against muted backgrounds
Unexpected visuals
Clear focal points
Pro Tip: In a sea of beige, be the red balloon.

7. Salience Bias

## Page 14

13
8. Goal Gradient Effect
The closer we feel to the finish line, the faster we move.
Humans are junkies for progress.
Use it by:
Progress bars ("You’re 75% there!")
Step-by-step visuals ("Pick size > Upload photo >
Confirm order")
Pro Tip: Always show how close they are to "done."

## Page 15

How you present info changes how it's received.
Same facts, different frames = different decisions.
Use it by:
Breaking down big numbers into daily actions ("3
sprays/day = 1 year of fragrance")
Showing cost per use, not just sticker price
Pro Tip: Frame it in terms that make the customer feel smart
for buying.
