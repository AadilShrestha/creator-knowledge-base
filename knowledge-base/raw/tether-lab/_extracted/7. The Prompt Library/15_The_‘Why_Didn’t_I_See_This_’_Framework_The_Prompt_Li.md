Instruction:  
  
I want to create 𝗽𝘀𝘆𝗰𝗵𝗼𝗹𝗼𝗴𝗶𝗰𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗿𝗴𝗲𝗱 𝗮𝗱 𝗵𝗼𝗼𝗸𝘀 using:  
  
\- Hindsight Bias – our brain’s favorite lie: “I knew it all along.”  
  
\- Bounded Rationality – the idea that we make decisions based on limited information, not perfect logic.  
  
These hooks should make people realize they’ve been missing something obvious—then hand them a clear, low\-friction upgrade they’ll feel smart for choosing.  
  
Step 1: Trigger “Wait, Why Didn’t I See This?”  
  
Use Hindsight Bias to expose a common mistake in retrospect. Make the reader feel like they should’ve caught it—but didn’t.  
  
Examples:  
  
• “You ever notice how nobody actually finishes their protein powder?”  
• “Of course the old way didn’t work. It was designed in 2004\.”  
• “Turns out, ‘add to cart’ doesn’t mean ‘buy.’ You knew that, right?  
  
Step 2: Show How the Brain Made the Best of a Bad Menu  
  
Now, leverage Bounded Rationality: remind them that they didn’t fail—they just chose from bad or incomplete options. Make them feel smart for surviving, not stupid for struggling.  
  
Examples:  
  
• “You weren’t doing it wrong. You just didn’t know there was another option.”  
• “You made the best choice… with what you thought you had.”  
• “This isn’t about willpower. It’s about better inputs.”  
  
Step 3: Offer a Simpler, Smarter Path Forward  
  
Wrap it up with a low\-friction alternative—something that feels so rational, it’s irrational not to try it now.  
  
Examples:  
  
• “What worked in 2020 won’t win in 2025\. This will.”  
• “One simple change. One giant leap for your sanity.”  
• “The smart choice isn’t always obvious. Until now.”
