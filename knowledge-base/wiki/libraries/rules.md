# Rulebook
> Every rule of thumb in the Tether Lab wiki, merged into one canonical DO / DON'T / IF…THEN rule per idea, grouped by job, with links to every timestamped source and to [contradictions](../contradictions.md) where Sarah's advice conflicts.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing or QA-ing a UGC script, creator brief, static, landing page or offer; deciding between two creative options; checking a remix of a competitor ad for known mistakes.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the draft (or the decision you face) + its domain (hook, copy, offer…) → the rules that apply, cited by ID (e.g. "fails H1, V1; fix per V2") → a revised draft or a decision with a source.
- **Rules:**
  - DO open the domain for the job (table below), apply every rule in it, and cite rule IDs in your QA notes.
  - DO treat **Ethics & compliance** (X1–X11) as hard constraints. They override any other rule and any source example.
  - DO follow the **Agent default** in [contradictions](../contradictions.md) when a rule is marked ⚔. Both positions are shown so you know the trade-off.
  - DON'T quote a rule as Sarah's words: rules are condensed. Open the cited timestamp for verbatim lines.
  - DON'T apply niche-specific rules (luxury, fashion, ingestibles, B2B) outside their IF condition.
- **See also:** [Ad teardowns](ad-teardowns.md) (precedents for each rule) · [Prompts](prompts.md) · [Hooks](hooks.md) · [Angles](angles.md) · [Rewrites](rewrites.md) · [Heuristics hub](../concepts/heuristics.md) · [Emotional zones](../concepts/emotional-zones.md) · [Three selves](../concepts/three-selves.md)

## Hooks & headlines
- **H1** **DO** keep static headlines to 4–7 words and ≤3 lines (subheads ≤2); cut quotes to one core line. — [Roast #1 0:15:00; 0:31:14; 0:37:06][r1]
- **H2** **DO** assume a 0.2-second read on statics and pass the "3-second / 7-year-old" test: the message must be instantly processable. — [May 7 0:13:29][m07] · [H101 e-book p.11][hb]
- **H3** **IF** the headline needs explaining **THEN** rewrite the headline; don't add sub-copy or bullets. — [M2 L3 03:30–03:45][h3]
- **H4** **DO** open on one hyper-specific situation instead of describing the customer. **IF** a founder/UGC video has a good script but high CPA **THEN** sharpen the opening to one situation; Meta is pulling too broad an audience. Make every trigger hook as specific as possible. — [Jun 18 0:35:53–0:41:19][j18] · [Jul 9 0:38:55–0:39:38][l09] · [Jun 11 0:47:37][j11]
- **H5** **DO** put heuristics mostly in the hook of video scripts. — [Jul 9 0:53:44][l09]
- **H6** **DO** test "This is how I [result] in [time]" against every "how to" hook. — [May 7 0:21:48][m07]
- **H7** **IF** the niche defaults to loss framing (weight loss, cravings) **THEN** test the gain flip ("keep it, swap it": "feed your cravings", not "kick"). — [Roast #1 0:58:42][r1]
- **H8** **IF** you test video hooks **THEN** vary them deliberately, one change at a time, so the reason a winner wins is visible; compare winner vs losers word by word. — [Roast #1 0:54:32][r1]
- **H9** **DO** use contrarian lines and put only half the message on the creative; the payoff goes in the primary text (open loop). — [Roast #1 0:09:27–0:11:31][r1]
- **H10** **DO** end key qualifiers or teasers with an ellipsis ("Businesses making over $25K a month…") in ads, landers and emails. — [Roast #2 0:51:32][r2]
- **H11** **DO** lift hooks and headlines from real reviews, comments and customer phrases (lived scenes, their jokes, their words). ⚔ Reviews alone re-recruit existing buyers: use them for language, market research for new-customer angles ([contradictions #14](../contradictions.md)). — [M2 L3 01:32][h3] · [Joanna 0:07:12–0:08:17][jw] · [Roast #1 0:46:04][r1] · [Jun 4 0:23:59][j04]
- **H12** **IF** a headline wins **THEN** find which *type* of the desire drove it (peer / status / inner confidence), test those types, and generate "a hundred different ways" to say the winner. — [Roast #3 0:42:03–0:46:24][r3] · [emotion specificity][c-emo-spec]
- **H13** **IF** the headline is ambiguous about who it's for **THEN** add subtext that pins the audience ("child's lunchbox", not adult). — [Roast #3 0:44:30–0:45:18][r3]
- **H14** **DON'T** use a headline that can be read as being about the model's body ("Not plastic" next to a full body). — [Roast #3 0:29:26][r3]
- **H15** **DO** call out what buyers have experienced over and over (failed attempts, "I tried X, Y, Z") and pair it with a mechanism, not just a promise. **IF** the category is full of repeat triers **THEN** put the call-out in the first 3 seconds*. — [Jul 2 0:07:17][l02] · [gambler's fallacy][c-gamblers]
- **H16** **IF** the benefit is in the future (insurance, supplements, fitness) **THEN** make it near, concrete and specific ("Next Tuesday your home is going to flood. Are you ready for that?"). — [Jun 4 0:17:41–0:19:46][j04]
- **H17** **DO** ask about *today*, not "in general", in observer-expectancy hooks ("Did you ___ today?"); **IF** cold traffic **THEN** keep it warm or funny, not judgmental; **IF** the product is a treat **THEN** flip accountability into permission. — [observer expectancy effect][c-observer]
- **H18** **IF** the ad targets the evaluate stage **THEN** use situational, first-person copy; **IF** exploration **THEN** general, high-level copy. — [Roast #3 0:34:44–0:35:44][r3]
- **H19** **DO** go "four levels deep" into the emotion behind the insight (defeated, not just "tried everything"); ask "why buy from *me*?" past "it's delicious". — [Roast #3 0:33:42–0:34:44][r3] · [Roast #1 0:03:09][r1]

## Copy & language
- **C1** **DO** write "you" copy: rewrite "we / I / our" as "you" (what to wear it with, who usually buys it, are they like me); keep "our" only for USPs (materials, manufacturing, process). — [May 21 0:17:56–0:18:57][m21] · [Roast #2 0:14:49–0:15:45][r2] · [Jul 16 0:24:09][l16]
- **C2** **DO** use the customer's own words ("nursing", not "feeding"); mine Reddit for verbatim lived-experience lines. — [Roast #2 0:46:20][r2] · [Jul 9 0:42:23][l09]
- **C3** **DO** replace claims competitors could also make with specifics only you can claim, and give flavours, blends and products proprietary names ("Naming is incredibly important"). — [Roast #1 0:05:15; 0:08:26][r1]
- **C4** **DO** replace generic audience nouns ("women", "customers", "sold", five stars) with typed people ("tired / frazzled women", "350,000 tea drinkers love us", "6,000 sock lovers"). — [Jul 16 0:39:20; 0:53:50; 0:54:50][l16] · [M2 L2 03:53][h2]
- **C5** **DO** name the precise emotion and go one level deeper than the generic one (what *kind* of confidence: "boardroom-level"); broad emotions make ads expensive. — [Jun 4 0:38:43][j04] · [Roast #2 0:17:58][r2]
- **C6** **DO** raise intensity with more intense words, not harsher ones (happy → elated, sad → depressed). — [Jul 16 0:17:47–0:19:00][l16] · [emotion specificity][c-emo-spec]
- **C7** **DO** use action verbs as "emotional accelerators" to raise intensity; **IF** the target emotion (e.g. empowerment) isn't being said **THEN** shift the verbs first. — [M3 L5 01:03][v5] · [M3 L6 01:46][v6]
- **C8** **DO** write to existing behaviour ("It's okay if the meal wasn't great, because you have dessert"), not to what you wish they did. — [Roast #1 0:08:26][r1]
- **C9** **DO** match the language level to the buyer's education, not to how simple the product is; **IF** targeting higher-income buyers **THEN** raise the reading level and verbs ("scale your brand"). ⚔ CreativeOS's Copy Grader advice is "first-grade level" so anyone can understand it (see [contradictions](../contradictions.md) row 20; default: simple words, elevated register for premium buyers). — [Roast #1 0:53:28][r1] · [Jun 18 0:15:40][j18] · [M2 CreativeOS 03:50][hc]
- **C10** **DO** lead with the emotion and justify with the feature ("delicious piece of calm"). **IF** fashion or home decor **THEN** lead with the product image / features / use case; emotion attaches after purchase. — [May 7 0:45:16–0:47:20][m07] · [Jun 11 0:14:51][j11] · [Roast #2 0:22:16][r2]
- **C11** **DO** state what the product solves emotionally (relief from social anxiety), not only the activity. — [May 21 0:24:20–0:25:31][m21]
- **C12** **DON'T** say "unique" or "fun flavours" when the image or a specific can show it: show, don't tell. — [Roast #1 0:07:22][r1]
- **C13** **DO** check every metaphor for logic ("blind spots" → *uncover*, not cover). — [Roast #1 0:26:02][r1]
- **C14** **DON'T** stack claims that contradict each other ("6 Day Results" vs "30 Doses") or reference something before introducing it ("Discount applied automatically" before any discount); order messages logically. — [Jul 16 0:55:36; 0:58:45; 0:59:20][l16] · [cognitive dissonance][c-dissonance]
- **C15** **IF** a bold result claim comes from a listicle or testimonial **THEN** keep it only if real and add "*not typical results". — [Jul 16 0:56:36][l16]
- **C16** **DON'T** call it a subscription; say "auto ship and save". **DO** say "one month" rather than "30 days" for return windows, and lengthen return windows. — [Jul 2 0:49:23–0:50:14; 0:54:24–0:54:43][l02]
- **C17** **IF** buyers want speed **THEN** "30 days"; **IF** they want quality **THEN** "six weeks". — [Jul 2 0:32:40–0:34:34][l02]

## Emotion & zones
- **E1** **IF** an ad has a good offer, strong headline and polished creative but won't convert **THEN** diagnose its emotional tone before anything else. **DON'T** answer a "should-work" flop with new targeting, a GIF version, a new UGC creator or "70 new hooks". — [M3 L1 00:55–01:08][v1]
- **E2** **DO** diagnose in this order: positive or negative? high or low intensity? the customer's state at exposure? what should they feel instead? Locate the core message in the headline and image. Assume distracted "dopamine scrolling" unless you know better. — [M3 L4 00:42–02:41][v4] · [M3 L6 01:28][v6]
- **E3** **DO** choose the zone that matches the audience's emotional context at exposure; no zone is wrong in itself. Zone 1 when they should feel okay inside; Zone 2 to make them act or for launches; Zone 4 for limited-time offers and health/safety, with care; **IF** the category is saturated **THEN** avoid Zone 3 ("a lot of marketers live" there); **IF** buyers are thoughtful **THEN** Zone 3 soft problem awareness fits. — [M3 L3 01:54–04:40][v3] · [V&I e-book p.7][vb]
- **E4** **DO** treat valence (what emotion) and intensity (how hard) as separate decisions: high intensity creates urgency and momentum; low intensity builds trust, recognition and reflection. — [M3 L2 01:21–02:13][v2]
- **E5** **IF** buyers are stressed and want fast solutions **THEN** a calm Zone 1 ad gets skipped; **IF** the product is high-trust or luxury **THEN** high-energy promo styling feels cheap; **IF** customers feel heavy emotion about the problem **THEN** check the ad isn't under-emotional. — [M3 L4 04:13–05:54][v4] · [V&I e-book p.8][vb]
- **E6** **DON'T** scrap a misfiring ad; tune it. **IF** only intensity is wrong **THEN** keep the core message and change the delivery: verbs → visual tone → emotional promise. **IF** it causes anxiety where you want calm **THEN** shift the visual tone toward subdued; **IF** the intended message isn't communicated at all **THEN** shift the emotional promise. — [M3 L5 00:25; 02:00][v5] · [M3 L6 01:06–02:10][v6]
- **E7** **DO** decide which emotion you're selling vs which you should be selling: Sarah's "most important" lever. **IF** a calm testimonial sells an energy product **THEN** sell stimulation / instant reward instead of indulgence. — [M3 L5 03:51; 05:41][v5]
- **E8** **IF** you're ~10% off on the map **THEN** expect the ad to "feel completely wrong"; fine-tune, don't rebuild. — [M3 L5 05:17][v5]
- **E9** **DO** brief visuals to the zone: Z1 morning light, soft pastels, gentle copy; Z2 bold type, energy visuals, verbs that punch; Z3 sighs, subtle sarcasm, tension before the solution (aim for longing, not bad feelings); Z4 harsh light, high contrast, a "visually wrong" scroll-stopper. — [V&I e-book pp.6–7, p.10][vb] · [M3 L5 07:59; 08:23][v5]
- **E10** **DON'T** run static emotions in a dynamic feed: design emotional arcs and "test all four… as often as you can", simultaneously. **DON'T** stop testing the other zones once one wins; zones stop working when seasons, life or the market change. — [M3 L5 09:18][v5] · [Jun 11 0:09:27; 0:17:00–0:19:08][j11]
- **E11** **DO** tag every ad in a competitor's library (or your own account) by zone. **IF** nearly all share one zone (≥80%* as a working threshold; Sarah said "every single one") **THEN** that's emotional stagnation, and the empty zones are your cheapest differentiation. — [Jun 11 0:07:22–0:08:23][j11] · [emotional stagnation][c-stagnation]
- **E12** **DON'T** mistake a format change for an emotional change ("a new format ≠ a new emotion"). — [Jun 11 0:05:19–0:07:22][j11]
- **E13** **IF** the category is uniformly aspirational (e.g. women's hair loss) **THEN** test Zones 3/4 **AND** "always, always, always end it with hope". — [Jun 4 0:28:12–0:30:17][j04]
- **E14** **DON'T** make people feel bad, trigger trauma or a negative sense of self; elicit the emotion already attached to their experience. **IF** you go negative **THEN** aim it at an industry belief the customer already holds and flatter them for noticing ("you're smart for noticing"). — [M3 L3 03:51–04:05][v3] · [Jun 11 0:20:12–0:21:13][j11]
- **E15** **DON'T** default to all-positive: pull "a little bit of a negative emotion", kindly, to show people they don't have to stay stuck. — [May 7 0:36:43–0:38:00][m07]
- **E16** **IF** a brand refuses negative valence **THEN** diversify within the positive side (Zone 1 vs Zone 2) and say the winner "a hundred different ways"; expression "doesn't have to come across in just text". — [Roast #3 0:42:03–0:43:07][r3]
- **E17** **DO** communicate one core emotion per ad: one headline, one CTA; supporting elements only support. ⚔ "Blended emotions do really well" (E18). Agent default: one emotion per ad; blend only two emotions that each won alone ([contradictions #5](../contradictions.md)). — [Roast #1 0:41:55; 0:43:41][r1] · [one core emotion per ad][c-one-emotion]
- **E18** **IF** two emotions win separately **THEN** blend them (headline from one, subhead from the other) and test the blend as a new campaign. **DON'T** blend untested emotions or stack two equal-weight headlines. — [Jun 4 0:46:18–0:48:21][j04] · [blended emotions][c-blended]
- **E19** **DO** map reviews to the nine mindstate emotions, keep the top ~3 plus any outlier, and write ~3 nuanced ads per emotion. ⚔ One core *brand* emotion (B2) vs ~3 per industry: default = one brand emotion, ~3 ad emotions ([contradictions #5b](../contradictions.md)). — [Jun 4 0:36:36–0:37:39][j04]
- **E20** **IF** an ad is expensive **THEN** check whether its emotion is generic before blaming the creative. — [Jun 4 0:38:43][j04]
- **E21** **DO** treat a guarantee or promise as the *security* emotion. — [Jun 4 0:48:21][j04]
- **E22** **DO** match the ad's emotional temperature to the customer's current moment and how they feel about the problem: optimise for feel, not words ("most marketers are going to optimize words"). — [M3 L1 01:45–02:01][v1]
- **E23** **DO** diagnose winners too, to find the small tweak worth carrying forward; keep testing with the zone prompts until it lands. — [M3 L6 00:12; 02:19][v6]
- **E24** **DON'T** use Zone 4 manipulatively: "this one can backfire". Map to resonate, not to manipulate. — [V&I e-book p.7; p.11][vb] · [M3 L3 03:40–03:48][v3]

## Selves & identity
- **S1** **DO** apply psychology in this order: identity → emotion → behaviour/tactics (heuristics, arrows, buttons, colours, format). ⚔ Jun 18: "I start with heuristics". Default: plan identity first, ship heuristic tweaks first ([contradictions #4](../contradictions.md)). — [May 7 0:06:14–0:09:40][m07] · [Jun 18 0:23:02][j18]
- **S2** **IF** ads fatigue fast and CPA won't drop **THEN** check whether you're selling a use case instead of an identity; pair identity with a behaviour insight (40 bottles on the counter). — [May 7 0:17:51–0:18:40][m07]
- **S3** **DO** identify which self is active (Actual = who they are now, Ideal = who they want to become, Ought = who they should be), then position the product around that tension and speak to the internal dialogue; the gap between selves drives buying. — [M4 L4 01:04; 06:14][s4]
- **S4** **DON'T** "rinse and repeat one self". **IF** most ads speak to the achiever/dreamer (Ideal) **THEN** expect the segment to "dry up": add Actual and Ought variants; don't echo the aspirational promise you've used for years. — [M4 L4 06:30][s4] · [M4 L3 00:58–01:28; 04:14][s3]
- **S5** **IF** a few ads or angles carry most of the spend **THEN** start self-shifted variants now and rotate before fatigue ("slamming on the brakes after you've already hit the ice"). **DON'T** reach for a new offer or landing page first. — [M4 L1 01:26–03:33][s1]
- **S6** **DO** diagnose with a small stratified sample (9 ads: 3 top, 3 mid, 3 flop), scoring copy *and* images 0–10 per self; for video, transcribe scripts or screenshot key clips. **IF** one self dominates **THEN** write the next variants for the other two. — [M4 L2 01:01–02:40][s2] · [M4 L5 03:44–05:09][s5]
- **S7** **DON'T** confuse "the self we keep writing for" with "the self customers buy in", and don't feel bad about over-indexing: it's normal bias. Fix it. — [M4 L2 02:40][s2] · [M4 L6 08:23][s6]
- **S8** **DO** start self rewrites from an existing winner, never a blank page; keep offer, CTA, landing page and price identical and test one self at a time. — [M4 L7 00:18–00:57][s7]
- **S9** **IF** a core message has worked consistently for a long time **THEN** treat it as brand and don't re-self it; **IF** the same message runs across the account and works only sometimes **THEN** re-self it. — [M4 L7 02:43–03:20][s7]
- **S10** **DO** match the proof type to the self: authority/press for Ought, peer reviews for Actual. — [M4 L7 02:30][s7]
- **S11** **DO** test selves in one campaign, one ad set, three variants, everything else identical, and read identity signals, not just conversions: Actual → lower CTR, higher AOV, stable; Ideal → high CTR, low AOV, fast clicks, little commitment; Ought → slow start (~2 weeks), then strong intent and high LTV. — [M4 L8 00:41–03:25][s8]
- **S12** **DON'T** scale on a single identity ("a pretty unsafe way to scale"); loop diagnose → reframe for a new emotional entry point → test. — [M4 L8 04:58–05:08][s8]
- **S13** **DON'T** lead cold ads with Ought "should" messaging; save it for prospects who've qualified (calls, retargeting, email). ⚔ The e-book maps Awareness to Ideal/Ought ([contradictions #17](../contradictions.md)). — [Jun 18 0:08:25–0:10:29][j18]
- **S14** **DO** write Actual-self copy as present-moment situations ("checked the dashboard 20 times today") and end with "that's okay, just change one thing". — [Jun 18 0:16:42–0:17:43][j18]
- **S15** **DO** layer self → emotion (valence and intensity) to build "a crap ton of working ads instead of just one to two at a time". — [M4 L6 02:30][s6]
- **S16** **IF** you have a top-of-funnel acquisition problem **THEN** you're probably writing for a mid-funnel buyer: widen the identities. — [May 7 0:20:05–0:20:34][m07]
- **S17** **DON'T** write off a purchase as impulse; find the two identities a triggering event connected. **DO** default to humour as the stackable second identity. — [May 21 0:45:03–0:47:29][m21]
- **S18** **DO** treat the customer as "hundreds of different avatars… wrapped into one person" and choose which identity to activate. — [M4 L3 02:06][s3]
- **S19** **DO** use an identity call-out instead of an offer to change who the algorithm finds ("50% off" → "You're the type of pet parent who cares about what's inside their pet supplements"). — [May 21 0:20:59–0:21:20][m21]
- **S20** **IF** the audience has been dismissed by experts (e.g. 30+ women buying supplements) **THEN** validate their own judgement first ("your gut feeling is right… you know your body best"), then use authority only of a type they trust; add a low-risk entry (trial sample, checklist). **IF** buyers research for months **THEN** affirm their savvy and give full information instead of discounts. — [May 21 0:42:49–0:43:57][m21] · [Jun 4 0:35:35][j04] · [validation over authority][c-validation]
- **S21** **IF** the buyer is a serial switcher **THEN** sell the end of switching, backed by a guarantee; **DON'T** frame the pile of products as the customer's failure, frame it as the category's. — [Jun 4 0:47:21][j04] · [sunk-cost fallacy][c-sunk]
- **S22** **IF** you want a new (younger) audience to switch **THEN** question their attachment and identity ("Are you actually a matcha girl…?"), don't list benefits; **IF** the market is brand-loyal **THEN** give an explicit reason to switch. — [Jun 18 1:03:25–1:05:31][j18] · [May 21 0:15:36][m21]

## Heuristics use
**Choosing and stacking**
- **HE1** **DO** stack 2–3 heuristics per ad, with processing fluency as the base, picked from the worksheet; **DON'T** use them standalone. — [M2 L5 07:17; 08:17][h5] · [M2 L4 07:06][h4] · [H101 e-book p.17][hb]
- **HE2** **DO** treat heuristics as supporting characters for one core message: headline first, heuristics second, format fourth. **DON'T** add heuristics until the base message is clear; they only help if they cut load. — [May 7 0:13:03; 0:15:48; 0:38:29][m07] · [Roast #3 0:17:51][r3]
- **HE3** **DON'T** stop at scarcity, social proof and authority ("very expected in DTC"). **DO** use The Decision Lab bias list to find others, filter by industry, and test the goal-gradient effect ("every time I use that thing it works incredibly well"). — [M2 L2 06:04][h2] · [Jul 2 0:03:03–0:05:30][l02]
- **HE4** **DO** choose heuristics by the customer's belief system, behavioural fit with the product and the season (price-sensitive vs authority-sensitive periods), not from a generic list. — [Jul 9 0:51:11][l09] · [Jul 2 0:13:34–0:15:40][l02]
- **HE5** **DO** test one heuristic per variant on top of a proven winner, changing the headline only; statics are the cheap testing ground. ⚔ Jun 11: one headline per image is too slow; default: headline-only tests just to refine a proven concept ([contradictions #6b](../contradictions.md)). — [Jul 9 0:29:12][l09] · [Jun 11 0:51:49][j11]

## Coverage & gaps
- **Built from** 660 harvested rule bullets (lesson, session, concept, framework, resource and tool pages). They were merged into **318 canonical rules** across 17 domains; about **342 bullets were near-duplicates** of another (the same rule appears on its session page, its concept page and often a framework page). Where several bullets merged, every timestamped source is kept on the rule.
- **Included beyond the brief's domain list:** "Humour, characters & messengers" and "Brand, category & positioning", because many harvested rules belonged there.
- **Interpretation:** rules are condensed paraphrases, not quotes (quotes are marked with quote marks). `*` marks wiki inferences (e.g. the ≥80% stagnation threshold in E11, the plane generalisation in O11). Rules sourced only to a concept page have no timestamp in the harvest; open that page for its own citations.
- **Gaps:** few rules on static ad copy length for primary text, on platform policy, or on TikTok-specific formats; the course doesn't cover them. Several rules come from one member case and may not generalise (CF12 hair colour, O21 gift bags). The CreativeOS reading-level advice (C9) conflicts with Sarah's "match the buyer" rule; see contradictions row 20.
