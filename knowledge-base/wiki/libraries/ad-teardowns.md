# Ad teardown library
> Every real ad, landing page, offer and campaign critiqued in Tether Lab, deduplicated into 152 tagged rows, grouped by niche then format, with Sarah Levinger's verdict, the fix, the principle and a link back to the timestamped source.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** (1) tearing down a competitor ad, so you can find the closest precedent and reuse Sarah's verdict; (2) QA-ing our own draft script, static or page against known failure modes before it ships; (3) looking for a proven mechanism in our niche to remix.
- **Inputs → outputs:** our niche + format + the draft or competitor ad → matching rows (same niche, same format, same failure) → the standard fix and principle to apply, plus links to the screenshot and the timestamped source to verify.
- **Rules:**
  - DO start at [Failure patterns](#failure-patterns): match the draft's problem to a pattern, apply the standard fix, then read 2–3 example rows.
  - DO treat a row as a precedent: same niche + same failure → same fix. Then open the source link and check the timestamp before quoting Sarah.
  - DO copy the **mechanism** (angle, hook structure, emotional move, proof type), never the headline or asset. Rewrite for our product, claims and customer language.
  - DON'T reuse invented proof seen in these rows (Net Positive's "Only 9 sets left", ChatGPT's invented press for High Camp, Chase's "any number" badge). True counts, real reviews and real press only ([contradictions #10](../contradictions.md)).
  - DON'T read wiki tags as Sarah's verdict. In **Zone / Self**, "(Sarah)" = her call, "(wiki)" = a tag from the source page, "(Gavin)"/"(Darian)" = a member's own tag, `*` = inferred in this library.

## Failure patterns
Recurring "what fails" themes across all rows, most frequent first. **Occurrences** = rows tagged with the pattern (one row can carry several). Tags are this library's classification of Sarah's critiques.

| Rank | Pattern | Occurrences | Standard fix | Example rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **F4 Generic, unownable or untyped claims and proof** | 16 | Use specifics only you can claim (named flavours, exact counts, mechanisms); type the crowd ("6,000 sock lovers", "350,000 tea drinkers"); go "four levels deep" into the situation; own one phrase | #6, #7, #8, #33, #38, #48, #49, #63, #75, #80, #82, #91, #107, #112, #118, #135 |
| 2 | **F2 Cognitive overload: too many elements, messages or words** | 13 | One message; 4–7-word headline, ≤3 lines; weight the important parts heaviest and stack call-outs 1-2-3 on a grid; one hero/one slide; A/B remove one element at a time | #38, #40, #49, #50, #51, #80, #81, #82, #118, #125, #131, #132, #141 |
| 3 | **F10 Ability gap: buyer can't tell fit, time-to-result, how much to buy, or hits friction** | 13 | "Who it's for" micro-labels; time sequences on the product shot; quantities in the buyer's units (dinners, washes, days); bundles sized to real consumption; easy-math prices and $ savings; name the friction and reassure | #26, #28, #48, #66, #67, #83, #84, #93, #94, #127, #130, #148, #149 |
| 4 | **F7 Leads with loss, offer, feature or use case instead of gain, identity or one core emotion** | 9 | Identity → emotion → tactics; flip loss frames to gain ("feed" not "kick"); find the one emotion all use cases share; emotion first, feature as justification | #18, #20, #21, #22, #29, #34, #54, #68, #96 |
| 5 | **F12 Spikes, then fades or won't scale** | 8 | Find why it won (desire type, identity vs use case, lived vs funny) and test that; frequency ≈2 isn't fatigue: vary the product treatment, keep the format; give launches a reason and time context | #9, #16, #43, #54, #68, #78, #117, #118 |
| 6 | **F8 Brand-centric copy and passive, missing or late CTAs** | 7 | "You" copy (keep "our" for USPs); buying verbs (get, grab, order yours now); buy buttons wherever the visitor is likely convinced; definitive CTA in the ad copy; mirror the ad's message on the page | #41, #49, #72, #80, #81, #83, #120 |
| 7 | **F1 Image, props or page don't carry the headline's promise** | 6 | Read image and text separately, then together; put the promised scene in the image; give every metaphor a literal visual and check its logic; audit props for what they prime; match the ad's vibe to the landing page | #6, #38, #41, #70, #73, #81 |
| 8 | **F5 Emotional misfire: wrong zone or intensity for the buyer's moment** | 6 | Diagnose the zone (V&I prompt), decide the target zone from the purchase motive, then shift verbs → visual tone → emotional promise; luxury/high-trust → low intensity | #1, #47, #66, #115, #121, #138 |
| 9 | **F6 Emotional or self stagnation across an account or category** | 6 | Tag every ad by zone and self; if one dominates, the empty zones/selves are the angles; run all zones at once and keep testing the losers | #35, #36, #37, #47, #53, #101 |
| 10 | **F11 Fabricated, risky-borrowed or reverse social proof** | 6 | True counts, real reviews, real press only ([contradictions #10](../contradictions.md)); hide "0 reviews"; borrow authority only where a real link exists; replace fake scarcity with drop language + a real reason | #72, #83, #102, #103, #111, #143 |
| 11 | **F15 Visual craft signals low quality or the wrong context** | 6 | Sharp assets (recreate fuzzy testimonials); structured fonts for utilitarian offers, handwriting only with a character; colour on your product, grey the competitor; one dark product on a vibrant ground; body text ≥17 px | #81, #83, #91, #98, #122, #125 |
| 12 | **F9 A variation drops the element that made the winner work** | 5 | Static → video: the winning message is frame 1; clone the winning look before changing copy; attach humour to the product, not the meme; copying a format needs the performer too | #9, #71, #79, #109, #146 |
| 13 | **F13 Tone risk: shame, attack, crudeness or a misreadable pairing** | 5 | Move shame onto a story character or add a "no shame" softener; question the trend instead of calling competitors scams; check demographic data before dropping edgy lines; lead with the message before skin | #32, #73, #79, #95, #104 |
| 14 | **F14 Luxury and price-perception errors** | 5 | Low intensity, white space, no bundles or discounts, restrict access; anchor against something far pricier; earned-status identity ("for those who have earned it"), quiet wealth cues | #82, #87, #89, #138, #140 |
| 15 | **F3 Two emotions or contradictory claims in one asset** | 3 | One core emotion, one headline, one CTA; blend only two emotions that each won alone (headline + subhead); make every claim agree and introduce offers before referencing them | #28, #49, #134 |

**Quick QA pass for any draft (derived from the table):** does the image show what the headline promises (F1)? Is there one message a viewer gets in 0.2 s (F2/F3)? Could a competitor make the same claim (F4)? Is the zone right for the buyer's moment (F5)? Is the proof real (F11)? On a page: does the buyer know it fits them and when to buy (F8/F10)?

## Coverage & gaps
- **Built from** the auto-harvest of every teardown-style table in the wiki: 597 data rows (session pages, brand case files, concept and framework pages). 54 of them are the brands-hub index rows (one line per brand, not a teardown), used here only for niches. Sources of every duplicate are kept in the Source column.
- **Screenshots:** linked where an existing image in `../assets/` shows that ad or page. Rows with "—" were spoken examples, described campaigns or screenshots not captured.
- **Left out on purpose:** the e-book mock ads ("This Pill Works When Others Don't", "You Know the Look.", "When the BASS drops", "TikTok's Most Viral Moisturizer"; see [heuristic pages](../concepts/heuristics.md)) and the e-book self-example lines (cookware, skincare, self-care; see [three selves](../concepts/three-selves.md)), because they are illustrations, not real ads. Brands mentioned only in passing are listed in the [brands hub](../brands/README.md#brands-mentioned-in-passing-no-case-file).
- **Interpretation:** failure-pattern tags, `*` heuristics and `*` zone/self tags are this library's classification. Many zone/self tags on source pages are wiki tags, not Sarah's verdicts; they are labelled.
- **Gaps:** few true video teardowns (most video rows are scripts or described hooks; only Net Positive V3 and The Only Bean were shown as running video).
