# KFC
> Fast-food chain. Sarah describes (never shows) its **$1 fries campaign** twice: once as quantity scarcity ("limited to four packs per customer") and once as reciprocity ("we wish we could give them to you for free"). Both are offer-framing lessons you can transfer to DTC.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing offer or purchase-stage ads; adding scarcity without a countdown; making a discount feel like a kindness.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a price offer → a per-customer quantity limit or a "we wish it were free" framing line.
- **Rules:**
  - DO try quantity limits per customer; they tell people "exactly how much you expect them to buy" and imply others' demand [01:41–02:15 M2 L2](../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/02-using-biases-that-sell.md).
  - DO frame an offer as a favour ("we'd give it free, but we can't, so…") to trigger reciprocity [0:11:51–0:13:34 Jul 2](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md).
  - DON'T quote the "~56%" lift as fact; it's Sarah's recollection ("something wild") [0:11:51](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md).
  - DON'T invent a limit you won't enforce (wiki rule; see [scarcity](../concepts/heuristics/scarcity-heuristic.md)).
- **See also:** [Scarcity heuristic](../concepts/heuristics/scarcity-heuristic.md) · [Framing effect](../concepts/heuristics/framing-effect.md)

## Transferable lessons (remix for our product)
- Offer script line: "We wish we could give [product] away free, but [honest reason]. So it's [price], and we'll add [bonus] so you get results."
- A per-order cap ("max 4 per customer") is a quieter scarcity signal than a timer, if it's real.
