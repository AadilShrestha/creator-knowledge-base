# Mirror Effect (Put the Shame on a Story Character)

> **"This is your experience… we're just gonna put it into a story so it doesn't get so close to you."** When a concept would shame the buyer, show *someone else* living it (often as a parody), so the viewer recognises themselves without being accused ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:50:00–0:50:52]).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a script, AI-generated concept or competitor ad uses guilt or shame ("you've been choosing what's easy, not what's right"); writing for shame-sensitive buyers (pet parents, parents, health); turning a harsh static into a UGC skit.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a guilt/shame line → (a) a skit in which a character experiences the guilt, and (b) a softened static with a "no shame" safety line, plus a backlash-risk note.
- **Rules:**
  - DO move the shame off the viewer and onto a character in a story: "a guy who just keeps passing his dog and the dog has a sad face every time" ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:50:00–0:50:30]).
  - DO use humor/parody to carry it (Lesley: "just parody") [0:50:30–0:50:52]. See [humor in ads](humor-in-ads.md).
  - DO add a softener when you test the direct version: "No shame, we just want to get your dog what he needs" [0:51:20–0:51:55].
  - DO note that "technically you are not shaming. Technically it's the dog that's shaming" when the accuser is a character [0:49:49–0:50:00].
  - DON'T ignore backlash risk: negative comments aren't necessarily bad, but "somebody on the brand team is gonna come back and say… we need to take that down" [0:50:52–0:51:20].
  - DO know what you're triggering: "I as a marketer would so much rather know what I'm triggering so I can very carefully make sure that I'm not doing it in a negative way" [0:51:55–0:52:57].
- **See also:** [Humor in ads](humor-in-ads.md) · [Ought self](ought-self.md) · [Emotional zones](emotional-zones.md) · [Micro-story](heuristics/micro-story.md) · [Story frameworks for ads](../frameworks/story-frameworks-for-ads.md) · [Vulnerability](heuristics/vulnerability.md)

## Definition
Sarah's name for showing the buyer's own uncomfortable experience happening to a third party inside a story, so the viewer sees themselves "in the mirror" at a safe distance. It keeps the emotional pull of guilt ([Ought self](ought-self.md), Zone 3–4) while removing the accusation. *(Wiki note: "mirror effect" is Sarah's label in this call; it isn't a standard bias name on The Decision Lab list.)*

## Why it matters
- Guilt pulls hard but from a stranger it feels accusatory (the same reason ought-self messaging belongs late in the funnel, [Live, June 18](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [0:08:25–0:10:29]; see [three selves](three-selves.md)).
- A character's experience lets the viewer laugh and recognise rather than defend.
- It lets you "walk the line and see how intense you can get" without crossing into shaming ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:51:55–0:52:57]).

## How to use it
1. Spot the shame line (often from ChatGPT: "what if your dog could choose?").
2. Ask: is this customer sensitive to shame? If yes, don't say it to them.
3. Write a skit where a character lives the guilt and gives in, ideally funny.
4. In parallel, write a direct static version **with a softener** to test intensity.
5. Monitor comments; agree with the brand team in advance what counts as acceptable backlash.

## Deploy in a UGC script
| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | Character repeatedly faces the silent accuser | Guy keeps walking past his dog in the kitchen; the dog gives a sad face every time ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:50:00]) |
| Turn | Character caves, comically | "Oh okay, I'll go buy it for you, geez, just stop looking at me like that." [0:50:00–0:50:30] |
| Close | Product + light CTA, no lecture | Product reveal; on-screen text (illustrative, not from course) |

## Deploy in a static
| Element | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Guilt, aimed at the choice not the person | "You've been choosing the easy route. It's time to choose the right route." [0:51:20] |
| Softener (bottom) | Remove the accusation | "No shame, we just want to get your dog what he needs." [0:51:20–0:51:55] |

**Visual cues:** the accuser is a character (sad-faced pet, kid, partner), not the camera staring at the viewer; comedic timing; warm resolution.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Skits where a pet, kid or partner "judges" the protagonist; parody of the buyer's bad habit.
- Direct "you've been failing your [pet/kids/body]" lines with no softener = higher backlash risk; note it.
- Tag: `concept-mirror-effect` (or `guilt-direct` if not displaced).

## Rules of thumb
- **IF** a concept shames the buyer **THEN** move the shame onto a story character or add a "no shame" softener, and expect some backlash [0:49:49–0:51:55].
- **DO** prefer knowing what you trigger over triggering it by accident [0:51:55].

## Common mistakes
- Running AI-generated guilt copy as-is.
- A softener so long it cancels the hook.
- Pointing the character's judgement at the camera (that turns it back into direct shame).
