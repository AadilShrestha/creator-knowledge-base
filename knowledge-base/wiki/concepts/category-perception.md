# Category Perception
> "Everybody has an association in their brain for websites": the brain files every brand into a category, and a store selling everything gets filed under "**Amazon**", where "Amazon will always be able to psychologically defeat you" ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:35:15–0:37:01]).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a brand sells several unrelated categories from one site or ad account; deciding whether a creative should look "Amazon-familiar" or distinct; checking whether a brand can move up-market; picking emotion-first vs features-first copy for a category.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the brand's catalogue, site structure and price position → the category the brain will file it under, a split/segment recommendation, landing-page palette per category, and the copy order (emotion-first or features-first).
- **Rules:**
  - DON'T sell many unrelated categories on one site. "Break the brands apart… segment down your category so the brain doesn't have to think." [May21 0:37:01–0:38:03]
  - IF you must keep one site THEN give each category its own landing pages and **colour palette** within the brand colours (men's green, women's yellow). [May21 0:38:03–0:39:06]
  - DO check which category buyers already file you in before raising price: "which category do I fit in and can I go up-market easily." [Jun18 0:25:06–0:27:17]
  - DO borrow familiar category patterns when you want instant recognition (Amazon-style USP icons on everyday PDPs), but not for elevated brands. [Jul9 0:11:19–0:13:23]
  - IF fashion or home decor THEN lead with the product/feature; emotion attaches "only after they get them on themselves". [May7 0:46:46–0:47:20; Roast2 0:22:16–0:22:35]
- **See also:** [Choice overload](heuristics/choice-overload.md) · [Cognitive load](heuristics/cognitive-load.md) · [Processing fluency](heuristics/processing-fluency.md) · [Luxury positioning](luxury-positioning.md) · [Perception management](perception-management.md) · [Color psychology](color-psychology.md) · [Landing-page psychology audit](../frameworks/landing-page-psychology-audit.md) (expected)

## Definition
The brain makes a fast association about what kind of store or brand it's looking at ("18 different shirts… *this is a shirt site*") and then judges everything through that category [May21 0:35:15]. The category sets the competitor set, the expected price and the expected shopping behaviour (browse like a department store vs buy one specialist thing).

## Why it matters
- **Department-store overload.** A site with supplements, minerals, hormonal health for men, women and kids "is all over the place… The brain gets a little bit overloaded with options." [May21 0:35:15–0:36:20]
- **You lose the comparison.** On Amazon you type "cat litter" and land in one category fast; on a multi-category DTC site "hormonal health" returns one or two products. [May21 0:36:20–0:37:01]
- **Conversion cost.** Happy Mammoth "had to split it apart because conversion rates just were in the trash". [May21 0:37:44–0:38:03]
- **Category caps pricing.** Raising prices can crash a brand when people "have them categorized in a different way"; Lamborghini slid into "commoditized luxury". [Jun18 0:25:06–0:27:17]
- **Category sets copy order.** Fashion and home decor are features-first; most other categories buy on emotion and justify with features. [May7 0:45:54–0:47:20]

## How to use it
1. **Name the category** a first-time visitor would assign in one glance (shirt site, supplement store, "Amazon").
2. **Count categories** on the landing page or store. More than one unrelated category → split or segment.
3. **Segment visually:** separate landing pages, distinct colour palettes, one category per ad → page path. [May21 0:38:03]
4. **Choose familiarity vs distinctiveness:** everyday product → familiar patterns (icons on PDP images) can help; elevated brand → stay distinct. [Jul9 0:12:14–0:13:23]
5. **Set the copy order** by category (features-first for fashion/home; emotion-first elsewhere).
6. **Before moving up-market,** confirm the brand isn't filed as commodity.

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Name one category and one problem, not the catalogue | "This is the only thing I use for [one problem]." (illustrative, not from course) |
| Body | Specialist framing vs the everything store | "Amazon's got knockoffs, but we do this full time." ([May21 0:08:55–0:09:20](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md)) |
| Fashion/home | Show the product on a body or in a room first | Fabric moving on the creator, then the feeling ([Roast2 0:09:33](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md)) |
| CTA | Send to the category page, never the mixed home page | "Link goes straight to the [category] page." (illustrative) |

**Visual cues:** one category per ad; the category's colour palette carried from ad to landing page; specialist cues (process, materials, founder) rather than a catalogue grid.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Ads that show a range of unrelated products, or link to a mixed home page → "Amazon-category" weakness to exploit with a specialist angle.
- Per-category colour systems and dedicated landers → mature segmentation.
- Amazon-style icon overlays on product images → familiarity play (everyday products).
- Tag: `pos-category` plus `cat-specialist`, `cat-everything-store`, `cat-familiar-pattern`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DON'T** run one site for many unrelated categories. **IF** forced **THEN** segment by landing page and palette. [May21 0:35:15–0:39:06]
- **DO** keep ad → page within one category. [May21 0:38:03]
- **IF** everyday product **THEN** familiar (Amazon-like) PDP patterns can help. **IF** elevated **THEN** stay clean. [Jul9 0:12:14–0:13:23]
- **IF** fashion or home decor **THEN** features/product first. [May7 0:46:46; Roast2 0:22:16]
- **DO** check category filing before raising price. [Jun18 0:27:17]

## Common mistakes
- Adding "15 more" SKUs (men's, women's, kids') after one hero product works. [May21 0:34:52–0:35:15]
- Positioning an umbrella store by a trait that one product breaks (a "for vegans" store that sells honey). [May21 0:38:03–0:39:06]
- Emotional storytelling for fashion before the buyer can see the product. [Roast2 0:22:16–0:22:35]
