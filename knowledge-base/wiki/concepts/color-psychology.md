# Color Psychology in Ads (Saturation, Trend Colors, Fuchsia, Greying Out, Patterns)

> **"Saturate up your colors on the product in particular, even if it seems like… that's really saturated… test it."** ([Roast My Ads #3](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:49:34]). Color is a cheap, fast lever that tells the brain where to look, what to value, and who the product is for. Put the color on *your* product, go opposite to the category, and test trend palettes.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** art-directing statics, thumbnails and UGC end cards; building a comparison ("us vs them") visual; planning a launch/new-color drop; choosing an iteration variable for a winning static; tearing down a competitor's palette.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the product, the category's dominant palette (from 10–20 competitor ads), any trend data → a palette decision (saturated product, opposite-of-category, desaturated "old way"), one color variant to test, and a background/pattern choice.
- **Rules:**
  - DO push **saturation up** on product and launch creatives. Sarah cites a study (from her podcast, uncited) that saturated colors make products seem higher quality "because they make them seem bigger"; phones vary in brightness and color settings anyway. [Roast #3 0:49:34–0:50:35]
  - DO **grey out / desaturate the competitor or "old way"** side of a comparison so the color sits on your product. [Roast #3 0:35:44–0:37:49]
  - DO test a **fuchsia ("flower pink", not baby pink)** static: "runs well for any brand in any industry I have ever worked for on Meta. I don't know why." [June 11 0:54:59]
  - DO go **opposite to the category palette**: "If everybody in the industry is pinks and pastels and grays, great, I want to be black and green… if they're green you go red." [June 11 0:43:27–0:44:31]
  - DO use trend palettes as cheap tests (Pinterest Predicts: "aura" pastels, cherries), and push them further rather than toning down (unicorn gradient → Lisa Frank palette). [Roast #3 0:26:19; 0:48:30–0:49:34]
  - DO remember color zones signal audience: pastel palettes say "this is for young children". [Roast #3 0:49:34]
  - DO use a background **pattern of objects the buyer already knows** (mandala of foods for wellness, baby bottles for moms, coffee cups for coffee). "The brain can't get enough of… items in repetition." [Roast #1 0:23:01–0:25:02]
  - DO give each product category its own landing-page color palette (within brand colors) if a multi-category store can't be split. [May 21 0:38:03–0:39:06]
  - DO keep text legible: white text on a busy green background needs a subtle black fade. [Roast #2 0:28:03–0:28:44]
  - DON'T put two pulsing elements in competing colors above the fold (red badge vs green button). [Roast #2 0:51:07]
- **See also:** [Salience bias](heuristics/salience-bias.md) · [Von Restorff effect](heuristics/von-restorff-effect.md) · [Contrast](heuristics/contrast.md) · [Processing fluency](heuristics/processing-fluency.md) · [Pattern disruption](heuristics/pattern-disruption.md) · [Creative primitives](creative-primitives.md) · [Hero image ↔ copy congruence](hero-image-copy-congruence.md)

## Definition
How hue, saturation, contrast and repeated-object patterns change attention, perceived quality and perceived audience in an ad. In Tether Lab, color is (a) an attention tool ([salience](heuristics/salience-bias.md), [differentiation / Von Restorff](heuristics/von-restorff-effect.md)), (b) a value signal (saturation = bigger/higher quality), and (c) a [creative primitive](creative-primitives.md) you can vary without changing the message ([Chase](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-guest-session-02-chase-mohseni.md) [0:19:10]).

## Why it matters
- Red stops the scroll: "as soon as you see it you stop" ([Roast #3](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:27:22–0:28:25]). That's why it must sit on *your* product, not the competitor's.
- Contrast overrides reading order: "if you have something heavy in color or specifically in contrast, the eyes will stop there first" ([Roast #2](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:24:27–0:25:08]).
- "Even a color or picture change would do something totally different to somebody's brain" ([June 18](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [0:59:15]).
- Patterns land subconsciously: the VITUS food pattern reads as a **mandala** ("yogi kind of a level of a pattern") and visualises *balance* ([Roast #1](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:23:01–0:24:01]).

## How to use it
1. **Audit the category palette:** tag the dominant colors in 10–20 competitor ads (with the [competitor teardown](../playbooks/competitor-ad-teardown.md) zone/self tags).
2. **Pick the opposite** for your hero color (pastels → black and green; green → red).
3. **Put the saturated color on the product**; desaturate everything that represents the old way.
4. **Choose a background:** plain single color (fluency), or a repeating pattern of objects the buyer knows (supports the text).
5. **Add one color test per batch:** fuchsia version; trend-palette version (Pinterest Predicts); saturation-up version. Keep message and layout constant.
6. **Check legibility** (fades behind text) and that only one element is animated/pulsing.

## Deploy in a static
- Break the 1080×1080 grid too: "take a bite out of the corner", push the image to one side, let an object run off the frame. "Statics are just all about breaking the grid." ([June 11](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md) [0:54:59–0:56:04])

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Where is the most saturated color? On their product (good) or on the thing they're attacking (gap)?
- Is their palette the category default (copy-able = ignore) or category-opposite?
- Trend colors (cherries, aura pastels) = they're running cheap trend tests.
- Pattern backgrounds: what known object is repeated, and does it support the headline?
- Tags: `visual-saturated`, `visual-desaturated-competitor`, `visual-fuchsia`, `visual-trend-palette`, `visual-pattern-bg`, `visual-category-opposite`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** saturate the product; **DO** desaturate the old way. [Roast #3 0:36:47; 0:49:34]
- **DO** test fuchsia in any industry. [June 11 0:54:59]
- **IF** the category is uniform in color **THEN** go the opposite way. [June 11 0:44:31]
- **DO** use repeating known objects as background patterns; the background must support the text ("images… get processed 60,000 times faster than the text", a popular uncited stat). [Roast #1 0:24:01–0:25:34]
- **DON'T** let two animated elements compete in different colors. [Roast #2 0:51:07]

## Common mistakes
- Making the competitor the most colorful thing in the ad.
- Toning down a trend palette "to be safe".
- A pattern that means something else to the subconscious than the headline says.
- Many colors and busy backgrounds that kill [processing fluency](heuristics/processing-fluency.md) ("we don't want a lot of colors").
