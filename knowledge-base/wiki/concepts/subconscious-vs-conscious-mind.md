# Subconscious vs Conscious Mind (Two-Systems Model)

> The fast, emotional subconscious (~11 million bits/sec) makes most buying decisions. The slow, logical conscious mind (~40 bits/sec) mostly signs off. "**Good ads talk to the boss. The subconscious.**" (e-book p.3)

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** setting the overall tone of a script (emotional/visual vs rational/spec-heavy), diagnosing why a competitor's feature-heavy ad underperforms, or justifying simplicity to a stakeholder.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a draft or competitor ad → a verdict on whether it speaks to the subconscious (fast, emotional, visual, relevant to a current need), plus fixes.
- **Rules:**
  - DO make the ad readable in a glance: emotional, visual, fast.
  - DO tie the ad to a state the viewer is likely in right now (hungry, tired, cold). That's the pizza mechanism.
  - DO match the associations the viewer already holds about the category (their "map").
  - DON'T build "long rational debates." The conscious mind handles only ~40 bits/sec.
  - DON'T treat the viewer as "a mini CFO" weighing options.
- **See also:** [Heuristics](heuristics.md), [Processing fluency](heuristics/processing-fluency.md), [Affect heuristic](heuristics/affect-heuristic.md).

## The model

| | Subconscious mind | Conscious mind |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | ~**11 million bits/second**, "screaming fast" ([L1 01:39]) | ~**40 bits/second**, "struggles through" (e-book) ([L1 02:07]) |
| Nature | Old ("been around for a very, very long time"), reactive, emotional, "not logic at all," reacting to its environment ([L1 01:34]–[01:57]) | "Very slow and calculated," controlled, deliberate ([L1 02:02]–[02:17]) |
| Role | Makes **most daily decisions**. We don't have much control over it, and it struggles to slow down and weigh options ([L1 02:33]–[02:57]) | Comes online for "concerted" decisions: buying a car, comparing computers ([L1 02:17]–[02:26]) |
| Job | "Record and make sure that it's keeping you safe and alive" ([L1 03:28]–[03:32]) | Makes good decisions using what the subconscious feeds it |

- **They work together.** "The subconscious mind is constantly feeding information to your conscious," usually without you knowing ([L1 02:57]–[03:12]).
- **Iceberg metaphor (e-book p.3):** the conscious mind is the tip above water, and the subconscious is the huge mass below.
- "When you see something, typically we just react to it. We don't make a conscious decision" ([L1 02:53]).

## Examples from the course
1. **Pizza:** you skipped breakfast and coffee. The subconscious has been tracking your glucose. It stops on a pizza ad and tells the conscious mind "we don't have any food in the system... We like pizza... We need to stop and get something" ([L1 03:12]–[03:57]). The e-book version: you "suddenly" crave pizza at 3PM. "It made the decision before your conscious mind even logged on."
2. **Coffee map:** the subconscious drafts "a map all day, every day of every single thing it knows about every object." Shown a DTC coffee ad, it checks: what coffee my mom drank, what I had this morning, my favourite coffee, my favourite cup, which beans made me sick. Then it decides whether to stop ([L1 04:12]–[04:56]).

## Teardown questions (derived from the model)
- Can the ad be understood without reading more than one line? (Low conscious load)
- Does it trigger an existing association or a current need? (The pizza and coffee "map")
- Is the persuasion carried by feeling and visuals or by argument? Argument means it's aimed at the conscious mind, which the course treats as a mistake (e-book p.17).

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** "talk to the boss. The subconscious." (e-book p.3)
- **DON'T** talk "only to the conscious mind. No long rational debates. Emotional, visual, fast." (e-book p.17)
