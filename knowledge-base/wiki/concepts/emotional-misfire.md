# Emotional Misfire
> **An emotional misfire is when an ad's emotional tone (its [valence](valence.md) and/or [intensity](intensity.md)) doesn't match the emotional state the customer is in, or the feeling they need to buy.** The words can be "right", but the feeling is wrong, so people scroll on. Sarah Levinger says this is the usual cause when an ad "should be working" but isn't.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** explaining why a competitor ad *wouldn't* transfer to our buyer, or why our ad with solid offer and creative isn't converting.
- **Inputs → outputs:** ad + customer state + purchase motive → misfire type (too calm / too intense / wrong motive / under-emotional / slightly off) + recommended lever.
- **Rules:**
  - DO check the misfire types table before blaming hooks, creators or targeting.
  - IF calm ad + stressed, fast-solution buyer THEN raise intensity.
  - IF high-intensity promo + luxury/high-trust buyer THEN lower intensity.
  - IF ad promise ≠ purchase motive THEN change the emotional promise.
- **See also:** [Valence-Intensity diagnostic](../frameworks/valence-intensity-diagnostic.md) · [Tone-shifting](../frameworks/tone-shifting.md)

## Definition
- "Your ad is kind of misaligned with what your audience believes about their problem, and it needs to shift emotionally in order for them to feel that buy." ([L1](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/01-the-real-reason-your-ad-isnt-working.md) [01:14])
- "The words on this ad are right, but the feeling is kind of wrong and customers feel this pretty deeply." (L1 [01:35])
- "When your ad's **emotional temperature** doesn't align with the moment that your customer is currently in… even great offers are going to fall completely flat." (L1 [01:45])
- E-book: "It speaks to logic, not emotion. It skips the vibe test. Your audience is emotionally elsewhere. And your ad isn't meeting them there."

## Symptoms
- Good offer, strong headline, beautiful well-designed creative, **still not converting**. (L1 [00:22])
- The team's reflex is to change targeting, make it a GIF, hire a new UGC creator, "draft 70 new hooks", and "tweak and tweak and tweak". None of it works. (L1 [00:47])
- Customers scroll past, or file it under "save for later" or "ignore". ([L4](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/04-diagnosing-emotional-misfires.md) [03:30])
- In the e-book this is "the 'WTF moment'… nine times out of ten? It's not the creative. It's the emotional tone."

## Types of misfire (with course examples)

| Type | Example | Outcome | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Too calm for an urgent mindset** | Zone 1 calm, soothing ad shown to a stressed buyer on a deadline who needs "A to Z as quickly as possible" | Completely ignored. "Their brain is attuned to finding quick solutions." | L4 [03:58–04:19]; e-book p8 |
| **Too intense for a high-trust product** | High-energy, promo-heavy ad for a luxury product. **Jaguar**'s "ridiculously intense, lots of colors" campaign. | Feels cheap. Luxury customers preferred low intensity, high valence. | L4 [04:19–04:54] |
| **Wrong emotional motive for the product** | Copper Cow Coffee's cozy Zone 1 testimonial, though people drink coffee "to boost energy" | "Save for later" or ignored | L4 [03:19–05:16] |
| **Under-emotional** | Ads that are "not emotional enough" compared with how strongly customers feel about the problem (Pela phone-case ads shown on screen) | Not enough motivation to cross "that line and into your landing page" | L4 [05:26–06:09] |
| **Slightly off** | Ad ~10% off on the map | "All it takes to feel completely wrong" | [L5](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/05-fixing-the-tone-without-rebuilding-the-ad.md) [05:17] |

*(Context, not from course: the Jaguar reference is to the brand's late-2024 rebrand campaign, which drew widespread backlash.)*

## How to diagnose and fix
1. Place the ad on the map (valence, then intensity). You can use the ChatGPT sentiment prompt.
2. Identify the customer's emotional state at the moment of exposure (scrolling, at work, stressed, at home "with like 17 kids").
3. Decide what you want them to feel instead.
4. If those differ, you have a misfire. Shift verbs, visuals or the [emotional promise](emotional-promise.md), then re-test.

→ [Valence-Intensity diagnostic](../frameworks/valence-intensity-diagnostic.md) · [Tone-shifting](../frameworks/tone-shifting.md)

## Common mistakes
- Treating a misfire as a creative or hook problem and endlessly rewriting. (L1)
- Concluding that a whole zone "doesn't work". It depends on what *this* customer wants to feel. (L4 [03:36])
- Only diagnosing losers. Sarah says to also diagnose winners to learn what tweak made the difference. ([L6](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/06-map-and-move-your-messaging.md) [00:12])
