# Native Advertising
> Native advertising fits the surrounding content experience; Tether Lab's identity, emotion, story, clarity, and testing teachings help determine whether the message inside that format is persuasive.

## Agent use

- **Use when:** explaining native ads, briefing feed-native images/videos, developing an article-style sales journey, or adapting a winning story into an advertorial.
- **Inputs → outputs:** placement + customer evidence + product facts + buyer stage + proof assets → message, native format, creative brief, matching destination, and test plan.
- **Rules:** Choose the message before visual styling. Match the placement without hiding the commercial relationship. Keep curiosity relevant and pay it off. Preserve the ad's promise on the destination page. Use genuine proof. Distinguish course teaching from production inference.
- **See also:** [Story frameworks](../frameworks/story-frameworks-for-ads.md), [three selves](three-selves.md), [creative testing](../frameworks/creative-testing-system.md), [landing-page audit](../frameworks/landing-page-psychology-audit.md), [Test Cream production pack](../../../creative/test-cream/README.md).

## 1. Definitions that prevent confusion

**Native advertising** is paid content designed to fit the form/function of its surrounding experience. A feed ad can look like the kind of photo, video, or explanation a person was already consuming. An article recommendation or sponsored article can fit a publisher's reading experience. A sales message still remains advertising and should be recognizable as such.

People commonly use the term in two ways:

1. **Feed-native creative:** a casual image, creator-style video, founder explanation, or other paid post shaped for a social feed.
2. **Publisher-native advertising:** a sponsored article or recommendation unit in an editorial environment, sometimes leading to an advertiser-owned article-style page.

**UGC** properly means user-generated content; **UGC-style advertising** borrows its creator/customer visual language and can be commissioned or synthetic. **Static** and **video** describe the asset medium. **Advertorial** describes promotional content written or presented in an editorial/article style. It can be a sponsored publisher article or an advertiser-owned landing page. An article-style landing page is not automatically a publisher placement.

One campaign may use a native social video, made in a UGC style, leading to an advertorial. A native image may instead lead straight to a product page. Native does not automatically mean amateur, ugly, cheap, a testimonial, an AI presenter, or a long article.

## 2. The creative job

Fit the context → earn attention with something relevant → make a useful point → introduce the product naturally → substantiate the promise → offer a clear next step.

This is an execution model, not a guaranteed buyer sequence. The KB's [TEEP framework](../frameworks/teep-journey.md) describes buyers moving among exploration, evaluation, and purchase rather than following a perfectly linear funnel. A casual format can serve any stage; stage depends on the message and proof.

## 3. What the KB teaches us to put inside it

### Identity before decoration

Use the [three selves](three-selves.md). Actual Self recognizes a present situation; Ideal Self connects with an aspiration; Ought Self invokes standards or responsibility. Sarah's later guidance favors Actual/Ideal for cold audiences and more qualified use of Ought framing. June 18 **00:05:15–00:10:29**.

For fictional Test Cream, the present situation is a person facing multiple bottles at bedtime. That is a stronger starting brief than a generic instruction to create a casual-looking cream ad. It remains an audience hypothesis until supported by customer research.

### One emotional reason to care

Choose [one core emotion](one-core-emotion-per-ad.md), supported by copy, face, props, pace, and color. Roast #1 **00:41:46–00:42:48**. In this demo the destination is relief, not simultaneous glamour, anxiety, urgency, status, and bargain excitement. See [emotional zones](emotional-zones.md).

### Specific situations over generic language

The [specificity teaching](heuristics/specificity.md) uses recognized moments to make the message relevant. July 9 **00:38:55–00:41:44**. For Test Cream, the illustrative moment is reading labels with a phone beside several bottles. It is not an invented clinical result or fake customer quote.

### Curiosity with a relevant answer

[Curiosity gaps](heuristics/curiosity-gap.md) and [open loops](heuristics/open-loop.md) earn attention when the reader almost understands something and wants the missing piece. Prompt Library #3 teaches resolving the hook with a useful insight. The payoff must relate to the actual product or buying question; unrelated sensationalism may attract unqualified clicks.

### A story needs a specific character and a structure

The May 14 session uses a skincare countertop example at **00:17:42–00:18:29**, then explains spending about two-thirds on setup at **00:19:45–00:20:41**. See [story frameworks](../frameworks/story-frameworks-for-ads.md). This is the instructor's creative guidance to test, not a universal length rule. A winning story can be adapted to an advertorial: May 14 **00:48:12–00:49:22**.

### Humor can open the conversation

The Joanna Wallace session discusses Sarah's recollection of Scary Mommy using humorous advertorials to make deeper parenting topics approachable. May 23 **00:11:27–00:12:29**. See [humor in ads](humor-in-ads.md). The transferable idea is a recognizable tension and a fitting joke, not copying someone else's customer story or fabricating vulnerability.

### The messenger changes the interpretation

The [messenger effect](heuristics/messenger-effect.md) makes the speaker part of the creative strategy. A real founder, employee, customer, practitioner, skeptical creator, and fictional presenter have different relationships to the product. May 23 **00:35:37–00:37:48**. A fictional character is not evidence of customer experience or expert endorsement.

### Natural-looking still needs clarity

[Processing fluency](heuristics/processing-fluency.md) calls for simple wording, familiar visuals, and one clear message. [Visual-message congruence](hero-image-copy-congruence.md) requires the image to carry what the copy promises. Intentionally unreadable captions, a cluttered frame, or excessive information do not become persuasive because they look unpolished.

### Show why the product fits the situation

The [Fogg behavior model](../frameworks/fogg-behavior-model.md) guidance highlights ability barriers such as suitability, fit, effort, scale, or durability. July 16 **00:05:50–00:06:30; 00:27:14**. An easy-to-follow demonstration can answer effort questions. Suitability or efficacy needs actual evidence, not just a natural-looking shot.

### Keep the promise after the click

[Landing-page audit](../frameworks/landing-page-psychology-audit.md): inspect cognitive load, message, and CTA; retain ad-to-page congruence. Roast #2 **00:03:42; 00:11:51; 00:19:19**; Chase **00:08:26–00:09:38**. If the ad is about a simple routine, the destination should explain that idea and product clearly before introducing unrelated messages. Product pages and educational articles can have different appropriate next actions.

## 4. Test Cream: three original executions

All examples below are fictional concept drafts, not real published ads, reviews, or demonstrated results.

**Feed-native image:** an ordinary bathroom-counter photo, multiple bottles, a phone, and a recognizably placed jar. Hook: “Why does bedtime skincare come with homework?” Primary text: “Phone open. Bottles out. Still checking the next step. Meet Test Cream: a little less production around the moisturizer step.” A product-page destination should continue the same idea and explain the actual product when facts exist.

**Feed-native creator video:** Maya voices the same recognized situation and shows the moisturizer step. The existing [30-second script](../../../creative/test-cream/script-and-shotlist.md) is one example. Its natural appearance does not turn a synthetic presenter into a genuine customer testimonial.

**Native card → advertorial:** sponsored recommendation headline: “When your evening skincare routine starts feeling like homework.” The article opens on the recognizable situation, discusses routine complexity, explains one moisturizer step, introduces the fictional Test Cream concept, then provides product facts/proof when available and a clear route to the product page. No fake independent publication, doctor, user result, or ingredient claim is required.

## 5. A practical brief and testing process

1. Name the placement, buyer stage, identity frame, and one emotion.
2. Gather actual product facts, customer language, available proof, and the destination page.
3. Write the message and curiosity payoff before choosing a creator or adding graphics.
4. Produce a small coherent set. For this demonstration, three hooks with the same image/body/offer/page is a reasonable starting exercise, not a prescribed media-buying budget.
5. Validate the message and proof, readable mobile layout, visuals, and post-click continuity.
6. Use [creative-testing guidance](../frameworks/creative-testing-system.md) to vary a known winner deliberately. Course sequence: message → supporting heuristics → format. Keep the winning message when moving from static to video. July 23 **00:18:56–00:21:32**.
7. Read results across the journey. Outbound clicks indicate interest; landing-page views show arrivals; conversion rate and acquisition cost address business outcomes. Attention metrics help diagnose video delivery. None alone proves the ad is profitable or explains the cause of a failure.
8. Treat mismatches as hypotheses: strong clicks with weak purchases can reflect poor-fit curiosity, the page, offer, price, trust, checkout, or tracking. Compare sufficient, comparable data before declaring a winner. There is no universal good CTR or cost threshold without the business context.

Metrics interpretation here is practical synthesis, not a documented publisher-network buying recipe. Do not infer competitor profitability from ad longevity alone; the KB explicitly cautions against that shortcut.

## 6. Common mistakes

- Treating native styling as the entire strategy.
- Equating static with native, or native with UGC.
- Writing sensational hooks with no product-relevant payoff.
- Dressing generic copy in a phone-photo aesthetic.
- Adding fake reviews, experts, screenshots, or personal results to imply authenticity.
- Turning a proven simple message into a video that delays or changes it.
- Testing headline, image, presenter, offer, and landing page together, then claiming to know which caused the result.
- Treating a high click rate as a sales result.
- Replacing a working format because the team is bored. See [format variation ladder](../frameworks/format-variation-ladder.md).
