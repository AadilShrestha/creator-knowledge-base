# Reverse Psychology

> **"Reverse Psychology works by encouraging someone to do (or believe) the opposite of what you actually want them to do—creating mental resistance, which paradoxically draws them closer to your point."** ([Prompt Library #3](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/03-curiosity-and-reverse-psychology.md))

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** cold/top-of-funnel hooks that should **qualify** the viewer; categories where buyers know the "right" habit but don't do it (supplements, skincare, fitness, finance); remixing a competitor's earnest benefit ad into a sarcastic version.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the behavior we want + the bad habit the audience keeps → a "Go ahead, keep [bad habit]…" hook, the consequences list, and the product as the obvious out.
- **Rules:**
  - DO frame it as permission: "Go ahead, keep doing what you're doing…"—then reveal why that path is broken (#3 step 2).
  - DO use it to filter. Sarah: "I love reverse psychology because it really does **qualify the customer at the top of the funnel**." [03:07–03:16]
  - DO call out uncomfortable truths that pre-qualify commitment: "supplements don't work overnight… It calls in that type of person that's ready to make a long-term commitment." [03:54–04:09]
  - DO keep it playful-sarcastic ("Story ads with sarcasm. Such a great way to draw people in at the top of the funnel." [03:36–03:46]).
  - DON'T mock the viewer. The target is the habit, not the person. The best output made Sarah say "I feel attacked… This is so me" [03:21–03:26]: recognition, not shame.
- **See also:** [Curiosity gap](curiosity-gap.md) · [Loss aversion](loss-aversion.md) · [Pain-point amplification](pain-point-amplification.md) · [Actual Self](../actual-self.md)

## Definition
- Prompt Library #3 (verbatim above). Step 3 adds the tension: "Leave a moment of confusion or a gap in logic. Your goal is to have the viewer internally protest the idea, which builds tension that can only be resolved by engaging deeper."
- Intended feel: "bold, slightly provocative, and intellectually fun to interact with."

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- "Don't buy this if…", "This isn't for you if…", "Go ahead, keep…", "Please don't try this", "How to [fail] in 3 easy steps".
- Sarcastic or deadpan tone with an earnest product turn at the end.
- Tag `lever-reverse-psychology`. Note whether it's used as a **qualifier** (filters buyers) or a **dare** (provokes action).

## Common mistakes
- Irony that never resolves, so viewers take the bad advice literally or miss the product.
- Shaming instead of teasing (pushes the ad into a harsh Zone 4 the category may not tolerate).
- Using it for premium/luxury buyers who expect a calm, elevated register.
