# Specificity

> **"Specificity is what makes bold claims believable."** (Prompt Library #13). The more "exact, tangible, or measurable" the language, the more trustworthy and actionable the message. In the live calls Sarah adds a second job: **specific situations find the right buyer**.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** every script. Specifically, writing hooks (situational call-outs), proof lines (numbers), scarcity badges (exact counts), and emotion labels. Also when a competitor's or our ad is "fine" but generic, or a video has a high CPA.
- **Inputs → outputs:** reviews, Reddit threads, real data → one hyper-specific situation, one exact number, one precise emotion per ad.
- **Rules:**
  - DO "anchor the insight in something specific. Use real numbers, comparisons, or examples" (#13 step 3).
  - DO call out **one hyper-specific situation** in the first seconds, one "only your buyer would recognise" ("hair clogging the drain"; the pilot's "gas cap left off"). Generic call-outs let Meta find the cheapest, not the right, buyer ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:55–0:41:44]).
  - DO use precise, odd numbers for counts: "we have **53** of these left" beats round numbers and also lifts AOV ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:47:00–0:48:04]).
  - DO name the specific emotion, not the umbrella (disappointment, not "fear"). "Generic emotions = expensive ads" ([Live, June 4](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:37:39–0:39:49]).
  - DON'T trade clarity for detail. One specific detail per beat keeps [processing fluency](processing-fluency.md).
- **See also:** [Prompt #13](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/13-counterintuitive-insight-and-specificity.md) · [Counterintuitive insight](counterintuitive-insight.md) · [Data-driven insight](data-driven-insight.md) · [Scarcity](scarcity-heuristic.md)

## Definition
"**Specificity** is what makes bold claims believable. The more exact, tangible, or measurable the language, the more *trustworthy and actionable* the message becomes. Specifics turn insight into impact." (Prompt Library #13; PDF p.26)

## Situational specificity in depth (live sessions)
*The planned "situational specificity" page is merged here. Sarah's phrase is "situational awareness": "CPA in my experience comes down to situational awareness" ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:55]).*

**Why it works.** A generic call-out lets Meta chase "the cheapest person for Meta", who "might not be the cheapest person for you". A specific situation lets the algorithm find the buyer who's ready ([Jul 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:55–0:39:38]; see [algorithm as library](../algorithm-as-library.md)). The pilot "gas cap left off" joke shows that "it happens in every community, in every industry" [0:40:40–0:41:44].

**The patterns Sarah uses**

| Pattern | Course example | Source |
|---|---|---|

**Test the "why" behind a situational winner.** For the burnt-dinner ad, Sarah splits iterations into two threads of ~3 statics: universal moments that test "it's just funny" ("You skipped breakfast, so it's gelato time") vs lived experience ("You spent three hours on dinner and he hated it…") ([Jun 18](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [0:39:07–0:41:19]).

**Where situations come from:** Reddit verbatims ("more hair in my shower than on my brush", Yana, [Jul 9 0:41:44](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md)), review keywords and calls with past customers ("I wasn't allowed to as a kid", [Roast #1 0:45:30–0:46:04](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md)), and "read between the lines" prompts on reviews ([Jun 11 0:27:40–0:28:44](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md)).

## Deploy in a UGC script
| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Situational call-out | "I'm so done seeing more hair in my shower than on my brush." (member line, [Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:41:44]) |
| Proof | Exact number + timeframe | "[N] days, [X] fewer strands in the drain." (fill in real data) |
| Offer | Precise odd count | "We have [53] left in this colour." (real count only) |
| Visual | Show the specific evidence | The drain with hair in it: "Show, don't tell." ([0:39:38–0:40:40]) |

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Exact numbers (odd, not round), named timeframes, named situations or places, insider jokes only that community gets.
- Score the hook: could anyone outside the target buyer identify with it? If yes, it's generic.
- Tag: `lever-specificity`, plus the kind: `spec-situation`, `spec-number`, `spec-emotion`.

## Common mistakes
- Round or vague numbers ("thousands of happy customers").
- A situation so broad Meta can't find the buyer (a high-CPA founder video, [Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:55]).
- Fabricated precision. A fake "53 left" is still fake scarcity.

## Pairs with
- **[Counterintuitive insight](counterintuitive-insight.md)** (Prompt #13): the specifics make the flip credible.
- **vs [Processing fluency](processing-fluency.md):** fluency is *easy to process*. Specificity is *concrete*. A concrete detail usually helps fluency, but a pile of details hurts it.
