# Bandwagon Effect ("Most Customers Buy…")

> **"If the website tells me that most people buy 36, I'm more likely to buy 36."** ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:37:45]). We support whatever becomes popular. In ads, tell people what most people like them choose, and they follow.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** adding a default-choice badge to a static, end card or PDP; writing a trend line ("everyone's switching to…"); writing switching hooks that question a fading trend; tearing down a competitor that leans on "most people…".
- **Inputs → outputs:** real sales mix (what most customers actually buy) or real usage data → one "most customers…" line or badge, or a trend-question hook.
- **Rules:**
  - DO test a tiny badge "Most customers buy [X]" above the bundles, and on the ad itself ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:37:45–0:38:47]; [Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:04:59–0:06:08]).
  - DO put the badge on the bundle that **actually** sells most. If you want them on a bigger tier, use transparency modelling: "Most people buy the 12 pack, but the 36 pack will get you 100 dinners a year" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:43:59–0:45:03]).
  - DO turn survey averages into a recommendation: "Most people go through this bag in three months, so you're going to want the four-month pack" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:47:10–0:49:11]).
  - DO question a trend rather than attack it when pulling switchers: "The girlies aren't doing matcha anymore" ([Live, June 18](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [1:04:29–1:05:31]).
  - DON'T lie: "we don't want to lie on our sites" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:44:21]).
- **See also:** [Social proof](social-proof.md) · [Decoy effect](decoy-effect.md) · [Anchoring effect](anchoring-effect.md) · [Switching behavior](../switching-behavior.md) · [Tribal belonging](tribal-belonging.md) · [In-group bias](in-group-bias.md)

## Definition
- The Decision Lab line Sarah read: "Why do we support opinions as soon as they become popular?… we're just going to support whatever everyone else is doing" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:37:45]).
- It sits **on top of** the [decoy effect](decoy-effect.md): with a 3/6/12/36 ladder and the badge "Most customers buy 36", "the brain is going to automatically say I should buy 36 too because most people do" [0:38:47].

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- "Most customers buy", "most popular", "everyone's switching", "X is out, Y is in", "join 10,000…", crowd montages.
- Tag `heur-bandwagon`. Add `heur-decoy` if it sits on a tier ladder.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** derive every "most customers" claim from real data (sales mix, survey average).
- **IF** the honest best-seller isn't your target **THEN** add a value line for the bigger tier instead of moving the badge.
- **DON'T** attack the current trend ("matcha sucks"); question it ([Live, June 18](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [1:04:29–1:05:31]).

## Common mistakes
- Badging the biggest bundle as "most popular" when it isn't.
- Using a faceless number ("10,000+ customers") instead of a tribe noun ("chai lovers") ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:25:12]).

## Pairs with / distinctions
- **vs [Social proof](social-proof.md):** social proof = "others like it, so it's safe" (reviews, stars, counts). Bandwagon = "others are *choosing this*, so I'll do the same" — it points to a specific option or trend.
- **[Decoy effect](decoy-effect.md):** the ladder the badge sits on.
- **[In-group bias](in-group-bias.md) / [Tribal belonging](tribal-belonging.md):** strongest when "most people" means "people like me".
