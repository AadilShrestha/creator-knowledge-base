# Goal Gradient Effect

> **"The closer we feel to the finish line, the faster we move. Humans are junkies for progress."** (e-book, p.14). Shortcut #8 in [Heuristics 101](../../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/README.md). Show people where they are in the process and how few steps are left.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** the product has a process (customisation, subscription setup, a routine, a fitness goal); writing the "how it works" beat; building step strips on statics; CRO and checkout copy; tearing down competitor "3 easy steps" ads.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the purchase or usage process → a 3-step strip with a "You are here" marker at step 1, or a short step line ("Buy, wear, achieve.").
- **Rules:**
  - DO show where they are ("You are here" → step 1) and how few steps remain.
  - DO compress the process to about three steps and keep it high level on acquisition ads.
  - DO use progress bars, completion steps and unlockable rewards.
  - DON'T bog acquisition viewers down in colours, sizes and options.
- **See also:** [Temporal discounting](temporal-discounting.md), [Processing fluency](processing-fluency.md), [Heuristic stacking](../heuristic-stacking.md).

## Definition
"The closer we feel to a goal, the more motivated we get to go after that goal" ([L4 02:18]).

## Why it works
"Progress, especially near the finish line, is pretty sensitive... **If it can see the carrot, it will just keep going after it.**" "Humans are very, very emotional about their goals" ([L4 02:24]–[02:41]).

## How to use it in ads (from the course)
- **"You are here" marker:** an arrow at the first action (pick a size), showing only two more steps to the outcome ([L4 02:50]–[03:14]).
- **CRO:** a progress bar at the top of checkout (cart → payment → shipping info → checkout) "to help people understand how close they are to the goal" ([L4 03:17]–[03:34]).
- Progress bars, completion steps, unlockable rewards ([L4 03:34]–[03:44]).
- **Short step line:** "Buy, wear, achieve." "Only three steps" ([L5 05:42]–[05:57]). It teaches "how long the process actually is" and "cuts down on... expectation issues" ([L5 06:05]–[06:15]).
- **E-book:** progress bars ("You're 75% there!"); step-by-step visuals ("Pick size > Upload photo > Confirm order"). Pro tip: "Always show how close they are to 'done.'"
- **E-book mock ad:** "You've Burned 312 Calories. Let's Make It 500." frames the viewer as already partway to the goal.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- "In just 3 easy steps," numbered step strips, arrows, "You are here" markers.
- Progress language: "75% there," "day 3 of 30," "you've already…"
- "Before/after in X steps" structures. Tag `goal-gradient` and note the number of steps shown.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** place people "in the sequence of where they are in the purchasing process." It's "huge for the brain" ([L4 03:46]–[03:54]).
- **DO** keep it high level on acquisition. Consumers "get kind of bogged down" in colours and sizes ([L5 06:15]–[06:26]).
- **DO** "always show how close they are to 'done.'" (e-book p.14)
