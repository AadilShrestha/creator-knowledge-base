# Contrast (Before vs After, Now vs Next)

> **"Contrast is a visual and emotional tool that makes differences *feel* dramatic."** (Prompt Library #8). Put two versions of reality side by side, such as before and after or struggle and confidence, so the gap does the persuading.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing the problem → result arc of a UGC script, planning split-screen, before/after or mirror visuals, or tearing down a competitor transformation ad.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the customer's raw "now" state (frustrations, habits, environment, emotion) + the "next-level" state → a two-state script or static with one exaggerated, *felt* difference.
- **Rules:**
  - DO define the "now" state first, and keep it "raw and true" (#8 step 1).
  - DO exaggerate the emotional difference with tone and visuals, so the viewer feels it "more than they think it" (#8 step 3).
  - DO make the "after" an identity shift, not just a surface-level change (#8 output rules). Pair it with [aspirational identity](aspirational-identity.md).
  - DON'T contrast on product features. Contrast states of the person.
  - DON'T make the "now" state humiliating. Zone 3 "pain-point whisper" is the ceiling for the before beat unless the category is high-stakes. *(Derived from [Emotional zones](../emotional-zones.md).)*
- **See also:** [Prompt #8: Contrast + Aspirational Identity](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/08-contrast-and-aspirational-identity.md) · [Aspirational identity](aspirational-identity.md) · [Salience bias](salience-bias.md) · [Framing effect](framing-effect.md)

## Definition
"When you compare two versions of reality—'before' vs. 'after,' struggle vs. confidence—it spotlights what's missing and what's possible. The emotional weight of the transformation becomes more tangible." (Prompt Library #8; PDF p.16)

Used with aspirational identity, "these tactics move the audience from passive interest to *identity-driven motivation*." (#8)

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance: the prompt describes the lever and its formats. The slot mapping and lines below adapt it to UGC and aren't in the course.*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line (illustrative, not from course) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Split screen or jump cut: "now" state left, "next" state right, with a one-line caption | "Me in March vs me now. Same job, same kids." |
| Problem | Creator lives in the "now" state: real environment, real habit | "This was my 3pm every day: second coffee, still foggy." |
| Mechanism | The product is the bridge between the two states | "The only thing I changed was [product]." |
| Proof | Repeat the same shot or angle in the "after" state | Same mirror, same light, different posture |
| CTA | Invite them to step into the "after" identity | "If you're done being the 3pm-crash person, link's below." |

**Formats named in the source:** "before/after video, side-by-side carousels, POV storytelling, mirror ads, internal monologue scripts" (#8).
**Visual cues:** split screen, matched framing for both states, colour or lighting shift (muted → bright), wardrobe or posture change.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Two states shown in one frame or one sequence: split screen, "day 1 / day 30", "me then / me now", swipe-to-compare carousel.
- The same camera angle repeated across time, or an internal-monologue VO that flips tone halfway through.
- Tag: `lever-contrast`. Also tag what's contrasted: `contrast-identity` (who they are) or `contrast-result` (the product outcome only, which is weaker per #8).

## Common mistakes
- A surface-level after (shinier skin) with no identity shift. #8 asks for "an identity shift, not just a surface-level change."
- A before state so mild it creates no gap, or so harsh it reads as shaming.
- Visual contrast only (colour pop) mistaken for narrative contrast. That's [salience](salience-bias.md), which grabs the eye but doesn't show a transformation.

## Pairs with
- **[Aspirational identity](aspirational-identity.md)** (Prompt #8): contrast exposes the gap and aspiration defines the destination.
- **vs [Framing effect](framing-effect.md):** framing presents the *same facts* differently. Contrast places *two different states* next to each other.
- **vs [Salience bias](salience-bias.md):** in Heuristics 101, "contrast" means a visual that stands out. Here it means a narrative gap between two states.
- **[Pain-point amplification](pain-point-amplification.md)** to sharpen the before state. **[What-if scenario](what-if-scenario.md)** shows the after as an imagined world instead of a real one.
