# Cognitive Load

> **"Cognitive load is a big brain killer."** ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:49:25]). How much work an ad or page asks of a brain that wants to conserve energy. Too much, and the viewer bails before your message lands.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** QA-ing any static, UGC frame, end card or landing page before it ships; tearing down a competitor ad that looks "busy"; deciding how many elements, callouts, choices or claims a creative can carry.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a draft (or competitor asset) → a load audit (count of elements, competing focal points, duplicated info, text blocks, choices) + a cut list + a weight order (heaviest = most important).
- **Rules:**
  - DO audit every page in this order: **cognitive load → message → CTA** ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:03:42; 0:11:51; 0:19:19]).
  - DO make "every single thing that's most important… heavy visually". Assume light elements get skipped ([Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:20:57]).
  - DO swap text blocks for icons, symbols, large numbers and arrows ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:35:54; 0:55:46]).
  - DO cap bullets at 3–5 items, ≤2 lines each; headlines ≤3 lines, subheads ≤2 ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:29:29–0:30:05]; [Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:36:37–0:37:38]).
  - DON'T put two moving/pulsing elements in competing colours in one view ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:51:07]).
  - DON'T use layouts that make the eye bounce (image slivers, text over images, overlays on text) ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:47:50–0:49:25]).
  - DON'T repeat the same information in a second format (list + table, the same testimonial twice) ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:36:00; 0:56:33]).
  - DON'T stack claims that compete ("6 Day Results" next to "30 Doses") ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:55:36]).
- **See also:** [Processing fluency](processing-fluency.md) · [Choice overload](choice-overload.md) · [Salience bias](salience-bias.md) · [Heuristic stacking](../heuristic-stacking.md) · [One core emotion per ad](../one-core-emotion-per-ad.md) · [Landing-page psychology audit](../../frameworks/landing-page-psychology-audit.md) · [Visual weight hierarchy](../../frameworks/visual-weight-hierarchy.md)

## Definition
- Sarah's framing: cognitive load "helps the brain understand whether or not it wants to exert energy on what it's currently looking at." The brain is "about a three-pound organ… mostly made up of water and fat" that "takes about **20 percent of the glucose** that you take in on a daily basis." Its only job is to keep you alive, so it judges "do I want to exert energy on this?" Whatever you show must be "well suited to a brain that wants to **conserve energy**" ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:03:42–0:05:18]).
- On choices: "every time I have too many options I get overwhelmed and I quit" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:09:55–0:10:24]).
- Heuristics exist to reduce it: "heuristics just cut down on cognitive load. We want to make it super simple for the brain to see this, process it and say yes" ([Live, May 7](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:15:48]).

## From the course
- **Ads (scroll speed):** with busy us-vs-them or before/after statics, "we have to be very, very careful of cognitive load… we are scrolling so fast… make sure it's not so intense that the brain doesn't know what to do with it and it will skip out." A busy ad can still work if it has "a natural progression for the eyes", heaviest → next heaviest, down an arrow. Keep it and test removing elements one at a time ([Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:18:54–0:23:01]).
- **Landing pages (lens 1 of 3):** the Roast #2 audit applies a fixed rule set: hero text must match the model's face; ~3 nav items; images fit one screen; dark fade behind light text; 3–5 bullets; one or two heavy sections max; combine near-duplicate bullets; icons and big numbers; no duplicated info; review mobile separately; formatting (big/bold first, light/small second) carries long text; colour-code repeated blocks; sharp, not fuzzy, images ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:05:18–0:58:14]). Full table on the session page.
- **Page fatigue:** "the farther they scroll, the harder it is to click that purchase button" ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:36:55]).
- **Bundle builders:** don't make shoppers pick from the whole catalogue. "Select two products that are already well mapped together and have them add a third" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:10:24]). See [IKEA effect](ikea-effect.md).
- **Jaxxon PDP:** the image sliver in the middle "is causing… a lot of cognitive load… you'll notice your eyes are just bouncing all over the place" ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:48:22–0:49:25]).
- **Message order is load too:** "discount applied automatically" before any discount is mentioned confuses; "order your messaging very, very carefully" ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:58:45–0:59:47]).
- **Multi-category stores:** a site selling supplements, minerals and hormonal health for men/women/kids makes "the brain… a little bit overloaded with options" and files you under "Amazon" ([Live, May 21](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:35:15–0:37:01]). See [Category perception](../category-perception.md).
- **Emotion is load:** "the brain gets a little tired when it's asked to process so much emotion" → [one core emotion per ad](../one-core-emotion-per-ad.md) ([Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:41:46–0:42:48]).

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance: the course audits statics and pages. These slots apply the same rules to video.*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (0–3 s) | One idea, one subject in frame, on-screen text ≤7 words | "Every bowl we gave him, he destroyed." ([Roast #3](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:33:42]) |
| On-screen text | One line per scene, never a stacked bullet list | — |
| Proof beat | Big number or icon instead of a spoken spec list | "89% of mamas said…" style stat card (real data only) |
| Claims | One headline claim per video; cut competing numbers | — |
| CTA | One action | "Order yours now." ([active CTA language](../active-cta-language.md)) |

**Visual cues:** one focal object per frame, clean background, the most important thing biggest/brightest, no text over busy b-roll (add a dark fade if you must).

## Deploy in a static
- Rank elements by weight before design: headline → image → max 3 callouts with a bold top line ([Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:36:37–0:38:39]).
- Stack text 1-2-3 on a left-aligned grid; scattered equal-weight blocks force the brain to choose where to start ([Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:34:26–0:36:37]).

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- High load: 4+ callouts of equal weight, headline + subhead + bullets + badges + stars all competing, text over a busy photo, two animated elements, contradictory claims, the same info twice.
- Low load: one headline, one product, a clear eye path, icons/numbers instead of text.
- Tag `heur-cognitive-load-high` (weakness to exploit) or `heur-cognitive-load-low`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** step back and look at the page "like a mountain scene": is anything hard to look at because too much is going on? ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:58:54])
- **DO** homework: audit your ads for what is visually heaviest; that's what gets seen ([Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:21:57]).
- **IF** a page is text-heavy (info product, agency) **THEN** let formatting do the work: big and bold first, light and small second ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:51:51]).
- **IF** you want to know what an element does **THEN** A/B test the ad with it removed ([Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:22:01]).
- **DON'T** add heuristics until the base is clear. Heuristics only help if they cut load ([Live, May 7](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:15:48]).

## Common mistakes
- Treating "more proof" (stars + badges + quotes + bullets) as always better. They're supporting characters; too many becomes load.
- Reviewing desktop only. A mobile crop can change the load completely ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:32:52]).
- Oversized images that need a scroll to see ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:08:25–0:10:31]).

## Pairs with / distinctions
- **vs [Processing fluency](processing-fluency.md):** fluency is the *upside* (easy = feels trustworthy). Cognitive load is the *cost* (hard = the brain bails). Lowering load raises fluency.
- **vs [Choice overload](choice-overload.md):** choice overload is cognitive load caused specifically by too many options.
- **[Salience bias](salience-bias.md):** use salience to direct the eye to one thing; competing salient things *are* load.
- **[Fogg Behavior Model](../../frameworks/fogg-behavior-model.md):** load lowers *ability*.
