# Authority Borrowing (Halo Effect)

> **"When we trust a brand, person, or source, we project that trust onto whatever is associated with them."** (Prompt Library #11). This is the *borrowed* variant of [authority bias](authority-bias.md). **The main authority page is [Authority bias](authority-bias.md).** This page only covers the borrowing and halo mechanics.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you can cite a recognised expert, institution, publication, study or well-known brand, and the audience is skeptical or performance-minded.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a citable source (study, expert quote, publication, partner brand) → a hook that leads with the source, and copy that positions our brand as the one *applying* the insight.
- **Rules:**
  - DO lead with "a surprising stat, quote, or expert-backed claim" (#11 step 1).
  - DO position the brand as the **applier**, not the owner: "You're not claiming the idea; you're making it *actionable*." (#11 step 3)
  - DO keep the tone "sharp, minimal, and confident. Let the logic lead—don't oversell." (#11 step 4)
  - DO check the audience actually trusts that type of authority. See the casting rules on [Authority bias](authority-bias.md).
  - DON'T imply an endorsement you don't have, or misquote a study. Borrowed authority that's exposed as false destroys the halo. *(Derived.)*
- **See also:** [Prompt #11: Authority Borrowing + Data-Driven Insight](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/11-authority-borrowing-and-data-driven-insight.md) · [Authority bias](authority-bias.md) · [Data-driven insight](data-driven-insight.md) · [Social proof](social-proof.md)

## Definition
"**Authority Borrowing** taps into the **halo effect**—when we trust a brand, person, or source, we project that trust onto whatever is associated with them. Referencing known names, institutions, or expert voices gives instant legitimacy." (Prompt Library #11; PDF p.22)

Target tone: "smart, elevated, and trustworthy—designed for skeptical minds", "anchored in hard proof, not hype."

## Borrowing vs the rest of authority
| Variant | What carries the trust | Example | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Authority borrowing** (this page) | An *external* name: a study, institution, publication, expert, or association with a known brand | "A Stanford sleep study found…", "As featured in…", "The method pro cyclists use" | here |
| Authority proof | Our own expert on screen (doctor, dermatologist quote card) | Maelove's dermatologist quote | [Authority bias](authority-bias.md) |
| Authority bias (cheap) | Sounding authoritative: technical terms, guarantees | "Plant-based biopolymer", "20-ft. drop guarantee" | [Authority bias](authority-bias.md) |

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance. Example lines are illustrative, not from course.*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Creator reads or holds up the source | "This [journal/expert] finding changed how I [do X]." |
| Mechanism | Translate the source into one takeaway | "Basically: [insight]. So I stopped [old habit]." |
| Proof | Show the source on screen (headline crop, citation) | Screenshot of the study title or the publication logo |
| CTA | The brand as the easy way to apply it | "[Product] is just that insight in a jar." |

**Formats named in the source:** "minimalist stat-based carousel, '1 stat that changed how I market' short-form video, expert quote + real-world proof pairing" (#11).
**Visual cues:** publication logos ("as seen in"), study headline screenshots, an expert's name and credential lower-third, institutional colour palettes.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- "Studies show", "According to [institution]", "Doctors at X", "As featured in", logo bars, borrowed athlete or celebrity names.
- Ask whether the brand claims the insight or *applies* it. Applying is the stronger pattern per #11.
- Tag: `lever-authority-borrowing`. Use `authority-proof` instead if the expert is the brand's own.

## Common mistakes
- Displaying the authority without an insight ("don't just display it", #11 step 2).
- Logo soup: too many "as seen in" marks hurts [processing fluency](processing-fluency.md).
- An authority type the audience distrusts. See [Authority bias](authority-bias.md).

## Halo effect from a borrowed voice or celebrity (live sessions)
- **HexClad × Gordon Ramsay ([Guest Session 01](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:42:01–0:42:21]):** Sarah to Joanna Wallace: "you already knew that your audience responds really well to harsh… cocky kind of humor because they responded to Gordon Ramsay, so you were able to kind of take his humor and just inject it into the brand and it worked… very halo effect." Joanna replaced the "Rolls-Royce of pans" mainstay (legal threat) with Gordon-voice lines like "the best [expletive] cookware on the planet" [0:04:06–0:06:10].
- **What's borrowed here is a register, not a credential:** the audience's affinity for the celebrity carries over to the brand voice. *(Wiki caveat: this is closer to association/affect transfer than the classic halo effect, where one positive trait colours judgement of unrelated traits. "Halo" is Sarah's label.)*
- **Risk:** borrowed equity can be withdrawn. "The Rolls-Royce of pans" was ~60% of the account when Rolls-Royce threatened to sue [0:04:58–0:05:10].

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Voice | Write in the register of the figure the audience already follows (check the demographics data first) | "The best [expletive] cookware on the planet." ([Guest 01 0:05:10](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md)) |
| Skeptic foil | A recurring character who doubts the borrowed authority on camera | "All right, Gordy… go off, Gordy." [0:36:17–0:36:43] → [Messenger effect](messenger-effect.md) |

- **DO** borrow a voice only if the brand already has a legitimate link to the figure (HexClad's Gordon partnership). **DON'T** imply a celebrity or brand association you don't have, and avoid leaning on another brand's name ("Rolls-Royce of…") as a mainstay.

## Pairs with
- **[Messenger effect](messenger-effect.md):** who delivers the borrowed authority on camera matters as much as the source.
- **[Data-driven insight](data-driven-insight.md)** (Prompt #11): the authority makes the stat credible, and the stat makes the authority useful.
- **[Counterintuitive insight](counterintuitive-insight.md):** a borrowed source that overturns a myth is the strongest version ("Bonus if it challenges a myth", #11 step 1).
- **[Social proof](social-proof.md):** the crowd validates, the authority certifies.
