# Choice Overload (Paradox of Choice)

> **"Choice overload is like one of the biggest drivers of bounce rates I've ever seen."** ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:07:35]). Too many options make people quit instead of choose. Fewer, pre-structured choices convert.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** an ad, end card or landing page offers several products, variants, bundles or paths; writing "choose your…" carousel or quiz ads; tearing down a competitor page with big menus or many bundle tiers.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the set of options a viewer must pick from → a reduced, ordered set (≤3 nav items, 2–3 pre-mapped bundle options, one pre-selected default, or a binary A-vs-B) + the label that tells each person which option is theirs.
- **Rules:**
  - DO keep top navigation to about 3 items ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:07:14–0:07:41]).
  - DO build bundles as "two products already well mapped together" + "add a third", not a full-catalogue builder ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:10:24]).
  - DO keep choice structure on PDPs "pretty small, pretty structured… so we don't give too many options" ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:12:29]).
  - DO pre-select the option you want and label who each option suits ("Best for most legs") ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:55:33]; [Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:08:50]).
  - DON'T sell many unrelated categories on one site. Split brands or segment each category with its own palette ([Live, May 21](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:35:15–0:39:06]).
  - DON'T show several products in one hero shot when varying a winning static: one product only ([Roast #3](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:52:41]).
- **See also:** [Cognitive load](cognitive-load.md) · [Decoy effect](decoy-effect.md) · [Bandwagon effect](bandwagon-effect.md) · [IKEA effect](ikea-effect.md) · [Category perception](../category-perception.md) · [Bundle psychology](../../frameworks/offer-and-bundle-psychology.md)

## Definition
- Named by Sarah as "paradox of choice" among the heuristics still to come in Heuristics 101 ([Stacking Psychology for Growth](../../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/05-stacking-psychology-for-growth.md) [07:31]).
- "We have to be very careful not to overload people with choice… too many things on a page makes people bounce immediately" ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:07:14–0:07:41]).
- "Every time I have too many options I get overwhelmed and I quit" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:09:55]). It is a special case of [cognitive load](cognitive-load.md).
- *The Elements of Choice* (Eric J. Johnson), Sarah's #3 book: "how you present options to people is going to drastically change what they choose and how much of it they consume", including the decoy and which option is pre-selected ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:15:58–0:16:33]).

## From the course
- **Navigation:** ANATATI's three nav items (Collections, Lookbook, About) = good ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:07:14]). "The more choices people have to make on the site, the more we're draining that energy" [0:10:31].
- **Category sprawl:** a wellness umbrella store selling vitamins, beauty supplements and hair dye gets filed as "Amazon", and "Amazon will always be able to psychologically defeat you". Happy Mammoth split its mom/kids/men's lines because conversion rates were "in the trash" ([Live, May 21](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:35:15–0:38:03]).
- **Bundles:** organise logically (small-, medium-, large-dog teeth bundles, then "what do you want to add on?") ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:10:24–0:11:27]).
- **Two selectors on one PDP** (pack size + subscription/one-time): reframe in uses ("30 days of use / 90 days of use") and pre-select ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:55:05–0:55:50]).
- **Binary choice ad (member test):** Aazar Shad's "Which Summer Chai Would You Choose?" (doc note: "Don't make people scroll through 6 options. Just give them a simple A vs B") came within ~$2 CPA of the winner but **didn't scale**. Sarah suggested pairing choice with scarcity: "the chai that sold out — which one would you pick?" ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:26:12–0:28:55]).

## Deploy in a UGC script
| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Binary choice question with two products on screen | "Which summer chai would you choose?" ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:26:16]) |
| Mid-script | Creator narrows the range for the viewer | "If you have thick hair, get this one. Fine hair, this one." (illustrative, not from course) |
| CTA | One recommended option, named | "Most people start with the [X] pack." (real best-seller only; see [bandwagon](bandwagon-effect.md)) |

**Visual cues:** at most two products side by side; a pointing gesture or circle on the recommended one; never a full product-range flat lay in a prospecting ad.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Signals of overload (weakness): mega-menus, 5+ bundle tiers, catalogue carousels as prospecting ads, "shop all" CTAs, multiple selectors with no default.
- Signals of good choice architecture: A-vs-B frames, pre-selected tiers, "most popular" labels, who-it's-for microcopy.
- Tag `heur-choice-overload` (problem) or `heur-choice-architecture` (done well).

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** decide the option you want them to take, then make it the default and the visually heaviest.
- **IF** you must show many options **THEN** pre-bucket them (by size, need, hair type) so the visitor only chooses within one bucket.
- **IF** testing a choice ad **THEN** stack it with another heuristic (scarcity) rather than running choice alone ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:28:55]).
- **DON'T** add a gift-bag option on the PDP; it splits buyers into gift vs self (Sarah's view, move it to cart) ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:23:30]).

## Common mistakes
- Equating more variants with more conversions.
- Prospecting with catalogue ads to a cold audience that has no frame of reference.
- Bundling subjective things (flavours), which forces a taste decision ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:16:21]).

## Pairs with / distinctions
- **[Cognitive load](cognitive-load.md):** the parent concept.
- **[Decoy effect](decoy-effect.md):** the opposite tool: *adding* a deliberate third option to steer choice, not to widen it.
- **[Bandwagon effect](bandwagon-effect.md):** "most customers buy X" resolves choice anxiety by showing the default.
- **[IKEA effect](ikea-effect.md):** co-creation works only if the choice set stays small.
