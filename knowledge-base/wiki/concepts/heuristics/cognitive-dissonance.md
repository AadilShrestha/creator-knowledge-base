# Cognitive Dissonance

> **"Instead of saying… you could buy three months of weight loss, there's cognitive dissonance that you're creating there, because people are going to go: pasta doesn't help you lose weight."** ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:39:48]). When an ad's claim clashes with what the viewer already believes (or two parts of the ad clash with each other), the brain resolves it the cheap way: it disbelieves the ad and scrolls.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** QA-ing a script or static for claims that fight a core belief; checking that headline, image, badges and landing page tell one consistent story; choosing between a USP-led and a use-case-led promise.
- **Inputs → outputs:** draft ad (+ its landing page) + the audience's core beliefs about the category → a list of conflicts (belief vs claim, claim vs claim, image vs text, order) and a rewrite that frames the benefit through a behaviour they already accept.
- **Rules:**
  - DO frame by the **behavioural use case**, not a USP that contradicts a core belief: "Weight loss is your USP but it is not the use case" → "You can make up to 15 dinners… more suited to your goal of losing weight" ([Jul 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:47–0:40:48]).
  - DON'T put competing claims side by side ("6 Day Results" + "30 Doses"): the viewer concludes "I don't have the ability to do this, or is this not real" ([Jul 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:55:36–0:56:50]).
  - DON'T reference something before it's introduced ("discount applied automatically" before any discount). "Order your messaging very, very carefully" [0:58:45–0:59:47].
  - DO keep metaphors internally logical ("blind spots" → "uncover", not "cover"): the brain makes "a very quick judgment call as to whether or not those two concepts make sense together, and if they don't… skip on out" ([Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:25:34–0:26:52]).
  - DO match overlay text to the model's facial expression; "the brain… will compare them: do these match?" ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:06:23–0:06:50]) (wiki tag: same congruence mechanism).
- **See also:** [Counterintuitive insight](counterintuitive-insight.md) · [Hero image ↔ copy congruence](../hero-image-copy-congruence.md) · [One core emotion per ad](../one-core-emotion-per-ad.md) · [Framing effect](framing-effect.md) · [Processing fluency](processing-fluency.md) · [First principles thinking](first-principles-thinking.md)

## Definition
- Context (not from course): the discomfort of holding two conflicting beliefs, or a belief and contradicting information; people reduce it, often by rejecting the new information.
- In the course, Sarah names it once, explicitly, for the low-carb pasta bundle ([Jul 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:39:48]). The other examples on this page are the same "these two things don't fit, so I'm out" mechanism; their dissonance label is a wiki tag.

## Two uses: avoid it vs. engineer it
| Use | When | Example | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Avoid** dissonance with a core belief the ad can't win against | Product claim fights a deep belief ("pasta isn't healthy") | Sell dinners, not weight loss | [Jul 2 0:38:47–0:40:48](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) |
| **Engineer** a short, resolvable tension, then resolve it in the ad | Hook that flips a belief the viewer only loosely holds | Counterintuitive insight: "creates cognitive dissonance—a kind of tension that demands resolution" | [Counterintuitive insight](counterintuitive-insight.md) (Prompt Library #13) |

Rule of thumb (wiki synthesis): engineer tension only when the ad itself delivers the resolution (proof, mechanism) within seconds; avoid it when the belief is a core identity/category belief the product can't overturn. Sarah's "question their attachment" switching hooks are the engineered kind ([switching behavior](../switching-behavior.md)).

## Deploy in a UGC script
| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Promise | Benefit expressed through an accepted behaviour | "I still have pasta night twice a week. It just fits my goal now." (illustrative, not from course, built on [Jul 2 0:39:48](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md)) |
| Proof | One claim per beat; no second number that undercuts the first | — |
| On-screen text | Matches what the creator's face is doing in that shot | Excited face → excited line ([Roast #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:06:23]) |
| Engineered tension (optional) | Flip a loose belief, resolve within ~5 s | "Is your matcha really doing what you think it is?" ([Jun 18 1:03:25](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md)) |

**Visual cues:** the image shows exactly what the line promises; badges don't contradict the headline; scene/props don't send a different life-stage signal ([context priming](../hero-image-copy-congruence.md)).

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- A claim the audience will instinctively reject (diet food promising weight loss, "detox" for skeptics).
- Badge stacks with numbers that tell different stories; offers referenced before introduced.
- Headline vs image mismatch; mixed metaphors.
- Tag: `heur-dissonance-risk` (a weakness to exploit) or `heur-dissonance-hook` (engineered tension resolved in-ad).

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** list the category's core beliefs (from reviews, Reddit, the first-principles prompt) before writing claims.
- **IF** the USP fights a core belief **THEN** sell the use case the belief allows.
- **DO** read the whole ad (and its landing page) in sequence: does each element make sense given only what came before?
- **DON'T** stack two emotional promises; that's dissonance at the emotion level ([one core emotion per ad](../one-core-emotion-per-ad.md)).

## Common mistakes
- Leading with the most impressive claim even when the audience can't believe it.
- Badge soup where numbers contradict.
- Clever headline whose verb breaks its own metaphor.
