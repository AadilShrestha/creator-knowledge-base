# Anchoring Effect (and Frame of Reference)

> **"We are anchoring everybody to a $79 option."** ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:35:50–0:36:44]). The first number, price or comparison object the brain sees becomes the yardstick for everything after it. Choose your anchor on purpose.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing price, value or offer lines in a script or static; positioning a premium/high-ticket product; ordering bundle tiers on the page an ad sends to; tearing down a competitor's "X of Y" comparison ("the Rolls-Royce of…").
- **Inputs → outputs:** product price + tiers + category price norm + a pricier comparison object → anchor choice (what is seen first), comparison line, tier order and savings shown in dollars.
- **Rules:**
  - DO decide what number the viewer sees first. Starting with the cheapest tier anchors everyone low, and bigger tiers then feel "way too high" ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:35:50–0:36:44]; "technically they should be doing this backwards" [0:41:30]).
  - DO compare a premium product to "something that's way more expensive than you" ([Live, June 18](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [0:30:27]).
  - DO show savings as a dollar amount, and keep tier steps easy to subtract (79 → 229, not 79 → 224) ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:37:47; 0:43:05]).
  - DO give the brain a **frame of reference** for quantity ("Buy a 36 pack, make 100 dinners"; "lasts 180 days / 15 washes") ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:40:48–0:41:53; 0:56:36]).
  - DON'T borrow another brand's name as your anchor without checking legal risk (HexClad's "Rolls-Royce of pans" drew a legal threat) ([Joanna Wallace](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:04:58–0:05:10]).
  - DON'T exploit the absence of a frame of reference to oversupply buyers; it creates churn (see [Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:24:05–0:25:09]).
- **See also:** [Decoy effect](decoy-effect.md) · [Framing effect](framing-effect.md) · [Luxury positioning](../luxury-positioning.md) · [Perception management](../perception-management.md) · [Bundle psychology](../../frameworks/offer-and-bundle-psychology.md) · [Contrast](contrast.md)

## Definition
- In the course, "anchoring" is Sarah's word for the buy-box order: the first price seen sets the reference point ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:35:50]). "The brain actually has an emotional reaction to numbers" [0:43:05].
- Sarah's broader idea, **frame of reference**: "humans… don't have a frame of reference for how much do I actually need", so they accept whatever reference you give them ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:31:50–0:32:40]). Wrap-up: "add those little badges that give a frame of reference" [0:56:36].
- *Wiki note:* the Maserati, "Rolls-Royce of pans" and "BMW of lunch boxes" examples below are tagged anchoring by the wiki. Sarah didn't use the word there.

## From the course
- **Nutrafol buy box:** Monthly $79 → 3-month $224 → 6-month $422. Starting at $79 anchors low, so the jump to $224 feels too much; 3 × $79 = $237 means "I'm saving like 10 bucks… I could just come back every month." Fixes: show dollar savings, widen the savings gap between tiers, use easy-math prices (229) ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:35:50–0:44:07]).
- **Price endings:** 7/9 endings are "true-ish", but "I don't really care what you end with"; even delineation matters more ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:43:05–0:44:07]). Jaxxon "$249 → $199, I can do that math really easily" [0:50:27].
- **Maserati (as Sarah tells it):** when buyers dried up around 2008, Maserati showed cars at boat shows and private-aircraft shows: "next to a $2 million G6 this car looks like a steal… $200,000 to $500,000, I can afford that." It also filtered for buyers with cash flow ([Live, June 18](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [0:29:21–0:32:35]).
- **Obvi 12-month upsell (~$400):** "crushing", because buyers have no frame of reference for how much they need ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:31:50–0:32:40]).
- **Category comparisons:** HexClad's "Rolls-Royce of pans" was 60% of the account until Rolls-Royce threatened to sue ([Joanna Wallace](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:04:58–0:05:10]). OmieBox review "This is the BMW of lunch boxes" helps justify a premium price in a ~$20 category ([Roast #3](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:43:07]).
- **Time units as reference:** "90 days" works in weight loss because the industry repeats it; for shampoo "I don't know how long 90 days is". Use the buyer's units ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:45:20–0:46:05]).

## Deploy in a UGC script
| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook / value beat | Premium comparison object | "This is the BMW of lunch boxes." (customer review, [Roast #3](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:43:07]) |
| Value beat | Price against a bigger reference | "It's less than [what I spend on X] a week." (illustrative, not from course) |
| Quantity beat | Frame of reference in use units | "One 36-pack is about 100 dinners." ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:41:53]) |
| Offer | Dollar saving, not % | "You save $[X] on the 3-month." (real numbers only) |

**Visual cues:** the product beside a much pricier object (Maserati-at-the-jet-show logic); a strike-through original price; big core price, small saving badge in its own colour ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:41:00–0:42:03]).

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- "The [luxury brand] of [category]", per-day/per-cup reframes ("under $0.50 per cup"), crossed-out prices, tier order on the landing page, "a $X value" bonuses.
- Ask: what is the first number or object the viewer sees, and does it make the real price feel small or big?
- Tag `heur-anchoring`.

## Rules of thumb
- **IF** going up-market **THEN** place or compare the product next to something far pricier ([Live, June 18](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [0:30:27]).
- **IF** a price block has several numbers **THEN** give each its own weight (size, strike-through, coloured badge) ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:41:30]).
- **DO** make bundle savings big enough to matter; ~$10 off three months reads as "I'll just buy monthly" ([Live, July 16](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:36:44–0:38:52]).
- **DON'T** anchor with an untrue "value" or competitor price.

## Common mistakes
- Leading the buy box with the cheapest tier by default without testing the reverse.
- Percent-only discounts that require math.
- Comparing to a famous brand name you don't have rights to.

## Pairs with / distinctions
- **[Decoy effect](decoy-effect.md):** the decoy is a deliberately unattractive option that makes the target look better; the anchor is the first reference point.
- **[Framing effect](framing-effect.md):** per-cup/per-day reframes are framing that resets the anchor.
- **[Luxury positioning](../luxury-positioning.md):** never discount; anchor high instead.
- **[Contrast](contrast.md):** side-by-side comparison makes the gap visible.
