# Micro-Story (Micro-Storytelling)

> **"Micro-Storytelling gives people something to care about—fast."** (Prompt Library #12). One real moment (awkward, painful, triumphant or shocking) builds emotional connection in a flash, so the ad feels like a human moment rather than a sales pitch.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing UGC or founder-video openings, turning a review into a script, or tearing down a competitor ad that opens on a moment instead of a product.
- **Inputs → outputs:** one customer moment (from reviews, Reddit, interviews) → a 1–3 line story beat with real stakes, where the product is the turning point.
- **Rules:**
  - DO tell "a single turning point, revelation, or unexpected emotional beat. Keep it lean." (#12 step 1)
  - DO use "real, relatable stakes. It doesn't have to be dramatic—just emotionally sharp, awkward, or oddly familiar." (#12 step 3)
  - DO make the brand "the *unseen twist* or the key to resolution", "the moment that changes the character's trajectory" (#12 step 4).
  - DO pick a hyper-specific situation only your buyer recognises ("hair clogging the drain"). Generic stories bring generic, expensive buyers ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:55–0:41:44]).
  - DON'T tell a full origin story. One beat per ad.
- **See also:** [Prompt #12: Micro-Story + Suspense](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/12-micro-story-and-suspense.md) · [Suspense](suspense.md) · [Affect heuristic](affect-heuristic.md) · [Specificity](specificity.md) · [Vulnerability](vulnerability.md)

## Definition
"**Micro-Storytelling** gives people something to care about—fast. It uses real moments (awkward, painful, triumphant, shocking) to forge emotional connection in a flash. Stories give context to transformation and make ads feel like *human moments*, not sales pitches." (Prompt Library #12; PDF p.24)

Target tone: "cinematic, vivid, and tension-filled—like an episode of a binge-worthy series… but packed into 15 seconds or 1 slide."

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance. Example lines are illustrative, not from course, except where a session is cited.*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Drop into the moment, mid-scene, first person | "There was more hair in my shower than on my brush." (member line praised by Sarah, [Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:41:44]) |
| Stakes | One sentence on why it mattered | "My wedding was in six weeks." (illustrative) |
| Turn | The product enters as the twist | "Then my sister handed me this." (illustrative) |
| Resolution / proof | Show, don't tell. The changed moment | Same shower, close-up, weeks later |
| CTA | Invite them into the same turn | "If that's your shower right now, start here." |

**Formats named in the source:** "15s video with a cut-to-black, carousel that stops on slide 4/5, text-based 'story post' that teases final answer in comments or CTA, UGC montage where punchline is withheld" (#12).
**Visual cues:** a POV or handheld shot, real environments (a bathroom, a car), a text-on-screen narration line, and the evidence object (the drain, the receipt, the empty bottle).

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- The first line is a past-tense moment ("The day I…", "My husband looked at me and…"), not a claim. The product appears late, as the turn.
- Tag: `lever-micro-story`. Add `lever-suspense` if the resolution is withheld.

## Common mistakes
- Story without stakes: a nice moment nobody cares about.
- The product arrives too late in a long story (a founder video with the product at second 18 and a high CPA, [Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:37:31–0:38:55]).
- A generic situation that "anyone" could have, so Meta finds the cheapest rather than the right buyer.
