# Authority Bias

> **"We trust perceived experts."** (e-book, p.9). Shortcut #3 in [Heuristics 101](../../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/README.md). It covers borrowing credibility from an expert (*authority proof*) and simply sounding authoritative (*authority bias*).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** the product is "medicalized" or solves a health or technical problem; writing claims, feature callouts or guarantee lines; choosing whether to cast an expert creator; tearing down competitor ads that use doctors, "clinically proven" or technical jargon.
- **Inputs → outputs:** product tech/material terms, guarantees, available experts, audience attitude to authority → an authority line (term, guarantee or expert quote) and a casting decision (doctor vs nurse vs peer).
- **Rules:**
  - DO check whether the audience trusts the authority before using it. Many boomers trust peers, nurses or functional practitioners over doctors.
  - DO use **authority bias** cheaply: authoritative terms ("plant-based biopolymer") and guarantees ("20-ft. drop guarantee," "Preserved Taste Guaranty") need no expert.
  - DO use **authority proof** (expert quote, face, credential) for medicalized products when the audience trusts that expert type.
  - DON'T assume "expert" means doctor. Perceived experts include well-read people, family and friends.
  - DON'T bury the ad in jargon. One authoritative term is enough, so keep [processing fluency](processing-fluency.md).
  - IF the audience has been let down by experts (e.g. women 30+ or 50+ in hormone/menopause health) THEN lead with **validation** ("your gut feeling is right… you know your body best") and, if you use authority, switch to the **type they now trust** (functional practitioners, naturopaths, acupuncturists). See [Validation over authority](../validation-over-authority.md) ([Live, May 21](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:41:44–0:43:57]; [Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:13:34–0:15:40]).
  - DO choose the **messenger** as carefully as the message: "who says the message is actually a little bit more important than what the message is" ([messenger effect](messenger-effect.md); [Joanna Wallace session](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:37:21]).
  - DO use specific authority titles, not "trusted by experts" ("Approved by Ayurvedic doctors", [Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:25:57–0:26:06]).
- **See also:** [Social proof](social-proof.md), [Processing fluency](processing-fluency.md), [Heuristic stacking](../heuristic-stacking.md), [Prompt Library](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/README.md) ("Authority Borrowing + Data-Driven Insights").

## Definition
- "We trust perceived experts, and I say that **perceived** on purpose" ([L2 04:16]).
- An expert can be "basically anybody." Not only doctors, lawyers or government officials, but "somebody who just has read a lot, somebody who knows a whole lot about the topic," family members, or friends ([L2 04:24]–[04:39]). E-book: "Not actual PhDs necessarily. Just anyone who seems to know more than us."

## Why it works
"Following authority figures used to mean survival. If we follow people who are in a position of authority, we have a better chance of staying on the planet for a lot longer" ([L2 04:45]–[04:54]).

## Authority bias vs authority proof
From [Stacking Psychology for Growth](../../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/05-stacking-psychology-for-growth.md) [02:41]–[03:13]:

| | **Authority proof** | **Authority bias** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | "Requires somebody in a position of authority to have said something" | "Just says that we are sensitive to people who are in a position of authority" |
| Looks like | "A quote, it's a five stars, it's their picture" | The brand talking like an authority: technical terms, guarantees |
| Example | Dermatologist quote on the Maelove ad | "Plant-based biopolymer" (Pela); "Preserved Taste Guaranty" (Firelight Flask) |

"You often don't have to resort to pulling in any sort of doctor-based quotes... a great way to do this is just to sound more authoritative" ([L5 02:29]–[02:41]).

## How to use it in ads (from the course)
- **Borrowed authority:** "Dr. Recommended," or a credentialed expert describing her experience. "We're basically borrowing authority from her because she has some sort of certification or award or expert status" ([L2 04:56]–[05:15]).
- **Technical-sounding terms:** "Anytime you can use larger scale words." Use "terms that somebody in a position of authority would use" ([L5 02:06]–[02:29]). E-book: "'plant-based biopolymer' = big authority energy."
- **Guarantees and long-term benefits:** they "automatically create a level of authority... this person obviously backs their product." People "feel a whole lot safer... the authority is going to take care of it if it doesn't work out for me" ([L5 04:10]–[04:38]). E-book: "Dermatologist-recommended," "Taste guarantee."
- **Badges:** "Clinically Proven" (e-book mock ad p.9).
- **Best fit:** "medicalized" products or ones that solve "some sort of a medical issue." Sarah says it's "used sparingly in the industry, but I wish we would use it more" ([L2 04:05]–[04:14]).

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance: the course shows authority on statics. The UGC slot mapping is not from the course.*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (authority proof) | Credentialed creator on camera with a name/credential lower-third | "As a [nurse / dermatologist / trainer], here's what I actually use for [problem]." |
| Hook (peer authority, for low-trust audiences) | Experienced peer: "I've tried every [category]…" | "I've read every study on [X] so you don't have to." |
| Mid-script | One technical term, explained simply | "It's a plant-based biopolymer, basically it absorbs the drop instead of cracking." |
| Pre-CTA risk reversal | Guarantee stated by the creator | "And there's a 20-foot drop guarantee, so if it breaks, they replace it." |

**Visual cues:** a lab coat or scrubs, credential lower-thirds, a "Clinically Proven" chip, a guarantee seal, spec callouts with leader lines.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- **Authority proof:** expert face, name and credential ("Board Certified Dermatologist"), "Dr. recommended," quote cards, press logos.
- **Authority bias:** dense technical nouns ("biopolymer," "peptide complex"), "clinically proven," "engineered," guarantee seals, warranty claims.
- Ask: *who is the authority, and does this audience trust that type?* Flag doctor-led ads aimed at older, authority-sceptical audiences as a likely mismatch ([L2 05:20]–[05:52]).

## Rules of thumb
- **IF** the product is medicalized **THEN** use authority. It's "so, so, so, so powerful" there ([L2 04:09]).
- **IF** the audience has had bad experiences with doctors (often boomers) **THEN** use peers, nurses or functional practitioners instead ([L2 05:29]–[05:52]).
- **DO** "sound more authoritative" before reaching for doctor quotes ([L5 02:36]).
- **DO** add a guarantee. It reads as authority and safety ([L5 04:10]).
- **DON'T** frame authority carelessly: "Choose your authority bias wisely" ([L2 05:54]).

## Live-session updates: right-type authority, validation, messengers, lived proof
*From the May–July 2025 live calls. These refine "choose your authority bias wisely" into concrete rules.*

### 1. Validation over authority (May 21)
- Asked about functional supplements for women 30+: they've been told "there's nothing wrong with you… here's some drugs", so they've become "a little bit sensitive to authority… not sure who should I believe". Nostalgia works, "but the best: validation. **Your gut feeling is right… XYZ is not serving you anymore**", "super crazy powerful." Say "you know your body the best. If you don't feel right, something's not right." Add a low-risk entry (trial sample, checklist). "Validation, validation, 100%." ([Live, May 21](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:41:44–0:43:57])
- Same move for research-heavy buyers: "You know a good deal when you see one. You're a smart, savvy shopper" ([Live, June 4](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:34:31–0:36:36]), and for industry call-outs: "you're smart for noticing" ([Live, June 11](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md) [0:20:12]).
- Full page: [Validation over authority](../validation-over-authority.md).

### 2. Authority still works if it's the right type (July 2)
- "It's not that authority bias won't work, it just means **what type of authority** do we need to use. Maybe it's not doctors, maybe it's functional health practitioners… acupuncturists… a naturopath." For women 50–90 brushed off in doctors' offices. "What type of doctor you use, though: critical." ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:13:34–0:15:40])
- ChatGPT will say "authority works for your customer type" but "won't be able to tell you what type of authority because it doesn't have context". Give it the persona doc and a heuristic list [0:19:48–0:20:56; 0:31:29].
- **Reconciling 1 and 2:** validation first (hand judgement back to the buyer), then, if needed, an authority from the camp the buyer already trusts. Not a contradiction; it's sequencing.

### 3. The messenger matters (May 23)
- Sarah: "who says the message is actually a little bit more important than what the message is." Employees close to the brand can be stronger messengers than top customers; HexClad's recurring skeptic Paige tests Gordon Ramsay's claims on camera ([Joanna Wallace session](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:35:37–0:37:48]). Full page: [Messenger effect](messenger-effect.md).
- A third-party character can carry authority as a person, not a badge: the vet "Dr. Riley" as narrator, "the trusted authority and emotional bridge" ([Live, May 14](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-14-live-with-sarah.md) [0:36:30–0:42:59]).

### 4. Lived proof: "how I" hooks (May 7)
- "How I activates the **authority proof** heuristic. How I says I actually went through this experience and I now know all of the speed bumps that you're about to hit." It also activates risk reduction ([Live, May 7](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:21:48–0:22:46]). See [Risk-reduction hooks](../risk-reduction-hooks.md).

### 5. Other live rules
- Specific titles beat generic claims: Aazar's variant replaced "Trusted by experts" with "Approved by Ayurvedic doctors" plus a big, recognisable badge ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:25:57–0:26:06]). It lost to scarcity in that test.
- "Anytime you can do logos of authority proof on sites… even if you've only had one authority-based person who has said nice things about you, put it on your site." Awards count too ([Roast My Ads #2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:56:55–0:57:13]).
- A guarantee or "promise" reads as **security**: "anytime you put guarantees anywhere in your ad, that speaks to security" (Maelove "No more guessing. That's our promise.", [Live, June 4](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:48:21]).
- Medical specificity as differentiation: "boost your mitochondria, the powerhouse of the body" instead of "activate your health" ([Live, June 11](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md) [0:43:27]).
- Never fabricate authority ("Featured in Outside and Men's Health" invented by AI, flagged in the Self-Targeting module follow-ups).
