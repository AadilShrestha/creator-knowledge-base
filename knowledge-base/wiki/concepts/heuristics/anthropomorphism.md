# Anthropomorphism (Personified Products)

> **"Making a personification out of things that are not alive… you can start to add googly eyes onto anything."** (Sarah, [Roast My Ads #3](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:57:59]). Give the product (or an object near it) a face, a personality and a bit of attitude. It stops the scroll because it's weird, and it gives people something to attach to.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a winning static format is getting stale for the team (not the audience); briefing a meme or humor ad; looking for a brand character/mascot; low-interest categories (insurance, supplements, household).
- **Inputs → outputs:** the hero product + the brand's working humor type and zone → 2–3 personified-product concepts (face, expression, one-line personality, what it's "about to do"), kept in the same zone/intensity as the winners.
- **Rules:**
  - DO personify the **product itself** so the joke attaches to the product, not just to "I like memes": make "the gelato the character… snarky… kind of pissy" ([Jun 4](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:31:21–0:33:26]).
  - DO keep the zone and intensity that already work when you add the face: "I would stick with the… valence zone that you're using though, and the intensity too" ([Roast #3](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:58:30–0:59:30]).
  - DO try it as one step on the format-variation ladder (close-up, crop, two products, tiny, in hand, **personified**) instead of abandoning a winning format [0:52:41–0:58:59].
  - DO consider a non-human, non-animal mascot for low-interest categories ("a paper clip… a car tire") that talks about what happens if they don't buy ([Jun 4](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:19:46–0:21:52]).
  - DON'T make the character so off-brand that the brand team pulls it; check how weird "they're into" ([Roast #3](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:57:00]).
- **See also:** [Fluency mechanism](../fluency-mechanism.md) · [Humor in ads](../humor-in-ads.md) · [Von Restorff effect](von-restorff-effect.md) · [Pattern disruption](pattern-disruption.md) · [Creative primitives](../creative-primitives.md)

## Definition
Attributing human traits (face, emotion, intention, voice) to non-human things. Sarah couldn't recall the term live ("there's a term for it and… my brain can't"); "anthropomorphism" is the wiki label ([Roast #3](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:57:59]).

## From the course
- **Evil-eyed ice cream (Roast #3, Milky lactase):** "What happens if we put like kind of like evil eyes on ice cream and make him have like a really evil smile, like he's about to do some damage… This sounds strange… but weird works, especially for in the algorithm and the actual feed… because it jumps out at you. I'm used to seeing ads with ice cream with a product next to them… an evil face on ice cream, now that's kind of funny." [0:57:59–0:58:30]
- **The "AI cow" (Darian's idea, Roast #3):** an AI cow punching someone in the stomach, Chick-fil-A-cow style; Sarah: "That would crush this… I would click." Variants: a cow in a robber mask, "the cows of the evil one". [0:56:53–0:57:59]
- **Gelato as the snarky character (Jun 4):** Ahmed's meme ad hit 3.5× average CTR and died fast because people attached to the meme identity. "What we technically need them to attach to is the product and the humor." Next test: "create your own meme this week where the gelato is the character… have Chat make some cartoons of gelato as a character… snarky… kind of pissy… kind of mean." She calls it a "fluency method". [0:30:17–0:33:26]
- **Mascots (Jun 4):** Mayhem, Flo, Chester Cheetah, the Broncos horse; for a new insurer, test a mascot, ideally "non-animal, non-human… a paper clip… a car tire". [0:19:46–0:21:52] → [Fluency mechanism](../fluency-mechanism.md)
- **Personified objects in slapstick (Joanna session):** Sarah lists personified objects and the TikTok "talking potato" under physical/slapstick humor [0:28:17–0:31:27] → [Humor in ads](../humor-in-ads.md). Tether's own mascot Dex (a red, deadpan British dinosaur) is a character-as-fluency example [0:34:35–0:35:37].

## Deploy in a UGC script
| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | Product "speaks" or reacts before the creator | Ice cream with evil eyes: "Oh, you thought you could have me today?" (illustrative, not from course) |
| Skit | Personified villain = the problem; product = the fix | The menacing "dairy" character vs the lactase card (illustrative, built on [Roast #3 0:57:59](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md)) |
| Recurring | Same character across ads (series) | "Snarky gelato" weekly meme ([Jun 4 0:33:26](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md)) |
| Mascot VO | Object narrates the consequence of not buying | A talking car tire for insurance ([Jun 4 0:20:00](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md)) |

**Visual cues:** googly or evil eyes and a mouth stuck on the product or trigger food; cartoon overlay on a real product photo; consistent character design across ads; expression matching the brand's humor type (snarky, deadpan, menacing).

## Deploy in a static
- Take the proven product-centre static and add a face to the product or the trigger food; change nothing else (one-variable test).
- Meme template with the product as the protagonist.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Faces on products/food, talking objects, cartoon mascots, an object with a name and attitude.
- Is the character the product (good: attaches to brand) or a random meme (risk: spikes then dies)?
- Tag: `heur-anthropomorphism` (+ `mascot` if recurring).

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** test the personified version against the plain winner with everything else held constant.
- **DO** match the character's attitude to the humor that already worked (snarky gelato because snark worked).
- **IF** the character wins **THEN** keep it and make it recurring ([fluency mechanism](../fluency-mechanism.md)).

## Common mistakes
- A cute face with no attitude or role (nothing to remember).
- A character that changes the zone (a menacing face on a calm Zone 1 brand).
- Generic memes that attach people to "memes", not the brand.

## Pairs with / distinctions
- **vs [Fluency mechanism](../fluency-mechanism.md):** anthropomorphism is the creative device; a fluency mechanism is the *recurring* character/asset that makes the brand easy to recall. A personified product becomes a fluency mechanism once it repeats.
- Pairs with [Von Restorff](von-restorff-effect.md) (weird stands out), [humor in ads](../humor-in-ads.md), [creative primitives](../creative-primitives.md) (one-variable variation).
