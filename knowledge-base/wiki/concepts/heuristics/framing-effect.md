# Framing Effect

> **"How you present info changes how it's received. Same facts, different frames = different decisions."** (e-book, p.15). Shortcut #9 in [Heuristics 101](../../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/README.md). Sarah calls it "my ultimate favorite" and the most versatile heuristic.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** communicating price, size, quantity, duration or value; writing value-angle scripts for budget-conscious buyers; tearing down competitor price and value claims.
- **Inputs → outputs:** absolute numbers (ml, price, count, months) → per-use or per-day reframes ("3 sprays/day = 1 year," cost per wear).
- **Rules:**
  - DO convert big or abstract numbers into small, familiar usage units (per spray, per wear, per day, per cup).
  - DO reframe price "in usage terms": cost per wear, long-term value comparison, savings.
  - DO frame so "the customer [feels] smart for buying."
  - DON'T assume the audience understands raw specs ("100 ml" means nothing to many buyers).
- **See also:** [Temporal discounting](temporal-discounting.md), [Processing fluency](processing-fluency.md), [Heuristic stacking](../heuristic-stacking.md).

## Definition
"How you present the information... will change how people perceive it. Especially if the numbers are the same" ([L4 04:15]–[04:25]).

## Why it works
"The brain does not evaluate absolute value." It won't look at a phone, remote or glasses and ask "what is this made of? What did that material cost? And what kind of value should I place on this? What it actually does is just compares to what is kind of the environment around it" ([L4 04:29]–[04:59]).

## How to use it in ads (from the course)
- **Break units down:** Sarah could have written "100 ml," but she wasn't sure the audience "knew what that meant." Perfumes on a dresser all say 100 ml but run out at different rates. So she framed it as **3 sprays/day = 1 year; 5/day = 6–9 months; 10/day = 3–5 months** ([L4 05:05]–[05:51]).
- "Try reframing your price in usage terms... cost per wear, or like long-term value comparison, savings." "Break it down into smaller units" ([L4 06:18]–[06:34]).
- Especially for "budget friendly people who really, really want a good bang for their buck" ([L4 06:38]–[06:47]).
- **E-book:** "Breaking down big numbers into daily actions ('3 sprays/day = 1 year of fragrance') · Showing cost per use, not just sticker price." Pro tip: "Frame it in terms that make the customer feel smart for buying."
- **E-book mock ad (p.15):** gummy bears, "Tastes Like Candy. Works Like a Supplement." This frames a supplement as a treat (a category reframe).

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance: not from the course.*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (value angle) | "[Price] sounds like a lot until you do the math." | "$90 sounds like a lot until you realise it's 25 cents a spray." |
| Proof beat | Creator does the per-use math on screen | "3 sprays a day and this lasts me a full year." |
| Comparison | Against the everyday alternative | "Less than one coffee a week." |
| Category reframe hook | "Tastes like [X]. Works like [Y]." | "Tastes like candy. Works like a supplement." |

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Per-unit math: "per day," "per wear," "per serving," "lasts X months," "less than a coffee."
- Size or quantity translated into usage (sprays, washes, cups).
- Category reframes ("Tastes like candy. Works like a supplement.").
- Note which absolute number they *avoided* showing. That tells you what the frame is hiding or translating.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** "take whatever it is you actually... are using as your benefit or your value and break it down into smaller units" ([L4 06:26]).
- **IF** the audience is value-driven **THEN** lead with per-use framing ([L4 06:38]).
- **DON'T** rely on raw specs the audience can't interpret ([L4 05:18]).
