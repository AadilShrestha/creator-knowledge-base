# Reframing Perception

> **"What if your problem isn't what you think it is?"** (Prompt Library #14). Reframing shifts a belief "by **changing the context** or the emotional angle." Instead of fighting resistance head-on, it "offers a new lens."

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** the audience has normalised, ignored or misread a problem, habit or identity, or blames the wrong cause (genetics, willpower, age). Also when tearing down "you've been looking at this all wrong" competitor ads.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a belief, behaviour or problem the audience "thinks they understand" → a new meaning for it + the emotion that new meaning stirs + the product as the outcome of the new view.
- **Rules:**
  - DO pick something the audience has "normalized, ignored, or misunderstood" (#14 step 1).
  - DO reveal a deeper truth: what they thought was "fine" is keeping them stuck, or what they dismissed is the key (#14 step 2).
  - DO pair the reframe with one named emotion ([Emotional trigger](emotional-trigger.md), #14 step 3).
  - DO sell "clarity, validation, and a new perspective", not just the product (#14 step 4).
  - DON'T attack the viewer's past choice. Validate it (see [Bounded rationality](bounded-rationality.md)) and then reframe.
- **See also:** [Prompt #14: Reframing Perception + Emotional Trigger](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/14-reframing-perception-and-emotional-trigger.md) · [Emotional trigger](emotional-trigger.md) · [Framing effect](framing-effect.md) · [Counterintuitive insight](counterintuitive-insight.md)

## Definition
"**Reframing** is a classic persuasion tool—it helps people shift from one belief to another by **changing the context** or the emotional angle. Instead of fighting resistance head-on, it offers a new lens. 'What if your problem isn't what you think it is?'" (Prompt Library #14; PDF p.28)

Together with an emotional trigger: "**change minds by changing meaning**, then **motivate action by amplifying feeling**." Target reaction: "*Wait… I've been looking at this all wrong.*"

## Reframing vs framing effect
| | [Framing effect](framing-effect.md) (Heuristics 101) | **Reframing perception** (this page) |
|---|---|---|
| What changes | The *presentation* of the same facts (per-day price, "95% fat-free") | The *meaning* of a problem, habit, belief or identity |
| Typical line | "Less than $1 a day" / "cost per wear" | "It's not genetics. You can reverse it." |
| Mechanism | Same information, different decision | A new cause or interpretation leads to a new feeling, which leads to new behaviour |
| Where in script | Offer and price beat | Hook and problem beat |

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance. Example lines are illustrative, not from course, except where a session is cited.*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | State the old belief, then flip it | "You thought X? Try again…" (format named in #14) |
| Problem | New meaning for the old problem | "I know people just say it's genetics. It's not. You can reverse it." ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:54:54–0:55:50]) |
| Emotion | Name the feeling the reframe stirs (relief, anger, pride) | "Honestly? I was relieved. It wasn't me." (illustrative) |
| Mechanism | The product acts on the *new* cause | "[Product] works on [new cause], not [old assumption]." |
| CTA | Invite them into the new perspective | "Stop blaming yourself. Start here." (illustrative) |

**Formats named in the source:** "'You thought X? Try again…' video, emotion-forward testimonial, confessional copy post, visual metaphor carousel" (#14).
**Visual cues:** a visual metaphor (the same object shown in a new light), a crossed-out old label replaced with a new one, the creator's expression shifting from resigned to relieved.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- "It's not your fault", "It's not X, it's Y", "What if [problem] isn't [assumed cause]?", "You've been told… but".
- Tag: `lever-reframing`. Use `framing-effect` instead if only the numbers or price presentation changed.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing it with framing: a per-day price isn't a reframe of the problem.
- Reframing into guilt or shame without relief. #14 wants the brand to be the "truth-teller" or "liberator."
- Reframing a belief your product can't actually act on.
