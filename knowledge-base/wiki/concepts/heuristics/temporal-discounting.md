# Temporal Discounting

> **"The brain craves instant rewards. Later feels uncertain. Now feels good."** (e-book, p.12). Shortcut #6 in [Heuristics 101](../../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/README.md). It means making the benefit feel immediate, and it has **nothing to do with price**.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing the benefit/promise line, the "how fast does it work" beat, or CTA copy; tearing down competitor ads that promise instant results.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the product's fastest truthful benefit and time-to-result → a time-to-benefit phrase ("one bite," "first use," "today") placed in the hook or promise beat.
- **Rules:**
  - DO state how soon the reward arrives: "today," "now," "first use," "in the next hour," "one bite."
  - DO make the reward "feel very close."
  - DON'T confuse it with discounts, cheapness, value or bundles.
  - DON'T promise "someday" results without a near-term payoff.
  - IF the product's benefit is in the future (insurance, supplements, fitness, prevention) THEN **bring the future closer**: turn the vague risk into a concrete event this week ("Next Tuesday your home is going to flood. Are you ready for that?") ([Live, June 4](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:17:41–0:19:46]). See [Bring the future closer](#bring-the-future-closer-future-focused-products).
- **See also:** [Scarcity heuristic](scarcity-heuristic.md), [Goal gradient effect](goal-gradient-effect.md), [Framing effect](framing-effect.md).

## Definition
- "We prefer immediate rewards over long-term rewards. That's all this one talks about" ([L3 04:30]–[04:37]).
- "People get this one very confused all the time... it has nothing to do with price, nothing to do with price." It's not about bundles or the value you communicate. "Humans really want rewards upfront" ([L3 04:13]–[04:48]).

## Why it works
"The brain is prioritizing really what's going to help us right now. It's trying to keep us alive. That's all the brain is trying to do" ([L3 04:48]–[04:58]).

## How to use it in ads (from the course)
- "Make it really easy for the brain to understand how long it's going to take them before they start to feel results" ([L3 05:25]–[05:30]).
- "Use words like today, now, first use, in the next hour, whatever it is, but make the reward feel very close" ([L3 05:47]–[05:59]).
- E-book: highlight immediate results ("Feel better today."); use fast-action language ("Start now," "First use."). Pro tip: "Remind them what's happening **right now** — not 'someday.'"
- E-book mock-ad CTAs: "Get yours today."

**Visual cues:** an immediate on-screen change, a clock or "day 1" captions, the first-use reaction shot.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Time-to-benefit words: "instantly," "in seconds," "one bite," "first use," "today," "tonight," "now."
- First-use reaction shots and "day 1" captions.
- **Disambiguate:** "60% off today" is a discount + time scarcity, *not* temporal discounting. Only tag `temporal-discounting` when the **benefit** is framed as immediate.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** "make the reward feel very close" ([L3 05:54]).
- **DON'T** use it to "convey how cheap our product is" ([L3 05:36]).
- **DO** remind them what's happening "right now — not 'someday.'" (e-book p.12)

## Bring the future closer (future-focused products)
*From [Live With Sarah, June 4, 2025](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:17:41–0:21:52]. The flip side of temporal discounting: if the brain discounts the future, products whose payoff *is* the future have to drag it into the present.*

- **The problem:** "Insurance is trying to activate something that humans don't have, which is future focus." "Supplements are technically in the insurance industry." [0:17:41] Personal trainers face the same wall: "everybody wants the six-pack… but nobody understands that it takes about three years… not 12 weeks." [0:18:45]
- **What fails:** "Instead of 'protect your home, protect your family, this is going to be important for the future you', it's too vague. We need to pick it up into chunks." [0:18:45]
- **What to do:** make a concrete, near-term, visual prediction. "**Next Tuesday your home is going to flood. Are you ready for that?**" / "**Tomorrow you're actually going to get a tree that's going to fall on your house.**" "Almost make it horoscope-style… a horoscope for your customers that will predict all the bad things that are going to happen to them, you'll have a higher chance of getting them into the system." Test "about nine to start". [0:18:45–0:19:46]
- **Add a carrier:** a mascot ([fluency mechanism](../fluency-mechanism.md)), ideally non-human and non-animal ("a paper clip… a car tire"), that talks about "what's going to happen to them if they don't get the insurance on board". [0:19:46–0:21:52]
- **Keep it humane:** these are Zone 4-style ads ([emotional zones](../emotional-zones.md)). In the same call Sarah says to "always, always, always end it with hope" [0:30:17].

## Common confusion
Context, not from the course: the name sounds like it's about price discounts. In behavioral economics, "discounting" means people value future rewards less. Sarah makes the same point: "nothing to do with price."
