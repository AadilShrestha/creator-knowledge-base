# First Principles Thinking

> **"First Principles Thinking strips away assumptions and rebuilds logic from the ground up. It's about questioning 'Why do we do this?' and reconstructing a better, truer answer."** ([Prompt Library #1](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/01-first-principles-thinking-and-loss-aversion.md))

Not a cognitive bias. It's a **message structure**: take a category "best practice", ask what the real goal is, and show the simpler, truer route. In ads it works as a belief-changer.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** the category runs on an unquestioned habit or "best practice" (how people usually solve the problem) and our product is a different route; or when a competitor ad opens with "Everything you've been told about X is wrong."
- **Inputs → outputs:** the conventional belief/process + the actual goal behind it + proof the usual method is weak → a premise ("The goal was never X, it was Y"), a narrative angle, and a reframed solution line.
- **Rules:**
  - DO run the prompt's three questions on the belief: "What is the actual goal behind this?" "Is there proof this method even works?" "What's the simplest, truest way to get the result?"
  - DO pair it with a cost ([loss aversion](loss-aversion.md)). The insight lands harder when the old logic is "costing them something valuable right now."
  - DO end in clarity: the solution should feel like "Of course—why didn't I see this before?"
  - DON'T attack a belief the buyer holds as identity without giving them a dignified exit. See [bounded rationality](bounded-rationality.md): "You made the best choice… with what you thought you had."
  - DON'T fight a core belief head-on in the claim itself. In the July 2 live call, saying "three months of weight loss" for pasta creates cognitive dissonance because "people already believe that pasta is not good for you". Talk about dinners instead ([2025-07-02](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:47–0:40:48]).
- **See also:** [Counterintuitive insight](counterintuitive-insight.md) · [Hidden truth](hidden-truth.md) · [Reframing](reframing.md) · [Framing effect](framing-effect.md)

## Definition
- Prompt Library #1 (verbatim above). Instruction steps: "1. Choose a conventional belief, process, or 'best practice' your audience follows today. 2. Deconstruct that belief using First Principles… 3. Reveal what they're losing… 4. Reframe the solution as clear, logical, and urgent."
- Intended feel: "bold, clarifying, and slightly uncomfortable—like the truth just clicked, and action is now urgent."

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance (wiki adaptation of the prompt steps).*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line (illustrative, not from course) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Question the ritual | "Why do we [category habit]? I actually looked it up." |
| Problem | The real goal vs. the habit | "The point of [habit] is [real goal]. [Habit] doesn't even do that." |
| Mechanism | Rebuild from the goal | "If the goal is [real goal], you only need [simple route]." |
| Proof | Evidence the old way fails / the new one works | Creator demo, number, before/after |
| CTA | "Of course" close | "Once you see it you can't unsee it. This is what I use now." |

**Visual cues:** creator crossing out or dismantling the old routine on screen, whiteboard/notes "goal → method" breakdown, side-by-side of the 7-step routine vs. the 1-step route.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Openers like "Why do we still…", "Nobody asks why…", "The [category] industry wants you to believe…", "It was never about X."
- The ad deconstructs a process (steps, ingredients, routines) rather than praising its own product first.
- Tag `lever-first-principles`. Usually co-tagged with `lever-loss-aversion`, `lever-counterintuitive-insight` or `lever-hidden-truth`.

## Common mistakes
- Contrarian for its own sake. #13 warns against flipping a norm "without being contrarian for its own sake" ([Counterintuitive Insight + Specificity](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/13-counterintuitive-insight-and-specificity.md)).
- Lecture tone. Long rational argument talks to the conscious mind; keep the reveal visual and fast ([Subconscious vs conscious mind](../subconscious-vs-conscious-mind.md)).
- No stakes. Without the loss half, the ad is an interesting fact, not a reason to act now.

## Pairs with
- **Prompt pairing:** [First Principles Thinking + Loss Aversion](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/01-first-principles-thinking-and-loss-aversion.md).
- **Close cousins:** [counterintuitive insight](counterintuitive-insight.md) (a truth that sounds wrong first), [hidden truth](hidden-truth.md) (something kept from them), [reframing](reframing.md) (changes the meaning, not the logic), [hindsight bias](hindsight-bias.md) ("why didn't I see this?" is the same payoff, prompt #15).
- First principles is the *logic* move; the others are mostly *revelation* moves. Use first principles when the product genuinely removes steps or rebuilds the method.
