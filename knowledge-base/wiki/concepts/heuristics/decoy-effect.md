# Decoy Effect

> **"Three different options but one of them is the one we want them to pick."** ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:15:40–0:16:21]). Adding a third option changes how people feel about the other two, so you can steer the choice toward the target bundle.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** designing or reviewing the bundle ladder on the page an ad sends to; writing offer lines for UGC end cards ("most people grab the 3-pack"); tearing down a competitor's tiered offer.
- **Inputs → outputs:** product, usage rate, AOV target → 3 (or 4) tiers with one deliberate target, odd quantities, a frame-of-reference badge and a "most customers buy" label.
- **Rules:**
  - DO build three options where one is the one you want picked ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:15:40–0:16:21]).
  - DO bundle **quantities or sizes**, not flavours ("a bundle of two, a bundle of three, a bundle of seven") ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:16:21–0:16:51]).
  - DO prefer odd numbers: they "seem like they were specifically chosen" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:16:51–0:17:12]).
  - DO size tiers to real consumption so the customer runs out just as results show ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:24:05–0:26:12]).
  - DO layer a [bandwagon](bandwagon-effect.md) badge on the ladder ("Most customers buy 36") ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:37:45–0:38:47]).
  - DON'T pick tier numbers because "they feel good" or because a competitor uses them ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:35:38–0:36:39]).
- **See also:** [Anchoring effect](anchoring-effect.md) · [Bandwagon effect](bandwagon-effect.md) · [Choice overload](choice-overload.md) · [Bundle psychology](../../frameworks/offer-and-bundle-psychology.md) · [The Decision Lab](../../tools/the-decision-lab.md)

## Definition
- The Decision Lab entry Sarah scrolled to: "Why do we feel more strongly about one option after a third one is added?" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:50]).
- "Technically what bundles are doing is using decoy effect" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:37:45]).
- She files it under **choice architecture** (from *The Elements of Choice*): "we get to decide" how options are presented and which is pre-selected ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:15:58–0:16:33]).

## From the course
- **Gelato (Ahmed Almarri):** dessert is a self-treat; "decoy effect is probably the biggest one for me". Don't bundle flavours (taste is subjective). Bundle quarts in 2/3/7. Pair conceptually ("peanut butter and jelly… vanilla and chocolate"); "if you're gonna bundle it's got to have a point" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:15:40–0:19:33]).
- **Low-carb pasta 3/6/12/36:** the ladder is already a decoy. Test a "Most customers buy 36" badge on top; ask why those numbers; research consumption and consider buckets of two (2/4/8) ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:34:50–0:38:47]).
- **1/3/6 → 1/2/5 packs (Faraz Khan):** the 1-pack is a tester; size the top tier so they run out at month five and must reorder ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:23:45–0:26:12]).

## Deploy in a UGC script
| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Offer beat | Creator names the target tier and why | "I went for the [3-pack] because that's when you actually see results." (illustrative, not from course) |
| End card | Three tiers, target highlighted, "most popular" label | (real best-seller only) |
| Static badge | Frame of reference on the target tier | "Buy a 36 pack, make 100 dinners." ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:41:53]) |

**Visual cues:** the target tier larger or pre-selected, a coloured "most popular" ribbon, the decoy priced so the target clearly dominates it.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Three or four tiers where one is visibly "better value", a "best value"/"most popular" ribbon, odd quantities, a near-useless middle option.
- Tag `heur-decoy`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** decide the target tier first, then build the decoy around it.
- **IF** the target isn't the honest best-seller **THEN** use transparency modelling, not a false "most buy" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:43:59–0:45:03]).
- **DON'T** oversupply: "you're going to create your own churn" ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:24:36]).

## Common mistakes
- Copying a competitor's 1/3/6 ladder.
- Bundles with no logic (cat toys in a dog-chew bundle) ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:18:52]).

## Pairs with / distinctions
- **[Anchoring](anchoring-effect.md):** order and first price set the reference; the decoy then makes the target look like the deal.
- **[Bandwagon](bandwagon-effect.md):** "most customers buy" on top of a decoy ladder.
- **[Choice overload](choice-overload.md):** keep it to 3–4 tiers.
