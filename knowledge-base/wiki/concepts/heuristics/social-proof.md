# Social Proof

> **"If others like it, it must be good."** (e-book, p.8). Shortcut #2 in [Heuristics 101](../../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/README.md). Crowds signal safety.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** adding a trust layer to a UGC script or static, writing review-based hooks, choosing badge copy, or tearing down testimonial/UGC-style competitor ads.
- **Inputs → outputs:** review count, customer count, best reviews and the customer "tribe" name → humanised proof lines, badges and testimonial beats.
- **Rules:**
  - DO name the people behind the number: "6,000 sock lovers have adopted…" beats "6,000 five-star reviews."
  - DO test "**Top Seller**" over "Best Seller" (Sarah cites studies showing top seller wins).
  - DO mine reviews for lived moments. They double as [affect](affect-heuristic.md) hooks (the flask line came from a review).
  - DON'T rely on bare stars. DON'T run social proof as the only heuristic, because it's "very expected in DTC."
- **See also:** [Authority bias](authority-bias.md), [Affect heuristic](affect-heuristic.md), [Heuristic stacking](../heuristic-stacking.md), [CreativeOS](../../tools/creativeos.md) (Social Proof and Testimonial collections).

## Definition
"If we assume that other people like it, it must be good, and I should like it too" ([L2 02:38]).

## Why it works
- It tells the brain very quickly whether something (an item, object, lead magnet or service) is **safe**. "If a lot of people have used it, I'm going to assume it's safe" ([L2 02:43]–[02:59]).
- "We have evolved to follow the crowd for safety" ([L2 02:59]). E-book: "Humans are pack animals. Following the crowd meant survival."

## How to use it in ads (from the course)
- Customer counts: "over 10,000 people trust us," "10,000+ customers served" ([L2 03:04]; e-book).
- Review counts: "6,000 five-star reviews" ([L2 03:04]).
- Reviews, testimonials, social media posts, UGC ([L2 03:17]; e-book).
- "Top Seller" badges (e-book; [L5 06:31]).
- **Humanise:** "Talk about the people instead of the actual five-star review... injecting some humanity into your social proof" ([L2 03:53]–[04:02]). E-book pro tip: "Show the people behind them. ('10,000 coffee lovers trust us.')"

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance: adapts the course rules (humanise the crowd, Top Seller) to UGC video. The slot mapping is not from the course.*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | "[N] [tribe] can't be wrong…" / "Why [N] [tribe] switched to [product]" | "Why 6,000 sock lovers switched to these." |
| Hook (review read) | Creator reads a real review aloud, on screen | "'I bought these for a gift, then drank them myself.' Same." |
| Mid-script proof | Scroll the reviews page; stitch/duet with other customers | — |
| CTA / end card | Tribe count + "Top Seller" badge | "Top seller for a reason. Link below." |

**Visual cues:** a review screenshot overlay, a star row, a round count badge ("4,842+ Sneaker Heads Love Us"), a "Top Seller" seal, multiple-creator montages.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Count badges ("10,000+," "6,000+ 5-star reviews"). Check whether they name a tribe ("sneaker heads," "sock lovers"). That's the stronger, humanised version.
- Testimonial quote cards, review screenshots, "as seen on TikTok," "TikTok's most viral…"
- "Top Seller" / "Best Seller" / "#1" seals.
- Multiple creators or customer montages in UGC. Note these as **crowd** proof (other customers), not authority proof (credentialed people).

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** "talk about the people instead of the actual five-star review" ([L2 03:53]).
- **DO** test "Top Seller" over "Best Seller" ([L5 06:35]).
- **IF** you have reviews **THEN** also mine them for experience lines (affect) and tribe names.
- **DON'T** stop at social proof, scarcity and authority. They're "very expected in DTC" ([L2 06:04]).
