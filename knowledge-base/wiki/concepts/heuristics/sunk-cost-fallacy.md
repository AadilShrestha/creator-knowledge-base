# Sunk Cost Fallacy

> **"They don't want to throw it away because they already purchased it. Lots of sunk cost fallacy going on in here."** ([Live, May 7](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:18:40–0:19:22]). People keep using, keeping or finishing things because of what they already spent. In ads, name that pile of past spending and offer the way out ("the last switch").

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** the category is full of serial switchers (skincare, supplements, haircare, pet products); writing "last time you'll switch" or "stop the product graveyard" angles; explaining add-on buying after a first commitment; tearing down a competitor's "replace them all" ad.
- **Inputs → outputs:** post-purchase behaviour research (what piles up at home, what people won't throw away) → a hook that names the pile, a relief promise ("one thing that actually works"), and a visual of the clutter.
- **Rules:**
  - DO research what happens after purchase: "Where is it stored, who's using it, how often do you think you use it compared to how often do you actually use it?" ([Live, May 7](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:18:40–0:19:22]).
  - DO name the sunk pile as lived behaviour, then release it: "You can throw away those 40 bottles… this is the last time you're ever going to switch" ([Live, May 7](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:19:57–0:20:05]).
  - DO pair it with security (a promise/guarantee), as in the Maelove blend ([Live, June 4](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:47:21–0:49:22]).
  - DON'T shame the buyer for the pile. Keep it relieving (Zone 1 resolution) or humorous ("product graveyard").
- **See also:** [Commitment and consistency](commitment-and-consistency.md) · [Loss aversion](loss-aversion.md) · [Switching behavior](../switching-behavior.md) · [Blended emotions](../blended-emotions.md) · [Customer research stack](../../frameworks/customer-research-stack.md)

## Definition
- Sarah: customers keep 15–50 half-used bottles on the counter because "I purchased the product so I shouldn't throw it away, I should use it" ([Live, May 7](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:18:40–0:19:22]).
- At the lamp store: "you already put in a little bit of effort, so you might as well just go all the way… I already ate two bites [of my son's ice cream], so I'm gonna eat the whole thing" ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:45:34–0:45:58]).

## From the course
- **Skincare client (very likely Maelove):** use-case ads ("do you have wrinkles, sunspots…?") fatigued. A pictorial survey surfaced an outlier: "I have 40 bottles… 30 bottles on my counter… 50… 15." Identity (holistic/natural) + behaviour (the pile) produced "this is the last time you're ever going to switch", which "crushed and had lots of longevity" ([Live, May 7](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:17:51–0:20:05]).
- **Maelove static:** "The last skincare switch you'll ever make / No more guessing. That's our promise." Achievement (stop switching; ~40 bottles, ~$150 spent and not thrown away) blended with security (the promise). Ran **8 months** ([Live, June 4](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:47:21–0:49:22]).
- **Pixar UGC script:** "now I can get rid of all those half-used bottles on my countertop… I can stop treating my bathroom counter like a product graveyard" ([Live, May 14](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-14-live-with-sarah.md) [0:16:17–0:18:29]).
- **Lamp store:** a real closing sale drew crowds who also bought full-price items — sunk cost + commitment ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:42:38–0:45:58]).

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** look for what customers won't throw away; that's the sunk-cost insight.
- **IF** the category is full of switchers **THEN** promise the end of switching, backed by a guarantee.
- **DON'T** frame the pile as the customer's failure; frame it as the category's.

## Common mistakes
- Writing use-case ads when the real pain is the history of failed purchases.
- Promising "the last one" without a guarantee to make it believable.
