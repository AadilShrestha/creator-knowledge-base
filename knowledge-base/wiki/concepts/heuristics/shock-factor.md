# Shock Factor

> **"Shock Factor plays on emotional jolt—it interrupts autopilot by showing something provocative, extreme, or counter-normative. It triggers surprise, sometimes even outrage or disbelief, and forces the brain to pay attention."** ([Prompt Library #4](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/04-shock-factor-and-transformation-shortcut.md), Skool version)

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a saturated feed where polite hooks die; the product has a genuinely surprising truth or a taboo the category avoids; tearing down an ad that opens on an outrageous line or image.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a category norm or polite taboo + a true, defensible provocative claim → a scroll-stopping hook (line + visual) and the tension beat that sets up the resolution.
- **Rules:**
  - DO "Start with a shocking or disruptive claim—something that contradicts industry norms, goes against polite conversation, or uses humor, taboo, or intensity to stand out." (#4 step 1)
  - DO "Create tension or emotional discomfort" (step 2) and then "immediately reveal or tease the shortcut" (step 3). Shock without a fast payoff is just noise.
  - DO aim for "I can't believe they just said that… but now I need to know how it works."
  - DON'T shock with something untrue or off-brand. The claim must survive the comments and ad review.
  - DON'T use it on Zone 1 buyers (wellness, intimacy, luxury) unless you soften it with humor. Shock is high-intensity, usually Zone 4 ([emotional zones](../emotional-zones.md)).
- **See also:** [Pattern disruption](pattern-disruption.md) · [Transformation shortcut](transformation-shortcut.md) · [Salience bias](salience-bias.md) · [Intensity](../intensity.md)

## Definition
- Prompt Library #4 (Skool page) defines it as above; objective: "something unexpected, exaggerated, or taboo", designed "to stop the scroll instantly, provoke strong emotional reactions".
- **Source note:** in the PDF version (p.8) the "Psychology Behind This Prompt" block for #4 is a copy of #3's (curiosity / reverse psychology). The Skool page has the correct shock-factor text. Use the Skool text.

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance.*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line (illustrative, not from course) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (0–2s) | Taboo said out loud / exaggerated claim | "Your [everyday item] is dirtier than your toilet seat." (only if true) |
| Tension | Let the discomfort sit 1–2 beats | Creator reaction, close-up, silence |
| Shortcut | Fast, simple resolution | "I fixed it in 10 seconds with this." |
| Proof | Show it | Demo / before-after |

**Visual cues:** Module 3's Zone 4 image direction applies: "an interruption in their feed — what would feel visually wrong enough to force the scroll to stop?" ([Valence + Intensity prompts](../../resources/valence-intensity-prompts.md)). Also "weird works" visuals: personified products, chaos animals ([Roast My Ads #3](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:56:53–0:58:59]).

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- First line or frame is something you wouldn't say at dinner: gross-out facts, exaggerated stakes, swear-adjacent humor, "this might get me banned".
- Polarized comments (outrage + "wait, is this true?").
- Tag `lever-shock-factor`. Record whether shock is **verbal** (claim) or **visual** (image), and what resolves it.

## Common mistakes
- Shock unrelated to the product, so the attention doesn't transfer.
- Trauma triggers or fear-mongering in sensitive categories ("we're not trying to trigger people's traumas", Module 3).
- Holding the shock too long. The prompt resolves "immediately".
- Opening on skin with no context: US viewers read it as "weird" and scroll ([Roast My Ads #3](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:20:31–0:21:08]). Shock needs a readable context.

## Pairs with
- **Prompt pairing:** [Shock Factor + Transformation Shortcut](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/04-shock-factor-and-transformation-shortcut.md): jolt, then a refreshingly simple escape.
- **Distinctions:** [pattern disruption](pattern-disruption.md) breaks the *expected format* (any surprise, not necessarily provocative). Shock breaks *social norms* (provocative, taboo, extreme). [Salience](salience-bias.md) is the general "stands out" mechanism under both.
