# Observer Expectancy Effect ("Being Watched")

> **"Have somebody staring at the screen being like 'did you take your creatine today?'… just being watched changes your behavior and you can do it for anything."** ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:21:41]). A direct, personal "I see you" question makes the viewer feel observed and nudges the behaviour.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** habit/replenishment products (supplements, skincare routines, pet food), retention emails/SMS, cart-abandonment messages, playful self-treat products; writing a direct-to-camera UGC opener.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the daily behaviour the product needs → a direct-address question hook ("Did you ___ today?") + a playful accountability follow-up.
- **Rules:**
  - DO have the creator stare straight into the lens and ask a yes/no question about today's behaviour ([Jul 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:21:41]).
  - DO keep it playful for treats: "Did you buy gelato today? No, you have not treated yourself enough." [0:21:48]
  - DO use it in retention and cart recovery: personal DMs to abandoned carts got "wow, we feel seen" and checkouts [0:22:00–0:22:39].
  - DO use it for acquisition too: "these are heuristics, they work for every human on the planet… 24/7" [0:22:39–0:23:03].
  - DON'T turn it into nagging guilt from a stranger in cold ads; accusatory "should" lands badly before a relationship exists ([Jun 18](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [0:08:25–0:09:27]) (wiki cross-reference).

## Definition
The Decision Lab entry on screen: "Observer Expectancy Effect: Why do we change our behavior when we're being watched?" ([Jul 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:21:29]). *(Wiki note: in research, "observer-expectancy effect" usually means a researcher's expectations biasing subjects; the "behaving differently when watched" sense is closer to the Hawthorne effect. The course uses The Decision Lab's framing, so this page does too.)*

## From the course
- **Creatine hook:** a creator staring at the screen: "did you take your creatine today?" "Just being watched changes your behavior." [0:21:41]
- **Gelato version:** "Did you buy gelato today? No, you have not treated yourself enough. Go buy yourself some gelato." [0:21:48–0:22:00]
- **Cart abandonment (attendee idea + Sarah's story):** Sarah saw followers' names on abandoned carts, DMed "why don't you check out", they replied "wow, we feel seen" and checked out. "Humans are busy… sometimes it's hard to remember that I have gelato in a cart." [0:22:00–0:22:39]
- **Retention vs acquisition (Lesley):** both. Prompt idea: "Chat, I want to use the observer expectancy effect in my retention emails… how should I use this?" [0:22:39–0:23:03]

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** ask about *today*, not "in general"; a specific moment feels observed.
- **IF** the product is a treat **THEN** flip accountability into permission.
- **IF** using it in cold traffic **THEN** keep it warm/funny, not judgmental.

## Common mistakes
- Preachy "you should" delivery that triggers resistance.
- Generic "Are you taking care of yourself?" with no concrete behaviour.
