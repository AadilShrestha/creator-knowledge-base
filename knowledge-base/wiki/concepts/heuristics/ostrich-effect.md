# Ostrich Effect

> **"Ostrich effect basically means that we're just going to ignore anything that's like negative. We just don't like it, it's icky."** ([Live, July 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:52:12]). People avoid looking at bad news about themselves. Dramatize the avoidance in the hook ("I'm just not going to look at it"), then break it ("even if you ignore it, it's still there").

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** the product solves a problem people avoid facing (hair loss, money/debt, health markers, aging, pet health, home damage); writing a founder or UGC hook; tearing down "denial" style openers.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the problem + its visible, everyday evidence → an avoidance hook (2–3 evidence beats + "I'm just not going to look at it"), a turn line, and a show-it visual.
- **Rules:**
  - DO stack small, visible pieces of evidence the person keeps ignoring: hair on the floor, hair on the car seat a friend notices ([Jul 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:52:39]).
  - DO turn it quickly: "Even if you ignore it, it's still there. You're still losing your hair… and I have a very good way for you to fix it." [0:53:14]
  - DO **show** the evidence on screen: "just showing helps a lot" [0:53:14].
  - DO put it in the hook: heuristics go "mostly in hooks… because it's easier to put them in there real quick" [0:53:44].
  - DON'T shame the viewer for avoiding it; the creator owns the avoidance ("I'm…"), and the tone stays empathetic. End with hope ([Jun 4](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:30:17]).

## Definition
The Decision Lab entry on screen: "Why do we prefer to ignore negative information? The Ostrich Effect, explained." ([Jul 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:53:15]). Sarah picked it at random from the list to show how any heuristic becomes a hook.

## From the course
Sarah's founder-video demo for a hair-loss shampoo ([Jul 9](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:52:12–0:53:32]):
- "Let's write a script that says something to the effect of: there's hair all over the floor and I'm just not going to look at it… there's hair coming out in my head and I'm not going to look at it… my friend is noticing that there's hair on my car seat when I get out. I'm just not going to look at it. That's ostrich effect."
- "You can display it by showing it… even if you ignore it, it's still there, you're still losing your hair and you really got to get a handle on this, and I have a very good way for you to fix it."
- Same call: situational evidence ("hair clogging the drain", "more hair in my shower than on my brush") lowers CPA by finding the right buyer [0:39:38–0:42:38] → [specificity](specificity.md).

## Deploy in a UGC script
| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (0–5 s) | Evidence + refusal, ×2–3 | "There's hair all over the floor and I'm just not going to look at it." ([Jul 9 0:52:39](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md)) |
| Escalation | Someone else notices | "My friend is noticing there's hair on my car seat when I get out. I'm just not going to look at it." [0:52:39] |
| Turn | Break the avoidance | "Even if you ignore it, it's still there." [0:53:14] |
| Solution | Low-pressure fix | "And I have a very good way for you to fix it." [0:53:14] |

**Visual cues:** creator turning away from the evidence (looking away from the drain, covering the mirror), comic exaggeration of avoidance, then a hard cut to looking straight at it.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Openers like "I've been ignoring this…", "I didn't want to look", "I pretended it wasn't happening", creator literally averting eyes.
- Tag: `heur-ostrich`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** use everyday, slightly funny evidence so avoidance reads as relatable, not tragic.
- **IF** the category is sensitive (women's hair loss) **THEN** keep the creator owning the denial and end on hope.
- **DO** pair with a peer messenger ([in-group bias](in-group-bias.md)) for the turn.

## Common mistakes
- Turning "you're ignoring it" into an accusation aimed at the viewer.
- Never showing the evidence (telling, not showing).

## Pairs with / distinctions
- **vs [Loss aversion](loss-aversion.md):** loss aversion counts what you're losing; ostrich effect dramatizes refusing to look at it. The turn line ("still losing your hair") brings loss in.
- Pairs with [specificity](specificity.md), [mirror effect](../mirror-effect.md) (a character avoids it, not the viewer), [in-group bias](in-group-bias.md).
