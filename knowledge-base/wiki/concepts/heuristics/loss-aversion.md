# Loss Aversion

> **"Loss Aversion triggers when people fear they're wasting time, losing money, or falling behind. It's more powerful than gain-seeking—and drives immediate decision-making."** ([Prompt Library #1](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/01-first-principles-thinking-and-loss-aversion.md))

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing the "cost of staying put" beat of a UGC script, turning a benefit into a loss ("stop wasting…"), or labelling a competitor ad that sells *avoiding* a loss rather than gaining a win.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the old way / status quo the buyer is stuck in + what it costs them (money, time, growth, peace of mind, momentum, relevance, trust) → a loss line for the hook or problem beat, and a Zone 4 or Zone 3 tone choice.
- **Rules:**
  - DO name a concrete loss the viewer is *already* suffering (time, money, falling behind). The prompt's list: "Money, time, growth, peace of mind, momentum, relevance, trust, etc."
  - DO pair it with a reason the loss is happening (the faulty belief). Loss alone is just fear. Sarah pairs it with [first principles thinking](first-principles-thinking.md) so the viewer sees "they've been *operating on faulty logic*—and that it's *costing them* something valuable right now."
  - DO resolve it: "Reframe the solution as clear, logical, and urgent."
  - DON'T trigger trauma. Module 3's warning for Zone 4 applies: "we're not trying to trigger people's traumas" ([Valence + Intensity prompts](../../resources/valence-intensity-prompts.md)).
  - DON'T stack loss + scarcity + countdown + pain amplification in one ad. Pick 2–3 levers ([heuristic stacking](../heuristic-stacking.md)).
  - IF the whole category already sells a loss (weight loss, "kick the craving", "cut sugar") THEN test **the flip**: a gain frame where the buyer keeps what they love and swaps the product ("My secret to feed junk food cravings"). See [Gain vs loss framing](#gain-vs-loss-framing-the-flip) ([Roast My Ads #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:54:32–0:58:42]).
- **See also:** [Scarcity](scarcity-heuristic.md) · [Pain-point amplification](pain-point-amplification.md) · [Temporal discounting](temporal-discounting.md) · [Framing effect](framing-effect.md) · [Emotional zones](../emotional-zones.md) · [Ought Self](../ought-self.md)

## Definition
- Prompt Library #1: loss aversion "triggers when people fear they're wasting time, losing money, or falling behind. It's more powerful than gain-seeking—and drives immediate decision-making."
- In the prompt's instructions it is step 3: "**Reveal what they're losing** by clinging to outdated thinking."
- Module 3 places loss in **Zone 4** (low valence, high intensity): "Where your fear and urgency lives… panic, scarcity… threats or loss", used for limited-time offers and health/safety categories ([The 4 Emotional Zones](../../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/03-the-4-emotional-zones.md) [03:27–03:45]).

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance: the course gives the definition and the prompt steps. Slots and line patterns are wiki adaptations.*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line (illustrative, not from course) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | "You're losing [X] every time you [old habit]." | "You're throwing out half your coffee budget every time you buy pods." |
| Problem | Quantify the running loss | "That's about [N] hours a month I spent on [task]." |
| Mechanism | Why the old way leaks value (first-principles reveal) | "The [old method] was never built to [real goal]." |
| CTA | Stop-the-loss framing | "Stop paying the [old-way] tax. Link below." |

**Visual cues:** money/time visibly draining (receipt pile, clock, bin full of wasted product), a "what you lose" side next to a "what you keep" side, Zone 4 "visually wrong" interruption images only if the stakes are real.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Words: "stop wasting," "you're losing," "don't miss," "falling behind," "before it's too late," "the cost of waiting," "you should have…".
- The ad sells the *avoided loss* (keep your money, don't lose your hair, protect your home) rather than a gain.
- Tag `lever-loss-aversion`. Add `scarcity-*` if the loss is about stock or time running out, `lever-pain-point-amplification` if the ad dwells on present suffering.

## Common mistakes
- Vague loss ("don't miss out") with no concrete thing lost.
- Loss with no exit. The viewer feels bad and scrolls. Always land the reframe and the product as the stop.
- Using it in a category whose buyers want calm reassurance (Zone 1 categories: wellness, luxury, intimacy, belonging). Check the [zone](../emotional-zones.md) first.
- Assuming loss always beats gain. In a niche where *every* ad is loss-framed, the one gain-framed ad can be the outlier winner (next section).

## Gain vs loss framing: "the flip"
*From the live sessions. This is the main caveat to "loss is more powerful than gain-seeking".*

**The case: The Only Bean (crunchy roasted edamame), Sofia Tataurova's account** ([Roast My Ads #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:54:32–0:58:42]).
- The account's top spender "for almost a year" was a video with the hook **"My secret to feed junk food cravings 🤫"**. Every other hook was about losing something: "The crunchy way to lose weight", "This protein hack should be illegal", "POV: you never have to think about protein again", and a static "People with diabetes are loving this salty, crunchy edamame snack…" [0:55:37–0:56:40]
- Sarah: "This is the only ad that talks about something you **gain**. You're **feeding** something… this one specific word is driving a huge chunk of the performance." The hook says "I'm not trying to kick my food craving, I'm actually feeding it with this specific product." [0:56:40]
- An attendee asked if that contradicts loss aversion. Sarah: "It does, but… for this customer type… there's nothing to solve. You should keep your food cravings and just swap it with something else… **no human on the planet wants to stop eating potato chips**." [0:56:53–0:58:42]
- Her rule for weight-loss and other loss-heavy niches: "**Flip it upside down**… you don't need to get rid of anything. Keep your potato chip craving, just switch out the chip." [0:58:42]

**Same pattern elsewhere in the course**
- Low-carb pasta: lead with "dinners" you still get to enjoy, not "three months of weight loss", which clashes with the belief that pasta is bad for you. Gabriela's line "Still enjoy your favourite dinner without feeling so guilty" and her recollection that "enjoy the things that you like" beat "don't feel guilty" for a snack brand ([Live, July 2](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:47–0:43:59]). See [cognitive dissonance](cognitive-dissonance.md).
- Anti-gray supplement: "a negative trigger but a positive behavior". The buyer is optimistic they can slow graying, so start with high-valence zones ([Live, June 11](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md) [0:29:47–0:32:53]).

**When to use which frame (wiki synthesis of the above)**

| Use a **loss** frame when… | Use a **gain / keep** frame when… |
|---|---|
| The buyer is already losing something they value (money, hair, time) and knows it ("You notice the difference when you don't have it", [Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:16:53]) | The "problem" is a pleasure the buyer doesn't want to give up (cravings, treats, comfort food) |
| The category is calm and positive, so loss stands out ([emotional stagnation](../emotional-stagnation.md)) | The category is saturated with loss frames, so gain stands out ([Von Restorff](von-restorff-effect.md)) |
| The stakes are real and the product stops the loss (insurance, health) | A loss claim would contradict a core belief ("pasta for weight loss") |

**Script move:** take the competitor's loss hook and rewrite the verb. "Kick / cut / stop / lose" → "feed / keep / enjoy / swap".

| Before | After | What changed (verb / visual / promise / self / intensity) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| "The crunchy way to lose weight" / "kick the craving" | "My secret to feed junk food cravings" / "Keep your potato chip craving, just switch out the chip." | Verb: lose/kick → feed/keep. Promise: give something up → keep it and swap. | [Roast #1 0:55:37–0:58:42](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) |
| "Three months of weight loss" (pasta bundle) | "Make up to 15 dinners more suited to your goal" / "Buy a 36 pack, make 100 dinners" | Promise: loss (USP) → gain in use units | [Live, July 2 0:38:47–0:41:53](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) |

**Spot it:** in a competitor's ad library, tally hooks as loss vs gain. If one long-running ad is the only gain-framed hook, that's probably the reason it wins. Compare winners and losers word by word ([Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:54:32]).

## Pairs with
- **Prompt pairing:** [First Principles Thinking + Loss Aversion](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/01-first-principles-thinking-and-loss-aversion.md). The belief is wrong (first principles), and it is costing you (loss).
- **Distinctions:**
  - vs [scarcity](scarcity-heuristic.md): scarcity is loss of *the offer* (stock/time running out). Loss aversion is loss of *value the buyer already has* (money, time, health).
  - vs [pain-point amplification](pain-point-amplification.md): amplification turns up the felt pain of the present. Loss aversion counts what that present is costing. They stack well in [What If + Pain Point](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/07-what-if-scenario-and-pain-point-amplification.md) (step 3: "Highlight how much time, money, confidence, or opportunity they're losing").
  - vs [framing effect](framing-effect.md): a loss frame is one kind of framing. Same facts, said as a loss.
