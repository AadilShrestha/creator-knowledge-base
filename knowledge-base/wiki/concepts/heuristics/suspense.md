# Suspense

> **"Suspense creates tension through incompletion."** (Prompt Library #12). Show "a moment before the moment" and the brain "demands resolution", which raises time-on-ad, clicks and engagement.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you need watch-time or click-through on a story-led ad, building carousels whose payoff sits on the last slide, or tearing down "wait for it" competitor ads.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a micro-story with a clear payoff → a cut point just before the payoff, plus where the payoff is delivered (end of video, last slide, landing page).
- **Rules:**
  - DO "hold back the full payoff or lesson. Build tension by cutting just before the resolution—or hinting that 'what happens next' changes everything." (#12 step 2)
  - DO make the product the key to the resolution (#12 step 4).
  - DO pay it off: on the last slide, at the end of the video, or on the landing page. *(Derived: an unpaid tease breaks trust and [ad-to-page congruence](../../tools/creativeos.md).)*
  - DON'T suspend something the viewer doesn't care about yet. Stakes come first (see [Micro-story](micro-story.md)).
- **See also:** [Prompt #12](../../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/12-micro-story-and-suspense.md) · [Micro-story](micro-story.md) · [Open loop](open-loop.md) · [Curiosity gap](curiosity-gap.md)

## Definition
"**Suspense** creates **tension through incompletion**. When people are exposed to an unresolved situation (a moment before the moment), their brain *demands resolution*. This increases time-on-ad, clicks, and engagement." (Prompt Library #12; PDF p.24)

Target feel: "**personal**, **immersive**, and **incomplete until clicked**—like someone started telling you a secret and then walked away."

## Suspense vs open loop vs curiosity gap
| Lever | What's withheld | Engine | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Suspense** | *What happens next* in a story | Narrative tension and stakes | here (Prompt #12) |
| [Open loop](open-loop.md) | The rest of an idea, a teaser or an almost-revealed insight | Unfinished thought, often with exclusivity | Prompt #2 |
| [Curiosity gap](curiosity-gap.md) | A piece of information we *almost* understand | Information gap ("Wait, what?") | Prompt #3 |

All three create incompletion. Suspense needs a story and stakes. The other two don't.

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance. Example lines are illustrative, not from course.*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line / edit (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | State the stakes and hint at the twist | "I almost returned it. Then day 5 happened." |
| Build | Escalate with one or two beats | Creator's face, a slow push-in, "I checked the mirror and…" |
| Cut point | Stop just before the reveal | Cut to black or a freeze frame. Text: "What happened next →" |
| Payoff | The reveal: end of video, last slide or landing page | Before/after reveal, product shown as the cause |
| CTA | Tie the payoff to the action | "See the full 30 days, link below." |

**Formats named in the source:** "15s video with a cut-to-black, carousel that stops on slide 4/5, text-based 'story post' that teases final answer in comments or CTA, UGC montage where punchline is withheld" (#12).
**Visual cues:** cut to black, freeze frame, "wait for it" text, ticking audio, a reaction shot before the thing is shown.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- "Wait till the end", "you won't believe what happened next", a carousel that stops mid-story, a reveal held to the last second.
- Measure: if it's a video, is the product revealed after the 3-second hold? The format trades early product visibility for retention.
- Tag: `lever-suspense`.

## Common mistakes
- Clickbait with no payoff, which burns trust and conversion.
- Suspense without stakes, so viewers just scroll.
- Withholding the *product* instead of the *outcome*. Viewers need to know what's being sold. *(Derived.)*
