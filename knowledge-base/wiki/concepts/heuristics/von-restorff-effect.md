# Von Restorff Effect (Sarah's "Differentiation Effect")

> **"If there's something in the space that's just like a completely different color or shape or size, we immediately get drawn to it because it's different."** ([Live, June 11](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md) [0:42:22]). The one item that differs from everything around it gets noticed and remembered. In ad terms: whatever the category and the feed look like, look *different* — and never copy the competitor.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** after a competitor teardown, deciding how our ad should look/sound so it isolates itself from the category; choosing an odd "carrier" for a static message; diagnosing why a "best-practice" ad gets ignored in a sea of lookalikes.
- **Inputs → outputs:** 10–20 competitor ads tagged for core word, colour, size, tone, format → an "opposite" spec for our ad (message, palette, scale, intensity, carrier) + 2–3 odd-carrier static concepts.
- **Rules:**
  - DON'T copy competitors. Find their core message, colour and tone, then "go crazy wild the opposite direction" ([Jun 11](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md) [0:42:22]).
  - DO flip one dimension at a time: "If they go big you go small, if they're green you go red, if they're stupid wild and loud you go very soft and subtle." [0:43:27–0:44:31]
  - DO carry the message on an odd object: sticky notes, notepads, dry-erase boards, chalkboards, writing on a hand, cardboard signs, all-text statics ([Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:31:14–0:32:15]).
  - DO add one strange visual element that encodes the benefit (a light switch on a supplement bottle, set to ON) [0:13:39–0:15:00; 0:32:15–0:33:21].
  - DO break the 1080×1080 square: bite out a corner, run the object off the edge ([Jun 11](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md) [0:54:59–0:56:04]).
  - DON'T isolate the wrong thing. In a comparison, grey out the competitor so the distinct colour sits on *our* product ([Roast #3](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:35:44–0:37:49]).
- **See also:** [Salience bias](salience-bias.md) · [Pattern disruption](pattern-disruption.md) · [Color psychology](../color-psychology.md) · [Emotional stagnation](../emotional-stagnation.md) · [Competitor teardown playbook](../../playbooks/competitor-ad-teardown.md) · [Anthropomorphism](anthropomorphism.md)

## Definition
- **Von Restorff (isolation) effect** (wiki context, not from course): an item that stands out from a group of similar items is more likely to be noticed and remembered.
- Sarah never says the name cleanly. On May 7 she lists the "Von Restorff effect" among heuristics to layer on a winning message ([May 7](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:14:42–0:15:20]). On May 28 she starts to say it, then corrects herself to "distinction bias" ([Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:32:15]). On June 11 she calls it the "**differentiation effect**" [0:42:22]. All three describe the same isolation mechanism. ⚠ "Distinction bias" is a different bias (over-weighting differences when comparing options side by side); treat Sarah's May 28 label as a slip.

## Attentional bias (merged here)
- Sarah pairs the two on June 11 as the reason to go opposite: "humans have a strange **attentional bias**… where we can only kind of concentrate on anything that's like our world view and we can't always pull our attention away from things that are just drastically different than everything else you see in the space" ([Jun 11](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md) [0:42:22–0:43:27]).
- Practical reading (wiki): attention is captured by what fits the viewer's existing focus *or* by what is drastically different from its surroundings. A creative that blends into the category is neither, so it's skipped. Attentional bias is only name-dropped this once, so it lives on this page rather than its own.

## From the course
- **Odd carriers win on statics (Roast #1):** a client's all-text statics "did so well" because they don't look like ads; sticky-note ads keep working; also notepads, dry-erase boards, chalkboards, writing on a hand, cardboard signs, a full-body poster. "Anything that's odd the brain is going to look at… that's why I always tell people the more creative you can get with your ads the better." [0:31:14–0:33:21]
- **Light switch on the bottle (Roast #1):** for VITUS's "OFF to ON" line: "Nobody would add a light switch to a bottle, but just having that strangeness added on to your product will help people understand what it's for… more clicks, more buys, just because it's odd." [0:13:39–0:15:00; 0:32:15–0:33:21]
- **Go the opposite way (Jun 11):** if AG1's core word is "activate", "I don't want to do anything that they're doing". Category in "pinks and pastels and grays" → be black and green. "Activate your health" → "boost your mitochondria, the powerhouse of the body" (small, specific, medical where they're big and vague). Liquid Death is the model. [0:42:22–0:44:31]
- **Emotional isolation too (Jun 11):** AG1's 15–20 ads all sit in Zone 1; the opening is the zones they never use → [emotional stagnation](../emotional-stagnation.md) [0:07:22–0:09:27].
- **Break the grid (Jun 11):** "Statics are just all about breaking the grid… you're just telling the brain, stop on this, because this looks weird." Fuchsia "flower pink" also runs well on Meta [0:54:59–0:56:04] → [color psychology](../color-psychology.md).
- **Weird works (Roast #3):** an evil-eyed ice cream or an "AI cow" next to the product: "weird works, especially… in the algorithm and the actual feed… because it jumps out at you" [0:57:59–0:58:30] → [anthropomorphism](anthropomorphism.md). Grey out the red competitor bowls so colour lands on ours [0:35:44–0:37:49].

| First frame | Message on an odd physical carrier | Creator holds a cardboard sign / sticky note with the hook written on it ([Roast #1 0:32:15](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md)) |
| Hook | Category-opposite claim | "Everyone says 'activate your health.' I care about one thing: my mitochondria." (illustrative, built on [Jun 11 0:43:27](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md)) |
| Tone | Opposite intensity to the category | Category shouts → creator whispers, deadpan; category calm → high-energy |
| Visual | One odd prop that encodes the benefit | A light switch flicked ON next to the product |

**Visual cues:** a palette no competitor uses; objects breaking the frame; handwritten carriers; a single desaturated "old way" next to a saturated product; scale flips (tiny product in a big frame).

## Deploy in a static
- Pick the category's dominant colour and pick its opposite for background or product highlight.
- All-text static on a solid or pattern background; or text on a sticky note/whiteboard/hand.
- Bite out a corner or let the product run off the edge of the square.
- Comparison layout: grey/desaturate the competitor side.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Does the ad look like every other ad in its category (same pastel, same flat-lay, same "activate/transform" word)? If yes, it's *not* using isolation — that's our opening.
- If it wins while looking odd (sticky note, cartoon, wrong-colour background, off-frame object), tag the odd element as the likely thumb-stop.
- Tag: `heur-von-restorff` (+ `odd-carrier`, `category-opposite`, `grid-break`).

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** run the competitor library through a teardown first; you can't be different without knowing the norm.
- **DO** make the odd element *relevant* (it encodes the benefit or the message), so the attention converts.
- **DO** keep one isolated focal element. Two weird things compete.
- **IF** you copy a competitor's format **THEN** at minimum flip palette, scale or tone.

## Common mistakes
- Copying the category leader because "it's working" (ad-library age isn't proof; [Jun 11](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md) [0:24:32]).
- Weirdness with no link to the product (it stops the scroll but nobody knows what it's for).
- Putting the distinctive colour on the competitor side of a comparison.

## Pairs with / distinctions
- **vs [Salience bias](salience-bias.md):** salience = what stands out in an absolute sense (contrast, bold colour, faces). Von Restorff = standing out *relative to the set* (category, feed, the other items in the frame). Use salience for in-ad hierarchy; Von Restorff for category positioning.
- **vs [Pattern disruption](pattern-disruption.md):** pattern disruption breaks an expected sequence (format, narrative, pacing). Von Restorff isolates an item from similar items. Sarah calls odd carriers "pattern disrupts" and "Von Restorff" in the same breath ([Roast #1](../../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:32:15]); in practice they stack.
- Pairs with [anthropomorphism](anthropomorphism.md), [color psychology](../color-psychology.md), [emotional stagnation](../emotional-stagnation.md) (zone isolation), [brand enemy](../brand-enemy.md).
