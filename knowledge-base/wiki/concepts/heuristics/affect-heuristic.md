# Affect Heuristic

> **"If it feels good, it must be good."** (e-book, p.10). Shortcut #4 in [Heuristics 101](../../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/README.md). People judge in a snap by how something *feels*, so drop them into an experience before any pitch.

**Spelling note:** the course audio, lesson page and e-book say "**Effect** heuristic." The slides say "**Affect** Heuristic," which is the standard term (*affect* = emotion). They mean the same thing.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing the **hook** and opening visual of a UGC script or static, mining reviews for scenes, or tearing down competitor ads that open on a moment rather than a product.
- **Inputs → outputs:** customer reviews and usage moments → an experience-first hook (a scene in one line), a feeling word, and a matching visual.
- **Rules:**
  - DO open on the experience, not the pitch: "create that good feeling **before making any sort of pitch**."
  - DO mine reviews for lived moments. The flask headline came from a customer review.
  - DO pair headline and image so they form one mental picture ("stronger than s'mores" over flames).
  - DO use a single loaded feeling word ("worry") when you can't fit a story. It "doesn't have to be a full-on story."
  - DON'T lead with price, bundle, values or features in the affect beat.
  - DON'T argue rationally. The e-book says "Emotional, visual, fast."
- **See also:** [Valence](../valence.md), [Intensity](../intensity.md), [Emotional zones](../emotional-zones.md), [Emotional misfire](../emotional-misfire.md), [Social proof](social-proof.md) (reviews), [Processing fluency](processing-fluency.md).

## Definition
- "We make snap judgments based upon how something emotionally feels" ([L3 00:38]–[00:43]).
- "It's all about how the ad makes you **feel**, not necessarily what it is trying to convey." You aren't selling a bundle, price, perception, values or social proof. "We're dropping a person into the experience that we are trying to call up in their mind" ([L3 00:43]–[01:09]).

## Why it works
"Something feels really good, safe, rewarding, or just like nice to be in. Our brain assumes that it's good, safe, like something that we actually need to pay attention to" ([L3 01:15]–[01:27]). E-book: "Humans make emotional snap judgments based on feelings."

## How to use it in ads (from the course)
- **Paint the desired experience:** "Put up the tent, brought the flask." (e-book). "Use visuals and language that kind of create that good feeling before making any sort of pitch" ([L3 02:03]–[02:09]).
- **Sell the feeling, not the feature** (e-book).
- **Take lines from reviews:** "This was an experience that came out of one of their reviews" ([L3 01:32]–[01:36]).
- **Pair headline and image** into "a dual... mental picture" ([L5 03:21]–[04:05]).
- **Use visual imagery in text form:** one feeling word ("worry") is enough. "The affect heuristic doesn't have to be a full-on like story-based example" ([L5 01:26]–[01:59]).
- **Pro tip (e-book):** "The faster you make them feel something, the faster they'll buy."

## Deploy in a UGC script
*Derived guidance: the course rules (experience before pitch, review-sourced moments, image and line paired) mapped onto UGC slots. The mapping is not from the course.*

| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (0–3 s) | "[Someone] did [the big thing], but I [did the small thing that mattered]." The scene is in the first line. | "He put up the tent, but I brought the flask." |
| Hook (feeling word) | "[Cost/effort] for us… 100% less [feeling] for you." | "4x the material cost for us, zero worry for you." |
| Opening visual | Shoot the moment itself (campfire, cosy couch, gym finish), not a product on white | — |
| Mid-script | Creator relives the moment, e.g. "the second we sat down by the fire…" | — |
| Pitch | Only after the feeling has landed | — |

**Visual cues:** golden-hour or lifestyle scenes, hands sharing the product, faces reacting, environments that match the headline (fire ↔ "s'mores").

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- The headline describes a **scene or moment**, not a feature ("He put up the tent…," "Feels like home, even miles away…").
- One loaded emotion word: worry, cozy, home, finally, guilt.
- The image matches the line (fire + s'mores). Note whether headline and visual are paired or fighting.
- There's no price, bundle or spec in the opening beat.
- Tag the emotion (and later its [valence](../valence.md)/[intensity](../intensity.md)) during teardown.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** "create that good feeling before making any sort of pitch" ([L3 02:03]).
- **DO** take scene lines from real reviews ([L3 01:32]).
- **IF** there's no room for a story **THEN** use one feeling word ([L5 01:49]).
- **DON'T** sell a bundle, price or values in the affect beat ([L3 00:55]).
- **DON'T** talk only to the conscious mind with "long rational debates" (e-book p.17).
