# Intensity (Emotional)
> **Intensity is the emotional *energy level* of a message: how strongly the feeling is experienced, from a whisper to a scream.** In Sarah Levinger's model it's *how* you're saying the emotion. Combined with [valence](valence.md) it places an ad in one of the four [emotional zones](emotional-zones.md).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** scoring how strongly an ad delivers its emotion; writing energy/framing/lighting directions in a brief; deciding whether a draft is under-emotional.
- **Inputs → outputs:** ad/script → intensity score (0–4 indicators, low/high) + list of levers to raise or lower it.
- **Rules:**
  - DO raise intensity with sharp verbs, saturated colour, close-ups, urgency, contrast. DO lower it with soft copy, desaturation, zoom-out, morning light.
  - DO keep the message constant when changing only intensity ("just the delivery").
  - DON'T put high-intensity promo styling on high-trust or luxury products.
  - DO flag flat ads. "Not emotional enough" is a common failure.
- **See also:** [Tone-shifting](../frameworks/tone-shifting.md) · [Valence](valence.md)

## The two poles

| | Low intensity (whisper) | High intensity (scream) |
|---|---|---|
| Arousal | Softer emotional impact | "Stronger emotional arousal" |
| Good for | Building **trust, recognition, reflection** | **Urgency**, creating **momentum** |
| Descriptors (e-book) | Soft, reflective, reassuring | Urgent, bold, aggressive |
| Copy feel | Soft, cozy, conversational ("please pay attention to us") | Quick, sharp, definitive ("hey, don't ignore this") |
| Zones | [Zone 1](emotional-zones.md#zone-1-high-valence--low-intensity-feel-good), [Zone 3](emotional-zones.md#zone-3-low-valence--low-intensity-pain-point-whisper) | [Zone 2](emotional-zones.md#zone-2-high-valence--high-intensity-hype--anticipation), [Zone 4](emotional-zones.md#zone-4-low-valence--high-intensity-danger-zone) |

## Why it matters
- "There are different levels of intensity and how you map those in your ad is going to **directly affect how well that ad performs**." (L2 [01:36])
- Intensity is the easiest dial to turn: "you may want to **dial it up or dial it back**, depending on where your customers sit emotionally with their problem." ([L3](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/03-the-4-emotional-zones.md) [01:09])
- Changing intensity keeps the message and changes the delivery: "Our core message really never changed, just the delivery did. And that's what intensity does." (L5 [02:00])
- Many ads fail on intensity: "Sometimes ads underperform not really because they're too emotional, but because **they're not emotional enough**." ([L4](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/04-diagnosing-emotional-misfires.md) [05:37])

## How to change intensity

| Lever | Turn it **up** | Turn it **down** |
|---|---|---|
| **Verbs / headline** | Action verbs, "the emotional accelerators". Quick, sharp, definitive. Give a reason to buy *right now*. ("seize") | Soft, conversational, gentle copy ("find") |
| **Colour** | More saturated/intense colour (soft baby pink → intense pink). Bold colours = more urgency. | Desaturate slightly. Desaturated tones = trust. Soft pastels. |
| **Framing** | **Zoom in** on the product: "Up close and personal is very intense for people." Sensory punch. | **Zoom out**, show more of the body. "Far away feels better." |
| **Light** | Harsh lighting, high contrast, jarring visuals (Zone 4) | Softer light. "Morning light is very calming to people." |
| **Faces / imagery** | Intense facial expressions, intense lifestyle imagery | Calm expressions, gentle scenes |

## Examples
- Copper Cow Coffee ad: **low intensity**. "No urgency… not really dramatic… no high arousal emotions like fear or excitement or outrage… soft pink background… gentle lighting." (L4 [02:52])
- **Jaguar**: an intensity misfire. Its "ridiculously intense, lots of colors" campaign was too high-intensity for luxury customers who preferred low intensity, high valence. (L4 [04:31])
- Skincare (e-book p9): "Nourish your skin while you sleep." → "Wake up to younger looking skin (overnight)."

## Common mistakes
- Running a calm, low-intensity ad at a stressed buyer who wants quick solutions. It gets ignored. (L4 [03:58])
- Using high-intensity promo styling on high-trust or luxury products. It "feels a little bit cheap". (L4 [04:19])
- Assuming more emotion is risky. Under-emotional ads are a frequent failure mode. (L4 [05:37])

## Background *(context, not from course)*
In affective science this dimension is usually called **arousal** (e.g., Russell's circumplex model of affect, 1980). Sarah's "intensity" maps onto it.
