# Risk-Reduction Hooks ('How To' → 'How I')
> "Some people don't actually want to change their life. They want to **reduce the risk of going through the process** of changing their life", so "how I [got the outcome] in [time]" beats "how to", and "you don't have to change all of you" beats "change your life" ([Live, May 7](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:21:48–0:23:45]).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** rewriting aspirational or "how to" hooks; writing founder, coach or creator openers; adding a guarantee, trial, return or "one small change" beat to a script; selling to anxious, research-heavy or burned-before buyers.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a draft hook/script or competitor ad + the buyer's biggest fear about the process (cost, effort, failure, wasted money) → "how I" hook variants, a "just change one thing" line, and one risk-reversal beat (guarantee, $0 trial, longer return window).
- **Rules:**
  - DO turn "This is how to [outcome]" into "**This is how I [achieved outcome] in [time frame]**". It "activates the authority proof heuristic" and "risk reduction behavior". [May7 0:21:48–0:22:46]
  - DO flip transformation into risk reduction: "you don't have to change all of you, you just have to be careful about these three things that always come up". [May7 0:23:06–0:23:45]
  - DON'T sell aspiration by default: "We think aspirational everything… stop doing that." [May7 0:22:46–0:23:06]
  - DO write actual-self lines that shrink the change: "Don't change everything, just change one thing." / "You're 70% of the way there." [Jun18 0:16:42–0:17:43]
  - DO add a guarantee; it reads as authority and safety ("the authority is going to take care of it if it doesn't work out"). [L5 04:10–04:38]
  - DO say "$0 trial", not "free trial"; fix the snag trial users look for. [Jul9 0:21:49–0:23:53]
  - DO lengthen return windows and say "one month", not "30 days". [Jul2 0:49:11–0:50:50]
  - DO affirm research-heavy buyers up front: they're afraid of getting burned, not hunting for value. [Jun4 0:34:31–0:36:36]
- **See also:** [Authority bias](heuristics/authority-bias.md) · [Loss aversion](heuristics/loss-aversion.md) · [Goal-gradient effect](heuristics/goal-gradient-effect.md) · [Validation over authority](validation-over-authority.md) · [Barrier buster](barrier-buster.md) · [Actual self](actual-self.md) · [Fogg Behavior Model](../frameworks/fogg-behavior-model.md) (expected)

## Definition
Hooks and beats that lower the **perceived risk of trying** instead of raising the desire for the outcome. Two families:
1. **Lived-experience framing** ("how I"): someone already hit "the speed bumps that you're about to hit, so I'm going to teach you how to avoid them… somebody else went and did the hard work for me." [May7 0:22:12–0:22:46]
2. **Risk reversal and small change:** guarantees, $0 trials, long returns, "change one thing", "you're already most of the way there".

## Why it matters
- **Fear beats aspiration for many buyers:** "fear is pretty big for humans", and risk reduction "often works a whole lot better". [May7 0:22:46–0:23:45]
- **Measured lift (attendee case):** an agency called "how to" → "how I" "a massive unlock in spend and results" for coaching clients spending six to seven figures a month. [May7 0:21:30–0:22:04]
- **Guarantees signal security.** "Anytime you put guarantees anywhere in your ad, that speaks to security" (Maelove "That's our promise", in an 8-month static). [Jun4 0:48:21]
- **Ability gaps kill buying.** Marketers over-prompt and under-deliver on "making it easy to do" ([Live, July 16](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:05:50–0:06:15]); risk reversal is a cheap ability boost. Prose's "Love your first order (or it's on us)" is "way more motivating". [Jul16 0:14:50]

## How to use it
1. **Name the buyer's fear of the process** (wasted money, effort, looking foolish, another failure).
2. **Rewrite the hook** from instruction to lived proof: "How I [outcome] in [time]".
3. **Shrink the change:** "You don't have to change all of you…", "just change one thing", "you're 70% of the way there".
4. **Add one risk-reversal beat:** guarantee, $0 trial, long return window, "or we work for free".
5. **Affirm the researcher:** "You know a good deal when you see one… here's all the information." [Jun4 0:35:35–0:36:36]
6. **Check honesty:** the "I" must be real (founder, creator, customer). Don't fabricate the story or the time frame.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- "How I…" founder/creator openers; "you don't need to…"; "just one change"; guarantee seals; "$0", "risk-free", "or we work for free"; long return windows.
- Contrast: "how to" and "change your life" openers = aspirational default (an opening for a risk-reduction remix).
- Tag: `hook-how-i`, `risk-reversal`, `risk-small-change`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** test "how I" against every "how to" hook. [May7 0:21:48]
- **IF** the category is uniformly aspirational **THEN** test risk-reduction framing. [May7 0:22:46–0:23:45]
- **DO** include one risk-reversal element (guarantee, trial, returns) per script or page. [L5 04:10; Jul16 0:14:50]
- **IF** buyers research for a long time **THEN** validate their savvy up front instead of adding discounts. [Jun4 0:35:35]
- **DO** consider a tiny paid entry ("buy us a coffee") when free trials attract tyre-kickers: "even a little bit of a monetary investment causes the brain to commit". [Jul9 0:23:53–0:24:24]

## Common mistakes
- Fake "I" stories or invented time frames (never fabricate proof).
- Overnight-transformation claims; customers know there's nuance ([Live, May 14](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-14-live-with-sarah.md) [0:41:04–0:41:54]).
- A guarantee buried in the footer where nobody sees it.
