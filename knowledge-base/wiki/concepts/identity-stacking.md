# Identity Stacking (Impulse Buys Don't Exist)
> "**The impulse buy doesn't actually exist.**" The need "exists somewhere in the subconscious for months, sometimes years", and a triggering event links two identities so that anything sitting at their intersection becomes an "instant buy" ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:45:03–0:47:06]).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** briefing novelty, gift or low-price products that look like "impulse" purchases; widening top of funnel when you don't know who the buyer is; adding a second identity layer (humor, parenthood, a hobby) to an ad; diagnosing why a competitor's "random" product sells.
- **Inputs → outputs:** product + candidate buyer identities (from reviews, Reddit, surveys) → the primary identity, one secondary identity to stack (default: humor), the triggering event that connects them, and 3–5 hooks that speak to the intersection.
- **Rules:**
  - DON'T treat sales as impulse buys. Look for the identity pair a triggering event connected. [May21 0:45:03–0:47:06]
  - DO find a **secondary identity** to connect. Default: "**I like funny stuff.** Humor is an identity and it works for almost every brand on the planet." [May21 0:47:06–0:47:29]
  - DO use puns, dad jokes, dry humour, memes: "Every time I've run a meme in an ad account it crushes." [May21 0:47:29–0:48:44]
  - DO test humour in organic first, then pull winners into the ad account. [May21 0:48:44–0:49:00]
  - DO work identity first, emotion second, tactics third. [May7 0:06:14–0:09:40]
  - DON'T let the joke become the identity on its own: tie it to the product (the gelato meme attracted meme lovers, then died). [Jun4 0:31:21–0:33:26]

## Definition
Every buyer holds several identities, built roughly "from year one to year seven" and switched on and off all day ("mom mode the moment her son walks in") ([Live, May 7](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:06:14–0:07:16]). A triggering event can fuse two of them, so a product that speaks to both lands as an automatic yes. Identity stacking is deliberately writing to two identities at once. Note: selves (actual/ideal/ought) are a different model; Sarah says identities are "a totally different topic" ([Live, July 16](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:33:00]).

## Why it matters
- **Sarah's own "impulse" buy.** A $7 Instagram book on executive functioning for children, from a creator she'd barely seen. A marketer would log an impulse buy, but she'd consumed psychology "for decades", and having kids at 29 and 31 "caused me to connect two different things in my brain: my love of psychology and my love of my children… any product that talks about nurturing the psychology of your children: instant buy." [May21 0:45:26–0:46:46]
- **Identities connect daily.** "You are going to have two different identities that are going to connect… based on which triggering events are on or off on the daily." [May21 0:46:46–0:47:06]
- **Identity drives emotion.** "The subconscious, the one that actually holds all the identity information, is the one that's using emotion to push the identity out." [May7 0:07:16–0:08:17]
- **Use-case ads fatigue.** A skincare client's use-case ads ("do you have wrinkles?") kept fatiguing; identity + behaviour ("last time you're ever going to switch") lasted. [May7 0:17:51–0:20:05]

## How to use it
1. **List identities** buyers hold (reviews, Reddit thread counts, picture surveys). [May7 0:29:20–0:32:06]
2. **Pick the primary identity** the product obviously serves.
3. **Pick a secondary identity** to stack. Start with humour ("highly universal"), then test others (parent, hobby, profession).
4. **Name the trigger** that fuses them (new baby, move, diagnosis, new job). See [triggering events](triggering-events.md).
5. **Write to the intersection** in the hook, not to the product feature.
6. **Test cheaply:** organic posts and memes first; scale what earns comments. [May21 0:48:44]
7. **Tie it back to the product** (product as the character, recurring mascot) so the audience attaches to the brand, not just the joke. [Jun4 0:33:26]

## Deploy in a UGC script
| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Call both identities | "If you're a psychology nerd *and* a mom, you need this." (illustrative, not from course; from Sarah's book story [May21 0:45:26]) |
| Hook (humor stack) | Meme or dry joke about the primary identity | Trash-panda meme on a "chaotic" tee (illustrative, not from course; [May21 0:47:06–0:48:44]) |
| Body | The trigger that connected them | "Ever since I had kids, anything about how their brains work…" (illustrative) |
| CTA | Low friction, identity-affirming | "Made for people like us." (illustrative) |

**Visual cues:** both identities visible in frame (a parent with a psychology book; a gamer in a kitchen); meme formats for the humour layer; the product as a character.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Two identity cues in one ad (profession + parenthood, hobby + humour); memes on a non-comedy product; a product that only makes sense to a niche intersection.
- Tag: `id-stack` plus the pair, e.g. `id-stack:humor+pet-owner`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DON'T** write off a purchase as impulse; find the two identities behind it. [May21 0:45:03]
- **DO** default to humour as the stackable second identity. [May21 0:47:29]
- **DO** test memes in organic, then move winners to ads. [May21 0:48:44]
- **IF** a humour ad spikes and dies **THEN** attach the joke to the product. [Jun4 0:32:26–0:33:26]
- **DO** prioritise identity → emotion → tactics. [May7 0:06:14–0:09:40]

## Common mistakes
- Guessing demographics ("moms or Gen Z?") instead of stacking a universal identity first. [May21 0:47:06–0:48:44]
- Writing to use cases instead of identities. [May7 0:17:51]
- A meme that recruits meme lovers rather than product buyers. [Jun4 0:31:21]
