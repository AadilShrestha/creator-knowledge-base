# The Algorithm as a Library (Meta Andromeda)

> **"Who is most likely to want to read this book… I have a set of books on psychology, I need to go out and find a lot of people who like psychology… You get to decide what's in your library."** ([Live With Sarah, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:19:49–0:20:40]). Sarah's mental model of Meta: every ad you run is a "book"; the algorithm finds readers who like the books already on your shelf. Your creative is your targeting, so to acquire new customers you must add books for people "you've never seen in this library before".

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** deciding what a new batch should *teach* the algorithm; diagnosing why acquisition stalls or CPA creeps up; pivoting an audience; explaining to a media buyer why a message/identity change moves CPA; choosing test structure.
- **Inputs → outputs:** current account winners (messages, identities, zones) + the audience you want → a "library audit" (what readers the current books attract) + new-book briefs (new identity, situation, zone) + a structure note.
- **Rules:**
  - DO keep running what works ("rinse and repeat because you still need people to convert") **and** branch out: "tell the algorithm, go find people who you've never seen in this library before." [May 7 0:40:00–0:40:26]
  - DO retrain by **changing the creative**, not (only) the pixel: "You can retrain this algorithm within like a couple hours… it's a computer, so it doesn't need weeks." [May 21 0:20:40–0:21:20; 0:27:42]
  - DO use identity call-outs to steer who gets found: "50% off" → "You're the type of pet parent who cares about what's inside their pet supplements" caused an "instant change in the [account]". [May 21 0:20:59–0:21:20]
  - DO call out a **hyper-specific situation** so Meta can find the right buyer: "The cheapest person for Meta might not be the cheapest person for you." [July 9 0:38:55–0:39:38]
  - DO name the **specific** emotion: "The only reason ads are expensive is because we're using a generalized term, so Facebook can't find anybody." [June 4 0:38:43–0:39:49]
  - DON'T run near-identical variants and expect clean learning: "the algorithm is like, all of these ads are the same… I don't know who to send this to." [June 11 0:51:49–0:52:52]
  - DO attach the hook to the **product**, not just a generic identity: a gelato meme pulled "I like memes" people, converted, then died. [June 4 0:31:21–0:33:26]
  - DO pick one test structure for what you want to learn and stick with it: "you're continuously training the math on the back end." [Roast #3 0:23:50–0:26:19]
  - DON'T rely only on conversion optimisation for acquisition: it "only lets us dip into mid to bottom". [May 21 0:12:56–0:13:50]

## Definition
Sarah's metaphor for Meta's delivery system ([May 7](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:39:20–0:40:00]): the pixel watches site behaviour, combines it with ad behaviour and builds "books on books" of every ad, conversion and behaviour ("here's our library of the Liquid Death brand"). It then finds people who like the books already in the library, "which is not great for acquisition". On May 21 she adds that it is "a mathematical statistical computer" tracking Meta behaviour plus a handful of site events ("like eight events, six events", approximate) [0:19:49–0:20:40]. On June 11 she calls it "just a wall… a veil between us and the consumers, and their behavior is telling that wall how high or low it should go" [0:52:52–0:54:59].

**Meta Andromeda** (Meta's 2025 ad-retrieval update) came up via member Jamie Ahmed: it "has rolled out", it's "a lot easier than even 60 days ago", and new pixels "shouldn't take long to train". Sarah: "I don't think we give the computer enough credit… it's built to adjust itself on an hourly… minute-by-minute basis." ([May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:28:26–0:28:55])

## Why it matters
- **Creative is targeting.** What you say decides who gets found: "It doesn't matter if you're the smallest fish in the smallest pond with a whale… if your message is better", Meta serves your ad ([June 18](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [0:51:57–0:52:58]).
- **Acquisition stalls** when every book is for the same reader ("If you're struggling to get top of funnel customers in, it's predominantly because you're making ads to a middle of the funnel customer", [May 7](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:20:05–0:20:58]); see [emotional stagnation](emotional-stagnation.md).
- **Generic ads are expensive**: if Meta can't find the precise buyer it keeps chasing whoever is cheapest *for Meta* ("if the price they can get a customer at is $77 and you need it to be $30, oh well", [July 9](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:55–0:39:38]).
- **Platforms favour volume for their own revenue** (Chase: Meta wants "you… to test 25 ads a month"; "follow the dollars", [Chase](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-guest-session-02-chase-mohseni.md) [0:10:43–0:11:13]); Sarah and Chase both argue for precision instead.
- "Competitors might be able to outspend you but they cannot out-create you" ([May 7](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:39:00–0:39:20]).

## How to use it
1. **Audit the shelf:** tag current winners by identity, situation, emotion/zone, self. Who are these books for?
2. **Decide the new reader:** an identity, situation or zone missing from the shelf (see [competitor teardown](../playbooks/competitor-ad-teardown.md) for gaps).
3. **Write books for them:** identity call-outs ("You're the type of… who…"), hyper-specific situations, precise emotions.
4. **Keep the converters running** alongside (rinse and repeat).
5. **Make variants genuinely different** in message or format so the algorithm can route them ([iteration vs variation](iteration-vs-variation.md)).
6. **Pivots:** swap the creative first; expect movement within hours, not weeks.
7. **Hold the structure** you chose for the month's learning goal; don't reshuffle mid-test.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- What reader does their library attract? (Tag 15–20 ads by identity/zone/self.) A single-reader library = an open lane for us.
- Offer-led copy ("50% off") trains toward deal seekers; identity-led copy trains toward a type.
- Ad-library longevity is not proof of a winner for big brands ([June 11](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md) [0:24:32]).
- Tags: `library-identity-callout`, `library-offer-led`, `library-generic`, `library-situation`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** add new books every batch; **DON'T** stop the converters. [May 7 0:40:00]
- **IF** pivoting **THEN** change creative first; a new pixel only if you must. [May 21 0:19:28–0:21:10]
- **IF** CPA is high on a good script **THEN** narrow the situation. [July 9 0:38:55]
- **DO** make each variant different enough to be routed. [June 11 0:51:49]
- **DO** pick a structure per learning goal and keep it. [Roast #3 0:25:44–0:26:19]

## Common mistakes
- Believing retraining takes weeks.
- Many near-identical ads ("2015-style" one-variable tests at scale).
- Humor or memes that attract an identity unrelated to the product.
- Optimising only for conversions and expecting net-new customers.

## Contradictions / notes
