# Creative Primitives

> **"There's a thing in engineering called data primitives. You can essentially do this with your creative primitives, i.e. copy, a product, a background, and you can do this really fast."** ([Chase Mohseni](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-guest-session-02-chase-mohseni.md) [0:06:25]). A creative is a stack of swappable parts; change one part at a time and you get a cheap, readable variation. "A new concept is a new color, a new icon." [0:21:11]

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** remixing a winning ad (ours or a competitor's mechanism) into a test batch; writing a variation brief for designers or creators; tearing down a competitor ad into parts; planning what changes between UGC cuts.
- **Inputs → outputs:** one winning (or approved seed) ad → a primitive inventory (copy line, product shot, background, color, icon, badge, crop/angle, CTA, model) + a batch of 3 variants that each change exactly one primitive, with the hypothesis for each.
- **Rules:**
  - DO decompose every ad into primitives before iterating: **copy** (a line, even one word), **product shot**, **background**, **color**, **icon**, **badge** (e.g. a social-proof badge), **crop / camera angle**. [Chase 0:06:25; 0:19:10–0:20:11]
  - DO change **one primitive per variant** so the winner is readable. [Chase 0:06:32–0:07:08; July 9 0:29:12]
  - DO launch **3 at a time** (3–5 max), keep what shows signal, add 3 more. [Chase 0:06:44; 0:58:21]
  - DO get one **seed asset** approved, then ship primitive-swaps without re-approval: "Permission equals speed." [Chase 0:48:08]
  - DO reuse existing assets: crop a photo-shoot image, take screen grabs from videos, "change the angle and everything about the story changes". [Chase 0:53:38–0:55:46]
  - DON'T put fabricated numbers on a badge primitive. Chase said you can put any count "whether you had five customers or 50,000"; that's fake social proof. [Chase 0:19:42]
  - DO choose which primitive to vary from customer insight, not taste: "You're dealing with commerce, you're not writing poetry." [Chase 0:55:46–0:56:48]

## Definition
The smallest independent building blocks of an ad that can be swapped without changing the concept: copy (headline/line/word), product shot, background, color, icon, badge, crop/camera angle (plus, in UGC, the model/creator, the opening shot, the CTA line). Chase borrows the idea from engineering "data primitives" ([Guest Session 02](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-guest-session-02-chase-mohseni.md) [0:06:25]). Variety in an account should mostly be built from primitive swaps, not net-new concepts [0:21:38–0:21:46].

## Why it matters
- Statics let anyone iterate quickly because they're built from primitives; video needs volume and "someone… that actually can instrument this" ([Chase](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-guest-session-02-chase-mohseni.md) [0:05:26–0:06:04]).
- One-primitive changes make the result interpretable; multi-variable changes don't ([July 9](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:29:12]: Aazar "iterated on the winning ad… otherwise… multiple variables and… not know which one won").
- Primitive swaps are cheap leverage: "Leverage creates margin… financial margin and time margin" [Chase 0:23:41].

## How to use it
1. Pick the seed: the winning ad (ours) or the competitor mechanism we're remixing (copy the mechanism, never the asset).
2. List its primitives in a table.
3. Write a hypothesis per primitive ("is it the redhead?", "is it the word 'feed'?", "is it the humor?").
4. Build 3 variants, one primitive each. Keep the core message, zone and intensity constant ([Roast #3](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:58:59–0:59:30]).
5. Launch, read signal, keep the best, add 3 more.
6. When copy and image both have winners, run the copy × image grid ([combination testing](../frameworks/creative-testing-system.md)).

## Deploy in a UGC script / creator brief
| Primitive | UGC version | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Copy | One changed hook line, same footage | Same b-roll, new on-screen hook (illustrative) |
| Opening shot | New first frame from existing footage | Screen grab or crop as the thumbnail (Chase [0:53:38]) |
| Product treatment | Close-up / in hand / two products | "One for you, one for another person" (Roast #3 [0:52:41–0:55:52]) |
| Creator | Same script, different creator type | Test hair color / look as one variable |
| Badge / overlay | Add one heuristic badge to the winning cut | "Only 9 sets left in this color" style (real counts only) |

**Visual cues:** identical layout across variants except one element; label each file with the primitive changed.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Pull 10–20 of their ads and group them: which share layout and copy but differ by one primitive? That's their iteration engine; the varying primitive is what they're testing.
- Long-running ads with many near-identical siblings = a scaled winner being varied.
- Tag each sibling: `var-copy`, `var-image`, `var-background`, `var-color`, `var-badge`, `var-crop`, `var-model`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** test 3, keep 1, add 3, until ~10 live with ~3 crushing. [Chase 0:58:52]
- **DON'T** "spray and pray" 50–100 variants: the best one may get $4 of spend. [Chase 0:43:13]
- **IF** a client/stakeholder must approve **THEN** get the seed approved once and ship primitive swaps. [Chase 0:48:08]
- **DO** keep zone and intensity constant while swapping visuals. [Roast #3 0:58:59]

## Common mistakes
- Changing image, copy and color at once (Roast #3 cherry statics: "several things change at once" [0:28:25]).
- Treating a primitive swap as a "new concept" in reporting.
- Fake numbers on badges.
