# Valence
> **Valence is the emotional *direction* of a message: whether the feeling it evokes is positive (feels good) or negative (feels bad).** In Sarah Levinger's model it's *what* you're saying emotionally. Together with [intensity](intensity.md) it places an ad in one of the four [emotional zones](emotional-zones.md).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** tagging the emotional direction of a competitor ad or our script, or deciding between a pull (aspiration) and a push (risk) angle.
- **Inputs → outputs:** headline/first line + image/first frame → valence label (positive/negative, score −2…+2) + dominant emotion.
- **Rules:**
  - DO name one dominant emotion and classify it as positive (joy, pride, excitement, hope, comfort) or negative (fear, frustration, anxiety, FOMO, anger).
  - DO change valence by changing the [emotional promise](emotional-promise.md), not just the verbs.
  - DON'T use negative valence to make people feel bad. Mirror their real experience.
- **See also:** [Intensity](intensity.md) · [Valence-Intensity diagnostic](../frameworks/valence-intensity-diagnostic.md)

## Definition
- Slide definition: "**Refers to the emotional direction of a message or experience.**" ([L2](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/02-understanding-valence-and-intensity.md) [00:30])
- "What kind of emotion you're trying to pull out from someone, aka like, **what am I trying to say?** Which emotion am I trying to get this person to feel?" (L2 [00:36])
- E-book: "**What emotion are we trying to evoke? (Good vs. bad)**"
- One-liner: "**Valence is what I'm saying. Intensity is how I'm saying it.**" (L2 [01:21])
- Terminology: the course uses **high/low valence** and **positive/negative valence** interchangeably. High = positive, low = negative.

## The two poles

| | Positive (high) valence | Negative (low) valence |
|---|---|---|
| Feels | Good | Bad / "a little bit lower emotional tone" |
| Example emotions (video) | Joy, pride, excitement, hope, comfort | Fear, frustration, anxiety, FOMO, anger |
| Example emotions (e-book) | Joy, pride, comfort, hope | Fear, FOMO, frustration, anxiety |
| Buyer mechanism | **Pulls** people toward something they want | **Pushes** people away from things they fear or dislike |
| Marketing style | "Aspirational marketing is almost always positive valence" | "Risk level marketing": *secure your future, protect something* |
| Zones | [Zone 1](emotional-zones.md#zone-1-high-valence--low-intensity-feel-good) (calm) and [Zone 2](emotional-zones.md#zone-2-high-valence--high-intensity-hype--anticipation) (hype) | [Zone 3](emotional-zones.md#zone-3-low-valence--low-intensity-pain-point-whisper) (whisper) and [Zone 4](emotional-zones.md#zone-4-low-valence--high-intensity-danger-zone) (danger) |

(L2 [00:46–02:07])

## Why it matters
- Where an ad's emotion falls on the valence line "is going to **directly affect the reaction** that we get from people who are actually reading the ad." ([L3](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/03-the-4-emotional-zones.md) [00:50])
- Valence decides whether the ad *pulls* (aspiration) or *pushes* (risk avoidance). Getting it wrong means speaking the wrong emotional language even when the copy is "right".

## How to use it
1. Name the **single dominant emotion** the ad communicates. Look at the headline and image first: "your core message really is in your headline and in your images." ([L4](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/04-diagnosing-emotional-misfires.md) [02:41])
2. Classify it: positive or negative? This is step 1 of the [Valence-Intensity diagnostic](../frameworks/valence-intensity-diagnostic.md).
3. To *change* valence, change the [emotional promise](emotional-promise.md) (the emotion you're selling). That's the biggest of the three [tone-shifting](../frameworks/tone-shifting.md) levers. Verbs and visuals mostly change intensity.

## Examples
- Copper Cow Coffee testimonial ("bought these for a gift… had to re-order!"): **positive valence** (enjoyment, indulgence, surprise, delight). (L4 [02:25])
- Rewrites with **negative valence**: "It's 2025. Why are we still drinking stale beans?" (Z3), "The coffee you should have gifted—before it sold out." (Z4). ([L5](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/05-fixing-the-tone-without-rebuilding-the-ad.md) [08:30])

## Common mistakes
- Treating negative valence as "making people feel bad". Sarah: "It is not our job to make people feel bad about something." Negative-valence ads should evoke the emotion already attached to the customer's real experience, never trigger trauma. (L3 [03:51])
- Defaulting everyone into low-valence pain-point ads (Zone 3) in a saturated market where "a lot of marketers live". (L3 [03:10])

## Background *(context, not from course)*
Valence (pleasant ↔ unpleasant) and arousal (calm ↔ excited) are the two dimensions of James Russell's **circumplex model of affect** (1980), the "well-known psychology model" behind this map. Sarah's "intensity" corresponds to arousal.
