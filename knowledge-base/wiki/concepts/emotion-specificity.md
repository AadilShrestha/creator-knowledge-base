# Emotion Specificity (Name the Exact Feeling)

> **"It's not fear that they're feeling, it's disappointment. It's not anxiety that they're feeling, it's depression… The only reason ads are expensive is because we're using a generalized term, so Facebook can't find anybody."** ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:43–0:39:49])

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** choosing the emotion a UGC script or static sells; rewriting a hook that uses an umbrella word ("confidence", "beautiful", "happy"); tearing down a competitor ad to see whether its emotion is generic (an opening) or sharp.
- **Inputs → outputs:** reviews / Reddit threads + the umbrella emotion → 3 specific sub-emotions, each with a situation and a more intense word, then one ad per sub-emotion.
- **Rules:**
  - DO replace every umbrella emotion with a specific one: disappointment not fear, depression not anxiety ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:43]).
  - DO write **three nuanced ads per emotion**: esteem → confidence / control / appeal [0:36:36–0:37:39].
  - DO split even the sub-emotion: "boss babe is a type of confidence, or cheeky" [0:37:39]; "boardroom-level confidence" for businesswomen ([Roast My Ads #2](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:17:58–0:18:30; 0:22:56]).
  - DO raise intensity through word choice, not harshness: happy → elated, sad → depressed ([Live, July 16](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:18:30–0:19:20]).
  - DO anchor the emotion in a situation ("a very specific situation… will draw one specific type of woman") ([Roast My Ads #2](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:22:56–0:23:13]).
  - DON'T name the feeling when *researching* it. In surveys ask "how did you feel…", never "was it fun?" ("Don't name the feeling… extract the emotion") ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:56:43–0:57:44]).
- **See also:** [Specificity](heuristics/specificity.md) · [Intensity](intensity.md) · [Blended emotions](blended-emotions.md) · [Emotional zones](emotional-zones.md) · [Marketing to Mindstates](../people/will-leach.md) · [Tone-shifting](../frameworks/tone-shifting.md)

## Definition
Emotion specificity means targeting the precise feeling a buyer has (and the precise variety of it) rather than the broad category. It's the emotion branch of [specificity](heuristics/specificity.md): claim, situation, count and emotion. Sarah drills down in three levels (wiki structure, her examples):

| Level | Example | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase emotion (one of Will Leach's nine) | Esteem | [Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:36:36] |
| Nuance of that emotion | Confidence / control / appeal | [0:36:36–0:37:39] |
| Type of the nuance | Boss-babe, cheeky, self-acceptance or boardroom-level confidence | [0:37:39]; [Roast My Ads #2](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:17:58; 0:22:56] |

## Why it matters
- **Cheaper delivery.** "Everybody experiences nervousness, but only a few people experience depression, and that customer type is very cheap to get in the door, especially when they're looking for something to solve their problem" ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:43–0:39:49]).
- **Stronger attachment.** "If we stay generalized… we're probably going to get people who are not as attached… the brand is being very neutral" ([Roast My Ads #2](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:17:58–0:18:30]).
- **Intensity without cruelty.** "More intense with your language doesn't necessarily mean harsher. Intensity of language has to do with the choice of words" ([Live, July 16](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:18:30]).

## How to use it
1. Paste reviews into ChatGPT with the nine emotions and ask for the top three ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:36:36]).
2. Drill down with valence-intensity: "is it actually esteem that they want, or do they want to be more appealing? Is it belonging, or do they just want to feel noticed?" [0:37:39–0:38:43].
3. For each emotion write three nuance ads; for each nuance choose the *type* and a situation it lives in.
4. Swap neutral words for intense ones (happy → elated).
5. Run one ad set per emotion; read which nuance wins; feed winners into [blended emotions](blended-emotions.md).

## Deploy in a UGC script
| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Specific feeling + situation, in her words | "Boardroom-level confidence" as on-screen text over a creator walking into a meeting (Jamie's line, [Roast My Ads #2](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:22:56]; scene illustrative) |
| Problem | The precise negative emotion, not the umbrella | "It wasn't fear. It was disappointment, every single time." (illustrative, not from course) |
| Payoff | Intense positive word | "I'm so happy I can't even stand it." (Sarah's "elated" example, [Live, July 16](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:18:49]) |

**Visual cues:** the creator's face and posture must show the *type* of emotion (smirk for cheeky, squared shoulders for boardroom). See [model image psychology](model-image-psychology.md) and [hero image ↔ copy congruence](hero-image-copy-congruence.md).

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Generic: "confidence", "feel amazing", "style that speaks", "feel beautiful". Specific: a named type or situation.
- Ask: could ten different products say this line? If yes, it's generic and an opening.
- Tag: `concept-emotion-specificity` with `emo-generic` or `emo-specific:<label>`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** write ~3 nuanced ads per emotion [Live, June 4, 0:36:36].
- **IF** an ad is expensive **THEN** check whether its emotion is generic before blaming the creative [0:38:43].
- **IF** a headline wins **THEN** test which *type* of that emotion drove it ([Roast My Ads #3](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:45:18–0:46:24]).
- **DON'T** confuse intensity with harshness.

## Common mistakes
- Stopping at the mindstate label ("esteem") and writing generic copy.
- Naming the feeling in survey questions, which leads respondents.
- Making it specific in words but not in the creator's face/body.
