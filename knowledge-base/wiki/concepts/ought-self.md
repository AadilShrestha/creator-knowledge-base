# Ought Self
> **"Who I think I should be."** The self driven by **duty, responsibility and external expectation**. Ads aimed at it say "*you have a standard to uphold and this is how you meet it*" and sell **relief and reassurance**. It's slow to start but produces loyal, high-LTV customers.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** classifying an ad as Ought-Self, or writing an Ought variant (duty, responsibility, protection). Often the least-used lane in competitor sets and the source of high-LTV buyers.
- **Inputs → outputs:** copy or script → Ought score + cues. Or: product + the role standard (parent, host, "real adult", pro) → Ought hook and script lines + authority proof.
- **Rules:**
  - DO look for or write: "should", "don't risk…", "don't let this fall through the cracks", "people are counting on you", "real adults…", "you know you should…", "your future self will thank you".
  - DO pay off the pressure with **relief and reassurance**: "this is how you meet the standard" [L4 05:36].
  - DO back it with authority or safety proof (press, expert approval, certifications, protection features).
  - DON'T overdo guilt. Sarah: "be very careful with this one" [L4 04:30].
  - IF an Ought variant is slow in week 1–2 **THEN** keep it running and watch add-to-carts and LTV [L8 03:25].
- **See also:** [3 selves](three-selves.md) · [Rewriting ads for new selves](../frameworks/rewriting-ads-for-new-selves.md) · [Testing ads by self type](../frameworks/testing-ads-by-self-type.md)

One of the [3 selves](three-selves.md), alongside the [Actual Self](actual-self.md) and the [Ideal Self](ideal-self.md).

## Definition
- Lesson 4: "I love ought self… but you have to be very careful with this one because ought self is all about **duty, responsibility, external expectation**." [04:30] "**Should** is the word that comes into mind here the most if you're going to target this person." [04:52] "It's not necessarily about dreams. **It's about duty.**" [05:14]
- E-book: "Driven by duty, pressure, or social expectation. These ads act like responsible reminders."
- Prompt doc: "Speaks to who they *think they should be*. Driven by duty, pressure, and risk avoidance." / "Duty and responsibility (should do)."
- Orientation: **social**. "This is what society thinks I should be." [L4 05:59]

## Why it matters
- **Usually under-used.** In the High Camp run, Ought played "a minor supportive role", appearing only in "justifications for better quality or cleaner habits" [L6 07:54]. It's often the biggest untapped pool.
- **Performance signature (L8): slow starts, strong intent, high LTV.** "We've had this ad running for like two weeks and nothing's going on. And then all of a sudden it will… hit." These buyers add to cart a lot. "As soon as they get attached to doing something out of duty, they will continue to do it… very, very loyal, very quickly because you feel this sense of responsibility to yourself, to your family." Sarah calls it "such a good customer type for businesses." [03:25–04:06]
- **Funnel fit (e-book):** Awareness ("urgency") and Conversion ("push with guilt"). "Nudge them off the fence with pressure or logic."

## How to target it
- **Message:** "you have a standard to uphold and this is how you meet it." You're selling **relief and reassurance** [L4 05:36]. Messaging about "safety, doing things the right way."
- **Lines from the course:**
  - "Don't let this fall through the cracks" · "People are counting on you" · "Do what your future self will thank you for" (L4)
  - "Because details matter, even outdoors." (L5)
  - "Don't Risk the Bottle" / "Keep your wine safe, sealed, and temperature-controlled — like a responsible adult." / "Real Adults Bring a Flask" (L7)
  - "You know you should be taking better care of yourself." · "You know you should be doing this. / Dermatologist-approved, zero guilt, full coverage." (e-book)
- **Customer language:** "I should really…" "It's about time I…" **Emotion:** guilt, pressure. **Trigger:** social norms or inner expectations.
- **E-book prompt:** "Write an ad that reminds the customer of what they should be doing. Emphasize responsibility, social proof, and consequences."
- **Social proof style:** authority and endorsement, e.g. "As seen in Vogue / Men's Journal / goop / Business Insider", "Dermatologist-approved" (L7, e-book).
- **Angles** (prompt 2 example): "Test Ought Self framing using duty, responsibility, and protection angles."

## Common mistakes
- **Leaning too hard on guilt.** Sarah warns "you have to be very careful with this one" [L4 04:30]. The e-book asks "Are we overusing guilt or pressure?" Pair the pressure with relief.
- **Killing Ought tests too early.** The slow start is expected [L8 03:25].
- **Not recognising "future self will thank you" as Ought.** It's a duty frame (e-book scores it Ought 9 vs Ideal 7).
