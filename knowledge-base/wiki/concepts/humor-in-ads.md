# Humor in Ads (Types, Craft Rules, Funnel Fit)

> **"Humor is an identity and it works for almost every brand on the planet."** ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:47:29]). Done well, humor makes the buyer feel understood, "and then their guard is down… now I can let that comedy in" ([Joanna Wallace](../people/joanna-wallace.md), [Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:02:02]). Done badly: "trying to be funny and failing is not cute" [0:44:08].

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing or briefing a funny UGC script, meme static or skit; tearing down a competitor's humor ad (which type, which insecurity, who delivers it, does the joke carry product info); adding a second identity to a novelty product; softening a guilt/shame concept.
- **Inputs → outputs:** persona + reviews/comments + product traits + who the audience follows → **one** humor type, 3–5 joke/hook lines lifted from customer language, a messenger or character choice, a funnel placement (acquisition vs retention) and a 2-week test.
- **Rules:**
  - DO pick **one** humor type for the brand and test it "for a couple weeks" before judging ([Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:00:00–0:00:11]).
  - DO joke about the persona's real **insecurities** from inside ("I'm not the only one"), self-deprecating, never insulting [0:01:01–0:03:04].
  - DO let customers write the jokes: read reviews and ad comments ("it's a weapon") and paste reviews into ChatGPT: "what type of humor do you think these people would find funny?" [0:07:12–0:09:21].
  - DO borrow the humor register of the figure the audience already follows (HexClad → Gordon Ramsay: cocky, brash, cursing) and check the demographic data before dropping it over complaints [0:03:04–0:06:10].
  - DO match the humor to the product's nature (a near-indestructible pan → "don't f*** with us") [0:06:10].
  - DO put **"meat information"** in every acquisition pun ("sharp as f*ck" says *sharp*; "to the point" says nothing) [0:32:29–0:34:35].
  - IF acquisition THEN intense, bizarre, exaggerated humor. IF retention (email, site) THEN clever, subdued, punny or character-based [0:33:34–0:34:35].
  - DO attach the joke to the **product**, not just to "I like memes": make the product the character ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:31:21–0:33:26]).
  - DO test humor cheaply in organic first, then pull winners into ads ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:48:44–0:49:00]).
  - DON'T beautify self-deprecating content; DON'T use exclamation points for deadpan; DON'T back out of the bit halfway ([Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:17:42–0:24:06]).
- **See also:** [Joanna Wallace](../people/joanna-wallace.md) · [Fluency mechanism](fluency-mechanism.md) · [Barrier buster](barrier-buster.md) · [Mirror effect](mirror-effect.md) · [Identity stacking](identity-stacking.md) · [Messenger effect](heuristics/messenger-effect.md) · [Skeptic format](../frameworks/skeptic-format.md) · [Anthropomorphism](heuristics/anthropomorphism.md)

## Definition
Humor in Tether Lab is a tool for **identity alignment and guard-lowering**, not a garnish. Two framings:
1. **Humor = being understood** (Joanna): brands that do it well "really know how to embody that person… they can talk about the insecurities, but they do it in a way that's not insulting" ([Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:01:01–0:02:02]).
2. **Humor = a second identity** (Sarah): "impulse buys don't exist"; a purchase happens when two identities connect. "I like funny stuff" is the most universal secondary identity, so stack it onto novelty products ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:45:03–0:48:44]; see [identity stacking](identity-stacking.md)).

## The humor types (Sarah's ChatGPT list + Joanna's craft notes)
| # | Type | Definition (as read) | Examples named | Craft rule | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Self-deprecation** | "Making yourself the punchline"; relatability | Kristen Wiig, Wendy's Twitter, Liquid Death quoting worst reviews | "You got to get ugly": double chin, bad bathroom light. "You can't beautify it." | [Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:16:42–0:18:43] |
| 2 | **Sarcasm** | "Saying the opposite of what you mean with a straight face" | Aubrey Plaza, Cards Against Humanity | "Use periods… [a period] is the version of a straight face." Keep the joke in disclaimers ("*Please do not use this as a weapon.") | [0:18:43–0:21:56] |
| 3 | **Absurd / surreal** | Bizarre, illogical, dreamlike | Old Spice guy on a horse; Skittles | "Commit to the bit and don't let go"; laugh at your own jokes | [0:21:56–0:24:06] |
| 4 | **Satire / parody** | Mocking people, trends, systems | The Onion, SNL, South Park, Duolingo bird, Joe Fenti's corporate skits, multi-character TikTok parodists | On a trend, find "your brand's spin… it has to be distinctive" | [0:24:06–0:28:17] |
| 5 | **Slapstick / physical** | Exaggerated movement or pain | Skittles, MrBeast, personified products, "talking potato" | Joanna's idea: T-rex suit cooking with tiny arms ("you can still sear a T-bone") | [0:28:17–0:31:27] |
| 6 | **Character-driven archetype** | A recurring persona | Tether's Dex (deadpan British dinosaur); HexClad's Paige the skeptic | See [fluency mechanism](fluency-mechanism.md), [skeptic format](../frameworks/skeptic-format.md) | [0:34:35–0:37:48] |
| — | Others named in passing | Deadpan, dark, exaggeration, bizarre, cocky/brash (Gordon), puns | — | Puns need meat information for acquisition | [0:00:00; 0:08:17; 0:32:29] |

**Craft rules across types:** rule of three ("two things that are pretty normal and then the third one is just off left field") [0:21:56]; commit to the bit [0:22:46–0:24:06]; hire comedians and an editor who understands timing, because "comedy done by non-comedians often is obvious" and "timing is everything" [0:43:04–0:45:14].

## Why it matters
- **Humor opens the door to deeper topics.** Scary Mommy started with top-level humor and only then went darker: "The only reason that they could dip into more serious topics is because they made people laugh" ([Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:11:27–0:12:29]).
- **Memes crush, then die.** "Every time I've run a meme in an ad account it crushes" ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:47:29]). But Ahmed's gelato meme hit 3.5× (also said 2.5×) average CTR and "died out fast" because people attached to "I like memes", not to the product; humor ads die fast "unless the whole brand stays humorous" ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:30:17–0:33:26]).
- **A winner can be funny or lived.** Ahmed's "You already burned dinner, but it's okay" static cut CPA ~$22 → ~$6; Sarah's test splits it into a "universal moments / funny" thread and a "lived experience" thread ([Live, June 18](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [0:35:53–0:41:19]).

## How to use it
1. List the persona's top insecurities and complaints from reviews and comments, in their words [Guest Session 01, 0:07:12; 0:12:29].
2. Ask ChatGPT (reviews pasted) which humor type they'd find funny; pick **one** [0:08:17–0:09:21].
3. Note who the audience follows and that person's register (cocky, deadpan, dry) [0:03:04].
4. Decide funnel placement: acquisition = intense/bizarre/exaggerated with product info; retention = cute/punny/character [0:33:34].
5. Make the product or a recurring character carry the joke ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:33:26]).
6. Test in organic first; move winners into ads; test for ~2 weeks per type.
7. When a funny ad wins, test *why*: universal-funny vs lived-situation variants ([Live, June 18](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [0:39:07–0:41:19]).
8. If a joke misfires, call it out ([barrier buster](barrier-buster.md)); if a concept shames, use a [mirror-effect](mirror-effect.md) parody.

## Deploy in a UGC script
| Script slot | Pattern | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (self-deprecation) | Confess the shared insecurity, unfiltered | "Mom, why does your stomach fold that way?" (kid line) ([Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:10:26–0:11:27]) |
| Hook (situational absurd) | Highlight one weird situation | "You already burned dinner, but it's okay, because we've got gelato." ([Live, June 18](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [0:35:53]) |
| Skeptic beat | Recurring doubter tests the claim | "All right, Gordy… whoa… go off, Gordy." ([Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:36:17–0:36:43]) |
| Proof (customer joke) | Real comment as testimonial | "Can be used as a weapon. Also the best pepper grinder I've ever used." [0:08:03] |
| Disclaimer | Keep the joke going, deadpan period | "*Please do not use this as a weapon." [0:20:49–0:21:56] |
| Character | Product as a snarky character | Cartoon gelato that is "snarky… kind of pissy… kind of mean" ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:33:26]) |

**Visual cues:** ugly, real framing for self-deprecation (front camera, harsh bathroom light); periods not exclamation marks in overlays; personified product (eyes, evil smile, [anthropomorphism](heuristics/anthropomorphism.md)); comedy-literate edit timing.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Identify: humor type, insecurity targeted, messenger (founder, creator, character, celebrity), whether the joke carries product information, whether the brand commits (recurring) or dabbles (one-off).
- One-off meme with a spike and no product attachment = it will die; a recurring character or voice = a fluency mechanism worth countering.
- Tag: `concept-humor` + `humor-<type>` (self-deprecation, sarcasm, absurd, satire, slapstick, character, pun, meme) + `funnel-acq` / `funnel-ret`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** one humor type, two weeks, then read it ([Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:00:00]).
- **DO** rule of three; periods for deadpan; commit to the bit [0:19:44–0:24:06].
- **IF** a humor ad spikes and dies **THEN** keep "humor works", test types, and tie the joke to the product ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:31:21–0:33:26]).
- **IF** you jump on a comedy trend **THEN** add a distinctive brand spin ([Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:27:13–0:28:17]).
- **IF** you double down **THEN** go all in like Liquid Death (use negative reviews in the humor); otherwise inject small and deepen [0:40:56–0:42:01].
- **DO** "everybody please test a meme this week" ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:30:17]).

## Common mistakes
- "Blanket comedy" that ignores the persona's insecurities [Guest Session 01, 0:01:01].
- Insulting the buyer instead of laughing with them.
- Cute puns in acquisition ads.
- A straight-laced editor cutting a comedian's footage.
- Copying another brand's comedy structure without comedy talent (HexClad copycats "fell flat") [0:43:04].
