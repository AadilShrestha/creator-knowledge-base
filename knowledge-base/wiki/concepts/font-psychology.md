# Font Psychology (Hedonic vs Utilitarian, Sizes, Weight, Punctuation)

> **"Customers are 5.43 times more likely to [buy] hedonic products… if they're paired with handwritten fonts."** ([Live With Sarah, July 9](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:05:37–0:07:08], citing a study she posted; uncited in the call). Match the typeface to the product's job (fun vs problem-solving), make it big enough to read, weight what must be read first, and let punctuation set the tone.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** choosing on-screen text style for statics, UGC captions and end cards; writing landing-page/advertorial specs; tearing down a competitor's typography; diagnosing why a "same copy, new font" variant flopped.
- **Inputs → outputs:** product type (hedonic or utilitarian), brand (personal brand? mascot?), placement (ad vs page, mobile) → font family, size floor, weight hierarchy, punctuation style.
- **Rules:**
  - DO use **handwritten fonts for hedonic (just-for-fun) products** (chocolate, kids' toys, treats). Handwriting is "interpreted as like a human". [July 9 0:06:08–0:07:08]
  - DO use **structured / system fonts for utilitarian, problem-solving products** ("system fonts like Times New Roman, something that has… structure"). The effect is "opposite" for utilitarian goods. [July 9 0:07:08–0:07:21]
  - IF a utilitarian brand wants handwriting THEN pair it with a **cartoon character** (Bart Simpson chalkboard) or a **solopreneur personal brand**. "If you pair a handwritten font with a character it works. If you pair a handwritten font with no character it doesn't work." [July 9 0:08:10–0:09:10]
  - DON'T let site body text drop below ~**17px on mobile**; ~**20** is easiest to read; "the brain really can't read anything below about a 12 point font size". [July 9 0:10:14–0:11:08]
  - DO make what must be read first "big… bold", and anything secondary "light, small". [Roast #2 0:51:51–0:52:22]
  - DO cap headlines at **3 lines** (subheads 2, call-outs 3 with the top line bolded). Four lines = "a paragraph, the brain will ignore it." [Roast #1 0:36:37–0:38:39]
  - DO use **periods, not exclamation points**, for deadpan and sarcasm: "it is the version of a straight face." [Joanna 0:19:44–0:20:49]
  - DO end qualifiers or teasers with an **ellipsis** to pull the reader on ("Businesses making over $25K a month…"). [Roast #2 0:51:32]
  - DON'T use tiny body text on PDPs for older buyers ("probably just skipping this entirely"). [July 16 0:20:40–0:22:30]

## Definition
How typeface style, size, weight and punctuation change what the brain believes about a product and whether it reads the text at all. Two jobs: **fit** (does the font's personality match a hedonic or utilitarian product?) and **fluency** (can the brain read it in a fraction of a second?).

## Why it matters
- Same ads, different font, opposite result: Darian's tax-debt client's clean **Poppins** text ads "were all crushing it"; identical copies in a "scratchy" handwritten font "all bombed" ([July 9](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:07:21–0:08:10]).
- Brands that simply **increased landing-page font sizes** have raised conversion "just because people can read" [0:11:08–0:11:19].
- Typography *is* hierarchy: "the way you design an ad is going to teach them what they should believe about your message" ([Roast #1](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:39:40–0:40:44]).

## How to use it
1. Classify the product: **hedonic** (pleasure, fun, treat) or **utilitarian** (solves a problem). Many are mixed (Sarah calls hair care "utilitarian with hedonic elements").
2. Pick the family: handwritten for hedonic; structured/system for utilitarian; handwritten on utilitarian only with a character or a personal brand.
3. Set sizes: ads readable at a glance; pages ≥17px mobile body, aim ~20.
4. Weight the hierarchy: the one message heaviest; supporting text light and small.
5. Set punctuation by tone: periods for deadpan/confidence; ellipses for "read the next thing"; avoid exclamation-point "trying to be funny".
6. Test font as a single variable on a winning static.

**Visual cues:** handwritten vs structured type, a bold top line in call-outs, 3-line headline max, periods on deadpan lines.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Font family vs product type: handwriting on a problem-solving product with no character = likely underperformer.
- Headline length (lines), bold/light hierarchy, readable size on mobile.
- Punctuation: exclamation points everywhere signal "trying"; periods signal deadpan confidence.
- Tags: `type-handwritten`, `type-system`, `type-handwritten-with-character`, `type-deadpan-period`, `type-too-small`.

## Rules of thumb
- **IF** hedonic **THEN** test handwritten; **IF** utilitarian **THEN** structured. [July 9 0:06:08–0:07:21]
- **IF** handwritten on a utilitarian product **THEN** add a character or personal brand. [July 9 0:09:10]
- **DO** keep ≥17px mobile body text; never below ~12. [July 9 0:10:14]
- **DO** limit headlines to 3 lines and bold the first line of call-outs. [Roast #1 0:37:06–0:38:39]
- **DO** use periods for deadpan humor. [Joanna 0:19:44]

## Common mistakes
- Copying a "human" handwritten trend onto a serious problem-solving offer.
- Shrinking page fonts to follow the image-heavy UX trend ("design is cyclical") [July 9 0:09:38].
- Everything bold (no hierarchy) or a 4-line headline.
