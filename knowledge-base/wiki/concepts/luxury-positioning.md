# Luxury Positioning
> Luxury is "a totally different strategy": buyers who want what not everybody can get "**want it to be hard to access**", so you never bundle or discount, you price up, gate access, and make the ad look like the calm, elevated experience they're about to buy ([Live, June 18](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [0:11:31–0:12:31]).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** the product is premium, high-ticket, luxury or "elevated" (or the brand wants to move up-market); writing scripts, statics, offers or creator briefs for affluent buyers; tearing down a competitor that looks premium (or one that looks premium but acts like a discounter).
- **Inputs → outputs:** product, price point, category, current offer mechanics, target buyer → a positioning call (luxury vs mass), offer rules (no bundle/discount, qualification CTA, time-based scarcity), visual spec (white space, few elements, no Amazon-style icons), language level, and a pricier comparison object.
- **Rules:**
  - DON'T bundle. DON'T discount. "Do not bundle, dear God, do not bundle." [Jun18 0:10:29–0:12:31]
  - DO price up and restrict access: "price yourself upwards in the market until you're so high that only a very select few can afford you." Maserati "doesn't do limited anything. They just jack that price up." [Jun18 0:19:49]
  - DON'T say "very limited spaces": it recruits "lack shopping mode" buyers who "probably don't have the cash". DO frame scarcity as **time** ("this only opens up once every six months") or **qualification** ("see if you qualify"). [Jun18 0:17:43–0:20:52]
  - DO compare the product with something far more expensive (Maserati at boat and private-jet shows). [Jun18 0:29:21–0:30:27]
  - DO decide *what* you qualify for (price, personality, situation) before writing qualifying copy. [Jun18 0:30:27–0:35:53]
  - DO use minimal text, "as much white space as possible", very few elements. No "TikTok made me buy it". [Roast1 0:51:22–0:52:26]
  - DO raise the language level to the buyer's education ("scale your brand", not "grow your business"). [Jun18 0:15:40; Roast1 0:52:26–0:53:28]
  - DON'T add USP icons to PDP images for elevated brands. [Jul9 0:12:43]
  - DO keep wealth signals **subtle** for Gen Z ("quiet respect"), not "loud wealth". [Jun4 0:05:04–0:07:10]

## Definition
A positioning strategy for buyers who value exclusivity. Sarah's claim is that it inverts most DTC "best practices": the offer stack (bundles, discounts, urgency) that lifts mass-market conversion signals *cheapness* here. "Luxury-focused or exclusivity-focused customers, people who like to get things that not everybody can get, want it to be hard to access… don't bundle, do not discount… because ought self gets activated differently for luxury products." [Jun18 0:11:31–0:12:31]

High ticket in general is about perceived value: "High ticket I truly believe isn't any different than selling low ticket. The only difference is the perception of the perceived value." ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:29:31]) See [perception management](perception-management.md).

## Why it matters
- **Offers recruit the wrong buyer.** Quantity scarcity ("limited spaces") "activates this part of the brain where it says this is going to go away real quick" and brings in lack-mode shoppers. Time scarcity recruits the committed buyer who will "dedicate time and money". [Jun18 0:17:43–0:19:49]
- **Context sets price.** "Next to a $2 million G6 this car looks like a steal." The venue also filters for buyers with cash flow. [Jun18 0:29:21–0:32:35]
- **The ad previews the experience.** "Your ad communicates what they're about to experience." [Roast1 0:51:22]
- **Price alone isn't enough.** Raising prices can crash a brand if people "have them categorized in a different way"; Lamborghini became "commoditized luxury" after drop-shippers' Lambo photos. "You have to know… which category do I fit in and can I go up-market easily." [Jun18 0:25:06–0:27:17] → [Category perception](category-perception.md)

## How to use it
1. **Confirm the buyer is exclusivity-driven** (not just price-insensitive). Ask whether they "want things to be hard in other areas of their life". [Jun18 0:11:31]
2. **Strip mass-market prompts:** bundles, % off, countdowns, "only N left", "TikTok made me buy it", Amazon-style icon PDPs.
3. **Choose the access mechanic:** a price step up, time windows ("opens once every six months"), or an application ("see if you qualify… not just because you can pay… the right mindset"). [Jun18 0:18:44–0:20:52]
4. **Pick the anchor object:** something far more expensive the buyer already owns or browses (a jet for a car). [Jun18 0:30:27]
5. **Write at the buyer's level:** elevated verbs and nouns; status by achievement ("for those who have earned it") rather than by display. [Jun18 0:15:40; Jul16 0:46:11]
6. **Design for calm:** white space, one focal object, few elements; props that keep the brand elevated (a wooden play gym, not a basketball). [Roast1 0:51:22; Roast2 0:46:56–0:47:34]
7. **Qualify by situation or personality, not only price:** "Some people are fun and can't afford you… we want somebody fun who can afford what you're providing." [Jun18 0:30:27–0:34:41]

**Visual cues:** white space, slow cuts, natural light, one product, subtle (not loud) wealth signals, no stickers, badges or price slashes, no icon overlays on product shots. Creator wardrobe and setting at the buyer's level.

## Deploy in a static
- Headline in white space, ≤ 3 lines, elevated vocabulary; product as the single focal point.
- No discount chip, no "selling out" sticker, no "TikTok made me buy it".
- Optional proof: one press logo or a longevity line, kept small.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- **True luxury:** no offer, minimal copy, generous white space, quiet palette, access language ("by appointment", "apply", "waitlist"), comparisons to bigger purchases, brand longevity cues (years, heritage).
- **Fake luxury:** premium photography plus a discount badge, bundles or countdowns; the brand is training buyers to wait for sales.
- Tag: `pos-luxury` (true) or `pos-luxury-leaky` (premium look + mass-market prompts).

## Rules of thumb
- **IF** luxury or exclusive **THEN** no bundles, no discounts, raise price, restrict access. [Jun18 0:11:31–0:12:31; 0:19:49]
- **IF** going up-market **THEN** show the product next to something far more expensive. [Jun18 0:30:27]
- **DON'T** use "limited spaces" to recruit premium buyers. **DO** use time windows or qualification. [Jun18 0:18:44–0:20:52]
- **IF** premium buyer **THEN** white space, few elements, elevated language. [Roast1 0:51:50–0:53:28]
- **IF** elevated brand **THEN** keep PDP images clean (no USP icons). [Jul9 0:12:43–0:13:23]
- **IF** Gen Z men's luxury **THEN** quiet respect, subtle wealth cues. [Jun4 0:05:04–0:07:10]
- **DO** match language to the buyer's education, not to how simple the product is. [Roast1 0:53:28]

## Common mistakes
- Premium visuals with a discount sticker or bundle ladder ("everyone, including some big names" told the athleisure brand to bundle). [Jun18 0:10:29]
- Raising prices without checking which category buyers file the brand under. [Jun18 0:26:10–0:27:17]
- Loud wealth (Lambo-era flexing) for a Gen Z buyer. [Jun4 0:05:04–0:07:10]
- Qualifying only on money when the real filter is personality or situation. [Jun18 0:30:27–0:34:41]

## Contradictions and caveats
- **Discounts this year.** On [July 9](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:00:57–0:01:14] Sarah says that because of tariffs and the economy, "do what you got to do" with discounts and bundles. That is a mass-market, short-term exception. She did not retract the luxury rule; keep "no discounts" for genuinely luxury positioning and flag any exception to the brand.
- **Quiet vs prompt-heavy men's jewelry.** June 4 says keep luxury signals subtle ("quiet respect"), yet on July 16 she allows that the prompt-heavy Jaxxon page may work because "for our dudes… we prompt… urgency, scarcity… offers" [Jul16 0:48:22–0:51:30]. Wiki reading: Jaxxon is mid-market (Cuban links, $199) rather than true luxury; test before assuming either.
- Maserati/Lamborghini histories are Sarah's recollection, not verified.
