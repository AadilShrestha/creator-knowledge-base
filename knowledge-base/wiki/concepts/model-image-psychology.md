# Model Image Psychology (Faces, Eyebrows, Hair, Bodies, Posture)

> **"The image gets processed before everything else."** ([Roast My Ads #3](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:32:37]). The model's face, hair, body and posture are the ad's first message, so cast and art-direct them as deliberately as you write the headline: "One woman's confident face will just crush and another woman's confident face will be like nobody paid attention at all." [0:06:17]

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** casting UGC creators or picking stock/AI models; writing creator briefs (expression, posture, wardrobe); tearing down a competitor static where the model is the hero; diagnosing why the same copy works on one image and not another.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the ad's core emotion/message + target self → a casting spec (expression, brows, lids, smile type, hair colour, body type, posture) + a check that face, body, hair and headline all say the same thing.
- **Rules:**
  - DO read the face first: **confident** = both eyebrows centred and slightly pulled in, lowered lids or a squint, a **smirk, not a smile**. **Seductive** = one eyebrow higher than the other. "They're two totally different faces." [Roast #3 0:06:28–0:08:23]
  - DO make every visual signal repeat the core message (expression + hair + posture + headline = "confident, ditch what doesn't serve you"). [Roast #3 0:12:36]
  - DO match the overlay text to the model's facial expression and body language: "The brain… will compare them: do these match?" [Roast #2 0:06:23–0:06:50]
  - DO use **normal, un-airbrushed bodies** for millennial women ("if she's got cellulite please just show me a real human"). [Roast #3 0:10:32–0:11:33]
  - DO use chest-out, open posture to signal confidence (elbows on the wall, not the whole body). [Roast #3 0:11:33–0:12:36]
  - DO treat hair colour as a variable: a redhead's confidence "is going to look different because of what we assume about people with red hair". [Roast #3 0:05:14–0:06:17]
  - DO let one expressive face carry the message instead of extra copy ("we don't actually have to say a whole lot"). [L3 03:14–03:30]
  - DON'T open with skin and no context for women's products in the US: viewers assume "this could be for something a little weird" and scroll. Message first, model second. [Roast #3 0:20:31–0:21:08]
  - DON'T treat Sarah's hair-colour patterns as laws. They're her account observations (and a member saw the opposite for blondes). [Roast #3 0:06:28; 0:09:29–0:10:32]

## Definition
The practice of choosing and directing the people in an ad by what their face, hair, body and posture communicate *before* any text is read. Sarah: "Body language is freaking huge… body language all starts in the face." ([Roast #3](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:07:21])

## Why it matters
- The brain scans the image first; text only confirms or contradicts it ([Roast #3](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:32:37–0:33:42]).
- Same headline, different model → different result: Net Positive's "Ditch Polyester" copy underperformed on other "same vibe" models that weren't redheads [0:05:14].
- Humans are "master pattern recognizers when it comes to faces and voices" ([May 14](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-14-live-with-sarah.md) [0:26:17]), which is also why Sarah warns against AI UGC where trust matters.
- A mismatch between face and text creates friction; a match lets the viewer "just keep scrolling" down the page ([Roast #2](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-25-roast-my-ads-2.md) [0:06:23]).

## How to use it
1. Name the one emotion the ad sells (see [one core emotion per ad](one-core-emotion-per-ad.md)).
2. Translate it into a face spec: brows, lids, mouth (smile vs smirk), gaze.
3. Add body: posture (open/chest-out = confidence), body type (real, un-airbrushed for millennial women), wardrobe.
4. Choose hair colour deliberately and test it as a variable (redhead vs others).
5. Write the overlay line to describe what the face is doing.
6. In video, put the message in frame 1 and bring the model in second if the model alone is ambiguous.
7. When a model wins, find more like her (or generate similar AI models) before changing the copy.

### Face cheat sheet (from Roast #3 [0:06:55–0:08:23])
| Read | Eyebrows | Eyes / lids | Mouth | Says |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Confident | Both centred, slightly pulled in | Lowered lids / squint ("filtering the light") | Subtle smirk | "I just don't care about what people think about me" |
| Seductive | One higher than the other | Lowered lids | Varies | Allure (what works for blondes in Sarah's accounts) |
| Proud (LEGENDS) | — | — | — | "Sets its own standards": the face carries the line ([L3](../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/03-emotion-clarity-urgency.md) [03:14–03:30]) |

## Deploy in a UGC script / creator brief
| Brief field | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Expression | Name brows, lids, mouth | "Confident: brows level and slightly drawn in, soft squint, smirk not grin." (illustrative, not from course) |
| Posture | Open, chest out, lean on a surface | "Lean on the wall with your elbows only." (illustrative, from Roast #3 [0:11:33]) |
| Body | Real, unretouched | "No smoothing filters; skin folds and texture stay." (illustrative, from Roast #3 [0:10:32]) |
| Opening frame | Message before skin (women's intimate/apparel) | Headline card on the grey background, then the model walks in (Roast #3 [0:20:01]) |
| Overlay | Describe the face | If she smirks, the line is defiant ("Ditch Polyester."), not sweet |

**Visual cues:** brow symmetry, lid height, smirk vs smile, chest-out posture, real skin, distinctive hair colour (red, bright blonde, curls) as a salience lever.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- What does the face say before you read anything? Does the headline say the same?
- Confident vs seductive brows; smirk vs smile; posture open or closed.
- Airbrushed vs real bodies; distinctive hair colour.
- In video: does the model appear before the message with no context?

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** check eyebrows first when choosing a "confident" face. [Roast #3 0:06:55]
- **IF** a model image wins **THEN** clone the look (same hair colour, expression) before changing copy. [Roast #3 0:08:23]
- **DO** use real bodies for millennial women. [Roast #3 0:10:32]
- **IF** the product shows skin (women's underwear, US) **THEN** lead with the message. [Roast #3 0:20:31]

## Common mistakes
- Swapping in "same vibe" models without checking brows, hair and posture.
- A smiling, sweet face under a defiant headline (mismatch).
- Over-retouching skin for an audience that wants real bodies.

## Contradictions / caveats
- **Blondes:** Sarah finds confident blondes "don't necessarily do well" (seductive works instead) [Roast #3 0:06:28]. Faraz Khan reports redheads *and bright blondes* "always performed super well" in his curly-hair video ads [0:09:29–0:10:32]. Treat hair colour as a test variable, not a rule.
