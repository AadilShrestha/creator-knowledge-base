# Brand Enemy (Pick an Enemy)

> **"This is something that's really powerful for every brand in here: pick an enemy… and go after that enemy pretty hard. For Sarah, my enemy is logic."** The enemy can be loud (Liquid Death vs "big water") or subtle (Apple vs enterprise-only computing) ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:23:13–0:27:42]).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** positioning a brand in a crowded category; writing call-out hooks ("if you hate X, this is for you"); retraining an account after a pivot; tearing down a competitor with a strong "us vs them" voice.
- **Inputs → outputs:** category norms + what the buyer resents → one enemy sentence, a loud version and a subtle version, plus the emotion the enemy frees them from (e.g. relief).
- **Rules:**
  - DO pick **one** enemy and make it "the core piece of your marketing" ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:25:23]).
  - DO name who the brand is **not** for: the BPE finds "what identity/enemy to go after to show people who you are not" ([Live, May 7](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:43:58–0:45:10]).
  - DO choose the volume: loud ("we're not for you, we're hardcore") or "real mellow" ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:25:50–0:27:16]).
  - DO state the enemy "so definitively" and never deviate. That's how Liquid Death took share ([Live, May 7](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:44:14–0:45:10]).
  - DO pair the enemy with the emotion you solve: for introverts vs hustle-culture networking, "emotionally what you're actually solving is… relief" ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:24:20–0:25:23]).
  - DON'T insult people. Sarah doesn't say "anyone who uses logic is stupid"; she says "psychology is how humans work" [0:27:16–0:27:42]. For switching, "question the trend", don't attack it (avoid "scammy" competitor attacks) ([Live, June 18](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [1:04:29–1:05:31]).

## Definition
A brand enemy is the belief, behaviour, institution or category default the brand stands against. It gives the core message a direction and lets buyers self-select into an in-group. Sarah lists enemy work under what she calls a fluency mechanism ("intense work on enemy… what we call basically a fluency mechanism, Liquid Death is one of them") ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:25:50]).

| Volume | Example | How it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loud | Liquid Death vs corporate "big water" / plastic bottles | Skull and crossbones; "murder your thirst"; "anybody who wants general water, we're not for you" | [Live, May 7](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:44:14; 0:50:37]; [Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:25:50–0:26:31] |
| Subtle | Apple vs computers only for enterprises ("we don't think you should have to be a billionaire to have a computer") | Never stated, but "they put it everywhere" | [Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:26:31–0:27:16] |
| Subtle | Sarah vs logic / guessing | "If you know that science is stronger than guessing, you're gonna want to come see this" | [0:27:16–0:27:42] |

## Why it matters
- "Most of the time brands want to be… everything to everyone, and this is how we just stagnate" ([Live, May 7](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:44:14]).
- An enemy filters: "If you hate XYZ, this is for you" pulls the right people and teaches the algorithm who to find ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:25:23–0:25:50]; see [algorithm as library](algorithm-as-library.md)).
- Going the opposite way of competitors is how Liquid Death stands out: "if Liquid Death is any indicator… we actually go crazy wild the opposite direction" ([Live, June 11](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md) [0:42:22]).

## How to use it
1. List the category's defaults and what buyers resent about them (reviews, Reddit).
2. Pick one enemy (a belief, a habit, a category default, a culture), not a named competitor.
3. Name the emotion the enemy causes and the emotion you deliver (anxiety → relief).
4. Write a loud line and a subtle line. Test both.
5. Add a call-out hook ("If you hate X…", "X people, we're calling you out").
6. Repeat it across every ad and the brand line ([core brand emotion](core-brand-emotion.md)).

**Visual cues:** visible symbols of the enemy (plastic bottle, LinkedIn-style networking event) shown dull or desaturated next to your product (see [color psychology](color-psychology.md)).

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- "Not for everyone", "we're not for you if…", mocking a category default, logos/visuals that invert the category look.
- Ask: what does this brand stand *against*? If you can't answer, there's an opening.
- Tag: `concept-brand-enemy` with `enemy-loud` / `enemy-subtle` and the enemy named.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** pick one enemy; loud or subtle both work [Live, May 21, 0:25:50–0:27:42].
- **DO** make the enemy a belief or default, not a person.
- **IF** your audience is sensitive **THEN** use the subtle version.
- **IF** you're retraining an account after a pivot **THEN** an enemy call-out is a fast way to change who the algorithm finds [0:20:40–0:25:50].

## Common mistakes
- Picking too many enemies, which blurs the brand.
- Insulting the buyer (or people they love) instead of a belief.
- Naming a competitor brand directly in ads ("scammy" territory).
