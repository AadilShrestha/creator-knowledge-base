# Triggering Events vs Motivators
> Triggering events are "what you experience in your life at distinct moments" ("I just moved", "I just bought a puppy") and are "pretty solid"; emotional motivators "wax and wane, they're very wavy" ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:39:40–0:40:44]).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing trigger-stage ads (the T in TEEP); generating hook/angle lists; deciding whether an ad talks to a life event or a mood; budgeting for trigger ads vs other stages; tagging competitor ads by journey stage.
- **Inputs → outputs:** product + buyer research → a list of concrete triggering events (life moments that start a search), each with one hook; a separate list of motivators (temporary states) to use as emotional colour; a stage tag per ad.
- **Rules:**
  - DO separate **triggering events** (stable life events: moved, wedding, puppy) from **motivators** (temporary moods: tired today, healing). [May21 0:39:40–0:40:44]
  - DO write trigger ads that talk *only* to the event: "You want to lose 30 pounds? Here's a product for you." [May21 0:06:19–0:06:55]
  - DON'T run only trigger and purchase ads; most brands stop there. The middle stages (exploration, evaluation) are "where we can get super creative". [May21 0:06:19–0:07:33]
  - IF budget is small THEN favour exploration, evaluation and purchase ads over trigger ads: triggers are endless and "great for big budgets". [Jun11 0:46:34–0:47:37]
  - DO make trigger hooks hyper-specific (a moment only the buyer would recognise). [Jun11 0:47:37; Jul9 0:38:55–0:41:44]
  - IF trigger-stage ads convert most THEN the market is low-educated: nurture more. [Jun4 0:21:52–0:22:56]
- **See also:** [TEEP journey](../frameworks/teep-journey.md) (expected) · [Specificity](heuristics/specificity.md) · [Identity stacking](identity-stacking.md) · [Temporal discounting](heuristics/temporal-discounting.md) · [Creative testing system](../frameworks/creative-testing-system.md) (expected)

## Definition
- **Triggering event:** a distinct real-world moment after which "this event happened and now I feel like I need to go do something". Examples Sarah gives: "I just moved", "I have a wedding coming up and I want to lose 30 pounds", "I just bought a puppy" [May21 0:39:40–0:40:11]; "you bought a pet, you sold your house, you gained 30 pounds" ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:11:24]).
- **Motivator:** a temporary emotional state: "I didn't drink enough coffee this morning" or "I'm finally starting to heal from some of my childhood traumas… and I'm motivated to continue that journey". [May21 0:40:11–0:40:44]
- In TEEP, the trigger is the entry stage and is one-way: "once the trigger phase happens… now you're kind of in the ecosystem" ([Roast My Ads #3](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-23-roast-my-ads-3.md) [0:13:39–0:14:42]).

## Why it matters
- **Triggers are where angles come from.** "A mom who wants to lose 30 pounds", "a family that just bought a house", "a marketer struggling with ads". [Jun11 0:45:32]
- **Triggers are endless.** "I'm getting to the point where it's uncomfortable to wear a seat belt… you could never ever run enough ads to hit them all." [Jun11 0:46:34–0:47:37]
- **Triggers fuse identities.** Sarah's kids (trigger) connected her psychology and mother identities, which made a $7 book an instant buy. [May21 0:45:26–0:46:46] → [Identity stacking](identity-stacking.md)
- **Purchase needs pressure, not just a trigger.** Purchase "usually doesn't happen until the emotional pressure that someone feels gets strong enough". [Jun4 0:12:28]

## How to use it
1. **Mine triggers** from reviews ("I bought this after…"), Reddit, surveys: list life events, not feelings.
2. **Write one trigger ad per event**, speaking only to that event, in the buyer's words.
3. **Make it specific:** the seat belt, the drain full of hair, the wedding in six weeks.
4. **Layer a motivator** as tone only (tired, overwhelmed), never as the whole angle; it fades.
5. **Place by budget:** big budget → trigger campaigns too; small budget → exploration/evaluation/purchase first. [Jun11 0:46:34]
6. **Read the stage:** if trigger ads win, nurture; if evaluation ads win, the market is educated, widen the top. [Jun4 0:21:52–0:22:56]

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Opens on a life event ("just moved", "new puppy", "postpartum", "diagnosed") → trigger stage.
- Opens on a mood ("tired?", "stressed?") → motivator; likely to fatigue faster.
- Tag: `teep-trigger` (+ `trigger:<event>`) or `motivator:<state>`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** distinguish stable events from wavy motivators. [May21 0:39:40–0:40:44]
- **DON'T** stop at trigger + purchase ads. [May21 0:06:19]
- **IF** small budget **THEN** run exploration, evaluation and purchase before trigger ads. [Jun11 0:46:34–0:47:37]
- **IF** trigger ads convert most **THEN** nurture more. [Jun4 0:22:56]
- **DO** make each trigger hook as specific as possible. [Jun11 0:47:37]

## Common mistakes
- Treating a mood as a trigger and building a whole angle on it.
- Trying to cover every trigger on a small budget.
- Generic "lose weight" triggers that let Meta find the cheapest, not the right, buyer ([Live, July 9](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:38:55–0:39:38]).
