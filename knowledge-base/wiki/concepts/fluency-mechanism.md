# Fluency Mechanism (Mascots and Recurring Characters)

> **"Fluency mechanisms for brands basically means I have someone to attach to something that makes it easy for me to recall… the brand… If Dr. Riley hits, please keep using Dr. Riley."** ([Live, May 14](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-14-live-with-sarah.md) [0:48:35–0:48:52]). A mascot or recurring character is "a character at the top of the brand that helps you remember the brand better" ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:19:46]).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a story or character ad wins and you're planning the series; the category is low-interest or future-focused (insurance, supplements); a humor ad spiked and died because people attached to the joke, not the brand; tearing down a competitor with a recurring face, mascot or narrator.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a winning character/story/joke, or a brand with no recognisable asset → a recurring character spec (name, look, voice, humor type, what it always says/does) and a series plan across formats.
- **Rules:**
  - DO keep a winning character: "If Dr. Riley hits, please keep using Dr. Riley" and extend into an advertorial, because people "want to see what happens to Dr. Riley" ([Live, May 14](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-14-live-with-sarah.md) [0:48:12–0:48:52]).
  - DO test a mascot in low-interest categories; for insurance Sarah suggests a **non-animal, non-human** one ("a paper clip… a car tire") that talks about what happens without insurance ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:19:46–0:21:52]).
  - DO make the **product** the character when humor works but doesn't attach: cartoon gelato that's "snarky… kind of pissy… kind of mean" (a "fluency method") [0:33:26].
  - DO pick a character nobody in your niche uses, so you "stand out just slightly" (Sarah's Dex) ([Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:34:35–0:35:37]).
  - DO give the character a fixed personality (Dex: British, deadpan) and keep it consistent [0:34:35].
  - DON'T change the character or line once it's earning recall. Repetition is the mechanism ([Live, May 7](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:48:09–0:51:00]).

## Definition
Sarah's term for any recurring brand asset that makes the brand easy to recognise and recall, so each new ad gets processed faster. It's [processing fluency](heuristics/processing-fluency.md) applied at brand level: "I want people to be able to speak the language of Tether really easily… when we see X we know it's Tether" ([Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:34:35–0:35:05]).

## Why it matters
- Recall: the character "helps you remember the brand better" ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:19:46]).
- Attachment: humor ads die fast "unless the whole brand stays humorous"; a character makes people attach to "the product and the humor", not just to memes [0:31:21–0:33:26].
- Continuity across formats: a winning story character can carry ads, advertorials and landing pages ("these stories blend super well throughout the entire ecosystem") ([Live, May 14](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-14-live-with-sarah.md) [0:48:12–0:48:35]).
- Differentiation: few brands in a niche use one (Dex) ([Guest Session 01](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) [0:35:05–0:35:37]).

## How to use it
1. Check whether a winner already has a character (narrator, creator persona, joke voice). If so, name it and keep it.
2. If not, choose a form: human persona, mascot, personified product. For insurance-like categories try a non-human, non-animal object.
3. Fix personality and humor type (one type; see [humor in ads](humor-in-ads.md)).
4. Give it one recurring job (predicts what goes wrong, tests the claim, reacts snarkily).
5. Test ~9 ads with it ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:18:45–0:19:46]); if it hits, extend into advertorials and organic.

**Visual cues:** consistent design, colour and voice; googly or evil eyes on products ([anthropomorphism](heuristics/anthropomorphism.md)); a named lower-third for human characters.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- The same face, voice, mascot or narrator across multiple ads; a series format ("Paige tests…").
- A personified product or object.
- Tag: `concept-fluency-mechanism` with type (`mascot`, `persona`, `narrator`, `personified-product`, `repeated-line`).

## Rules of thumb
- **IF** a story character wins **THEN** keep the character and build an advertorial ([Live, May 14](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-14-live-with-sarah.md) [0:48:12]).
- **IF** low-interest or future-focused category **THEN** test a mascot ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:19:46]).
- **IF** a humor ad spikes and dies **THEN** turn the product into the character [0:33:26].
- **DO** keep it consistent; recall comes from repetition.

## Common mistakes
- Swapping characters every batch.
- A character with no job (decorative mascot).
- Animal mascots in a category where everyone already uses one (Sarah's preference for non-human, non-animal objects in insurance).
