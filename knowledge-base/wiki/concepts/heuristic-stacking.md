# Heuristic Stacking

> Layer **two or three** mental shortcuts in one ad. "Your customer's brain isn't running one shortcut at a time. It's stacking them, fast. So should your ads." (e-book p.16)

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** composing any new script or static, remixing a competitor ad (keep their stack, swap in our proof), or QA-ing a draft that relies on a single lever.
- **Inputs → outputs:** product facts (proof, guarantee, tech terms, reviews, steps, usage numbers) → a stack plan (base + emotion + trust + optional attention/motion) mapped to ad elements (headline, image, badge, callouts, CTA).
- **Rules:**
  - DO use 2–3 heuristics per ad. "Using only one shortcut" is a listed mistake.
  - DO start from [processing fluency](heuristics/processing-fluency.md). Stacking must not add clutter.
  - DO assign each heuristic to a different element (headline = affect, badge = social proof, callout = authority, footer line = goal gradient).
  - DON'T shout or oversell. "The ad isn't shouting. It's whispering all the right things to the subconscious."
  - IF your stack's trust layer is a doctor quote and the audience distrusts doctors THEN swap in authority *bias* (terms, guarantee).
- **See also:** [Heuristics hub](heuristics.md), [Stacking Psychology for Growth](../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/05-stacking-psychology-for-growth.md), [Prompt Library](../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/README.md) (each prompt pairs two levers).

## Definition
Combining several heuristics in a single ad so their effects compound: "an exponential effect. These shouldn't be used in standalone ways. These really should be used in tandem with each other combined into one particular ad" ([L4 06:59]–[07:14]).

## Why it works
- "They're more persuasive if you layer them on top of each other." Together they "diminish the amount of brain processing they have to do to be able to actually take that ad, understand what you're trying to say and decide that it is for them" ([L5 07:12]–[07:31]).
- "Capitalize on the amount of mental shortcuts that already exist in the brain and you can get to your goal faster" ([L4 07:14]).
- E-book: "Stack shortcuts = faster, easier decisions. ✅"

## How to stack (steps)
1. **Base:** processing fluency. Clean layout, one message, no bullets ([L5 01:00]).
2. **Emotion:** the [affect heuristic](heuristics/affect-heuristic.md) in the headline or image (a scene, or one feeling word) ([L5 01:26], [03:18]).
3. **Trust:** [authority bias](heuristics/authority-bias.md) (technical term, guarantee) or [social proof](heuristics/social-proof.md) ("Top Seller") ([L5 02:06], [04:10], [06:26]).
4. **Optional attention/motion layer:** [salience](heuristics/salience-bias.md) (contrast) or [goal gradient](heuristics/goal-gradient-effect.md) (steps) ([L5 04:38], [05:42]).
5. **Check:** at least 2–3 heuristics, and it still reads in 3 seconds? (e-book p.11, p.17)

Final CTA of the course: next campaign, "especially if you're going to be using statics... just pick two or three and layer them on top of each other," using the worksheet as a cheat sheet ([L5 08:06]–[08:31]).

| Fitness shorts (apparel) | Static | "For those who live in fitness clothing" | **Fluency** (plain grey, short callouts) + **Social proof** ("Top Seller") + **Goal gradient** ("Buy, wear, achieve.") | — | Test "Top Seller" vs "Best Seller" | Goal gradient fits almost any ad | [L5 05:26]–[07:02] |

## Stack templates (agent shorthand)
*Derived from the three course examples. The names are ours, not Sarah's.*

| Template | Layers | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| "Calm Expert" (Pela) | fluency + affect (feeling word) + authority bias (term + guarantee) | Premium or technical products, sustainability claims |
| "Scene + Seal" (Firelight Flask) | affect (scene image + line) + authority (guarantee) + salience (contrast) | Lifestyle and gift products |
| "Simple Path" (fitness shorts) | fluency + social proof (Top Seller) + goal gradient (3-step line) | Apparel and acquisition ads |

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** "layer at least two or three." (e-book p.17)
- **DO** use the lesser-known heuristics too. They're how you get "a whole lot more success... keeping our costs low and reaching more people" ([L5 00:21]–[00:35]).
- **DON'T** convince: "I don't think anybody should be out here convincing anybody to do things" ([L5 08:35]).
