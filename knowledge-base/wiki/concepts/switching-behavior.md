# Switching Behavior
> "If you really want to do switching behavior in marketing, the only thing that you can do is get them to **question their attachment** to it" — benefit lists only pull people already in pain; switchers move when their current choice or identity is put in doubt ([Live, June 18](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-18-live-with-sarah.md) [1:05:31–1:06:33]).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** the product must win buyers from a competitor, a category leader or a trend (matcha → turmeric, Lactaid → Milky); writing hooks for brand-loyal markets; or the reverse: positioning a product as the **last** switch a serial switcher will make.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the incumbent choice (brand, category, trend), why buyers are attached (identity, habit, belief), our difference → "question the attachment" hooks, identity questions, a switcher testimonial angle, a story structure (SCQA) and an A/B landing-page plan.
- **Rules:**
  - DO make people **question their current choice or trend**, not list benefits: "Is your matcha really doing what you think it is?" [Jun18 1:03:25–1:04:29]
  - DO question the **identity** attached to the choice: "Are you actually a matcha girl, or do you want to be the type that does A, B, C?" Drinks "almost become a part of your identity". [Jun18 1:03:25–1:06:33]
  - DON'T attack the incumbent ("matcha sucks", "the dusty tea is a scam"); question the trend ("the girlies aren't doing matcha anymore"). [Jun18 1:04:29–1:05:31]
  - DO A/B test a separate switching landing page so you "don't cut off the legs of the business". [Jun18 1:04:29]
  - DO use **SCQA** for switching stories: the mid-story question reframes the cause ("are you sure it's your shampoo?"). [May14 0:08:16–0:09:00]
  - IF the market is brand-loyal (Sarah: Australia) THEN "you got to show me why I should switch". [May21 0:15:36–0:15:46]
  - IF selling to serial switchers THEN promise the end of switching ("The last skincare switch you'll ever make"). [Jun4 0:47:21–0:49:22; May7 0:17:51–0:20:05]
- **See also:** [Cognitive dissonance](heuristics/cognitive-dissonance.md) · [Reframing](heuristics/reframing.md) · [Counterintuitive insight](heuristics/counterintuitive-insight.md) · [Bandwagon effect](heuristics/bandwagon-effect.md) · [Sunk cost fallacy](heuristics/sunk-cost-fallacy.md) · [Identity stacking](identity-stacking.md) · [Brand enemy](brand-enemy.md) · [Story frameworks for ads](../frameworks/story-frameworks-for-ads.md) (expected)

## Definition
Getting a buyer to leave a current product, brand, category or trend for yours. The underlying bias is **status quo bias** (preferring things as they are), which Sarah names in Heuristics 101 but never teaches as its own lesson ([Stacking Psychology for Growth](../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/05-stacking-psychology-for-growth.md) [07:17–07:31]: "There's the Ikea effect. We've got status quo bias, paradox of choice"). Her operational answer in the lives: attachment is identity and belief, so switching copy must create doubt about the current choice, then offer a better identity fit.

## Why it matters
- **Benefits recruit the already-converted.** A golden-milk tea's benefit list ("reduces inflammation, joint pain") pulls people already experiencing the problem, typically older buyers. To win Gen Z matcha drinkers you have to "drill down into belief". [Jun18 1:01:19–1:04:29]
- **Beliefs change when people second-guess them.** "The easiest way to change the subconscious is to cause yourself to second-guess things, and that's what we do in marketing all the time." ([Live, July 9](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:54:54])
- **Loyalty varies by market.** US buyers compare price and quality; Australian buyers are "very brand loyal… you got to show me why I should switch". [May21 0:15:13–0:15:46]
- **Serial switchers carry sunk cost.** Maelove's buyers had "40 bottles on the counter" they wouldn't throw away; the promise to stop switching "crushed and had lots of longevity". [May7 0:17:51–0:20:05]

## How to use it
1. **Name the attachment:** what do they use now, and what does it say about them (identity), what do they believe it does (belief)?
2. **Write the doubt line:** "Is your [current choice] really doing what you think it is?" [Jun18 1:03:25]
3. **Write the identity line:** "Are you actually a [current-choice] person, or…?" [Jun18 1:03:25–1:04:29]
4. **Optional social cue:** frame the trend as moving ("Matcha is out, turmeric is in"; "10,000 chai lovers are switching"). [Jun18 1:04:29; Jul9 0:25:12]
5. **Story:** SCQA (situation → complication → the question that reframes → answer). [May14 0:08:16]
6. **Proof from switchers:** a long-time user of the leader says yours is better ("I have used Lactaid for years. Milky is better."). [Roast3 0:51:00]
7. **Landing page:** A/B a switching variant, keep the core page for current buyers. [Jun18 1:04:29]

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Doubt questions about a rival or trend, "I quit X" stories, "used X for years, this is better", "the last Y you'll ever buy", "X is out, Y is in".
- Aggressive "X is a scam" attacks (MUD\WTR-style) → note the backlash risk.
- Tag: `pos-switching` plus `switch-doubt`, `switch-identity`, `switch-testimonial`, `switch-last-switch`, `switch-attack`.

## Rules of thumb
- **IF** you want a new audience to switch **THEN** question their attachment and identity; don't list benefits. [Jun18 1:03:25–1:05:31]
- **DON'T** call the competitor a scam. **DO** question the trend. [Jun18 1:04:29–1:05:31]
- **DO** A/B a switching landing page instead of replacing the current one. [Jun18 1:04:29]
- **IF** the market is brand-loyal **THEN** give an explicit reason to switch. [May21 0:15:36]
- **DO** use SCQA when the story needs a reframing question mid-way. [May14 0:08:16]
- **IF** the buyer is a serial switcher **THEN** sell the end of switching. [Jun4 0:47:21]

## Common mistakes
- Leading with benefits to people who aren't yet in pain.
- Attacking the thing the buyer identifies with, which triggers defence instead of doubt.
- Rewriting the whole site for the new audience and losing the current one.
