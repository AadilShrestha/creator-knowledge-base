# Blended Emotions (Merging Two Winning Emotions in One Ad)

> Test each emotion on its own first; when two win separately, **"squish them into one ad"**: one emotion in the headline, the other in the subhead. Sarah's Maelove blend, "The last skincare switch you'll ever make / No more guessing. That's our promise.", **"ran for eight months straight"** ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:46:18–0:49:22]).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you (or a competitor) already have two separately proven emotional messages and need a longer-lived evergreen ad; tearing down a long-running competitor static that seems to say two things at once.
- **Inputs → outputs:** two winning ads, each tagged with its emotion (e.g. achievement, security) → one headline (emotion A) + one subhead (emotion B) + the supporting proof, tested as a new campaign against the parents.
- **Rules:**
  - DO split emotions first: one campaign, one ad set per emotion, ~9 ads each ("nine different ads that just say esteem in slightly different ways") ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:14:34–0:15:38; 0:46:18–0:47:21]).
  - DO blend only **after** both emotions win on their own: "when esteem and belonging both crush… squish them into one ad" and run it as a new blended-emotion campaign [0:46:18–0:47:21].
  - DO blend by **taking the headline from one winner and the subhead from the other**: "I just took the headline and the subhead and squished it" [0:48:21].
  - DO know where each emotion sits in the ad and don't over-index on one [0:14:34].
  - DON'T start with blends. Untested blends make it impossible to know which emotion did the work (wiki reading of [0:14:34–0:15:38]).
  - DON'T blend two emotions inside one headline line, or add a third competing headline. That's the FireClub mistake ([Roast My Ads #1](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) [0:41:46]).
- **See also:** [One core emotion per ad](one-core-emotion-per-ad.md) · [Emotion specificity](emotion-specificity.md) · [Marketing to Mindstates](../people/will-leach.md) · [Creative testing system](../frameworks/creative-testing-system.md) · [Iteration vs variation](iteration-vs-variation.md)

## Definition
A blended-emotion ad carries two purchase emotions that have each been proven in separate tests, arranged so the brain still reads one clear message: the headline carries one emotion, the subhead (or the guarantee/offer line) carries the second. "More blended emotion actually does really, really well", but only once you know where each emotion sits ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:14:34]).

### Blended emotions vs one core emotion (resolving the apparent contradiction)
On [May 28](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) Sarah cut FireClub's ad to **one core emotion** ("one headline, one CTA… raise dopamine one time", [0:41:46–0:42:48]). On [June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) she praises a two-emotion blend. They don't conflict (wiki synthesis):

| | FireClub (fails) | Maelove blend (wins) |
|---|---|---|
| Emotions tested first? | No, both were new | Yes, each won alone |
| Structure | Two competing *headlines* of equal weight | One headline + one subordinate subhead |
| Do the two emotions point the same way? | No ("two totally different emotional reasons") | Yes: the switch (achievement) is made safe by the promise (security) |
| Read in 0.2 s? | No | Yes, one message: "stop switching, it's guaranteed" |

Rule: an ad can carry two *proven, complementary* emotions in a headline/subhead hierarchy. It can't carry two *untested, competing* emotional headlines.

## Why it matters
- Each emotion alone fatigues; a blend can reach both emotional sets at once and last much longer (8 months for Maelove) [0:47:21–0:49:22].
- Guarantees read as **security**: "anytime you put guarantees anywhere in your ad, that speaks to security" [0:48:21]. So a subhead promise is the easiest second emotion to bolt onto an achievement or transformation headline.
- Most industries have about three emotions plus one brand-specific outlier ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:13:31–0:14:34]), so the blend candidates are a short list.

## How to use it
1. Map reviews to the nine *Marketing to Mindstates* emotions with ChatGPT and keep the top 3 ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:36:36–0:37:39]). See [emotion specificity](emotion-specificity.md).
2. Write ~9 ads per emotion (3 nuances × 3), one ad set per emotion, and run them [0:14:34–0:15:38].
3. Identify two emotions that each win.
4. Take winner A's headline and winner B's subhead (or prompt: "Chat, blend me, make me a headline" with both emotions named [0:50:27]).
5. Keep the supporting proof (authority quote, badges) as supporting characters ([one core emotion per ad](one-core-emotion-per-ad.md)).
6. Launch the blend as a new campaign; keep the parents running until the blend proves itself.

| Element | Carries | Maelove example |
|---|---|---|

| Subhead | Emotion B (security / guarantee) | "No more guessing. That's our promise." |
| Supporting proof | Authority, social proof | Board-certified dermatologist quote; "As seen in ELLE, InStyle, Self" |
| Visual | The product set that ends the switching | Four Maelove products |

**Visual cues:** the before state of emotion A (cluttered counter), then the single-product resolution; the guarantee as a seal or spoken line, not a second hero.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- A long-running static whose headline and subhead hit different emotions that reinforce each other (transformation + guarantee, belonging + pride).
- Ask: are both emotions doing work, or is the subhead filler? If the subhead removes the main risk of the headline's promise, it's a blend.
- Tag: `concept-blended-emotions` + both emotion labels (e.g. `emo-achievement`, `emo-security`).

## Rules of thumb
- **IF** two emotions win separately **THEN** blend them and test as a new campaign [0:46:18–0:47:21].
- **DO** treat a guarantee/promise as the security emotion [0:48:21].
- **DO** give ChatGPT the current emotion *and* the desired emotion when blending ("they feel nostalgia currently but they want freedom") [0:50:27].
- **DON'T** blend untested emotions or stack two equal headlines.

## Common mistakes
- Blending on day one and learning nothing.
- Giving both emotions equal visual weight (see [visual weight / one core emotion](one-core-emotion-per-ad.md)).
- Picking two emotions that pull opposite ways (thrill vs calm).
- Using umbrella emotions ("confidence") instead of the specific one ([emotion specificity](emotion-specificity.md)).
