# Heuristics (Mental Shortcuts) in Ads

> **"Heuristic equals mental shortcut."** These are the fast rules of thumb the subconscious uses to decide whether something deserves attention and action. Ads that press the right shortcuts "sell before your customer even realizes why." This is the hub for every heuristic page.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** tearing down any competitor ad (label which shortcuts it uses), choosing the psychological mechanism for a new script, or QA-ing a draft for a missing trust, emotion or attention layer.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a competitor ad or our draft → a heuristic label list (2–3 expected), gaps, and suggested additions using the pages below.
- **Rules:**
  - DO label every ad with the heuristics it uses, using the slugs below (`scarcity-heuristic`, `social-proof`, `authority-bias`, `affect-heuristic`, `processing-fluency`, `temporal-discounting`, `salience-bias`, `goal-gradient-effect`, `framing-effect`).
  - DO stack **2–3** per ad, with [processing fluency](heuristics/processing-fluency.md) as the base ([Heuristic stacking](heuristic-stacking.md)).
  - DO prefer the less obvious six (affect → framing). Scarcity, social proof and authority are "very expected in DTC."
  - DON'T "convince" or manipulate. "Make it easier, not sneakier."
  - DON'T write long rational arguments. Talk to the [subconscious](subconscious-vs-conscious-mind.md): emotional, visual, fast.
  - DO use the full catalog in [Every heuristic page](#every-heuristic-page-catalog) (54 pages) for labels beyond the nine. Tag slugs = file names.
  - DO treat heuristics as **secondary characters** that support one core message: "Heuristics for me are secondary characters" ([Live, May 7](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) [0:13:03–0:13:29]). Lock the headline first, then test one heuristic per variant on the winner ([Live, July 9](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:29:12]).
  - DO pick heuristics from the customer's **belief system and the season**, not a generic list ([Live, July 2](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:13:34–0:15:40]; [Live, July 9](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:51:11]). In video, put them "mostly in hooks" [0:53:44].
- **See also:** [Heuristic stacking](heuristic-stacking.md), [Subconscious vs conscious mind](subconscious-vs-conscious-mind.md), [Heuristics 101 e-book](../resources/heuristics-101-ebook.md), [Prompt Library](../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/README.md), [Valence–Intensity Diagnostic](../frameworks/valence-intensity-diagnostic.md).

## Definition
- In behavioral science, a heuristic is a mental shortcut: "a really fast way for the brain to decide, is this going to be something that I want to focus on? Or is it something that I am not interested in giving my attention to?" ([L1 00:21]–[00:31]).
- E-book: "fast rules of thumb to make decisions quick." Lesson 2 uses "bias" and "heuristic" interchangeably for these shortcuts.

## Why they matter for ads
- "The brain has one job: keep you alive." It won't debate "every ad, every product, every feature list. That would take too long. Burn too much energy." (e-book p.5)
- The three questions the brain's shortcuts answer (e-book p.5):
  1. **Does this look safe?**
  2. **Is this something people like me do?**
  3. **Will this solve my immediate need?**
- "If you understand these shortcuts, you don't need to 'convince' people. You just need to press the right button — and let their brain do the rest." (e-book p.5)
- Heuristics speed the brain through its "double check system... so you can get to the sale faster" ([L2 00:06]–[00:16]). They lower processing load and so lower costs ([L5 00:30]–[00:35]).

The lesson titles group them by job: **Emotion, Clarity, Urgency** (affect, fluency, temporal discounting) and **Hook Attention, Guide Behavior** (salience, goal gradient, framing). "Emotion," "clarity," "urgency" and "attention" are effects of these heuristics, not separate heuristics.

### Quick teardown decision table

| If the ad… | It's using |
|---|---|
| Says "only N left," "selling out," "max 2 per customer" | Scarcity |
| Shows review counts, testimonials, "Top Seller," a named crowd | Social proof |
| Shows an expert or credential, technical jargon, a guarantee | Authority bias (proof if a person is cited) |
| Opens on a scene or feeling ("He put up the tent…") | Affect heuristic |
| Has one line, one product, a clean background | Processing fluency |
| Promises results "today," "one bite," "first use" | Temporal discounting |
| Has a single high-contrast focal object or an odd visual | Salience bias |
| Shows steps, "You are here," progress | Goal gradient effect |
| Reframes numbers per use/day, or reframes the category | Framing effect |

### More teardown signals (live-session heuristics)

| If the ad… | It's using |
|---|---|
| Is the one thing in the feed or category that looks different (all-text, sticky note, black among pastels, a light switch on a bottle) | [Von Restorff effect](heuristics/von-restorff-effect.md) |
| Gives the product a face or attitude (googly eyes, evil grin, talking mascot) | [Anthropomorphism](heuristics/anthropomorphism.md) |
| Says "Most customers buy X" / "everyone's switching to…" | [Bandwagon effect](heuristics/bandwagon-effect.md) |
| Shows a 3-tier offer where one tier is obviously the deal | [Decoy effect](heuristics/decoy-effect.md) |
| Puts a pricier comparison next to the product ("the BMW of…", struck-through price) | [Anchoring effect](heuristics/anchoring-effect.md) |
| Says "we wish we could give this away for free…" or gifts something unasked | [Reciprocity](heuristics/reciprocity.md) |
| Asks the viewer to pick, build or swipe to choose | [IKEA effect](heuristics/ikea-effect.md) |
| Names the pile of half-used products / money already spent | [Sunk-cost fallacy](heuristics/sunk-cost-fallacy.md) |
| Uses a small first step ("$0 trial", "see if you qualify", checklist) | [Commitment and consistency](heuristics/commitment-and-consistency.md) |
| Opens with "I've tried everything and nothing worked…" | [Gambler's fallacy](heuristics/gamblers-fallacy.md) |
| Opens with "I've been ignoring this…" | [Ostrich effect](heuristics/ostrich-effect.md) |
| Stares into camera: "Did you ___ today?" | [Observer expectancy effect](heuristics/observer-expectancy-effect.md) |
| Uses a peer with the same problem ("I'm losing mine too") | [In-group bias](heuristics/in-group-bias.md) |
| Leans on *who* is speaking (employee, skeptic, nurse, founder) | [Messenger effect](heuristics/messenger-effect.md) |
| Has one nav item / one choice / one CTA, or overwhelms with many | [Choice overload](heuristics/choice-overload.md) |
| Is busy, has competing claims or text over images | [Cognitive load](heuristics/cognitive-load.md) (a weakness to exploit) |
| Makes a claim that clashes with a belief the viewer holds | [Cognitive dissonance](heuristics/cognitive-dissonance.md) (a weakness to exploit) |

## Every heuristic page (catalog)
Every file in `concepts/heuristics/` (54 pages), grouped by the job it does in an ad. **Source key:** H101 = taught in [Heuristics 101](../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/README.md) · PL = a [Prompt Library](../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/README.md) lever · Live = introduced in a live session (May–July 2025).

### 1. Attention: stop the scroll
| Heuristic | One-line definition | Best use in UGC / statics | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Salience bias](heuristics/salience-bias.md) | Whatever stands out gets attention | One high-contrast focal object or odd visual in the first frame | H101 |
| [Von Restorff effect](heuristics/von-restorff-effect.md) (incl. attentional bias) | The one item that differs from its surroundings gets noticed and remembered | Look the opposite of the category (colour, size, tone, format: all-text, sticky notes) | Live |
| [Pattern disruption](heuristics/pattern-disruption.md) | Breaking a predictable pattern snaps attention back | Unexpected first frame, format or line | PL |
| [Shock factor](heuristics/shock-factor.md) | A provocative or extreme jolt forces attention | Taboo or exaggerated opener, resolved fast | PL |
| [Anthropomorphism](heuristics/anthropomorphism.md) | A product given a face or attitude stops the scroll and invites attachment | Personified product or mascot skit (snarky gelato, AI cow) | Live |
| [Curiosity gap](heuristics/curiosity-gap.md) | An information gap creates an itch to close | Hook that withholds the key fact | PL |
| [Open loop](heuristics/open-loop.md) | An unfinished thought or story demands resolution | Half-headline on the creative, payoff in the copy; in-medias-res openers | PL |
| [Suspense](heuristics/suspense.md) | Tension from showing "the moment before the moment" | Delay the reveal to hold watch time | PL |

### 2. Clarity and ease: make it easy to say yes
| Heuristic | One-line definition | Best use in UGC / statics | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Processing fluency](heuristics/processing-fluency.md) | Easy to understand = trustworthy | Base layer: one message, short lines, clean frame | H101 |

### 3. Trust and proof: "is this safe, do people like me do this?"
| Heuristic | One-line definition | Best use in UGC / statics | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Social proof](heuristics/social-proof.md) | If others like it, it must be good | Typed crowd ("10,000 chai lovers"), reviews, UGC volume | H101 |
| [Bandwagon effect](heuristics/bandwagon-effect.md) | We follow what most people (like us) choose | "Most customers buy X" badge on ads and buy boxes | Live |
| [Authority bias](heuristics/authority-bias.md) | We trust perceived experts | The right *type* of expert for this audience; guarantees; one technical term | H101 |
| [Authority borrowing (halo effect)](heuristics/authority-borrowing.md) | Trust in a known source transfers to what it's associated with | Lead with a study, publication or celebrity voice; brand as the applier | PL |
| [Messenger effect](heuristics/messenger-effect.md) | Who says it matters more than what is said | Cast deliberately: skeptic, employee, peer, lived-experience "how I" | Live |
| [In-group bias](heuristics/in-group-bias.md) | We treat people like us better | A peer with the same problem delivers the line | Live |
| [Tribal belonging](heuristics/tribal-belonging.md) | People seek identity through shared language and struggle | Insider language, "for people who…" | PL |
| [Anonymity](heuristics/anonymity.md) | Hidden identity reads as raw honesty | Faceless confession, "overheard" format | PL |
| [Vulnerability](heuristics/vulnerability.md) | Showing imperfection builds trust fast | Creator admits doubt or struggle on camera | PL |
| [Reciprocity](heuristics/reciprocity.md) (incl. Ben Franklin effect) | A visible favour makes buyers want to give back | "We wish we could give this away for free, but…" + a bonus | Live |
| [Data-driven insight](heuristics/data-driven-insight.md) | A surprising stat makes people believe and act | Number as the hook, turned into a takeaway | PL |

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** layer at least two or three shortcuts (e-book p.17).
- **DON'T** overcomplicate. "Simpler wins." (e-book p.17)
- **DON'T** manipulate. "Customers aren't dumb. Make it easier, not sneakier." (e-book p.17)
- **DON'T** talk only to the conscious mind. "No long rational debates. Emotional, visual, fast." (e-book p.17)
