# Perception Management
> "**Marketing is about perception management.**" High-ticket and low-ticket products differ only in "the perception of the perceived value", so the job is to manage *how* the buyer sees the product, its price and its real use moment ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:29:31–0:30:28]).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** positioning a high-ticket or misunderstood product; choosing the "real job" angle for a category where every competitor says the same thing; writing offer, bundle, price, trial or subscription wording that an ad or landing page shows; tearing down why a competitor's price feels fair (or not).
- **Inputs → outputs:** product + price + the category's default claim + real usage behaviour (reviews, surveys) → the true usage moment to sell, a frame of reference in the buyer's units (dinners, washes, days of use), price/time framing choices, and a positioning line.
- **Rules:**
  - DO study the product's real usage behaviour before writing. Every product has "a whole behavioral set around" it. [May21 0:30:28–0:30:50; 0:31:41–0:33:05]
  - DO sell the painful real moment, not the category cliché: people "buy mattresses for the experience of waking up", not "better sleep". [May21 0:30:50–0:31:41]
  - DO give a **frame of reference** in the buyer's own units: "Buy a 36 pack, make 100 dinners", "last you 180 days, 15 washes". [Jul2 0:40:48–0:41:53; 0:55:50–0:57:05]
  - DO choose time units by the outcome the buyer wants: fast → "30 days"; high quality → "six weeks"; return window → "one month" (sounds longer). [Jul2 0:32:40–0:34:34; 0:49:11–0:50:50]
  - DO reframe costly asks as access: "You're paying upfront to get to the front of the line." [Jul9 0:33:23–0:37:31]
  - DO rename offers to what the buyer gets: "auto ship and save", not "subscription"; "$0 trial", not "free trial". [Jul2 0:53:44–0:54:43; Jul9 0:21:49–0:22:03]
  - DON'T claim the USP as the use case (low-carb pasta ≠ weight loss): it creates [cognitive dissonance](heuristics/cognitive-dissonance.md). [Jul2 0:38:47–0:40:48]
  - DO identify who buys vs who uses (mattresses: often the husband buys, the wife consumes). [May21 0:43:57–0:44:25]
- **See also:** [Framing effect](heuristics/framing-effect.md) (the copy-level tool) · [Luxury positioning](luxury-positioning.md) · [Category perception](category-perception.md) · [Anchoring effect](heuristics/anchoring-effect.md) · [Decoy effect](heuristics/decoy-effect.md) · [Bundle psychology](../frameworks/offer-and-bundle-psychology.md) (expected) · [Fogg Behavior Model](../frameworks/fogg-behavior-model.md) (expected)

## Definition
The strategic layer above the [framing effect](heuristics/framing-effect.md). Framing changes how one message is worded; perception management decides **what the product is understood to be, what it's worth and what moment it serves**, then makes every ad, offer and page reinforce that. Sarah's high-ticket example: a $50k car is worth it because "I believe there's no way I could build this car myself… a giant machine… a full brand" [May21 0:29:31–0:30:28].

## Why it matters
- **Same triggers, different perception.** "The same emotional triggers will still hit… for similar products, but it depends on how they view the product." Culture changes the perception of a category, so study the market you enter. [May21 0:30:28–0:31:41]
- **Everyone sells the same cliché.** "Almost every single mattress company is saying the exact same thing: get better sleep." The real moment ("waking up is a very painful experience for most humans") is open. [May21 0:30:50–0:31:41]
- **The brain lacks a frame of reference.** Buyers "don't have a frame of reference for how much do I actually need to see results" (Obvi's $400 12-month upsell "crushing"), and "90 days" means nothing for shampoo. [Jul2 0:31:50–0:32:40; 0:45:20–0:46:05]
- **Numbers are emotional.** "The brain actually has an emotional reaction to numbers." Easy mental math (79 → 229) and dollar savings raise ability. [Jul16 0:37:47–0:44:07]

## How to use it
1. **List the category's default claim** (what every competitor says).
2. **Map real usage behaviour:** where the product lives, who uses it, how often, the worst moment around it (mattress: side of the bed, blanket hogs, morning person vs night owl). [May21 0:31:41–0:33:05]
3. **Pick the true moment** to sell (waking up, dinner, the drain full of hair) and write the positioning line around it.
4. **Set the frame of reference:** usage units, "most customers buy X", how long it lasts. [Jul2 0:55:50–0:57:05]
5. **Frame time and price** for the outcome the buyer wants (speed vs quality), with easy math and dollar savings. [Jul2 0:32:40–0:34:34; Jul16 0:37:47–0:43:05]
6. **Name offers by benefit** ("auto ship and save", "$0 trial", "pay for access").
7. **Check congruence:** does any claim clash with what buyers already believe (pasta ≠ weight loss; "6 Day Results" next to "30 Doses")? [Jul2 0:38:47; Jul16 0:55:36–0:56:50]

**Visual cues:** show the real usage moment (alarm going off, morning stretch, a fridge full of dinners); show quantity as outcomes (stack of meals, calendar of days) rather than a pack count.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Does the ad sell the category cliché or a specific real moment?
- Is value expressed in buyer units (dinners, washes, days) or only in packs and percentages?
- Offer names: "subscribe & save" vs "auto ship"; "free trial" vs "$0 trial".
- Tag: `pos-perception` plus the move: `perc-real-moment`, `perc-frame-of-reference`, `perc-time-frame`, `perc-access`.

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** treat high ticket as perceived value, not a different psychology. [May21 0:29:31]
- **DO** research the product's behavioural set before writing the angle. [May21 0:30:28–0:33:05]
- **IF** every competitor says the same benefit **THEN** find the adjacent real moment they ignore. [May21 0:30:50]
- **IF** buyers want speed **THEN** "30 days"; **IF** quality **THEN** "six weeks". [Jul2 0:32:40–0:34:34]
- **DO** express quantity in the buyer's units. [Jul2 0:55:33–0:57:05]
- **DO** make savings concrete (dollars) and price steps easy to subtract. [Jul16 0:37:47–0:44:07]
- **DON'T** promise what the product's category can't credibly deliver. [Jul2 0:38:47]

## Common mistakes
- Repeating the category promise ("better sleep") and competing on noise.
- Leaving the buyer to guess how much they need (oversupply → churn; undersupply → no results). [Jul2 0:24:05–0:26:12]
- Out-of-order or competing claims that make the offer feel unreal. [Jul16 0:55:36–0:59:47]
