# Validation Over Authority
> For audiences let down by experts, the strongest move is to hand authority back to the buyer: "**Your gut feeling is right… XYZ is not serving you anymore**" is "super crazy powerful… validation, validation, 100%" ([Live, May 21](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) [0:42:33–0:43:57]).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** the audience is skeptical of doctors, experts or brands (women 30+ in hormone/holistic health, 50+ women after menopause dismissals, research-heavy shoppers, people "tired of being sold"); choosing between an expert-led and a validation-led hook; casting the messenger in a UGC brief.
- **Inputs → outputs:** audience + their history with authority (reviews, Reddit) → a validation hook, a decision on whether to use authority at all and which *type* (peer, nurse, functional practitioner, naturopath), plus a low-risk entry (trial sample, checklist).
- **Rules:**
  - IF targeting 30+ women in supplements THEN lead with validation of their own judgement: "You know your body the best. If you don't feel right, something's not right." [May21 0:42:33–0:43:57]
  - DO pair validation with a low-risk entry: a trial sample or a checklist. [May21 0:43:17–0:43:57]
  - DO keep authority if you use it, but switch to the **type this audience trusts** (functional health practitioners, acupuncturists, naturopaths for women 50+). "It's not that authority bias won't work, it just means what type of authority do we need to use." [Jul2 0:13:34–0:15:40]
  - DO affirm research-heavy buyers up front: "You know a good deal when you see one. You're a smart, savvy shopper." [Jun4 0:34:31–0:36:36]
  - DO go negative about the **industry**, positive about the viewer: "you are a smart shopper for identifying that". DON'T trigger a negative sense of self. [Jun11 0:20:12–0:21:13]
  - DON'T tell an anxious person they're anxious ("pro tip"). [Jul2 0:14:39]
- **See also:** [Authority bias](heuristics/authority-bias.md) · [Messenger effect](heuristics/messenger-effect.md) · [In-group bias](heuristics/in-group-bias.md) · [Social proof](heuristics/social-proof.md) · [Risk-reduction hooks](risk-reduction-hooks.md) · [Actual self](actual-self.md) · [Ought self](ought-self.md)

## Definition
A hook strategy that builds trust by **confirming what the buyer already believes about themselves** (their instinct, their savvy, their experience) instead of borrowing trust from an outside expert. It's the counterweight to [authority bias](heuristics/authority-bias.md) for audiences who have become "a little bit sensitive to authority… not sure who should I believe" [May21 0:42:49–0:43:17].

**Nuance (not a contradiction):** Sarah never says "drop authority". On [July 2](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) [0:13:34–0:15:40] she says authority still works for distrustful audiences "as long as the authority is correctly chosen". So the rule is: validate first; if you add authority, choose the messenger this audience trusts.

## Why it matters
- **Many women have been dismissed.** Doctors told them "there's nothing wrong with you… here's some drugs" or "maybe it's just menopause… it's possible it's anxiety". [May21 0:42:49; Jul2 0:13:34–0:14:39]
- **These buyers research hard.** 30+ women are "very independent" and "incredibly sensitive to information… they'll research a lot". [May21 0:41:53–0:42:33]
- **Researchers fear getting burned.** Long-consideration buyers "have high anxiety… too focused on the fact that they don't want to make the wrong decision." Validation shortens the window better than discounts. [Jun4 0:34:31–0:36:36]
- **People want the truth.** "They're tired of being sold… it literally just means they're tired of not hearing the truth." ([Live, June 11](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md) [0:26:35])
- **Authority mismatch backfires.** Boomers who had bad experiences with doctors may trust peers, nurses or functional practitioners more ([Using Biases That Sell](../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/02-using-biases-that-sell.md) [05:20–05:52]).

## How to use it
1. **Check the audience's authority history** (reviews, Reddit: "my doctor said…").
2. **Write the validation line** in their words: gut feeling, knowing their body, being a smart shopper.
3. **Name the industry truth** (not their fault): what's been failing them.
4. **Choose the messenger:** a peer like them, or the practitioner type they now visit. See [messenger effect](heuristics/messenger-effect.md).
5. **Offer a low-risk next step:** trial sample, self-check checklist. [May21 0:43:17]
6. **End with hope**, not fear ([Live, June 4](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) [0:30:17]).

| Body | Hand authority back | "You know your body the best. If you don't feel right, something's not right." ([May21 0:43:17]) |
| Proof (if used) | Trusted-type messenger | "My functional-medicine practitioner finally explained…" (wiki example from [Jul2 0:13:34–0:15:40](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md)) |
| Industry call-out | Flatter the noticing | "A lot of [greens] have junk in them, and you're smart for noticing." ([Jun11 0:20:12](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-11-live-with-sarah.md)) |
| CTA | Low-risk entry | "Try the sample first." / "Take the checklist." ([May21 0:43:17–0:43:57]) |

**Visual cues:** a peer creator who looks like the buyer (age, life stage), at home not in a clinic; no white coats unless the practitioner type is trusted; the checklist or sample on screen.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- "You're not crazy", "trust your gut", "you know your body", "you're right to be skeptical", "smart shoppers know…" → validation.
- Doctor-led ads aimed at audiences who distrust doctors → mismatch to exploit.
- Tag: `trust-validation` (vs `authority-proof` / `authority-borrowing`).

## Rules of thumb
- **IF** the audience has been dismissed by experts **THEN** validate first; authority second and only of a trusted type. [May21 0:42:49; Jul2 0:14:39–0:15:40]
- **DO** give AI tools context on the audience's authority history; generic prompts return "authority works" without the type. [Jul2 0:19:48–0:20:56]
- **IF** buyers research for months **THEN** affirm their savvy and give full information instead of discounts. [Jun4 0:35:35]
- **DO** aim negativity at the industry and praise the viewer for noticing. [Jun11 0:20:12–0:21:13]

## Common mistakes
- Doctor-led hooks for menopausal or 50+ women who distrust doctors. [Jul2 0:13:34–0:15:40]
- Telling an anxious buyer they're anxious. [Jul2 0:14:39]
- Throwing discounts and jokes at research-heavy buyers who "don't trust easily". [Jun4 0:35:35–0:36:36]
