# 11.1 Checkouts Walkthrough (Part 1)
> By the time someone reaches checkout the selling is done, so the checkout's job is to avoid friction and doubt; this part walks through three live checkouts (Dr. Clean, David Protein, Clarifion) and shows urgency timers, price justification, AOV widgets, order bumps and, above all, the missing "this is not a subscription" line.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** designing or auditing a checkout / order page (Shopify or custom) for a DTC or direct-response offer; deciding what to put on the order page besides the form; or writing checkout microcopy (button, guarantee, subscription disclosure).
- **Inputs → outputs:** an offer, price points, guarantee and shipping terms → a checklist of checkout elements to keep, add or test, plus microcopy; competitor checkout screenshots → teardown rows (below).
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** treat the checkout as "a formality" that follows education, emotional connection, features/benefits and social proof: "at this point, it's just time to buy". But "we can't assume the person is going to buy." [00:00–01:27]
  - **DO** state "one-time purchase, no subscription" when the order is not recurring: "I really think it hurts people's conversions every time by not saying no subscription if it's not a subscription." [10:58–11:26, 19:53]
  - **DO** review checkouts on mobile: "mobile is actually the way I would really look at most of this stuff." [11:53]
  - **DO** keep the checkout's reassurance stack (guarantee, support, SSL, testimonials) near the form; it "helps push you over the edge" for pricier or doubtful buys [10:25].
  - **DO** use "Complete order" style buttons: "Complete order, not buy now. Complete order feels nice to complete." [09:57]
  - **DO** show total savings and what is in the order summary. Dr. Clean shows "total savings" in the summary [13:03].
  - **DO** run price benchmarks before judging a price: he checked Instacart, Publix, Target and Sprouts for all-purpose cleaners ($1.99-$8; premium organic around $6-$7) before judging Dr. Clean's $19.95 [05:03–08:22].
  - **DON'T** use fake scarcity. He shows a "59% left in stock" bar, says it is "probably not true… like fake scarcity, but like, everyone does it, not saying you should do it, not condoning it, just saying." [04:04] Only use real stock and real timers.
  - **DON'T** pre-add a "shipping warranty" or similar paid extra "sneakily": "It's free money for them, another reason why it's direct response", but he calls it "very sneaky" and contrasts it with Clarifion's more visible bump [12:29–13:03, 17:12]. Treat as a trust risk and a regulatory risk; make add-ons opt-in and visible.
  - **IF** you cannot read the subscription/terms (e.g. text is an image) **THEN** expect distrust: "it's a picture, so you can't copy and paste it… it's shady AF"; he refuses to buy [18:45–19:25].
  - **IF** the order page asks for a lot of copy because the price is high versus the category **THEN** the extra proof is doing its job: "Why do I need all this copy… to sell a cleaning spray? Because I'm going to charge you… triple the cost of what the market would normally charge." [10:25]

## Key ideas

### 1. Scope and why checkouts get a short module [00:00–01:27]
- "I did a module back in the day of my Profit Fix Formula on checkouts, and honestly not a whole lot has changed, so I don't think this will be a really long section." But if the course covers the whole funnel, "I would feel weird not also taking time to go through checkout." [00:00]
- Plan: look at several brands' checkouts now; in the upsell module he will actually buy the products to see their upsells ("my wife's gonna be like, what the hell, all these random things come") ([14.1](14-01-upsells-overview.md), [14.2](14-02-upsells-case-studies.md)). ### 2. Dr. Clean checkout (mobile) [02:00–13:32]
- **Entry.** An advertorial about cleaning ovens (from the advertorial module, [9.1](09-01-advertorials-overview-and-examples.md)) leads to this all-stains PDP, "so not just about clean ovens, okay, interesting." He likes the PDP design ("really clean and sexy", [10.2](10-02-in-depth-case-studies-dr-clean-and-calvin-tran.md)). Margins are "probably great", cost to make "maybe like $1.50"; this is his guess, not data [02:00–03:33].
- **PDP price display.** The CTA says "Get 65% discount", i.e. the price is hidden until you click. He notes sometimes price is shown in the product selection, sometimes hidden, and would do a second PDP video rather than let this become "endless PDPs" [02:33–03:02] (that became [10.2](10-02-in-depth-case-studies-dr-clean-and-calvin-tran.md)).
- **Top of checkout.** Discount already applied; "65% off", a stock bar ("59% left in stock"), a 10-minute reservation timer ("so a lot of urgency"), then testimonials, before/afters, a benefits recap, guarantee and "works on all surfaces / removes all stains" [03:33–05:03].
- **Why all that proof?** "We just said we're gonna buy it, why are we seeing all this?… I'm guessing the spray is pretty expensive for what is essentially a common household cleaner, drop-shipped from China, most likely." (Speculation about sourcing.) [04:34]
- **Price benchmark he ran.** Single bottle $19.95. Other all-purpose cleaners he looked up (store pages; location was not set so one price was in wrong currency): Method about $1.99-$4.99, Meyers $4.99, Lysol $5.99-$8, Everspring about $3, a $5.99 Target option, Sprouts organic brands $6.29-$6.99, a premium Australian lemon myrtle brand at the top of the market. His conclusion at this point: Dr. Clean is far above the category at $19.95; the price falls with more bottles [05:32–08:52]. **Correction in [10.2](10-02-in-depth-case-studies-dr-clean-and-calvin-tran.md) [09:05–09:33]:** a single bottle includes roughly six tablets, so per-cleaner price may actually be lower than the category. The checkout page never says so.
- **Package selector.** Four options (1, 2, 3, 4 units), not 1-3-6. "Who knows why not 1, 3, 6?… probably they've tested it, they seem pretty dialed in." Multi-purpose cleaning tablets are included as a bonus "no matter what", but "it's not super clear"; he would sell the tablets harder in the selector [09:02–09:57].
- **Form and payment.** Express checkout (PayPal), card and address fields, "all transactions are SSL secure and encrypted", total and savings in the summary, button "Complete order" [09:57]. Below: guarantee, "happy customers", "professional customer support" [10:25].
- **Missing: subscription clarity.** Not obviously a subscription (he does not inspect the card step), "but I'm not sure why they aren't really clear… this is not a subscription, one-time purchase. I almost guarantee if they put that on their checkout, they would increase their conversion rate, because people are really wary of subscriptions" [10:58–11:26].
- **Missing: address autocomplete.** No auto-complete on the address field, which usually "helps to reduce shipping errors" [11:26–11:53].
- **Fees disclosure.** "Sales tax, processing, handling fees will be applied at the end of the ordering process. So what does that mean, right?" (unclear fees line) [11:53–12:29].
- **Hidden order summary and "shipping warranty".** Mobile hides the order summary until opened. Inside: a "shipping warranty" line item already included, which he removes. Shipping is not free on the single bottle ("fine, no big deal. Do the other ones include free shipping? That's another thing I would consider doing"). He calls the pre-added warranty "very sneaky… free money for them" and the checkout overall "really dialed in… kind of crazy that they're charging as much for a cleaner, but good DR elements." [12:29–13:32]

### 3. David Protein checkout (branded e-commerce, Shopify) [13:32–16:10]
- Branded product page with one-time purchase or subscribe-and-save. He is skeptical of the product ("these bars are like not good", and says he is unsure whether a quoted Huberman endorsement is real; he will order to find out) [13:32–14:04]. No verification in the files.
- The flow is a "classic Shopify checkout": two-step (information, then shipping and payment), express pay options at the top, 12 bars per box at "a little over three dollars a bar", free shipping above about $70 [14:04–14:37, 15:38–16:10].
- **Cart upsell widget.** A tiered progress offer: free shipping at two cartons, free gift at three, and a better perk at four; "they're really trying to push you to subscription" ("Why subscribe?"); "more flavors you can add" is "a Shopify widget… trying to up your order value, your AOV" [15:09].
- **Objection handling in the cart.** "Fast and secure shipping · Satisfaction guarantee · Temperature-protected packaging". He guesses the temperature question (live in Arizona, Florida, Las Vegas) "might be a big objection I have" [15:38].
- At payment: "options to add other flavors before it's too late… a little bit of scarcity", plus a discount code field [16:10].

### 4. Clarifion checkout (combined page, order bump) [16:10–20:28]
- Clarifion's page doubles as product page and checkout: pick 1, 3 or 6 units, enter a credit card on the same page ("checkout right on this page"). 10% off for joining the email list: "they've got margin to spare on this, which this device costs them like $3 or something" (his guess) [16:10–17:12].
- **Order bump:** a two-year protection plan. "It's worth noting… the clean spray people have like a little shipping warranty that's very sneakily added. At least Clarifion's is not as sneakily added." He leaves it unchecked [17:12–17:43].
- **Terms.** The subscription/terms text is an image, "so you can't copy and paste it… it's shady AF." He cannot tell whether it is a subscription: "Let's see. Is this a subscription? I don't know." Result: "I don't really trust these people, so I'm not going to buy from them" and therefore will not capture the upsells [17:43–19:53].
- Takeaway repeated: "it really hurts people's conversions every time by not saying no subscription if it's not a subscription" [19:53]. Part 2 continues with more checkouts [20:23].

## Steps / framework
**Checkout teardown checklist (derived from the three examples; use on any competitor or our own page):**
1. Entry context: what ad/advertorial/PDP preceded it? Was the price visible before checkout?
2. Urgency: timer (real?), stock bar (real?), discount applied banner.
3. Reassurance stack: guarantee, support, SSL, testimonials, benefit recap, objection lines (shipping, temperature, refill).
4. Package selector: number of tiers, per-unit price, bonus clarity.
5. Order mechanics: express pay, steps (1-page vs 2-step), address autocomplete, fees text, button label.
6. Add-ons: bump (visible? opt-in?), cart widget for AOV, protection plan.
7. Clarity: one-time vs subscription statement; terms readable as text.
8. Mobile: order summary hidden/collapsed? form above the fold?
9. Record findings in the teardown table.

## How to apply
- [ ] Add "one-time purchase · no subscription" (or the true recurring terms in plain text) next to the button.
- [ ] Keep a reassurance block (guarantee, support, testimonials) on the checkout; place it above the fold on mobile.
- [ ] Put per-unit math beside the package selector when our price is above the category (run the price benchmark first).
- [ ] Make every add-on (protection plan, shipping warranty) unchecked by default and legible; reject pre-checked paid extras.
- [ ] Use timers and stock bars only if the reservation or stock is real.
- [ ] Add address autocomplete (he notes most checkouts have it) and show savings and shipping early.
- [ ] Test the package ladder (1-2-3-4 vs 1-3-6) rather than assume.
- [ ] Capture the order-confirmation and upsell steps in the same teardown ([14.1](14-01-upsells-overview.md)).
