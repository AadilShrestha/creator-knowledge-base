# Classic VSL structure
> One straight-line spine for any consumer-product VSL: Hook/Micro-lead → Lead → Background → Discovery → UMP → UMS → Product Build-Up and Reveal → Close (first CTA at the crescendo) → FAQ; "the more closely you can generally follow this beat by beat structure, the better."

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** outlining, drafting or auditing a VSL (long, medium or in-feed), a long text sales letter, or a long advertorial that runs the same beats; locating where an objection, ingredient list or story belongs; diagnosing an underperforming VSL.
- **Inputs → outputs:** product facts, verified mechanism (UMP/UMS), spokesperson and true credibility facts, true origin/development story, real proof, offer, guarantee → a beat outline with section budgets, 2+ micro-lead options, and a zig-zag/claims audit.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** follow one order: hook/micro-lead, lead, background, discovery, UMP/UMS, product build and reveal, close, FAQ. "For any kind of consumer physical product, you're basically always following the structure." [04-02 00:56–01:50; 15:01](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
  - **DON'T** zig-zag (story → mechanism → more story → "five signs of…"). Subtracting a support point is fine; adding blocks is risky. [04-02 34:31–36:57](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md); [04-05 15:47–17:40](../lessons/04-05-vsl-product-buildup-and-reveal.md)
  - **DO** treat the lead as "an advertisement for the rest of the video sales letter": tease, don't teach. [04-03 16:19–17:14](../lessons/04-03-vsl-leads-after-the-opening-hook.md)
  - **DO** make the UMP the surprising "real cause" ("99% of the answer… the missing 1%") and the UMS a macro solution "logically connected" to it, then the micro proof. [04-04 09:26; 43:09–45:29](../lessons/04-04-vsl-background-stories-and-unique-mechanisms.md)
  - **DO** keep the mechanism repeatable after one or two hearings; don't "peel back the onion in three or four or five different layers". [04-02 42:54–44:59](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md); [04-04 51:54–53:44](../lessons/04-04-vsl-background-stories-and-unique-mechanisms.md)
  - **DO** explain why the out-of-the-box version fails before the reveal ("if the solution was Ashwagandha, go buy it on Amazon, you won't have a product to sell"). [04-02 16:57–17:55](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
  - **DO** make the close the longest section; the first CTA is the crescendo, everything after is "free rolling". [04-06 01:00–02:28](../lessons/04-06-vsl-closes.md)
  - **DO** fix the lead first when a VSL underperforms, unless retention falls off a cliff mid-script. [04-05 30:58–39:52](../lessons/04-05-vsl-product-buildup-and-reveal.md)
  - **IF** a big objection is native to the market **THEN** answer it inside the UMP, and again in close/FAQ. [04-02 37:26–39:48](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
  - **IF** supplement **THEN** reveal ingredients in the UMS ("found a solution in studies"), not first at the product reveal. [04-02 39:48–42:17](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
  - **IF** the VSL runs in-feed **THEN** use the in-feed spine (hooks → education → pain + promise → quick background → UMP → UMS → build-up → close); **IF** on-page **THEN** the classic outline, "ESPECIALLY the lead". [04-11 04:02–08:53](../lessons/04-11-vsls-on-landing-pages-vs-vsls-as-in-feed-ads.md)
  - **DON'T** invent the struggle/prototype story, testimonials, "live shoppers", price anchors or scarcity. Stefan on the Nooro build-up: "This is all [made up], right?… But that story is so powerful." **Hard stop:** use the true development story or shorten the section. [04-02 21:45](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
  - **DON'T** lean on "big pharma doesn't want you to know": "not really compliant… I personally don't really use them." [04-02 1:00:42–1:01:15](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)

## Definition
The course handout "Very Simple Sales Letter Outline" (04-01 docx), used through modules 4-7: Hook/Microlead · Lead · Background (Credibility and Origin Story) · Discovery · UMP/UMS · Product Build-Up and Reveal · Close · FAQ. [04-01](../lessons/04-01-vsls-section-overview.md); [04-02 01:25–01:50](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md) Stefan: "This is an outline from five, six, seven years ago… it's still basically the same outline that everyone's using." [04-02 32:36](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md) Caveat: "especially any consumer product… if you're doing info, high ticket, other things, obviously there's nuance." [04-02 15:01](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)

## Why it matters
- Most struggling scripts fail by deviation: "if you're deviating all over the place, you're not good." [04-02 54:44](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
- The lead decides how many people reach the pitch; with a 60% vs 20% hook rate, every later optimisation works on 600 vs 200 of 1,000 viewers (his arithmetic). [04-05 39:52–40:21](../lessons/04-05-vsl-product-buildup-and-reveal.md)
- The same structure feeds every Claude VSL project and brief in modules 5-7 (see [vsl-build-with-claude](../frameworks/vsl-build-with-claude.md)).

## How to use (steps)
1. Confirm placement (in-feed vs on-page) and length band ([vsl-length-bands](vsl-length-bands.md)).
2. Fill the beat table below from the brief; mark every unverified fact `[VERIFY: …]`.
3. Budget sections with the percentage table.
4. Write 2+ micro-lead options; one lead. [04-03 35:22](../lessons/04-03-vsl-leads-after-the-opening-hook.md)
5. Draft beat by beat; don't add blocks after the build-up starts. [04-05 16:44–17:12](../lessons/04-05-vsl-product-buildup-and-reveal.md)
6. Audit with the zig-zag + claims prompt (below), then [vsl-review](../rubrics/vsl-review.md).

### The beats (handout support points, condensed; "you don't necessarily have to use everything")
| # | Section | Support points (handout) | Teaching notes | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Pre-lead / micro-lead | Hook-heavy opening line; quickly relate to pain; quickly relate to promise | 1-2 sentences or a 30-60 s block; test many | [04-01](../lessons/04-01-vsls-section-overview.md); [04-03 10:58; 46:22](../lessons/04-03-vsl-leads-after-the-opening-hook.md) |
| 1 | Lead | Call out problem; promise solution (+ save time/money); tease emotional discovery story; tease UM; tease contrarian nature; fascinations; brief credibility; qualifiers; brief skepticism; a few broad testimonials | Open loops and say "you'll see that in this video"; repeat one "one thing" phrase; one big idea, then small hooks | [04-03 14:20–15:20; 23:48; 49:41](../lessons/04-03-vsl-leads-after-the-opening-hook.md) |
| 2 | Background story | Who I am + credibility; me or someone close was just like you; traditional solutions failed; trigger event; search for truth | Keep moving; check in with the viewer ("Have you ever…?") | [04-04 07:59–08:57; 49:26–51:25](../lessons/04-04-vsl-background-stories-and-unique-mechanisms.md) |
| 3 | Discovery | "It wasn't until… I truly cracked the code" | One transition beat | [04-02 10:23](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md) |
| 4 | UMP | Real cause; surprising, possibly counterintuitive; credibility and proof | Health: how the system should work → why it isn't → external culprit | [04-04 09:26; 16:19–17:19](../lessons/04-04-vsl-background-stories-and-unique-mechanisms.md) |
| 5 | UMS | Real solution at macro level; logically connected; dig into micro; citations | Supplements: ingredients revealed here | [04-04 43:09–45:29](../lessons/04-04-vsl-background-stories-and-unique-mechanisms.md); [04-02 39:48](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md) |
| 6 | Product build-up + reveal | Seeks out-of-the-box version; it's flawed; must create it; setbacks; breakthrough; proof; others ask; product born | True development facts only | [04-05 00:00–04:47](../lessons/04-05-vsl-product-buildup-and-reveal.md) |
| 7 | Close | Details; dismiss alternatives; build value; testimonials; how to use; scarcity; price justification and anchor; personal mission; price reveal; smart people buy more → **1st CTA** → what happens after click; bonuses; guarantee → **2nd CTA** → two choices; urgency → **3rd CTA** → thank you | Only "a couple sentences" on the biggest bundle before the button; reason-why for every price and deadline | [04-06 01:00–14:38; 38:42](../lessons/04-06-vsl-closes.md) |
| 8 | FAQ | "Back to being their friend"; recap product, use, urgency, guarantee, how to buy → **4th CTA** | Optional; test vs none | [04-06 10:11–11:38; 34:05](../lessons/04-06-vsl-closes.md) |

### Section budgets (full-length VSL)
| Section | Doc "Rough Percentages" | Stefan spoken | Nooro medium VSL (measured in 04-05) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead | 5-10% | 3-5% | ~5% |
| Background | 10-15% | 5-10% | ~7% |
| UMP | 20% | 15-20% | ~14% |
| UMS | 15% | 15% | ~15% |
| Build-up and reveal | 10% | 10% | ~14% |
| Close | 35% | up to 35% | ~45% |
Source: [04-05 doc; 20:08–26:24](../lessons/04-05-vsl-product-buildup-and-reveal.md). 04-06 gives the close as about 30-45% of the script. [04-06](../lessons/04-06-vsl-closes.md) **Agent default:** use the doc column as the starting budget; let the close run 35-45%. Readiness model: viewer is "50 to 75% ready" when the product is born; the close takes them to 100%. [04-05 29:24–30:28](../lessons/04-05-vsl-product-buildup-and-reveal.md)

### In-feed vs on-page spine (04-11 doc)
| Placement | Spine |
|---|---|
| In-feed (Meta ad) | Hook or multiple hooks (curiosity heavy) → education → entertainment (sometimes) → call out pain + promise solution → quick background → UMP → UMS → product build-up and reveal → close |
| On-page | "More often follows classic VSL outline", especially the lead |
Source: [04-11 04:02–05:01](../lessons/04-11-vsls-on-landing-pages-vs-vsls-as-in-feed-ads.md).

### Audit prompt (derived, from 04-01)
```text
You are auditing a VSL script against the RMBC outline (Hook/Microlead, Lead, Background, Discovery, UMP, UMS, Product Build-Up and Reveal, Close, CTAs, FAQ).
For each part, return: (1) start-end timestamp or paragraph range, (2) a verbatim line that proves the part exists, (3) which support points from the outline are present/absent, (4) a one-line fix.
Then list: structural zig-zags (a part that appears, ends, then reappears), claims that need substantiation, and fabricated-looking proof (unverifiable counts, live-viewer numbers, deadlines).
```

## Common mistakes
- Teaching the mechanism in the lead ("that's a different part of our structure"). [04-03 17:14; 26:19](../lessons/04-03-vsl-leads-after-the-opening-hook.md)
- Leads "a thousand words long" in a mid-length VSL. [04-02 09:55](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
- Abstract metaphors ("riot" on the skin → fixed to "silent war"). [04-03 44:55–45:52](../lessons/04-03-vsl-leads-after-the-opening-hook.md)
- Over-long emotional story; "mommy, where's your hair" stories are "played out for the most part". [04-02 1:01:15–1:03:21](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
- Sections that sprawl: a 1,600-word "punch in the gut" vs ~550-750 in pro VSLs. [04-05 18:40–20:08](../lessons/04-05-vsl-product-buildup-and-reveal.md)
- Withholding the first button behind paragraphs of bundle pitch: "your AOV is great… but the conversion rate sucks". [04-06 13:13–14:11](../lessons/04-06-vsl-closes.md)
- Over-pushing the largest bundle; "I'd rather just get the customer." [04-06 28:40–29:09](../lessons/04-06-vsl-closes.md)
- "Trial" wording in the guarantee (reads as auto-bill). [04-06 29:36](../lessons/04-06-vsl-closes.md)
- Moving an in-feed VSL on-page without a classic lead. [04-11 09:59–11:01](../lessons/04-11-vsls-on-landing-pages-vs-vsls-as-in-feed-ads.md)
