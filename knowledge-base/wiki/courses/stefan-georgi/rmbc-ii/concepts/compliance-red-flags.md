# Compliance red flags
> Most RMBC II example scripts, swipes and LLM outputs carry the same 14 claim types (disease/drug claims, invented experts, fake reviews, misused studies, fake scarcity, income promises, hidden continuity…). Record them as course content, mark them "structure only; claims not reusable", and replace each with the compliant pattern below before anything ships.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** before reusing any RMBC example, swipe or prompt output; after every LLM draft (VSL, advertorial, PDP, upsell, email, ad); when a brief, hook list or teardown contains a claim; when auditing a competitor page for "what not to copy".
- **Inputs → outputs:** a draft or swipe + our verified facts (product claims file, real reviews, real inventory, real offer terms, real experts) → a flag table (line, claim type, why it fails, fix), a cleaned draft with `[VERIFY: …]` placeholders, and a list of facts still needed.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** label every course example "structure only; claims not reusable" when citing it. (Brief rule; [AGENTS.md](../../../../../../AGENTS.md) rule 3.)
  - **DO** replace every unverified number, name, credential, study, review count, deadline and stock line with `[VERIFY: …]` until a real source is attached. ([AGENTS.md](../../../../../../AGENTS.md) rule 2.)
  - **DO** use structure/function or appearance wording for supplements and cosmetics ("supports healthy [function]*", "visibly reduces the look of…"); never diagnose, treat, cure, prevent, "reverse" or replace a drug. (derived; see Regulatory anchors)
  - **DON'T** invent experts, patients, customers, testimonials, AI avatars presented as customers, or celebrity endorsements. [04-01 Compliance](../lessons/04-01-vsls-section-overview.md); [12-04 v4 06:41–07:10](../lessons/12-04-tiktok-ads.md); [13-01 20:12](../lessons/13-01-hidden-factors-for-optimal-funnel-structure.md)
  - **DON'T** treat "it's an editorial" or "it's a composite story" as permission to make things up. Stefan: "you can figure out ways… to ensure anything that you have is congruent, truthful… or, you know, it's an editorial, so you can kind of not". **Hard stop.** [09-02 53:09](../lessons/09-02-claude-project-for-advertorials.md)
  - **DON'T** use fake scarcity, timers or stock bars. Stefan on a "59% left in stock" bar: "probably not true… like fake scarcity, but like, everyone does it, not saying you should do it". [11-01 04:04](../lessons/11-01-checkouts-walkthrough-part-1.md) On "almost 1,000 people live shopping": "Obviously not compliant." [04-02 30:12](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
  - **DON'T** call a health cause "the root cause": "that's a diagnostic thing. Pretty much everyone does it in health… but you're not supposed to." [03-01 18:46](../lessons/03-01-unique-mechanisms-overview.md)
  - **DON'T** use "big pharma doesn't want you to know": "they're not really compliant… I personally don't really use them." [04-02 1:00:42–1:01:15](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
  - **DON'T** hide a subscription: "super shady and not sustainable long term". [11-02 21:35](../lessons/11-02-checkouts-walkthrough-part-2.md)
  - **IF** the offer is biz-op, coaching or financial **THEN** no income/return promises without substantiation and a typical-results disclosure; Stefan: "you can't use as much social proof with make money stuff for FTC reasons". [12-01 00:43:19](../lessons/12-01-meta-ads.md)
  - **IF** an LLM wrote it **THEN** assume every named person, number and citation is invented until checked. [14-03 Claims check](../lessons/14-03-claude-projects-for-upsells.md); [10-01 Compliance check](../lessons/10-01-pdp-overviews-and-claude-processes-for-writing-new-ones.md)
  - **IF** the category is health, finance, telemedicine or income **THEN** route to human/legal review before launch. (derived)

## Definition
A red flag is any line in a course example or model output that (a) asserts a fact we cannot prove, (b) makes a regulated health, earnings or financial claim, or (c) creates a false impression (fake proof, scarcity, endorsement, price, format or billing). The lesson pages flag these in their "Compliance check" / "Claims check" / "Compliance flags" sections; this page merges them by type.

## Why it matters
- The course is built on swiping winning DR copy, and that copy is often aggressive. Stefan: "I don't write aggressive shit anymore, but I'm morally agnostic." [04-02 57:45](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md) He studies Alpilean because "for every one weight loss black hat VSL that does like $20 million a month… there's a hundred or a thousand others that all made even more egregious or more aggressive claims that never did this" (instructor claim). [04-02 58:15](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
- The course's own prompts produce fabrications: Stefan asks Gemini to "create a fictional spokesperson and other relevant data" for the Airbnb and financial briefs. [05-03 03:31](../lessons/05-03-airbnb-arbitrage-example.md); [05-05](../lessons/05-05-financial-promo-example.md) Those outputs then flow into VSL, upsell and email projects.
- Copying a structure is safe; copying a claim inherits someone else's legal risk ([AGENTS.md](../../../../../../AGENTS.md) rule 3).

## How to use (steps)
1. Run the derived audit prompt below on the draft.
2. Classify each flagged line with the table (type #1-14).
3. Replace with the compliant pattern; bracket unknowns `[VERIFY: …]`.
4. List facts needed from the brand (studies on the finished product, review exports, inventory, guarantee terms, expert consent).
5. Run [llm-output-claim-audit](../rubrics/llm-output-claim-audit.md) and send regulated categories to review.

### Audit prompt (derived, not from the course)
```text
Audit the copy below for compliance red flags. For each line that contains one, return: the line verbatim; flag type (disease/drug claim, drug comparison, cosmetic-as-drug wording, invented person or credential, invented testimonial/review/count, unverified or misapplied study/authority, fake scarcity/urgency, income/earnings claim, investment/return promise, conspiracy or diagnostic "root cause" framing, weight-loss result claim, hidden or unclear subscription, undisclosed ad/fake news format, deceptive price or dark pattern); why it is risky; a compliant rewrite using [VERIFY: ...] placeholders. Do not invent replacement facts. Then list every fact I must supply.
[paste copy]
[paste our verified facts file]
```

## Common mistakes
- Treating claims inside the course's own prompts (e.g. "Lose up to 71 lbs of fat fast", "fat cell hypothermia") as harmless format examples; they bias outputs. [02-04 Flags](../lessons/02-04-unified-research-process.md)
- Copying a competitor's numbers: "copying numbers is copying claims". [10-01 Compliance check](../lessons/10-01-pdp-overviews-and-claude-processes-for-writing-new-ones.md)
- Trusting a model's citation: "one of these studies comes from Harvard. I have to confirm if Harvard actually does." [07-03 v1 02:31](../lessons/07-03-3-7-minute-vsl.md)
- Assuming native's "lower" compliance standards mean none. [09-01 §11; v2 16:11–16:42](../lessons/09-01-advertorials-overview-and-examples.md)
- Reading participant opinion as legal advice (the financial promo "detaches the claim from the promise", "very safe"). [04-07 47:32–49:22](../lessons/04-07-long-form-financial-vsl-examples-and-break-downs.md)
- Leaving template placeholder text live on a page (Happy Mammoth product blurb). [14-02 v3 01:40](../lessons/14-02-upsells-case-studies.md)

## Regulatory anchors (derived; not taught in the course; not legal advice)
- **[Example/Case]:** FDA dietary-supplement rules (DSHEA; 21 CFR 101.93) allow structure/function claims with the standard disclaimer and bar disease claims.
- **[Example/Case]:** FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022) requires competent and reliable scientific evidence matched to the specific claim, product, dose and population.
- **[Example/Case]:** FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023) require real endorsers, honest opinions and disclosure of material connections.
- **[Example/Case]:** FTC final rule on fake reviews and testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, announced Aug 2024) bans fake or AI-generated reviews and fake social-proof indicators.
- **[Example/Case]:** FTC Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR Part 437) and earnings-claim guidance govern income promises.
- **[Example/Case]:** ROSCA (US) requires clear disclosure, express consent and simple cancellation for negative-option (subscription) offers.
- **[Example/Case]:** TCPA (US) governs marketing calls and texts (consent).
Check current rules and platform policies (Meta, TikTok, Google, native networks) at time of use.
