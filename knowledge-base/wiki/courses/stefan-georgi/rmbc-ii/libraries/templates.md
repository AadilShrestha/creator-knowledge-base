# Templates library: RMBC II

> Every structure, outline and fill-in formula in RMBC II, in one place: VSL outlines by length (long, medium, 3–7 min, short), the classic VSL section by section, advertorial and PDP section orders, checkout elements, upsell scripts, email sequences (abandon, welcome, promo, reorder, winback), webinar structure and funnel flows, each with its lesson and timestamp.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** outlining any asset before drafting (the outline-first gate), auditing a competitor asset against a known structure, or picking a length band.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the brief (product, offer, audience, awareness, mechanism, real proof) + target asset and length → pick the matching skeleton here → outline with our facts in each beat → draft → claim audit.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** outline first and check the structure "instead of zigs and zags" before any draft. [09-02 27:01–27:45](../lessons/09-02-claude-project-for-advertorials.md); [07-01 v1 28:33–40:12](../lessons/07-01-long-form-vsl.md)
  - **DO** treat support points as a menu: "you don't necessarily have to use everything", and order inside a point can mix. [04-01 docx](../lessons/04-01-vsls-section-overview.md)
  - **DO** scale the outline to the length band: medium VSLs keep 3–4 lead jobs and merge UMS with the product; short VSLs keep hook → mechanism → solution → CTA. [04-08](../lessons/04-08-medium-vsls-structures-and-examples.md); [04-09](../lessons/04-09-short-vsls-structures-and-examples.md)
  - **DO** fill every proof, scarcity, testimonial, doctor and number slot with our real data or a `[VERIFY: …]` bracket. ([AGENTS.md](../../../../../../AGENTS.md))
  - **DON'T** reuse example fills marked "structure only; claims not reusable". Course examples carry invented doctors, studies, review counts, deadlines and disease/income claims.
  - **DON'T** use timers, "only N left", "sold out twice" or "price goes up tomorrow" unless literally true. [11-01](../lessons/11-01-checkouts-walkthrough-part-1.md); [15-03](../lessons/15-03-1-3-promotional-sequences.md)
  - **IF** the ad already explained the product **THEN** use the short pre-sell or PDP variant; **IF** it only teased **THEN** use the long advertorial or long PDP. [09-01 36:42–45:09](../lessons/09-01-advertorials-overview-and-examples.md); [10-01 05:07–09:47](../lessons/10-01-pdp-overviews-and-claude-processes-for-writing-new-ones.md)
- **See also:** [prompts](prompts.md) (the Claude requests that produce these outlines) · [hooks](hooks.md) · [ad-teardowns](ad-teardowns.md) · [vsl-length-bands](../concepts/vsl-length-bands.md) · [classic-vsl-structure](../concepts/classic-vsl-structure.md) · [vsl-build-with-claude](../frameworks/vsl-build-with-claude.md) · [Evolve templates](../../../evolve/copywriting/libraries/templates.md) · [Evolve Start Here templates](../../../evolve/start-here/libraries/templates.md)

### A2. The classic VSL outline (verbatim course handout "VSL Overview and Structure")
Use as the master skeleton for long-form; medium and short versions below compress it. [04-01 docx](../lessons/04-01-vsls-section-overview.md) (the 06 copy of the close is in B6.)
```text
Very Simple Sales Letter Outline:
- Hook/Microlead
- Lead
- Background (Credibility and Origin Story)
- Discovery
- UMP/UMS
- Product Build-Up and Reveal
- Close
- FAQ

Sales Letter Outline:
* Support points - you don't necessarily have to use everything
** The order within a point is not necessarily in that order - they can also "mix" with each other

1) Pre-Lead/MicroLead
 1. Hook heavy or attention grabbing opening line.
 2. Quickly related to the pain-point prospect faces
 3. Quick related to the promise of a solution

2) Lead (These don't have to be in order) (Open a bunch of loops with fascinations, and say - I'm going to get to that in just a moment, or a few minutes, or "and you'll see that in this video")
 1) Call Out Problem
 2) Promise Solution to Pain Point + Promise to Save Time/Money
 3) Tease Emotional Discovery Story
 4) Tease Unique Mechanism Behind Solution
 5) Tease Contrarian Nature of Mechanism (This Flies in the Face of What You've Been Told Before)
 6) Working in "Fascinations" as Incentive For Continuing With Copy (I'll Answer This Riddle For You)
 7) Briefly Mention Credibility Builders
 8) Qualifiers - Who This Works For. Is It For Everyone? Is It For a Certain Subset? Speak to Your Market.
 9) Briefly Address Skepticism.
 10) Include a Few Broad Testimonials if Available (About Benefits But Don't Give Away Product)

3) II. Background Story
 1) Who I Am + Credibility Builders When Appropriate (ie. A Doctor or Expert)
 2) "Me or Someone Close to Me Was Just Like You (In Pain)" - Here's The Emotional Story About It
 3) Traditional Solutions & Advice Not Working
 4) Trigger Event - Problem or Pain Point Escalated to Where Avatar Realizes Something MUST Change
 5) Search for Truth/Answers

4) III. Unique Mechanism of the Problem
 1) Explaining the "Real Cause" of the Problem; You've Had 99% of the Answer But This is the Missing 1%
 2) Should Be Surprising, Possibly Even Counterintuitive
 3) Backed by Credibility and Proof Elements

5) Unique Mechanism of The Solution
 1) Now That You Know the Real Cause of the Problem, This is the "Real Solution" on a MACRO level.
 2) This Should Be Logically Connected to the Problem
 3) Once They Have the MACRO Solution (A Theory) - They Dig Deep To Prove The Theory (And Get Into The MICRO).
 4) Backed By Credibility and Proof Elements, Including Citations and Scientific Sources.

6) V. Product Buildup + Reveal
 1) Now That Avatar Knows The Solution, He Looks For An "Out-of-the-Box" Version; Something That Will Just End His Quest and Solve The Problem Right There
 2) But Discovers That Out-of-the-Box Is Flawed
 3) Has to Do It Himself And Create Novel New Solution
 4) Starts Testing It or Working On It. There Are Problems and Setbacks
 5) But Eventual Success/Breakthrough
 6) Proof Product Works
 7) Others Asking For It
 8) PRODUCT Is Born!

7) VI. Close
 1) Product Details (What's Included, What Makes it Special, Unique Selling Propositions)
 2) Discuss and Dismiss Other Alternatives (Expensive, Ineffective, Side-Effects, Poor Quality, Unproven)
 3) Keep Building Up Value of -PRODUCT-
 4) Additional Testimonials
 5) How to Use The Product
 6) Scarcity: Demand Is High, Supplies Are Limited, Out-of-Stocks Are Common
 7) Personal Mission of Avatar to Make Things Better - Tied to Emotion
 8) Price Justification & Reveal - Other "Solutions" Are More Expensive & Consequences of Inaction are Costly (Cost Can Be Either Material or Immaterial)
 9) This Solution is Better and Will Save You Money, It's Normally $X Which is Bargain, But Right Now You Can Get It For Just $Y!
 10) Smart People Buy More

8) First Call to Action
 1) What Happens After You Click The Button
 2) Optional: Valuable Free Bonuses Revealed
 3) Guarantee - You'll Most Likely Have a Great Experience, But If For Any Reason You Don't, No Risk and No Hassle

9) Second Call to Action
 1) Two Choices - You Can Go On Suffering, Or You Can Take Action Today and Change Your Life forever; The Choice Is Yours
 2) Urgency 2 - But You Really Must Hurry As This is The End of the Presentation And There's No Guarantee How Long Offer Will Be Available

10) Third Call to Action
 1) Thank You For Watching

11) VII. FAQs
 1) Back to Being Their Friend; Helping To Clear Up Any Confusion They May Have
 2) Reminding Them of What Product Is, What It's Used For, Importance of Taking Action Today, Terms of Guarantee, and How to Buy!
```
Compliance on this skeleton: "Scarcity: Demand Is High, Supplies Are Limited" and "Urgency 2" are fill-only-if-true slots. "Citations and Scientific Sources" means real, checkable citations.

### A4. Long-form host-and-expert financial VSL (observed skeleton, 17 beats)
Structure only; claims not reusable (wealth promises, political claims). [04-07 02:29–1:07:00](../lessons/04-07-long-form-financial-vsl-examples-and-break-downs.md)

| # | Beat | Copy as |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contrarian opening (VO) | Challenge a belief the viewer holds, promise the unheard side; secret, hero, recency, WIIFM |
| 2 | Spokesperson intro | 3–4 specific, checkable career facts |
| 3 | Host-and-expert interview begins | Host asks the three questions the lead raised |
| 4 | Immediate objection handling | Pre-empt the identity objection ("this isn't X") |
| 5 | Specifics | A checkable specific the viewer could look up |
| 6 | Number + dimensionalisation | Big figure → 3 relatable comparisons |
| 7 | Villain and hero | Name the culprit and champion for this audience |
| 8 | Precedent story | A past event that did the same thing |
| 9 | The viewer's role | "It's the investors who act on this opportunity" |
| 10 | "Hole in the story" objection | Re-open skepticism before the reveal |
| 11 | Proof visual | Show, don't only tell |
| 12 | Urgency | A real time window only |
| 13 | Hero payoff | Validate the audience's identity with citable talk |
| 14 | Expert's track record | After the big idea if the reveal might disappoint |
| 15 | Bridge to the real mechanism | "How did it happen before" historical bridge |
| 16 | Mechanism reveal | Tie it to what the market already cares about |
| 17 | Offer | Low front-end report (not detailed in the files) |

**Lead construction checklist (derived, 04-07 05:50–09:14):** 1 market + current belief → 2 one line challenging it → 3 secret, desirable object, why hidden → 4 hero and villain the audience already has → 5 timing + WIIFM → 6 several reasons to care (altruistic, self-interested, tribal) → 7 end on 3 curiosity questions and the spokesperson.

**Generated financial script section order (05-05, ~7,100 words):** HOOK → THE HIDDEN FORTUNE (guest credentials) → the "trust" no one talks about → why you haven't heard → … → offer. Mechanics: host voices the objection after each big claim; mechanism by analogy to a known royalty business; "best case scenarios" hedge; "why now" policy catalyst; each bonus introduced as an answer to a host question; risk disclaimer in-script. [05-05](../lessons/05-05-financial-promo-example.md)

### A6. Medium VSL (6–16 min) = classic outline, compressed
[04-08](../lessons/04-08-medium-vsls-structures-and-examples.md)

| Classic beat | Medium treatment |
|---|---|
| Hook / micro-lead | One line: pain + promise + attention; education or UGC framing |
| Lead (10 points) | Keep 3–4: call out problem, promise solution, educational component, contrarian nature |
| Background | One-line credibility, mini story, traditional solutions failed, "discovered something new", **tease the payoff again**; skip trigger event and search for truth |
| UMP | Root cause, simple, step by step, one small new fact; past solutions are band-aids; **expand the pain point** ("not JUST X, also Y and – God forbid – even Z") |
| UMS | Merged with the product reveal, or "product education" |
| Build-up + reveal | A few sentences: "I had to find a solution" / "I partnered with…" |
| Close | Product details, how to use, deep future pacing, objection handling, price, guarantee, scarcity, CTAs |
| FAQ | Optional; test it |

**Nooro adapted outline (on-screen, condensed):** 1 micro-lead → 2 lead (call out problem; promise + save time/money; educational component; contrarian nature) → 3 background (who I am; someone like you in pain; traditional solutions failing; spokesperson discovered something new; keep teasing the payoff) → 4 UMP (root cause step by step; expand the pain; why past solutions were band-aids) → 5 introduce product AS the UMS (how it solves the UMP; very fast build-up; each feature tied to the UMP; back to the promise; optional testimonials) → 6 close (future pacing → objection 1 → how to use → future pacing → personal mission → price justification + reveal → "what if it doesn't work?" guarantee → brief future pacing → more objections → "doesn't it make sense to take action today?" → brief social proof → crossroads → future pacing → guarantee again → discount reminder → urgency/scarcity → CTA → guarantee + discount reminder → explicit command, last urgency, thanks). [04-08 frames 13:54–47:19; v2 45:46](../lessons/04-08-medium-vsls-structures-and-examples.md)

**MiamiMD adapted outline (UGC lead → expert, condensed):** 1 micro-lead → 2 UGC lead (call out; promise; education = reviewing popular products as expensive/temporary; tease a different product, unnamed; tease UMP/UMS; "I tried it even though skeptical, fast results"; others noticed; "if you're tired of this, try it") → 3 expert contrarian hook ("When I tell people [common solution] is making it worse, they think I'm crazy…") → 4 background (credentials; "I use it and so do others"; how it works) → 5 UMP (same three lines as Nooro) → 6 product education (three components, each = what it is, what it does, proof; picture of them combined) → 7 build-up (nobody combined them; partnered/created; smart people already using it; easy and affordable) → 8 close (details → future pacing → testimonial 1 dismisses an alternative → testimonial 2 bought multiple → price → "in a moment I'll show you how to save more" → guarantee → CTA 1 → how to use → smart people buy more → "if you pass now you may never see it again" → CTA 2 + fast checkout → what happens after click → recap → deep future pacing → final CTA + biggest package → sign-off). [04-08 frames 08:24–33:18](../lessons/04-08-medium-vsls-structures-and-examples.md)

Claim risk for both fills: substantiation needed (device and skin claims; study lines per ingredient must be real).

## F. Advertorials

### F1. Purposes, elements, lengths
- **Purposes:** do the selling legwork; sell the click (to VSL/PDP); compliance (an "interstitial"); keep ad → page congruence. [09-01 v2 00:00–00:33](../lessons/09-01-advertorials-overview-and-examples.md)
- **Elements:** educational (mechanism and/or product), personal (emotional), curiosity-driven; on story pages the order is personal/emotional → curiosity → education. [09-01 11:01–11:35](../lessons/09-01-advertorials-overview-and-examples.md)
- **Length bands (doc):** short 200–500 words · medium 500–1,000 · long 1,000–2,000. The Clarifion "long" page is ~2,900 words (derived), so bands are "guidelines, not golden rules" (conflict logged in contradictions notes). [09-01 doc](../lessons/09-01-advertorials-overview-and-examples.md)

### F2. Advertorial section skeleton (derived from the examples)
1. Headline + dateline/byline in article style, matching the ad's pain point and avatar.
2. Personal, emotional opening with a curiosity gap.
3. Escalation (what the problem costs) and why obvious fixes fail.
4. Education: the real cause (UMP).
5. Discovery / turning point (peer, expert, accident) and product reveal with its mechanism.
6. Proof: real reviews, tests, experts.
7. Future pacing and, where it fits, multi-unit logic.
8. Price justification vs alternatives; offer; honest urgency.
9. Soft CTA to PDP or VSL ("check availability", "watch now").
10. Compliance: "Advertorial/Advertisement" label, disclaimers, citations.

### I1. The upsell formula and offer types
- **Order:** reaffirmation first → new or expanded problem second → irresistible offer third. [14-01 v2 00:34–02:01](../lessons/14-01-upsells-overview.md)

## J. Email sequences
Samples in the course were written by Claude for hypothetical brands, with invented stats, customers and scarcity: structure only. Sender personas ("Sarah at…", "Dr. [Expert Name]") must map to a real person.

### J1. Abandon cart (7 emails over ~7 days)
**Design:** email 1 at trigger · email 2 at +6 h · then ~1/day · the last day can hold 1–3 emails · one abandonment reason per email (map in [angles](angles.md) A049–A055) · 2–3 subject options + subhead each · P.S. support line (phone, hours) + CTA on every email. [15-01 v2 00:01–14:33](../lessons/15-01-1-1-abandon-cart-sequences.md)

| # | Timing | V1 discount-led (50% → 60%) | V2 emotion-led (discount from day 3: 40% → 55%) | V3 book-a-call (no discount) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trigger / day 1 | Reconnect + future pacing, social proof, testimonials, code | "Mirror moment" story + "you're not broken, wrong tools" | Recap the presentation; "what stopped you?" |
| 2 | +6 h / day 2 | Social proof + "why trust us" (guarantee, shipping, support) | Mechanism/education | Objection crushing: "I know this sounds too good to be true..." |
| 3 | +1 d / day 3 | Customer story + future pacing + price anchor | Long testimonial + first discount (40%) | Fear-of-failure story |
| 4 | +1 d / day 4 | FAQ | "But what if it doesn't work for me?" + risk reversal | Market-timing urgency with logic |
| 5 | +1 d (48 h left) / day 5 | Ingredient science + guarantee | Discount increase (55%), 48-h window | Scarcity + why spots are limited (real only) |
| 6 | +1 d / day 6 | "I NEVER do this": discount upgrade | 24 h left, future self vs regret | Peer pressure: what others did while you waited |
| 7 | final hours / day 7 | Last chance + testimonials + midnight deadline | "This is it" emotional close | Future regret, final email |

## K. Webinar (guest: Dylan Vanas; all numbers guest claims)

### K1. Funnel order
Ads (15–20 creatives → 50–100 variations) → minimal registration page (headline, sub-headline, date, host bio; no video/testimonials) → optional $17–$49 self-liquidating offer → detailed confirmation page (video, calendar links, community, proof) → reminder cadence (daily email; day before 2 emails + 2 SMS; day of 3 emails + 3 SMS incl. 1 h before and "live now"; calendar-add button) → live session → simple order page (timer, value stack, deliverables) → upsell → order confirmation with onboarding-call booking + qualification survey. [16-04 v2 15:14–25:03](../lessons/16-04-webinars-live-talk.md)

**Hard stop:** the "we broke the internet, here's the new link" SMS is deceptive; do not use. [16-04 v2 17:20–17:51](../lessons/16-04-webinars-live-talk.md)

### K2. Slide skeleton
```text
INTRO (about 8 min): Headline | Sub-headline | Hero's journey | Reverse authority | Hook till end (bonus) | Differentiator | Elephant in the room | Future pace
CONTENT (about 30-40 min): The what, not the how | Secret 1 (external belief) | Secret 2 (internal belief) | Secret 3 (mechanism belief) | proof under each | Recap
CLOSE (about 15-30 min): Bridge | Offer reveal | Value stack (proof + benefit per item) | Price drop | Link drop | 3-5 bonuses | Financing and payment plans | FAQs | Text-in CTA
```
Length 60–90 min to start (advanced up to 2.5 h); real price and CTA done by 1:15. Scripts (elephant in the room, two-options bridge, information-alone bridge, price drop, financing line, fast-action bonuses, text-in CTA): see [16-04 prompts section](../lessons/16-04-webinars-live-talk.md) and [prompts](prompts.md). The price-drop story and value ladder are staged: use only real reference prices; fast-action bonuses only with real limits. [16-04 v2 36:37–56:11](../lessons/16-04-webinars-live-talk.md)
