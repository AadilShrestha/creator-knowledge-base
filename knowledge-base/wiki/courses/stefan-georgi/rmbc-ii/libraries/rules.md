## 1. Research

### Order and inputs
- **RS01 DO** run the stages in order, research → mechanism → brief → copy, and never start a stage without the previous stage's output document. [01-01 04:40–06:33; 11:29–13:54](../lessons/01-01-welcome-to-rmbc-ii-and-in-depth-course-overview.md) [08-01 05:28–06:47](../lessons/08-01-rmbc-ii-mid-course-recap.md)
- **RS02 DO** merge three deep-research outputs (product/market awareness, competitor, psychographic) into one unified research document before any copy; the combining prompt is just "combine them into one". Feed LLMs the unified doc, not the three raw reports. [01-01 06:33–07:24](../lessons/01-01-welcome-to-rmbc-ii-and-in-depth-course-overview.md) [02-02 00:28–01:51; frame 03:00](../lessons/02-02-competitor-research-walkthrough-and-examples.md) [02-04 00:29](../lessons/02-04-unified-research-process.md)
- **RS03 DO** define "the right person" before writing (coffee example: the coffee lover who drinks too much and wants fewer negative effects, not non-coffee drinkers) and judge an ad only by "its ability to get the right kind of people to consistently take that desired action". [01-02 06:53–10:31](../lessons/01-02-foundational-knowledge-and-the-state-of-dtc-marketing-in-202.md)
- **RS04 DO** assume the buyer is less informed than you. [02-01 v2 06:56](../lessons/02-01-product-market-awareness.md)
- **RS05 DO** research the customer "before we spend a dollar on ads" (the 83-question deep-research prompt), then put the research into a per-brand LLM project so every idea and script request starts from it. (guest: Max Finn) [12-04 v2 00:00:56–00:01:25; v2 06:54–07:51](../lessons/12-04-tiktok-ads.md)
- **RS06 IF** you are modelling a proven VSL, PDP or advertorial **THEN** you can shortcut much of the research, though the full process is still the ideal. [02-04 04:10–05:11](../lessons/02-04-unified-research-process.md)

### Market awareness
- **RS07 DO** define "product aware" and "most aware" at category level, not brand level, for small DTC brands: "they're aware that this category of product exists and they're close to buying." [02-01 02:49–03:48; v2 02:59–03:22](../lessons/02-01-product-market-awareness.md)
- **RS08 DO** run the awareness prompt in at least two LLMs and compare ("you'll still have a general gauge"); read only the TAM estimate, the % per stage and the verdict. [02-01 v2 09:40; 10:45; 21:18–22:59](../lessons/02-01-product-market-awareness.md)
- **RS09 DON'T** treat the awareness percentages as measurements; they are LLM estimates. (derived) [02-01](../lessons/02-01-product-market-awareness.md)
- **RS10 DO** aim cold-traffic copy at problem-aware and solution-aware people, the biggest reachable groups. **DON'T** write direct response for the unaware unless the market already wants the outcome (biz-opp, money, freedom): moving them to problem-aware "is a whole extra step". [02-01 17:01–20:38; 50:13; v2 27:49–28:20](../lessons/02-01-product-market-awareness.md)
- **RS11 DON'T** assume the audience knows why your category or ingredient works; solution-aware but not product-aware readers need to be taught "why your product is the best solution". [02-01 26:58–27:28](../lessons/02-01-product-market-awareness.md)
- **RS12 IF** nearly everyone is already product-aware (GLP-1 meds) **THEN** a static ad plus a simple landing page is enough. **IF** the market is problem-aware or unaware of your category (injectable NAD+) **THEN** expect mechanism, big idea and a long advertorial or VSL; without that education paid social cost "$1,000, $2,000 to make a sale" (instructor claim). [02-01 34:02–38:52](../lessons/02-01-product-market-awareness.md)
- **RS13 DON'T** reuse one message across awareness stages. [02-01 41:17–44:00; 50:13–50:42](../lessons/02-01-product-market-awareness.md)
- **RS14 DO** state the awareness stage at the top of every research doc and brief (derived), and use it as an input for every format, "not just for VSLs… but also all the ads". [02-01](../lessons/02-01-product-market-awareness.md) [08-01 01:00–01:31](../lessons/08-01-rmbc-ii-mid-course-recap.md)
- **RS15 IF** results are "some sales, no scale" **THEN** check whether the copy assumes beliefs the audience does not hold. [02-01 25:11–26:58](../lessons/02-01-product-market-awareness.md)
- **RS16 IF** a biz-opp has plateaued at "$1 to $3 million a year" **THEN** the next pool is the larger problem-aware/unaware market (instructor claim). [02-01 v2 19:41–20:10](../lessons/02-01-product-market-awareness.md)

### Competitor research
- **RS17 DO** pull three things from each competitor report: core messaging (claims and promises), pricing, and what customers love and dislike. Skip "the philosophy"; only similar products matter here. [02-02 13:14–14:21; 20:02–21:35](../lessons/02-02-competitor-research-walkthrough-and-examples.md)
- **RS18 DO** rewrite the competitor-type line per market ("D2C brands … eCommerce or Direct Response sales funnels" vs "coaching programs, training platforms, and gurus"). [02-02 18:10–18:37](../lessons/02-02-competitor-research-walkthrough-and-examples.md)
- **RS19 IF** a report is "an overview" without language **THEN** add the phrase-mining question ("specific phrases, claims, verbiage … these competitors repeatedly use") and rerun, or switch LLM. [02-02 29:34–31:31; 47:30–48:12; 50:10](../lessons/02-02-competitor-research-walkthrough-and-examples.md)
- **RS20 DO** mine competitor complaints as objections and positioning gaps. **IF** every competitor says "premium, affordable, clean design" **THEN** a specific pain-point promise stands out (instructor view). [02-02 40:30–41:29; 44:55–45:30](../lessons/02-02-competitor-research-walkthrough-and-examples.md)
- **RS21 DON'T** read every citation in a deep-research report ("you don't need to"). ⚖️ C1 [02-02 16:57–17:40](../lessons/02-02-competitor-research-walkthrough-and-examples.md)
- **RS22 DON'T** accept first-pass numbers: price, revenue and guarantee data differed across three models for the same brand. Verify before use. (derived) [02-02](../lessons/02-02-competitor-research-walkthrough-and-examples.md)

### Running research across LLMs
- **RS23 DO** run every research prompt in at least two models (Stefan "typically" uses Gemini and ChatGPT, sometimes Perplexity; Max Finn adds Claude and Grok); **IF** one model is weak on a section **THEN** use the other's; **IF** one gives nothing useful **THEN** "go try a different LLM". [02-02 33:04–33:51; 50:10–50:37](../lessons/02-02-competitor-research-walkthrough-and-examples.md) [02-03 v2 32:23; v2 35:49](../lessons/02-03-psychographic-research.md) [12-04 v2 03:37–04:34](../lessons/12-04-tiktok-ads.md)

### Psychographic research
- **RS24 DO** answer all 22 questions in the four blocks (Demographic Insights, Other Existing Solutions, Curiosity, Corruption), with quotes "in their own words". Ask the same questions in every market; only the demo line changes. [02-03 v2 00:00; 03-1 prompt doc](../lessons/02-03-psychographic-research.md)
- **RS25 DO** read the full reports once when you are new to a market ("there will always be little insights … that might get missed"), skim fast, and log every interesting line, story or stat in a separate notes doc. ⚖️ C1 [02-03 v1 01:05–02:33; v2 36:55](../lessons/02-03-psychographic-research.md)
- **RS26 DON'T** move slowly: "You cannot move slow anymore." **IF** overwhelmed **THEN** the Claude merge prompt gets "99% of what we want"; run it generically (leave the niche out). [02-03 v1 01:33–02:33; v2 48:14; v3 02:41](../lessons/02-03-psychographic-research.md)
- **RS27 DON'T** leave in-prompt examples unexamined; they steer output (Gemini returned "Ancel Keys" for a skincare brief because the example named him). **DO** label example lines "from a DIFFERENT PRODUCT in a DIFFERENT NICHE" so the model copies the format. [02-03 v2 23:50–26:33](../lessons/02-03-psychographic-research.md) [02-04 03:10–04:10; docx](../lessons/02-04-unified-research-process.md)
- **RS28 DO** capture the market's like/dislike language about existing solutions ("melt in", "greasy", "sat on the skin"); **IF** you want richer language **THEN** run the follow-up quotes prompt in the same chat and save it to its own doc. [02-03 v2 10:12–11:20; v2 42:40–47:07](../lessons/02-03-psychographic-research.md)
- **RS29 IF** the market believes a rival solution works **THEN** contrast on its dislikes (cost, injection, "it's a medication") instead of denying efficacy. [02-03 v1 16:47–17:43](../lessons/02-03-psychographic-research.md)
- **RS30 IF** a quote, statistic or historical story will appear in copy **THEN** verify it at the source first; LLM "quotes" drift between passes. **IF** it is a suppressed/forgotten story **THEN** validate the history. [02-03 v1 21:08](../lessons/02-03-psychographic-research.md) (derived) See X13.

### Unified research prompt
- **RS31 DO** use the unified research prompt as written ("you can honestly just copy and paste it in as is"); unfilled `_NICHE_` placeholders still worked. [02-04 01:25; 07:55–08:20](../lessons/02-04-unified-research-process.md)
- **RS32 DO** tell the model which awareness segments to focus on: default "Problem Aware and Solution Aware"; change per market (GLP-1: solution-aware; biz-op: problem-aware and even unaware). [02-04 02:10–03:10](../lessons/02-04-unified-research-process.md)

### Ingredient research
- **RS35 IF** you sell a supplement **THEN** run the ingredient-research prompt for studies, claims and citations; substantiation of any health claim is still required. [01-01 07:24](../lessons/01-01-welcome-to-rmbc-ii-and-in-depth-course-overview.md) [02-05 00:00–01:02](../lessons/02-05-ingredient-deep-research.md)

## 2. Unique mechanism

### Definition and shape
- **MC01 DO** write the mechanism as two linked halves: the unique mechanism of the problem (UMP), which explains why past attempts failed, and of the solution (UMS), which explains why ours succeeds "where other alternatives have failed". A solution-only mechanism does not explain past failure. [03-01 02:01; docx](../lessons/03-01-unique-mechanisms-overview.md) [03-05 Genesis p.1](../lessons/03-05-creating-a-claude-project-for-unique-mechanisms.md)
- **MC02 DO** frame the UMP as the "real cause" and a knowledge gap: "You've had 99% of the answer but this is the missing 1%"; surprising, possibly counterintuitive, with credibility and proof. [03-01 02:01; 09:47](../lessons/03-01-unique-mechanisms-overview.md) [04-01 docx §4](../lessons/04-01-vsls-section-overview.md) [04-04 09:26; 13:27](../lessons/04-04-vsl-background-stories-and-unique-mechanisms.md)
- **MC03 DO** keep it simple and explainable in a sentence: one metaphor, one cause, one recap line, "as few cognitive leaps as possible"; a reader should repeat it after hearing it "one or two times". Simpler mechanisms win more often in health and beauty. [03-01 10:50; 22:29](../lessons/03-01-unique-mechanisms-overview.md) [03-04 20:19–20:50; 30:44](../lessons/03-04-ideation-validation-and-expansion-walkthrough-eye-serum-exam.md) [04-02 13:04; 14:31](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md) [04-04 51:54–53:44](../lessons/04-04-vsl-background-stories-and-unique-mechanisms.md)

## 5. VSLs

### Structure and order
- **VS01 DO** follow one spine in order: hook/micro-lead → lead → background → discovery → UMP/UMS → product build-up and reveal → close → FAQ. [04-01 v1 02:53; docx](../lessons/04-01-vsls-section-overview.md) [04-02 00:56–01:50; 15:01](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
- **VS02 DON'T** zig-zag: subtracting from a section is fine, adding is risky. **DON'T** insert random blocks ("five myths about…", a quiz, extra story, extra proof) between sections or after the build-up starts. [04-02 34:31–36:57](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md) [04-05 15:47–17:40](../lessons/04-05-vsl-product-buildup-and-reveal.md) [09-02 27:01–27:45](../lessons/09-02-claude-project-for-advertorials.md)
- **VS03 DO** "pay attention and have humility": the people who keep struggling with VSLs "feel like they know everything… and don't implement". [04-01 v1 02:23](../lessons/04-01-vsls-section-overview.md) [04-02 00:00–00:56](../lessons/04-02-vsl-fundamentals-classic-structure.md)
- **VS04 DON'T** put the hook on a different promise than the lead. (derived) [04-01](../lessons/04-01-vsls-section-overview.md)
- **VS05 DO** keep each section short and moving: a "punch in the gut" section ran about 550 words in two big publisher VSLs and about 750 in one of Stefan's, vs 1,600 in a student draft; **IF** a section runs ~3x the benchmark **THEN** cut to what advances the narrative. ### Hook and lead

- **VS11 DO** repeat one simple "one thing" phrase ("five tasty superfoods") and the core promise (about 15 uses of "$150 trillion" in one promo). **DON'T** let a story-driven VSL drift from it. - **FN01 DON'T** use one funnel template or default to a VSL; the page after the ad depends on platform, price and awareness. Score the offer on the 15 factors. [01-01 20:52–22:56; 27:34–28:30](../lessons/01-01-welcome-to-rmbc-ii-and-in-depth-course-overview.md)
- **FN02 DO** keep congruence, "a consistent feeling experience for a prospect throughout their journey": video platforms → video pages, article-style native → advertorials; **IF** YouTube **THEN** educational/entertaining video ads and a page with another video. [01-02 11:23–13:05; 15:52](../lessons/01-02-foundational-knowledge-and-the-state-of-dtc-marketing-in-202.md)
- **FN03 DO** run a few core flows, not endless combinations: Ad > VSL > Checkout; Ad > Advertorial > VSL > Checkout; Ad > Advertorial > VSL > PDP > Checkout; Ad > PDP > Checkout; Ad > Advertorial > PDP > Checkout. Test shapes against each other ("Both probably work"). [01-02 19:27; 25:40–27:20; docx](../lessons/01-02-foundational-knowledge-and-the-state-of-dtc-marketing-in-202.md)
- **FN04 DO** score each of the 15 factors 1–5 (higher = less copy needed: "the higher the rating, the less copy you need to add") and map the total: 46+ lean, 26–45 medium, 25 or less heavy. Read the scale direction per row; rows 6, 7, 8, 9, 11 and 12 are phrased differently. [13-01 08:21; 10:17; 43:51; doc](../lessons/13-01-hidden-factors-for-optimal-funnel-structure.md)
- **FN05 DO** combine factors rather than read one alone ("$2,000 and you have no trust… you're still going to probably need longer funnels"), and **DON'T** assume a high price forces long copy when superiority is demonstrable (the multi-thousand-dollar mattress). [13-01 15:40; 39:20](../lessons/13-01-hidden-factors-for-optimal-funnel-structure.md)
- **FN06 DO** raise demonstrability before adding copy: a strong demo or mechanism animation lets a supplement or gadget use 1–10 minute in-feed mini-VSLs. **IF** rows 1 and 5 are low and rows 2, 14 and 15 can be raised **THEN** raise those assets (demo, UGC, expectations timeline) before building a longer funnel (derived). [13-01 11:46–12:44](../lessons/13-01-hidden-factors-for-optimal-funnel-structure.md)
- **FN07 DON'T** treat the total as a "golden rule"; test, and change one factor at a time (add BNPL, add a stronger demo). Odd funnels work: "you just got to test… seven or eight out of 10 [copycats] would not". [13-01 48:27; 1:08:00–1:08:55; doc step 4](../lessons/13-01-hidden-factors-for-optimal-funnel-structure.md)
- **FN08 IF** the market is highly aware of the solution and believes it works **THEN** skip the VSL and pre-sell on price, trust and why you are the better source (static ad → short lander → questionnaire). [13-01 03:02–07:24](../lessons/13-01-hidden-factors-for-optimal-funnel-structure.md)
- **FN09 IF** the pain isn't urgent (anti-aging, "be a millionaire in five years") **THEN** lead with "an immediate pain point that is symptomatic of the long-term one". [13-01 31:13–32:46](../lessons/13-01-hidden-factors-for-optimal-funnel-structure.md)
- **FN10 IF** a funnel converts poorly (even with a high score) **THEN** diagnose in order: step conversion rates against benchmarks → ad metrics (CPM/CPC) → AOV, before adding pages. [13-01 48:59–53:03](../lessons/13-01-hidden-factors-for-optimal-funnel-structure.md)
- **FN11 DO** treat factor rows 9 and 13 as the ones that change the upsell/ascension plan, not only page length. [13-01](../lessons/13-01-hidden-factors-for-optimal-funnel-structure.md) [14-01](../lessons/14-01-upsells-overview.md)

### Economics
- **FN12 DO** state the business goal first (net profit, revenue, new customers, lifestyle); the default is net profit. [16-03 01:53–03:46](../lessons/16-03-funnel-economics-with-spg.md)
- **FN13 DO** compute four numbers before touching copy: CPA/CAC, AOV (day-0 gross), COGS (product, fulfilment, processing fees; not payroll) and gross profit per customer = AOV − CPA − COGS. [16-03 03:46–05:42](../lessons/16-03-funnel-economics-with-spg.md) [14-01 v1 11:14](../lessons/14-01-upsells-overview.md)
- **FN14 DO** treat "does this offer lose money on day 0?" as the fork: +$20 and −$200 per customer need "a very different direction". [16-03 11:33–12:37](../lessons/16-03-funnel-economics-with-spg.md)
- **FN15 DO** pull the levers in order of thought: lower CPA (better ads, higher conversion, other traffic, abandoned-cart recovery, branded search), lower COGS, raise AOV, raise LTV. [16-03 13:07–29:47](../lessons/16-03-funnel-economics-with-spg.md)
- **FN16 IF** day-0 gross profit is negative **THEN** you need cash or predictable rebills; "going negative to acquire customers is actually a totally fine model if you know what you're doing", but Stefan would "rather not… unless there's a very compelling reason". **IF** cash is limited or the business is self-funded **THEN** raise AOV before scaling spend. [14-01 v1 10:43–11:14](../lessons/14-01-upsells-overview.md) [16-03 08:06–09:08; 49:29–51:28; PDF p45](../lessons/16-03-funnel-economics-with-spg.md)
- **FN17 DO** get benchmarks from real operators (media buyers, peers with the same offer type, a paid mastermind) before judging a conversion rate or CPA. [16-03 32:18–33:15](../lessons/16-03-funnel-economics-with-spg.md)
- **FN18 DO** watch CPA; conversion rate is "a metric to mark that that has happened". **DON'T** read a "50% conversion lift" as a win until it is converted to CPA and profit per day. [16-03 34:41–38:03](../lessons/16-03-funnel-economics-with-spg.md)
- **FN19 DO** look for friction by comparing competitors' flows (the peptide thesis: simplify into a quiz to "probably double conversion rates"), and split-test price in both directions. [16-03 26:52–28:52; 34:41–35:37](../lessons/16-03-funnel-economics-with-spg.md)
- **FN20 DON'T** treat LTV math as safe: the example uses competitor data, a six-month window, a 50% margin and an averaged stick rate ("back of napkin"). **IF** CPA rises with scale **THEN** set the ceiling from LTV and goals, not today's CPA. [16-03 43:03–43:31; 48:30; 50:30–51:28](../lessons/16-03-funnel-economics-with-spg.md)
- **FN21 DO** justify recovery flows with funnel math (100 checkout visitors, 20 buyers, $5,000 spend = $250 CPA vs $150 AOV; recovering 15 more gives $142.86 CPA). [15-01 00:29–03:44](../lessons/15-01-1-1-abandon-cart-sequences.md)

## 12. Upsells

- **UP01 DO** write upsell #1 as three beats in order: reaffirm the purchase → new or expanded problem → irresistible offer. [14-01 doc; v2 00:34](../lessons/14-01-upsells-overview.md) [14-02 v4 04:59; v6 02:17–03:16](../lessons/14-02-upsells-case-studies.md)

Pick the offer by its logical connection to what was just bought. [14-01 v2 11:22](../lessons/14-01-upsells-overview.md) [14-02 v4 05:03; v5 06:29; v6 03:16; 04:44; 27:25](../lessons/14-02-upsells-case-studies.md)
- **UP03 IF** the product is a consumable sold one-time (supplement, cleaner, skincare) **THEN** default upsell #1 to more of the same (a deeper-discount stock-up): "Almost all of these did more of the same… because it works", though "it's not always the best". **IF** results are fast **THEN** stress savings and continuity. [14-01 v2 02:01; 09:15; 11:22–12:41](../lessons/14-01-upsells-overview.md) [14-02 v4 22:27](../lessons/14-02-upsells-case-studies.md)

- **UP05 IF** the front end is info, a book or coaching **THEN** sell implementation, done-for-you, templates or faster results, not more of the same. [14-01 v2 15:07–15:47](../lessons/14-01-upsells-overview.md) [14-02 v5 06:56; v6 27:25](../lessons/14-02-upsells-case-studies.md)
- **UP06 DO** put community and done-for-you/done-with-you in later upsells, not #1, and test how many upsells and in what order ("Test it. Find out."). [14-01 v2 16:19–17:16](../lessons/14-01-upsells-overview.md) [14-02 v1 15:46; 16:55](../lessons/14-02-upsells-case-studies.md)
- **UP07 IF** the audience is direct-response (supplement VSL funnels) **THEN** use a video upsell that re-educates on the mechanism, adds the consequence of stopping and closes with a one-time stock-up deal; keep upsells 6–16 minutes. ⚖️ C10 [14-02 v4 05:03–05:32; 14:42](../lessons/14-02-upsells-case-studies.md) [14-03 00:00](../lessons/14-03-claude-projects-for-upsells.md)
- **UP08 DO** open by saying the order is being prepared, they're in the right place and not to hit back. [14-02 v6 02:17; 11:19; Copy Accelerator PDF p.5](../lessons/14-02-upsells-case-studies.md)
- **UP09 DO** make upsells 2+ shorter and more direct ("you really mean business"); **DON'T** spend another 1,000 words reaffirming, and **DON'T** assume they took the previous upsell. [14-02 v6 34:03–36:49](../lessons/14-02-upsells-case-studies.md) [14-03 02:38](../lessons/14-03-claude-projects-for-upsells.md)
- **UP10 DON'T** blame the front-end product when introducing the next offer (it "is God's gift"; the new problem is time, effort, consistency or implementation), and **DON'T** add a new product or discovery story when the offer is more of the same. [14-02 v6 17:31–18:01; 22:17–22:44; Claude Upsell Process docx](../lessons/14-02-upsells-case-studies.md)
- **UP11 DON'T** accept lines that insult logic or make the viewer "feel like shit"; quote them back and ask for an empathetic, logical version. [14-03 02:38–04:11; 03:09; doc 3.1](../lessons/14-03-claude-projects-for-upsells.md)
- **UP12 DO** run the Upsell Project as taught: instruction block in the Project, five swipes as PDFs, model the named swipe's structure, length, style and tone section by section; give a landing-page link for physical products or the brief for info products. [14-03 00:00–01:01; 03:09–03:41; 05:12](../lessons/14-03-claude-projects-for-upsells.md)
- **UP13 IF** the upsell is a call **THEN** sell the call, not the price. [14-03 08:17–09:07](../lessons/14-03-claude-projects-for-upsells.md)

## 16. Compliance hard stops

These override every rule above. Most course examples (VSLs, briefs, advertorials, upsells, emails, generated outputs) contain invented doctors, testimonials, studies, counts, disease and drug-comparison claims, income claims and fake scarcity. The lesson pages flag them as course content: **structure only; claims not reusable.** Compliant pattern: our verified facts, `[VERIFY: …]` placeholders for anything unconfirmed, structure/function wording for supplements. Full pattern list: [compliance red flags](../concepts/compliance-red-flags.md); per-draft checklist: [LLM output claim audit](../rubrics/llm-output-claim-audit.md).

- **X01 DON'T** ship any person, credential, testimonial, study, statistic, review count, rating, price anchor, guarantee term, bonus or scarcity line that an LLM generated. Brief and script generators invent them by design ("Harvard researchers…", "97% of eye creams fail", "45% wrinkle reduction in 60 days", "Laura, 42", "Dr. Elias Vance", "$12,000+ monthly passive income", "85% success rate"). Bracket as `[VERIFY: …]` and replace with real, documented proof or cut. Even Stefan says of the demo testimonials: "to be compliant, you would obviously want to put real testimonials".
