# 15. The "Why Didn't I See This?" Framework
> A **hook generator** (3–5 hooks, not full concepts) in three steps: (1) [hindsight bias](../../../concepts/heuristics/hindsight-bias.md) exposes a mistake in retrospect, (2) [bounded rationality](../../../concepts/heuristics/bounded-rationality.md) absolves the viewer ("you chose from a bad menu"), (3) a low-friction upgrade they'll "feel smart for choosing." It's the only prompt with built-in example lines, and it is **Skool-only** (not in the PDF).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing **switching / competitor-conquest hooks** (the viewer already uses the old way or a rival product), when you need hooks that criticise the old way **without** blaming the customer, or when a competitor's winner opens with "You ever notice how…" / "Turns out…". It's ideal for the first 3 seconds of a UGC script.
- **Inputs → outputs:** brand + product + URL (+ the old way they use + why it's a "bad menu" + our low-friction alternative) → 3–5 three-part hooks (realisation → absolution → smart path).
- **Rules:**
  - DO keep all three beats. Skipping step 2 turns it into blame ("you were stupid"). Step 2 is what makes people "feel smart for surviving, not stupid for struggling."
  - DO update the date-bound example lines ("designed in 2004", "What worked in 2020 won't win in 2025") to the current year before using them as references.
  - DO make the step-3 alternative genuinely low friction (one change, one step, a trial). The prompt wants something "so rational, it's irrational not to try it now."
  - DON'T use it to expand into full scripts in the same prompt. Generate hooks first, pick winners, then write the body with another prompt (e.g. [#1](01-first-principles-thinking-and-loss-aversion.md) or [#13](13-counterintuitive-insight-and-specificity.md)).
  - DON'T paste the Unicode bold text into tools that choke on it. Swap "𝗽𝘀𝘆𝗰𝗵𝗼𝗹𝗼𝗴𝗶𝗰𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗿𝗴𝗲𝗱 𝗮𝗱 𝗵𝗼𝗼𝗸𝘀" / "𝟯-𝟱 𝗮𝗱 𝗵𝗼𝗼𝗸𝘀" for plain text if needed (see the plain-text variant below).
- **See also:** [Hindsight bias](../../../concepts/heuristics/hindsight-bias.md) · [Bounded rationality](../../../concepts/heuristics/bounded-rationality.md) · [#14 Reframing + Emotional Trigger](14-reframing-perception-and-emotional-trigger.md) · [#1 First Principles + Loss Aversion](01-first-principles-thinking-and-loss-aversion.md) · [Prompt Library hub](README.md)

## The prompt (verbatim)
Copied from the Skool lesson page, including its Unicode-bold words and the missing closing quote in the third example. **This prompt does not have the standard Objective / Psychology / Instructions / Format / Output structure of #1–#14.** It's written as a first-person instruction with examples.

```text
Instruction:

I want to create 𝗽𝘀𝘆𝗰𝗵𝗼𝗹𝗼𝗴𝗶𝗰𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗿𝗴𝗲𝗱 𝗮𝗱 𝗵𝗼𝗼𝗸𝘀 using:

- Hindsight Bias – our brain’s favorite lie: “I knew it all along.”

- Bounded Rationality – the idea that we make decisions based on limited information, not perfect logic.

These hooks should make people realize they’ve been missing something obvious—then hand them a clear, low-friction upgrade they’ll feel smart for choosing.

Step 1: Trigger “Wait, Why Didn’t I See This?”

Use Hindsight Bias to expose a common mistake in retrospect. Make the reader feel like they should’ve caught it—but didn’t.

Examples:

• “You ever notice how nobody actually finishes their protein powder?”
• “Of course the old way didn’t work. It was designed in 2004.”
• “Turns out, ‘add to cart’ doesn’t mean ‘buy.’ You knew that, right?

Step 2: Show How the Brain Made the Best of a Bad Menu

Now, leverage Bounded Rationality: remind them that they didn’t fail—they just chose from bad or incomplete options. Make them feel smart for surviving, not stupid for struggling.

Examples:

• “You weren’t doing it wrong. You just didn’t know there was another option.”
• “You made the best choice… with what you thought you had.”
• “This isn’t about willpower. It’s about better inputs.”

Step 3: Offer a Simpler, Smarter Path Forward

Wrap it up with a low-friction alternative—something that feels so rational, it’s irrational not to try it now.

Examples:

• “What worked in 2020 won’t win in 2025. This will.”
• “One simple change. One giant leap for your sanity.”
• “The smart choice isn’t always obvious. Until now.”

Your Task:

Use this framework to write 𝟯-𝟱 𝗮𝗱 𝗵𝗼𝗼𝗸𝘀 that:

 - Start with a hindsight-flavored realization
 - Reframe the user’s past choice as rational, but limited
 - Offer a present-day solution that feels smart, obvious, and easy
```

## Why the pairing works
- **Hindsight bias**, "our brain's favorite lie: 'I knew it all along.'" The prompt uses it "to expose a common mistake in retrospect. Make the reader feel like they should've caught it—but didn't."
- **Bounded rationality**, "the idea that we make decisions based on limited information, not perfect logic." The prompt uses it to "remind them that they didn't fail—they just chose from bad or incomplete options."
- The sequence protects self-image. The viewer gets the "aha" of hindsight without the sting of blame, then a solution that feels like the obviously smart move. That's why the output is "a clear, low-friction upgrade they'll feel smart for choosing."

## When to use / when not to

| Use it when | Avoid it when |
|---|---|
| The viewer already uses an inferior method or competitor | The audience is new to the category. There's no past choice to look back on |
| You want criticism of the old way that still validates the customer | You need full concepts or scripts. This outputs hooks only |
| You need many hook variants to test fast | The "better option" isn't really easier. Step 3 collapses |

## Expected output
3–5 hooks that each "start with a hindsight-flavored realization", "reframe the user's past choice as rational, but limited", and "offer a present-day solution that feels smart, obvious, and easy." Each hook can be one line or a 2–3 line sequence (one line per step).
