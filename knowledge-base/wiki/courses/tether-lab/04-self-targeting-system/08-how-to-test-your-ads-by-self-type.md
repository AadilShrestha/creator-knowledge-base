# How to Test Your Ads by Self Type
> Put the Actual, Ideal and Ought versions of one ad into **one ad set**, change nothing else, and read the results as **identity signals** (who responds fastest, strongest, deepest), not just conversions.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing the test plan for a set of self variants, and reading results to decide which self to scale next.
- **Inputs → outputs:** three variants (A / I / O) of one ad + campaign context → a test brief (structure, controls, metrics, read-out timing). Later: metrics per variant → a self verdict and next iteration.
- **Rules:**
  - DO put all three variants in **one ad set, in one campaign** that gets good spend [00:57].
  - DO hold everything else constant: product, landing page, price, offer, and design as close as possible [01:18], [01:35].
  - DO read results as identity signals: speed, strength, depth of response, not "which converted best" alone [00:41].
  - IF a variant shows low CTR but high AOV **THEN** it's likely an Actual-Self win. Judge it on AOV and stability [02:12].
  - IF a variant shows high CTR but low AOV **THEN** it's typical Ideal. Check conversion before scaling [02:50].
  - IF an Ought variant is flat for the first ~2 weeks **THEN** don't kill it yet. Watch add-to-carts and LTV [03:25].
- **See also:** [Testing ads by self type](../../../frameworks/testing-ads-by-self-type.md) · [Rewriting ads for new selves](../../../frameworks/rewriting-ads-for-new-selves.md)

## TL;DR
- A self-test **tests identity, not "which ad converts best"**. Conversions are only part of the picture [00:26].
- **Setup:** 1) three variants (Actual, Ideal, Ought) in **one ad set in one campaign** that gets good spend · 2) **keep everything else the same** (product, landing page, price) · 3) **watch for pattern differences** [00:57].
- **Expected patterns** (from Sarah's own analyses):
  - **Actual**: lower CTR, **higher AOV**, stable returns.
  - **Ideal**: **high CTR**, low AOV, fast clicks, lots of engagement but little commitment.
  - **Ought**: **slow start**, then strong intent (add-to-carts) and **high LTV** [02:12].
- Brands stall not from laziness or lack of skill but from **bias**: targeting the same self over and over without realising [04:16].
- The loop: **diagnose → reframe angles for a new emotional entry point → test systematically**. No new offers or complicated funnels needed [05:08].

## Key ideas

### What you're actually testing [00:00]
- "Be very self-aware about how you test."
- "You're not going to be testing which ads convert the best… conversions are a piece of this puzzle, but they're not everything." [00:26]
- "What we are testing… is **identity**. So who's responding the **fastest**? Who's responding the **strongest**, the **deepest**? And that's… where the power of this system kicks in." [00:41]

### The 3-step test setup [00:50]
1. **One ad set, three variants.** Take the three versions (one Actual, one Ideal, one Ought) and put them "in one specific ad set in one campaign." Campaign structure varies these days. Use "whichever campaign is going to give these ads good spend" [00:57].
2. **Keep everything else the same.** "Same product, same landing page, same price, same everything. The only thing that's going to be different is the ad." [01:18]
3. **Run and watch for pattern differences in performance** [01:28].

![Three designed variants: Ideal "Spill The Tea, Not Your Wine."; Ought "Don't Risk the Bottle… Real Adults Bring a Flask"; Actual "For Wine Lovers Who Know… You Already Bring the Good Stuff"](../../../assets/tether-lab/04-self-targeting-system/07-three-versions-0230.jpg)
*The three self variants that go into the single ad set (same slide as lesson 7, shown again at [00:50]).*

**Caveat on the demo creative** [01:35]: the three example ads "are sort of the same, but they're not actually the same." She designed two of them herself because she didn't have the original creative file. "In an actual test though, I want you to do everything as close to… carefully constructed as possible. That way we can get some really good data on how is the messaging actually hitting." So match layout, image and design, and **change only the self-messaging**.

### What each self looks like in the data [02:04]
Sarah bases these on "a bunch of statistical analysis that I've done on running a bunch of these ads through the system."

![Slide: Actual Self – Lower CTR, Higher AOV, Stable returns; Ideal Self – High CTR, Low AOV, Faster clicks; Ought Self – Slow starts, Strong intent, High LTV](../../../assets/tether-lab/04-self-targeting-system/08-performance-patterns-0200.jpg)
*The performance signature of each self. This is the key reference slide for reading a self-test.*

| Self | Signals | Why (Sarah's explanation) |
|---|---|---|
| **Actual** | **Lower CTR · Higher AOV · Stable returns** [02:12] | In present-moment mode "you're usually not going to be click happy. You're just… scrolling through, trying to get to what you want to see." But AOV runs higher "because you're so aligned… with that particular product that your identity gets activated after the ad." |
| **Ideal** | **High CTR · Low AOV · Faster clicks** [02:50] | People who want to become something are "really click happy because they're trying to figure it out… in this kind of exploratory evaluative mode… somebody give me an answer." AOV is lower because "they don't really trust the product yet." "Lots of engagement on your ads, just like not a whole lot of commitment." |
| **Ought** | **Slow starts · Strong intent · High LTV** [03:25] | "We've had this ad running for like two weeks and nothing's going on. And then all of a sudden it will… hit." These customers have "strong intent… lots of add to carts, high LTV." "As soon as they get attached to doing something out of duty, they will continue to do it." They become "very, very loyal, very quickly because you feel this sense of responsibility to yourself, to your family." Sarah: "such a good customer type for businesses." |

Implications for test management *(my reading, not stated as rules in the course)*:
- Don't kill an Ought variant early for a slow start. Sarah describes ~two weeks of nothing before it hits.
- Don't crown an Ideal variant on CTR alone. Check AOV and conversion.
- Judge Actual variants on AOV and stability, not click-through.

### Why brands stall [04:16]
- "Most brands kind of stall out, not because they're lazy or unskilled… it's not because we're bad at creative… I think we stall out mostly because we hit a wall. **We're targeting the same version of the same customer over and over and over.** And the worst part is we don't even realize we're doing it… because we get bias. Marketers are human too." [04:21]
- Symptoms: "your ads may be working, but maybe they're not scaling… your angles might be converting, but then they tend to fatigue… we can't get… any other angle to work. It's just this one. And what happens if that one stops working?"
- "If you narrow in on [one] identity, it's a pretty **unsafe way to scale**." [04:58]

### The full system, in one line [05:08]
- "**Diagnose** who we're speaking to, **reframe** all of our angles for that kind of new emotional entry point, and then **test** it all using… a very systematized messaging program that matches how your customers see themselves today in three different versions."
- This makes your ads "a little bit more of this **auto-target** kind of a system and reduce angle fatigue, angle rut" [05:30].
- "No new offers are needed, no complicated funnels, really just a deeper understanding of sense of self… Self-discrepancy theory is massive for marketing."

### Homework [05:54]
- "This week, I want you to go **run a self-targeting scan**, pick your next segment, open the door to buyers, and really just go target the ones you haven't even met yet… and see if you can activate a totally new customer, because at the end of the day, that's how we scale our ad accounts."
- Resources are "down below" (the e-book and prompts). DM or comment with questions [06:09].

## Knowledge units

**Rules of thumb**
- **DO** test for identity (who responds fastest, strongest, deepest), not just conversions [00:41].
- **DO** use one campaign, one ad set, three self variants, everything else identical [00:57]–[01:18].
- **IF** Actual is activated well **THEN** expect lower CTR, higher AOV, stable returns [02:12].
- **IF** Ideal is activated **THEN** expect high CTR, low AOV, fast clicks, lots of engagement, little commitment [02:50].
- **IF** Ought is activated **THEN** expect a slow start (maybe ~2 weeks), then strong intent (add-to-carts) and high LTV and loyalty [03:25].
- **DON'T** scale on a single identity. "If you narrow in on identity, it's a pretty unsafe way to scale" [04:58].
- **DO** loop: diagnose → reframe angles for a new emotional entry point → test [05:08].

## How to apply
- [ ] Build one ad set per test with exactly three ads (A / I / O), identical except for self-messaging.
- [ ] Track CTR, AOV, add-to-cart rate and LTV/repeat rate per variant, not just CPA or ROAS.
- [ ] Give Ought variants time before judging them.
- [ ] Record which self wins per product and funnel stage, and feed it back into Prompt 2 next cycle.
