# Guest Session 02: Chase Mohseni on Statics, the 3 Vs of Creative and Small-Batch Testing
> Open Q&A with **[Chase Mohseni](../../../people/chase-mohseni.md), CEO of [CreativeOS](../../../tools/creativeos.md)**. His argument: start with **static ads** (most brands under $5M shouldn't touch video), test **3 to 5 at a time**, and judge creative on the **3 Vs (Vibe, Variety, Velocity)**. "Variety" should mostly mean **variations on what already works** (new image, color, badge or copy line), not net-new concepts. Pair copy lines with images to find "customer genomes" and build up the account one small batch at a time.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** planning a test batch of statics or UGC remixes; deciding how many variants to make and what to change; checking an ad against its landing page for "vibe" congruence; writing creator briefs that reuse one approved "seed" asset; deciding between net-new concepts and variations; setting realistic CAC expectations with a client.
- **Inputs → outputs:** one winning (or approved seed) ad, its landing page, and the customer cohorts → a 3–5-asset batch where each asset changes **one creative primitive** (image, copy line, background, color, badge, icon, crop), plus a vibe check (ad ↔ landing page) and a copy × image test matrix.
- **Rules:**
  - DO start with statics. Move into video only once you have budget and a working account. "If you're sub five million dollars, you shouldn't mess around with video at all." [0:04:49]
  - DO test **3 at a time** (3–5 max). Keep what shows signal, kill the rest, add 3 more. "Volume is evil until you have data." [0:06:44; 0:07:58; 0:58:21]
  - DO run every concept through the **3 Vs**: **Vibe** (does the ad match the brand and the post-click page?), **Variety** (does each customer cohort get a version that speaks to it?), **Velocity** (how fast can an idea become an approved, live asset?). Velocity comes last: speed with the wrong vibe is worthless. [0:08:19–0:12:50]
  - DO build variety from **creative primitives**: copy, product, background, color, icon, badge, crop or camera angle. "A new concept is a new color, a new icon." [0:06:25; 0:19:10; 0:21:11]
  - DO keep three buckets live in the account: **net-new experiments**, **scaling winners**, and **variations of the scaling winners**. Spend most effort on variations. [0:21:46–0:22:31]
  - DO test copy against images as a grid (copy A × images A–D, then copy B × images A–D). Different pairs win with different customer types ("customer genomes", "combination marketing"). [0:56:48–0:57:55]
  - DO get one **seed asset** approved, then ship its variations without new sign-off. "Permission equals speed." [0:48:08]
  - DO treat a record month (e.g. CAC $25 against a usual $50) as an **outlier**, not the new baseline. Aim for a steady grind ($50 → $45 → $42 → $39). [0:30:40–0:31:44]
  - DON'T "spray and pray" 20+ ads and let the algorithm sort it out. In a 50–100-ad batch the best ad may get $4 of spend and never show signal. [0:10:43; 0:43:13]
  - DON'T chase net-new concepts at 3× the rate you need them. You burn out, overspend, and never learn what to double down on. [0:21:11–0:22:31]
  - DON'T copy test-volume playbooks from 9-figure brands (HexClad, Ridge, Jones Road, Cuts) if the brand is under $10M. [0:40:00–0:41:01]
  - DON'T expect publish-ready output from AI narrative video editing. Lower the bar for organic, raise it for the ad account. [0:28:35]
  - IF there's no unique landing page THEN duplicate the home page and change the copy. Only about 30% of the ads Chase has seen run to a unique landing page. [0:18:43–0:20:11]

## TL;DR
- **Statics first.** They're cheap to make and test, and anyone can iterate on them. Video needs a lot of volume and someone who can "instrument" the testing. Most brands under $5M should stay on statics. [0:04:18–0:06:32]
- **3 Vs of creative:** **Vibe** (brand and post-click congruence; you get less than half a second on the site), **Variety** (what each cohort inside your ICP wants to see), **Velocity** (time from idea to an approved live asset). Velocity sits below the other two. [0:07:58–0:12:50]
- **Variety ≠ net new.** It's mostly variations of what works: a new image, a new word in the copy, another shot from the same photo shoot, a new background, icon or badge, a crop. [0:19:10–0:22:31]
- **Small-batch testing:** launch 3, keep the one with signal, add 3, and keep going until you have about 10 live ads and 3 that are "crushing". [0:58:52]
- **Leverage creates margin (time and money). Margin pays for experiments.** Work out what works and double down, then bet on the "bleeding edge" with what's left over. [0:23:17–0:25:24]
- On clients: a lucky month isn't a baseline. Set expectations from abundance, not fear, and treat the client's pressure as fear about their P&L. [0:29:39–0:37:58]

## Key ideas and Chase's methodology

### 1. Why statics first [0:04:18–0:07:35]
- "TL;DR it works. It is the best place to start actually when you are scaling your business and then move into video. I think people get ahead of their skis a little bit on video." [0:04:18]
- **Threshold:** "If you're sub five million dollars, you shouldn't mess around with video at all. Just iterate through [statics]. You might get one [video] that's viral but you're going to spend so much time." He brushes off the Veo 3 hype ("vo3" in the transcript): very few people are actually good at prompting. [0:04:49]
- **His data source:** "Before this I was VP of growth at an AI video SaaS company which got acquired, so I have all the data around static versus video." [0:05:17]
- **Why video is hard for small brands:** "You have to test a lot. You have to have a lot of volume to actually figure out what's going to work, and people get really discouraged" after 3–5 videos flop. Video needs "someone there that actually can **instrument** this." [0:05:26–0:06:04]
- **Why statics work:** anyone in the room can make and iterate them ("Sanket can do it, Leonardo can do it, Blaster can do it"). [0:06:04]
- **Creative primitives:** "There's a thing in engineering called **data primitives**. You can essentially do this with your **creative primitives**, i.e. copy, a product, a background, and you can do this really fast." [0:06:25]
- **Batch logic:** "Rather than going and making [a lot of] static ads, **make three, test those, have your next batch of three new ones**. When you find something that has any sort of signal (okay, I like this background; the customers I'm trying to acquire are responding to this), then iterate from there. What people do is they go **way too wide** instead of saying: the first thing I need is a **beachhead**." [0:06:32–0:07:08]
- **Analogy:** "Spaghetti at a wall… then your wall has spaghetti sauce all over it. Make a bowl of pasta, put it on a plate, see who in your audience likes which version of the pasta, and then fill the kitchen with that specifically." [0:07:14–0:07:35]

### 2. The 3 Vs of Creative (Vibe → Variety → Velocity) [0:07:58–0:12:50]
"Volume is evil. Volume is evil until you have data." Chase calls this "a framework that we use internally that I'm going to start putting on social."

| V | Chase's definition | What an agent checks | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vibe** | "How does your creative match your brand so that when people are interacting with an ad and then they go to the **post-click experience** [website, mobile or desktop]… do those two things align?" Example: a fun ad on a serious brand. Going from "an ad by **Dr. Squatch**" to the **Venus et Fleur** site: "yo, what are we… I'm out." People bounce fast: "you have **less than half a second** to get [them] to stick with you." Vibe is "the first port of call in your creative journey." | Does the ad's tone, palette, humor and promise match the landing page? Same emotional zone and self on both sides? | [0:08:26–0:09:38] |
| **Variety** | Your ICP contains several **archetypes or cohorts**, "and within that group of people they have variety that they want to see within your creative." Use tight 3–5-ad test cycles to find what to iterate on, rather than "here's 20 ads, the three archetypes will figure it out, the algorithm will work it out." | One variant per cohort or self; variants built from primitives | [0:09:38–0:10:43] |
| **Velocity** | Not "test fast, test a lot." "Velocity is the **quality and quickness with which you can get from an idea to an agreed-upon asset that can go live**," whether you're agreeing with yourself or a five-person committee. That's when you start getting signal from "your real boss, which is your **customer**." | Time from brief to approved live asset; approval steps | [0:10:43–0:11:47] |

- **Order matters:** "Velocity being **subservient to the other two**, because if you have bad stuff that doesn't align, it doesn't matter if you're able to turn out 50 ads in a day. If they don't match those two things you're never going to convert at the highest level possible, or get your customers to stick with you, expand, and then tell their friends." [0:11:47–0:12:50]
- **Ferrero aside:** from a podcast about Ferrero (Kinder, Tic Tac, Nutella, Ferrero Rocher; "maybe 20 billion dollars in volume every year"), the founder said "I'm just building for **Mrs Valeria**, this is the only person I care about." [0:09:57–0:10:22] *(Outside context: Michele Ferrero's imagined typical customer, "la signora Valeria".)*
- **Why platforms push volume:** "Follow the dollars. What makes Meta more money?… They want to know that you, Sarah, are going to test **25 ads a month**, because they've said you've got to test 25 ads a month." Ad spend drives the platform's earnings forecasts. [0:10:43–0:11:13]
- Sarah links the 3 Vs to the course: "what we learn in here in particular is all about that vibe… vibe comes down to how well can you actually communicate the message." [0:12:50] (Vibe in Tether Lab terms = matching [emotional zone](../../../concepts/emotional-zones.md) and [self](../../../concepts/three-selves.md) across ad and page. *Wiki interpretation.*)

### 3. Templates as a "sandbox" for non-designers [0:12:50–0:18:00]
- Sarah's framing: templatization fixes velocity. "I usually duplicate the same kind of ad over and over… because I have one design that worked." [0:12:50]
- Chase (flagging his "financially vested interest"): the main constraint early on is **capital**, so you can't pay people while you focus on your "zone of genius". Tools like CreativeOS, Canva or bought template packs let you make things "quickly and at a high fidelity". "Not everyone is natively creative and there's nothing wrong with that… once you give people a **sandbox** to play in, their creativity, or what they've learned about their customers, which is the most important piece, can flourish. But when you say: blank canvas, figure it out… 'I'm not a painter.'" [0:13:50–0:14:54]
- **His mission statement:** "How do you make anyone into a world-class creative in **five minutes or less**? By giving them the tooling and the **spine** of everything, and then letting their creativity flourish." [0:14:54–0:15:12]
- "More brands than you'd think that you like and know and trust" use templates (Sarah: "can confirm"). [0:15:12]
- **Video equivalent:** **usebutter.com** (Butter), "a lot of motion-graphic style videos… it looks kind of like a static ad but we've animated it, and it looks really high-end." [0:16:13–0:16:50]
- **Sarah's view:** 8–9-figure brands she consults for "have a **templatized library of their own** that they just reuse… they don't deviate styles." DTC wrongly thinks "variety means it has to be a brand new ad every single time… brand new design, brand new concept." Real variety is "what's the best way to convey the information we're trying to convey: is it a graph, a symbol, an icon, a UGC, a static?" Templatizing ads is "one of the best ways to **systematize growth**… I don't have enough brain space to be that creative all day long." [0:16:59–0:18:00]

### 4. Variety is misunderstood: variations over net new [0:18:00–0:23:17]
- **Outlier stat:** "Only **30 percent** of all ads that I have seen… run to any sort of unique landing page." "Everyone [thinks] an LP is so hard to create. Well, you can just make a **clone of your home page and change the copy**." [0:18:43–0:19:10]
- **What variety actually is:** "a new kind of copy, a new word in your copy, a different product shot from that same photo shoot, a background color, a background change, an icon." Also a social-proof badge ("50,000 people like this"). [0:19:10–0:20:11] *(Chase adds "whether you had five customers or 50,000, you can put whatever you want on there." Don't follow that part: it's fabricated social proof, and it clashes with the course's "make it easier, not sneakier" stance ([Sarah Levinger](../../../people/sarah-levinger.md)) and with advertising law.)*
- "Diversity and variety: those are the two terms that people don't understand." [0:19:42]
- **No agency needed for an LP test:** "Click duplicate, change the copy… do the Bruce Almighty," instead of a full new LP build wired up by a dev agency. [0:20:11–0:20:26]
- **CR maths (as stated):** a $10M brand with a $75 AOV that lifts conversion rate from "a dismal 2% to 3%" makes "seven million" more. [0:20:48–0:20:56] *(Wiki check: with the same traffic, a 2% → 3% lift is +50% orders, so about +$5M on $10M. The point stands, but the figure looks overstated.)*
- **Where to find alpha:** "If everyone is doing the same thing, what is the thing they're not doing well? No one is focused on… what real variety means… A new concept is a new color, a new icon." [0:21:11]
- **Net-new overproduction:** people "are generating net new concepts at **3×** what they need to, when they can just make variations on what they have already… **Variations is what variety actually should be mostly predicated on**." [0:21:38–0:21:46]
- **Account structure:** "Your ad account needs to have your **net new experiments**, your stuff that's **scaling and working**, and then the **variations on the scaling stuff**," split between video (incl. UGC) and static ("10 different variations of what you have"). [0:21:46–0:22:14]
- **Cost of net-new-first:** "You're going to burn yourself out. Second, you're going to spend way more money than you need to. Third, you're not actually going to know what you can double down on." [0:22:31]
- **Leverage and margin:** "Figure out what works, double down, and while that's going you have time and you have margin to experiment into new things… Everything is about creating **leverage**." Example: search "purple fitness" in a 10,000-item library, get 15 results, edit 7 "right now" and have them "in my ad account in five minutes". "Leverage creates margin, and there's two types: **financial margin and time margin**." [0:22:31–0:23:52]
- "A **simple idea taken very seriously** actually changes the world." The DTC darlings "we work with all of them" all do the same thing: "what works, we're gonna double down, have variety within what works, scale those, and then test net news." [0:24:23–0:25:11]
- **The Sydney Sweeney example:** a big stunt only makes sense because the account is already "firing like crazy" and has margin to spare: "If they didn't have their ad account working so well, would that stunt have been as effective? No." It "got a huge amount of publicity and earned media." [0:25:24] *(Chase doesn't name the brand. Wiki interpretation: probably Dr. Squatch's Sydney Sweeney "Bathwater" soap launch, mid-2025. Unconfirmed.)*
- "**Start small and then win big** instead of thinking you've got to hit a home run out of the gate. Four singles gets you a run… 10 yards at a clip gets you first downs." [0:25:57–0:26:11]

### 5. First principles over guru playbooks [0:26:24–0:27:53]
- "Pretty much everything that you're taught online is because people are selling you [something]." Sarah's material is different because "if I start a grocery store tomorrow, **creative psychology hits no matter what**, but the way you're telling me to do my ad account for my supplement brand doesn't work the same way." [0:26:24–0:27:17]
- Ask "what are the **first-principle** things we understand about creativity and people? Those things have generally worked for a thousand years." Machiavelli's *The Prince* still describes politics and business 500+ years later ("600 years ago" as said). "The tools around what we do change… we're still advertising the exact same way that **David Ogilvy** did, we just have different tools." [0:27:17–0:27:53]

### 6. Baselines, outliers and CAC [0:29:39–0:32:45]
- Net new is "less about net new, more about **audience and messaging**: when you do something new it's because you're either hitting a new audience or you have a message that's cutting through." Always keep experiments running, but the goal is "a **consistent** $50 CAC" first. [0:29:39]
- **The e-com baseline mistake:** "You have one good month and that's the month you baseline off of. 'We hit 25 this month.' Well, you have 24 other months of data. What CAC [were] all those other months? 50. Okay, that's your baseline. **25 is an outlier**." Take the spread as a distribution "because you deserve it", or reinvest it into "something that can take you from 50 to 45 baseline." [0:30:40–0:31:18]
- "Chasing fairy dust" vs grinding "50 to 45 to 43.50 to 42, and in a year we're at 39. We cut 20%." "You don't cut CAC in half, and it's not sustainable because… the algorithm gods just change their minds every so often, your audience shifts." Focus: "How do you connect to your audience and what is the messaging." [0:31:18–0:32:45]

### 7. Why the industry chases volume and "the one ad" [0:40:00–0:47:21]
1. **Social echo chamber:** "Twitter: 100 voices and 100,000 lurkers." "What Ridge, Jones Road, Cuts, all of these nine-figure [brands], HexClad says about how you test creative is actually **detrimental to a brand that is sub $10 million**." From about $20M you can "take fringes of this." HexClad will do "like **345 million** dollars this year in revenue" ("from what I have been told"). [0:40:00–0:41:01]
2. **Macro economy:** "If I'm gonna spend this money… I just want to make sure there is **home-run potential**." [0:41:01–0:42:06]
3. **AI tooling:** people believe AI gives volume *and* quality. He asks: have you used **Arcads**, **Icon**, etc.? How good is the quality? "Do you have the budget to test all of the volume they're putting out?" Speaking "from a place of 'I was a part of the problem'", having been a provider: "Go test 100 ads. Well, what if the best ad you had out of that batch of 50 gets **four dollars of spend**?" Instead: take 5, keep the better ones on, turn the rest off, repeat. "Eventually you're going to find the one, and it's going to get enough spend to give you signal." Decide "what is a testing cycle where I feel good about signal." [0:42:06–0:44:13]
4. **Time compression and workload:** Chase journaled the full list of steps one person needs to get creative out the door. "Oh my god, the amount of things." In 2022 "we're gonna make one, just rip it." Now: research and so on, "overwhelming [but] necessary… once you get to the end of a cycle you go: man, this better hit." Find "the stack you feel comfortable with at your size of business." The core problem: "how do we go from **kernel of an idea in your head to actual asset in platform that is measurable data**… compress the time to live creative," whether ad, email or website. [0:44:13–0:45:15]
5. "There's just a lot more steps that will make something really effective in a **post-iOS 14 world**… a need to do… **Systems are freaking critical**." [0:46:20–0:47:21]

### 8. Agency and client management (Darian's follow-up) [0:32:45–0:38:00]
- Agency work is different: "**client management is actually the job**, because you're good at your job." It's about **expectation setting**: "This is a one-off. I work on 25 other accounts and every so often something hits… we ride it until the wheels fall off, but it's not recreatable every single time." [0:33:47]
- A script for the client: "This is an anomaly. We love anomalies, we will work to recreate them, but we cannot expect this anomaly to be the thing we consistently count on… There's a non-zero chance it performs at the mean or a little better." Then reframe the win: "I brought this down 15–20% month on month, we're still spending the same, which means more customers at a better margin." [0:34:50–0:35:52]
- **Mindset:** "Go from a place of scarcity [to] **abundance**: I'm good at my job, there will be more clients." Avoid "**energy vampires**." A bully "just needs to get punched in the nose a little bit… they were actually scared to begin with" ("punch a shark between the eyes"). [0:35:52–0:36:55]
- **Client psychology:** their pushing comes from fear: "they're dealing with their P&L every day… LTV… new purchase is not coming within that three-month window." "They kind of want a psychology session… Think about their side of the fence, and then you'll be able to craft a story." [0:36:55–0:37:58]
- Darian: "goes back to what you were saying the other day, Sarah: **20 or 30 ads a month is kind of all you need**." [0:37:58]
- Sarah: "Every human on the planet is really just looking for their parents… people have labeled me **brand therapist**… half the time my job is just to listen for a good 20 minutes so they can data dump." [0:38:27–0:38:49]
- Sarah: "There are odd beliefs in D2C that aren't factually real… like this idea that we need **net new concepts at scale**. That's insane, nobody can come up with that many ideas." The "**iteration versus variation** conversation that was big in 2022… now nobody's talking about that anymore." [0:39:00–0:39:31]

### 9. What a "variation" actually is (Nerf-party attendee) [0:52:34–0:58:52]
- "It's really up to you… what you believe will resonate with your customer." Example: "My customers are women 35–45 and this message has been working… how do you freshen it up? Maybe a **new lifestyle image**." [0:53:38–0:54:13]
- **Crop and screen-grab trick:** "People will take **screen grabs from their videos**, or take a photo-shoot image and just **crop it**." Example (brand unnamed): the full photo is a woman throwing a travel bag in the air ("absolutely absurd"); the cropped ad showed only "the cut-off arms with the thing in the air". "They just changed the angle." [0:53:38–0:55:08]
- Filmmaker lens: "Open up the aperture or just **change the angle** and everything about the story changes." "There's your side, there's their side, and then there's the truth… maybe it was just a front [shot] that needed to happen." [0:55:08–0:55:46]
- "Think less about what you like and more about your customer… **You're dealing with commerce, you're not writing poetry**." ## Framework / steps (Chase's small-batch loop, as an agent procedure)
*Wiki synthesis of [0:06:32], [0:21:46], [0:48:08], [0:56:48], [0:58:52]. Expected page: [3 Vs of Creative](../../../frameworks/three-vs-of-creative.md).*
1. **Vibe check first.** Put the ad (or the competitor ad you're remixing) next to its landing page. Same tone, palette, promise, [emotional zone](../../../concepts/emotional-zones.md) and [self](../../../concepts/three-selves.md)? If not, fix the ad or point it at a cloned page with matching copy.
2. **Pick the seed.** One approved concept: angle, hook and layout. Get sign-off once (client, team or yourself).
3. **Batch of 3.** Make 3 assets that differ by **one primitive each**: image, copy line, background or color, badge or icon, crop or angle.
4. **Read the signal**, keep the best, turn off the rest, then add 3 new variations of the keeper.
5. **Copy × image grid.** Lock the best image and test new copy lines against it. Then take each winning copy line back across the image set to find pairs per cohort ("customer genomes").
6. **Grow the account** to about 10 live ads with about 3 scaling. Keep the three buckets running: net-new experiments (small share), scaling winners, variations of winners.
7. **Baseline honestly.** Report the median CAC, not the best month. Put outlier gains into the next small step down.

### Rules of thumb
- **DO** start with statics; video only once the account works and the budget can absorb video testing (Chase's line: sub-$5M → no video). [0:04:49]
- **DO** test in batches of 3 (3–5 max) and iterate on whatever shows signal. [0:06:44; 0:58:21]
- **DON'T** launch 20–100 ads at once and let the algorithm pick. Winners can starve at $4 spend. [0:10:43; 0:43:13]
- **DO** check vibe (ad ↔ brand ↔ landing page) before anything else. [0:08:26]
- **DO** define variety by cohort: each archetype inside the ICP gets the version it wants to see. [0:09:38]
- **DO** measure velocity as idea → approved live asset, not as ads per day. [0:11:26]
- **IF** velocity is high but vibe or variety are off **THEN** stop shipping. Speed only multiplies bad creative. [0:11:47]
- **DO** make most new creative **variations** (copy word, product shot, background, color, icon, badge, crop), not net-new concepts. [0:19:10; 0:21:46]
- **DO** keep net-new experiments, scaling winners and variations of winners running together. [0:21:46]
- **IF** there's no dedicated LP **THEN** duplicate the home page and change the copy. [0:19:10; 0:20:11]
- **DO** get one seed asset approved, then ship variations without re-approval ("permission = speed"). [0:48:08]
- **DO** test copy and image as a matrix to find per-cohort pairs. [0:56:48]
- **DO** baseline on the typical month, not the best one. Reinvest outlier gains into small, durable cuts. [0:30:56–0:31:44]
- **DON'T** copy 9-figure brands' testing volume below roughly $10–20M revenue. [0:40:46–0:41:01]
- **DON'T** expect AI narrative video editing (e.g. Opus) to be ad-ready without heavy human editing. [0:28:35]
- **DO** use e-commerce ad patterns even for non-e-com clients (e.g. local services). E-com "is still the best at actual ad marketing". [0:50:01]
- **DO** base creative choices on the customer, not your taste: "You're dealing with commerce, you're not writing poetry." [0:55:46]
- **DON'T** put made-up numbers on social-proof badges (wiki rule; contradicts an aside at [0:19:42]).

## How to apply
- [ ] For each competitor ad you tear down, open its landing page and write one line on **vibe match** (tone, zone, self, palette).
- [ ] When remixing a winning ad, list its primitives (hook line, image, background, color, badge, crop, CTA) and plan **3 variants** that change one primitive each.
- [ ] For UGC: get one approved creator script (the seed), then brief variations as new opening shots, crops, b-roll swaps or a single changed hook line rather than new concepts.
- [ ] Map variants to cohorts or selves (Variety) and track a copy × image grid.
- [ ] Track idea-to-live time (Velocity) and cut approval steps by getting seed assets signed off.
- [ ] Report CAC against the median month, never the best one.
