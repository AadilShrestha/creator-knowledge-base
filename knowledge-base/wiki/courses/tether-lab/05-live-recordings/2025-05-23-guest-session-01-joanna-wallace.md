# Guest Session 01: Joanna Wallace (HexClad) on Using Humor in Ads
> Open Q&A on **humor in performance creative** with [Joanna Wallace](../../../people/joanna-wallace.md), who ran paid-media creative at HexClad (she has a comedy background). Her method: know the persona's insecurities and laugh *with* them; take the humor style from the people your audience already loves (Gordon Ramsay → cocky, cursing); mine reviews and comments for the jokes; pick **one** humor type and test it; carry real product information in every pun; run recurring characters (Paige the skeptic); and hire comedians and comedy-literate editors. Sarah adds a five-type humor taxonomy (from ChatGPT), the rule of three, "commit to the bit", humor as a top-of-funnel door to deeper topics, and the **messenger effect**.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing or briefing humorous UGC/ads; tearing down a competitor's funny ad (which humor type, what insecurity, who delivers it); choosing a brand's humor style; writing objection-handling copy for friction points (credit-card trials, shipping delays); planning a recurring creator character.
- **Inputs → outputs:** persona + reviews/comments + product traits + who the audience follows → one chosen humor type, 3–5 joke/hook lines lifted from customer language, a character or messenger choice, and a 2-week test plan; plus a teardown row per competitor humor ad.
- **Rules:**
  - DO pick **one** humor type per brand (exaggeration, bizarre/absurd, dark, deadpan, sarcasm, self-deprecation…) and test it for a couple of weeks before judging. [0:00:00]
  - DO build humor on the persona's real **insecurities and complaints**: self-deprecating, "I'm not the only one", never insulting. Make them feel seen, then their guard drops. [0:01:01–0:03:04]
  - DO borrow the humor style of the figure your audience already follows (HexClad → Gordon Ramsay: cocky, brash, cursing). Check the demographics before assuming who'll be offended. [0:03:04–0:06:10]
  - DO match the humor to the product's nature (a near-indestructible pan → "don't f*** with us"; a realtor → homes hold good *and* bad memories). [0:06:10–0:07:12]
  - DO mine reviews and ad comments for the brand's words ("pots and pans", not "cookware") and for jokes customers already make ("it's a weapon") and run them as "real user quote" statics. [0:07:12–0:08:17]
  - DO paste reviews into ChatGPT and ask what type of humor these people would find funny, then test that type. [0:08:17–0:09:21]
  - DON'T beautify self-deprecating content: ugly angles, double chin, bad bathroom lighting. [0:17:42–0:18:43]
  - DO use **periods**, not exclamation points, for deadpan and sarcasm ("it is the version of a straight face"). Keep the joke going in disclaimers ("*Please do not use this as a weapon."). [0:19:44–0:21:56]
  - DO use the **rule of three**: two normal items, one off-the-wall. [0:21:56]
  - DO **commit to the bit**: double and triple down. Backing out halfway reads as a lack of confidence. [0:22:46–0:24:06]
  - DO put **"meat information"** in every pun for acquisition ads ("sharp as f*ck" says *sharp*; "to the point" says nothing). Save cute puns for email, retention and the website. [0:31:27–0:34:35]
  - IF acquisition THEN intense, bizarre, exaggerated humor. IF retention THEN clever, subdued, pun- or character-based humor. [0:33:34–0:34:35]
  - DO consider a recurring **character** or mascot as a fluency mechanism (Tether's Dex) and a recurring **skeptic** who tests the claims on camera. [0:34:35–0:37:48]
  - DO use people close to the brand (employees) as messengers: **who** says it shapes how it's heard. [0:36:43–0:37:48]
  - IF there's friction (credit card for a free trial, a funnel bottleneck, a joke that went too far) THEN **call it out** with humor and say why they're still fine. [0:38:48–0:40:56]
  - DO hire comedians or a comedy consultant, and an **editor who understands comedic timing**. Comedy written by non-comedians "is obvious". [0:43:04–0:46:13]

## TL;DR
- **Humor = being understood.** The brands that do it well "embody that person" and joke about insecurities in a way that's self-deprecating, not insulting. Viewers feel "I'm not the only one", their guard drops, and they let the comedy (and the product) in. [0:01:01–0:03:04]
- **Steal the voice your audience already loves, and check the data.** HexClad's "Rolls-Royce of pans" (60% of the account) had to go after a legal threat. Joanna replaced it with a cursing, Gordon-style "best [expletive] cookware on the planet". Some boomers complained, but the data showed many boomers love cursing. It scaled to "most durable…", "best sale on the planet" and a pepper grinder "you don't want to f*** with". [0:04:06–0:06:10]
- **Customers write your jokes.** Comments called the HexClad pepper grinder "a tank… a weapon". That became "real user quote" statics: "Can be used as a weapon. Also the best pepper grinder I've ever used." with "*Please do not use this as a weapon." [0:07:12–0:08:17; 0:20:49–0:21:56]
- **Five humor types** (Sarah's ChatGPT list): self-deprecation, sarcasm, absurd/surreal, satire/parody, slapstick/physical, plus character-driven archetypes. Pick one, commit to the bit, use the rule of three, and use periods for deadpan. [0:15:37–0:31:27]
- **Funnel fit:** acquisition likes intense, bizarre, exaggerated humor and every pun must carry product info. Retention (email, site) can be cute and punny. [0:31:27–0:34:35]
- **Messengers and characters:** HexClad's recurring skeptic Paige ("Go off, Gordy") and Tether's planned deadpan British dinosaur Dex. The **messenger effect**: who says it matters more than what's said. [0:34:35–0:37:48]
- **Call out the friction.** For Lingopie's credit-card-for-free-trial barrier: "Yeah, we're sketched out about having to put in a credit card too." Hire comedians and comedy-literate editors: "trying to be funny and failing is not cute." [0:38:48–0:46:13]

## Key ideas and methodology

### 1. Start from the persona's insecurities, not "blanket comedy" [0:01:01–0:03:04]
- "The number one thing is to understand your **personas** and your audience and what they would find funny… it's really about what their emotions are and how you can make them feel heard and seen and understood." [0:01:01]
- Brands that do it well "really know how to embody that person… they can talk about the **insecurities**, but they do it in a way that's not insulting… self-deprecating, but not so far that… you're making the person feel bad". The goal is "I'm not the only one who has that problem". [0:01:01–0:02:02]
- Mechanism: "then they feel understood and then their **guard is down**… now I can be open about that problem and I can let that comedy in." Dentures example: someone else who has them can joke about the cup and the smell in the morning. [0:02:02–0:03:04]
- Instagram memes work the same way: the algorithm serves you embarrassing situations that apply to you, "and then you feel really seen… I can poke fun at myself because I feel understood and in a community of like-minded people". [0:02:02–0:03:04]

### 2. Borrow the voice your audience already responds to [0:03:04–0:06:10]
- Asked whether to study the humor of people the audience follows (Gordon Ramsay's "dark… intense… very brash"), Joanna: "Absolutely." [0:03:04–0:04:06]
- HexClad's core before Gordon: "**cocky, but in a confident** [way]… very proud of their product… 'you'll never find anything else that's better'… 'you can slum it but you'll come back to us'". [0:04:06]
- She "took my cue from him" because Gordon was already part of the brand, and "there's a certain persona that really responds to Gordon". [0:04:06]
- **Case:** when she arrived, "60% of the account was **the Rolls-Royce of pans**" and Rolls-Royce threatened to sue. Replacement mainstay for statics: "the best [expletive] cookware on the planet", which "screams Gordon". (The transcript only says "that's cursing"; the exact word isn't spoken.) [0:04:58–0:05:20]
- Boomers commented "cursing is inappropriate", but "I went and looked at the data… there's a ton of boomers who love cursing, so it's not alienating everybody". Not all content curses; "there's going to be a diverse amount". [0:05:10]
- Scaled into "most durable… on the planet", "the best sale on the planet", and a pepper grinder "you don't want to [f***] with". "Be careful you don't overuse it… you might be surprised who's responding to it." [0:05:10–0:06:10]
- Sarah: "**Match your type of humor to the product that you sell**." The pan looks indestructible, so "don't f*** with us" fits. Her (pre-recording) realtor example: homes hold good and bad memories, so joke about the bad ones. [0:06:10–0:07:12]
- Later Sarah names this a **halo effect**: "your audience responds really well to harsh, cocky kind of humor because they responded to Gordon Ramsay, so you were able to… inject [his humor] into the brand." [0:42:01] *(Wiki note: borrowing a celebrity's humor is closer to association/transfer; "halo" is Sarah's label.)*

### 3. Mine reviews and comments for words and jokes [0:07:12–0:09:21]
- "Your customers are going to give you that humor… read the reviews, read the comments left on your ads. People are going to tell you how they describe your brand and product." [0:07:12]
- Language rule (humor or not): "If people aren't saying cookware, say **pots and pans**… maybe cookware is too fancy." [0:07:12]
- HexClad pepper grinder: comments called it "a tank… a weapon… so heavy and big". (The obvious crude joke was "too far… that's not going to match with this brand.") They built statics from **real user quotes**: "Can be used as a weapon. Also the best pepper grinder I've ever used." labelled "real user quote". [0:07:12–0:08:17]
- Sarah's prompt: put all your reviews into ChatGPT and ask "**what type of humor do you think these people would find funny?**" (deadpan, dark, bizarre, exaggeration…). It gives you a direction; otherwise you test many humor types "and then get inaccurate data signals". [0:08:17–0:09:21]
- Starting rule (end of the pre-recording answer): "**Choose a type of humor first**, pick one… exaggeratory, bizarre, dark humor, deadpan… comedian level… pick one for the brand and then test that for a couple weeks until you can understand how does this hit with the audience." [0:00:00–0:00:11]

### 4. Mom humor: the unseen side, and kids who tell the truth [0:09:21–0:11:27]
- "Moms overall are just tired… acknowledge the struggle… having a child is not all beautiful Instagram photos and cute outfits. It's dirty diapers and no sleep, and you get to work and there's vomit on your sweater and you didn't realize." [0:09:21–0:10:26]
- Second direction: "**your kids are ridiculous**… the mean stuff that kids say that they don't realize is mean… 'what are those red dots on your face', 'you look tired'… when the kid insults the parent." Sarah: "Mom, why does your stomach fold that way?" [0:10:26–0:11:27]

### 5. Humor as the door to deeper topics (Scary Mommy) [0:11:27–0:12:29]
- Sarah: the Scary Mommy blog (~2010–2015) ran "crap tons" of advertorials, BuzzFeed-style. It started with top-level humor ("my kids are my co-workers and I kind of don't like my co-workers sometimes") and got darker ("sometimes I kind of wish I wasn't a mom… do you feel it?"). "The only reason that they could dip into more serious topics is because they made people laugh." [0:11:27–0:12:29]
- "If you can make them laugh at the top of the funnel, by the time they get to the bottom of it then they're going to be able to connect a whole lot deeper with you, because you were able to connect with them on an emotional level before you sold to them." [0:12:29]
- **"What are they complaining about?"** You have two options: revel in it ("vent and complain… that negative path") or "laugh maniacally… laugh it off". Both are forms of comedy. [0:12:29; 0:14:37–0:15:37]

### 6. The Golden Turd Award (team culture) [0:12:29–0:15:37]
- Joanna's team ritual: nominate yourself or a friend in a spreadsheet for "polishing a turd", i.e. making the best of a bad situation (an influencer sent horrible footage and you still made a top performer; a sale changed at the last second). Winners get a golden turd sticker and a comic "certificate of authenticity" mailed home. [0:12:29–0:14:37]
- Why: agency life means frustration with clients and influencers. "If you stop it… you scream and then you… laugh at it", instead of going down "venting and complaining and anger and resentment and then you hate your job". [0:13:33–0:15:37]

### 8. Voice differs by channel; puns must carry "meat information" [0:31:27–0:34:35]
- **Brian West's question:** must humor be a brand-wide through line, or can it be woven in where relevant? Joanna: "It depends… dip your toes, and if it starts to work and you get momentum, then you might want to make it a real part of your brand." You also need the right people writing it internally. [0:31:27–0:32:29]
- At HexClad, the email writer is "much more on the **pun** side", so emails and ads have different personalities. The website can "get cutesier" because people are already there with intent. [0:31:27–0:32:29]
- "Being cutesy in an ad has to also be very direct and very immediately understandable… just because it's a fun pun doesn't mean it's necessarily doing work." [0:32:29]
- **Knife example:** "to the point" next to the knife's tip "doesn't really do anything for us because it's not saying anything. It's clever, it's cute, it works for retention, but what am I saying about the product?" vs "**sharp as f*ck**" (Whisper: "sharp as buck"): "now I'm telling you it's sharp". "You have to have some sort of **meat information** in your play on words." [0:33:34–0:34:35]
- Sarah's synthesis: **acquisition** enjoys "intense humor, bizarre things, exaggerated things, really intense irony". **Retention** enjoys "clever things, a little bit more subdued humor, or… character-based humor… about the brand itself". [0:33:34–0:34:35]

### 9. Characters, messengers and the skeptic format [0:34:35–0:37:48]
- **Dex (Tether):** Sarah's "tiny little character… a red dinosaur and his name is Dex… supposed to be British and very deadpan". It's a **fluency mechanism**: "I want people to be able to speak the language of Tether really easily… when we see X we know it's Tether." Nobody in the research niche uses a character, "so I stand out just slightly from my competitors". [0:34:35–0:35:37] → [Processing fluency](../../../concepts/heuristics/processing-fluency.md)
- **Paige the skeptic (HexClad):** an actress, comedian, content creator and strategist, hired for it. Through line: "she is the skeptic… putting herself in the viewer's shoes of 'I don't believe you, this is an ad'… Paige is going to test it because she knows you don't believe the other ads." [0:35:37–0:36:43]
  - Example: Gordon says it's non-stick and you can flip an egg. Paige: "All right, Gordy…" then "whoa… go off, Gordy." Scratch resistance: she mashes potatoes in the pot and reluctantly agrees. Also done for knives, "back and forth with an expert", or watching a commercial. "Don't believe us? Paige doesn't believe us either." [0:36:43]
- **Messenger effect** (Sarah): "who says the message is actually a little bit more important than what the message is." Studies (uncited) on putting an **employee** in an ad: "she's so close to the brand that she probably knows better than even our top customers", so a receptionist, admin or PM sharing their experience "is critical for how the message gets interpreted". [0:36:43–0:37:48] → [Authority bias](../../../concepts/heuristics/authority-bias.md) · expected [Messenger effect](../../../concepts/heuristics/messenger-effect.md)

### 10. When it goes wrong, or there's friction: call it out [0:38:48–0:42:01]
- **Walker's question:** you took comedy too far; how do you recover? Joanna: "**Honestly, freaking call it out.**" [0:38:48]
- **Lingopie** (a Duolingo competitor; Whisper "lingo pie"): a free trial that requires a credit card is a barrier, and people forget to use trials. Her proposed copy on the card-entry page: "**Yeah, we're sketched out about having to put in a credit card too**… don't worry, you will get a notification… we won't charge it." Sarah's name for it: "**barrier buster**". [0:38:48–0:39:50]
- Trial email: "We know you're super busy… we sign up for 10 different subscriptions and then immediately forget about them too… you have three days left… here's a few quick ways you could use it, or let's be real, you've had a busy week, let's just extend it." [0:39:50]
- Principle: whether you went too far, have a product problem or a funnel bottleneck, "call it out… because you're not hiding it, people are noticing it… either they're gonna say 'they're trying to cover it up' or they respect you for being like 'yeah we know, and here's directly how… despite the thing you're concerned about, you're good'." [0:39:50–0:40:56]
- Sarah: **Wendy's Twitter** either claps back ("you knew who we were when you followed us"), blames a rogue intern, or shrugs. Also the Katy Perry tweet (chat). Two paths: "do what **Liquid Death** does… take people's negative reviews and use it more in your humor… double triple down", or "inject it in small places… by very carefully studying your consumers… start very high level… then… more intense with the emotions". [0:40:56–0:42:01]

### 11. Who should write and edit the comedy [0:42:01–0:46:13]
- "**Hire comedians.** First of all they are very quick with it, they are very good at pivoting and adapting, embracing different personas." Comedy has to pivot with the persona. [0:43:04]

## Q&A
**Q0 (asked before the recording starts; question not captured). Answer's closing takeaway: [0:00:00]**
- Choose one humor type for the brand (exaggeration, bizarre, dark, deadpan, comedian-level), test it for a couple of weeks, read how the audience responds. Joanna recalls a "cheeky, funny one… doesn't take itself too seriously" as memorable. [0:00:00–0:00:11]

**Q1 (Gabriela Núñez). I love humor but it can go wrong fast. A past client (full-mouth restoration, boomer audience) used humor that felt offensive (showing dentures). How do you know when you're stretching or it isn't right for your audience, and how do you articulate a brand voice that's funny but still professional? [0:00:11–0:01:01]**
- Understand the persona's emotions and insecurities. Be self-deprecating "but not so far" that you make the person feel bad. Aim for "I'm not the only one". When people feel understood their guard drops and the comedy lands. For dentures: speak as someone who also has them (the cup, the morning smell). [0:01:01–0:03:04]

**Q2 (follow-up, attendee not identified). Should we study the humor of people our audience already follows? E.g. HexClad and Gordon Ramsay, before he was part of the brand. [0:03:04]**
- "Absolutely." Find the brand's core (HexClad: cocky-confident) and the celebrity's voice (brash, cursing), check the demographics data, use it in a mix, and scale what works without overusing it. [0:04:06–0:06:10]

**Q3 (Elena, chat). One of my brands targets moms with young kids. Any mom humor that works? [0:09:21]**
- Acknowledge the unseen struggle (diapers, no sleep, vomit on the sweater) or "your kids are ridiculous": the brutally honest things kids say. Sarah adds Scary Mommy's humor-first, deeper-later arc. [0:09:21–0:12:29]

**Q4 (Brian West, read by Sarah; his chat: "*weave it in and out"). Does humor need to be a through line across the brand, or can it be woven in at relevant moments? [0:31:27]**
- Either. Dip your toes and expand if it gets momentum. Test it, have the right writers, and let each channel have its own voice. Ads must be direct and informative; email and website can be cute. [0:31:27–0:34:35]

**Q5 (Walker LeVan, chat: "If you ever take comedy too far – how do you backtrack or recover?"). [0:38:48]**
- Call it out openly (and the same for product problems and funnel friction). Example: Lingopie's credit-card barrier and trial-reminder emails. Sarah: Wendy's clapbacks; Liquid Death doubling down on bad reviews. [0:38:48–0:42:01]

**Q6 (Sarah). Anything you want to end with, your direction for humor? [0:42:01]**
- Hire comedians or a comedy consultant, and an editor who understands timing. [0:43:04–0:45:08]

### Rules of thumb
- **DO** choose one humor type and test it for a couple of weeks before switching. [0:00:00]
- **DO** joke about the persona's insecurities from inside ("me too"), never at them. [0:01:01–0:02:02]
- **DO** study the humor of people your audience follows and borrow the register. [0:03:04–0:04:06]
- **DO** check the demographic data before dropping an edgy line over a few complaints. [0:05:10]
- **DON'T** overuse a working shock device; keep a diverse mix. [0:06:10]
- **DO** match the humor to the product's nature. [0:06:10]
- **DO** read reviews and ad comments for customers' words and jokes; turn them into "real user quote" ads. [0:07:12–0:08:17]
- **DON'T** run a joke that's funny but off-brand (the crude pepper-grinder joke). [0:08:17]
- **DO** ask ChatGPT, with all your reviews pasted in, which humor type the audience would find funny. [0:08:17–0:09:21]
- **DO** use humor at the top of the funnel to earn the right to deeper, darker topics later. [0:12:29]
- **DO** identify what customers complain about; either commiserate or laugh it off. [0:12:29; 0:14:37]
- **DON'T** beautify self-deprecating content. [0:18:43]
- **DO** use periods for deadpan and sarcasm; avoid exclamation points. [0:19:44–0:20:49]
- **DO** keep the comedy going in disclaimers and asterisks. [0:20:49–0:21:56]
- **DO** use the rule of three (normal, normal, absurd). [0:21:56]
- **DO** commit to the bit; don't back out halfway. [0:22:55–0:23:02]
- **IF** you jump on a comedy trend **THEN** add a distinctive brand spin. [0:27:13–0:28:17]
- **DO** let each channel have its own voice (ads direct, email and site punny). [0:32:29]
- **DON'T** run a pun in an acquisition ad unless it carries product information. [0:32:29–0:34:05]
- **IF** acquisition **THEN** intense, bizarre, exaggerated humor; **IF** retention **THEN** clever, subdued, character-based humor. [0:33:34–0:34:35]
- **DO** consider a recurring character/mascot nobody in your niche has, as a fluency mechanism. [0:34:35–0:35:37]
- **DO** use a recurring skeptic who tests claims on camera. [0:35:37–0:36:43]
- **DO** consider employees and close-to-brand people as messengers. [0:36:43–0:37:48]
- **IF** a joke misfires or there's product/funnel friction **THEN** call it out openly and reassure. [0:38:48–0:40:56]
- **IF** you double down on humor **THEN** go all in (Liquid Death); otherwise inject small, start high-level and deepen. [0:40:56–0:42:01]
- **DO** hire comedians or comedy consultants; **DON'T** let a straight-laced editor cut comedy. [0:43:04–0:45:08]

## How to apply
- [ ] From reviews and ad comments, list the persona's top 5 insecurities/complaints and the exact words customers use.
- [ ] Ask ChatGPT (reviews pasted) which humor type fits; pick **one** and write a 2-week test.
- [ ] Note the celebrities/creators the audience follows and describe their humor register; borrow it.
- [ ] Write 3 "real user quote" statics from funny customer comments, with a deadpan disclaimer if needed.
- [ ] For every pun, check it states a benefit; move pure-cute puns to email.
- [ ] Brief a recurring creator character (a skeptic who tests claims, or a mascot) and an editor with comedy timing.
- [ ] List every friction point (trial card, shipping time, price) and write a "call it out" line for each.
- [ ] When tearing down a competitor's funny ad, record: humor type, insecurity targeted, messenger, product info carried, and whether the brand commits.
