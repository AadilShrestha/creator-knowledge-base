# Live With Sarah! (May 7, 2025)
> The first weekly Q&A of the community. Sarah gives her priority order for applying psychology (**identity → emotion → behavior/tactics**, heuristics last), shows how one **core emotion** per brand becomes a message you repeat forever ("a reliable piece of calm"), explains picture-based surveys and how survey wording fakes answers (the cashmere flop), why "how to" → "how I" hooks work (authority + risk reduction), and works through her stock FAQs: why ads don't convert, which format to run, how much to spend, how to know an ad is working.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** deciding what to fix first in a teardown or brief (identity vs emotion vs heuristic vs format); finding one core emotion that covers many use cases; rewriting aspirational hooks into risk-reduction or "how I" hooks; writing identity-led ICPs; designing customer surveys; judging if a competitor's ad has really "hit" (comment signal).
- **Inputs → outputs:** reviews / survey answers / Reddit threads + product → (1) the identities the buyer holds, (2) the emotion each identity pushes out, (3) one core brand message, then (4) heuristic and format variants of that message → static headline batch, then motion formats.
- **Rules:**
  - DO prioritise in this order: **identity first, emotion second, behavior/tactics third** (heuristics, arrows, rounded buttons, colours, format). Tactics only boost a message that already hits. [0:06:14–0:09:40]
  - DO lock the **core message (headline) first**, then add one heuristic per variant in batches of 5–10 on the same creative. Heuristics are "secondary characters". [0:12:23–0:14:42]
  - DON'T iterate the core message when iterating. Iterate the **format** of the winning message: static → something that moves (GIF, carousel, animation) → bigger formats. [0:14:42–0:15:48]
  - DO write for **identity, not use case**. "Do you have wrinkles / sunspots / dry skin?" fatigued; "holistic natural girl + the 40 bottles on your counter" ran long. [0:17:51–0:20:05]
  - DO find **one core emotion shared by every use case** and turn it into a brand message you repeat everywhere ("a reliable piece of calm"). [0:41:21–0:43:43; 0:47:47–0:50:59]
  - DO lead with the emotional reason. Features are the **logical justification**, so they go second ("reliable source of calm in a delicious format"). Exception: **fashion and home decor are features-first**. [0:45:54–0:47:26]
  - DO flip aspirational "change your life" messages into **risk reduction** ("you don't have to change all of you, just be careful about these three things"). [0:22:12–0:23:45]
  - DO turn "how to" hooks into **"how I [achieved outcome] in [time frame]"** (authority proof + risk reduction). [0:21:48–0:22:46]
  - DON'T be afraid of some negative emotion. Most ads that don't convert are **too soft** or carry the wrong message. Think personal trainer, not cheerleader. [0:35:37–0:38:29]
  - DON'T trust ChatGPT's customer-type list on its own. **Verify every type on Reddit** (thread count), then TikTok. [0:30:31–0:31:34]
  - DON'T label survey images with names that signal status (the "cashmere" problem). Use ~4 images, and never offer leading either/or choices. [0:26:31–0:29:00]
  - DO drop stale research. November (Black Friday) behaviour is useless in May. [0:10:15–0:10:51]
  - DO judge an ad's depth by **comment volume and emotional intensity**, and by unprompted organic content about the brand. [0:53:44–0:55:37]
  - DO pitch psychology to media buyers **in the metric they own** (CPA, CTR). [0:32:39–0:33:40]

## TL;DR
- **Priority stack:** identity (formed in years 1–7, switched on and off all day) → the emotion that identity pushes out → behavior and tactics (heuristics, arrows, buttons, colours, format). "If the core message doesn't hit the identity, arrows aren't going to do anything." [0:06:14–0:09:40]
- **Testing order:** headline/core message on a static first; then 5–10 heuristic variants of the same message; then the same message in motion formats. The brain decides in **0.2 seconds**, "not three". [0:12:23–0:15:48]
- **One core emotion per brand.** A mushroom-gummy brand with "18 million use cases" kept hearing "I know I can go back to this" and "quiet the noise", which became **"a reliable piece of calm"**. Say it until you're sick of it. That's how brands (and Sarah's "psychology-based creative") get owned. [0:41:21–0:43:43; 0:47:47–0:51:32]
- **Identity + behaviour ICP:** a skincare client's use-case ads fatigued. Pictorial surveys showed customers have "40 bottles on the counter" (sunk cost). The identity-plus-behaviour ad "this is the last time you're ever going to switch" crushed and lasted. [0:17:51–0:20:05]
- **"How to" → "how I"** hooks activate authority proof and risk reduction. A $90k/day info-product client went from "Make six figures from your sofa" to **"Turn your organization skills into the college fund"** (stay-at-home mums; high-valence/high-intensity → high-valence/low-intensity). [0:21:48–0:25:43]
- **Surveys lie when worded badly.** A fashion brand's picture survey labelled fabrics, everyone picked cashmere, and the cashmere sweater didn't sell (people chose who they want to be). [0:27:20–0:28:46]

## Q&A

### Q1 (Lesley Cramer): There's so much psychology. How do you decide which heuristic or tactic wins over another (e.g., your arrow post)? [0:04:25–0:09:40]
- Sarah has studied psychology and advertising for ~17 years (since about 20; she turns 37 this year). Her Twitter is full of studies and models "because it's just interesting", but an ad-account creative strategy needs a structure. [0:05:14–0:06:14]
- **Layer 1: identity.** "At the core of ourselves… almost always identity", built roughly **from year one to year seven** while parents teach you how the world works, how to gain resources, attract mates and succeed. So the parent's generation shapes the worldview (boomer vs millennial parent, a hint at generational marketing). [0:06:14–0:07:16]
- **Identities switch on and off all day.** Teaching/entrepreneur mode now, "mom mode" the moment her son walks in. [0:07:16]
- **Layer 2: emotion.** "It is better for us to prioritize the identities of our customers first, their emotions second, because the emotions are actually built from the identity… the subconscious, the one that actually holds all the identity information, is the one that's using emotion to push the identity out." Ask: which identities does my customer hold, then which emotions come out of each identity and what are they attached to? [0:07:16–0:08:17] See [subconscious vs conscious mind](../../../concepts/subconscious-vs-conscious-mind.md).
- Sarah's own identities: entrepreneur, mom, "avid coffee drinker", lover of humour ("comedians, cat memes… if it's funny it's for Sarah"). They "combine really easily". [0:08:12–0:08:28]
- **Layer 3: behavior/tactics.** "Arrows, rounded buttons, colors… even like ad format, all secondary to identity and emotion." Arrows "are basically like a secondary… just to boost results in the account, not necessarily the core piece." [0:08:28–0:09:40]

### Q2 (attendee, probably Walker LeVan): What's your timeline from nothing → research → a finished ad? [0:09:40–0:12:06]
- Sarah is "constantly gathering". She has a **research roster** (to be released as a course; she ran through it with Walker) of high-level industry/market research, "because how our industry shifts changes how people feel about the actual product." [0:09:52–0:10:15]
- **Cadence:** monthly research and weekly research, constantly **updating, not adding**. "You should not be keeping all of your research." November data is Black Friday behaviour, useless in May, unless you deliberately track seasonality month by month. [0:10:15–0:10:51]
- Storage doesn't matter (spreadsheet, chat, notepad) "as long as it's continuously updated and kept clean". Then she does **"combination marketing"**: "I never ever ever just want to like ideate stuff because Sarah's brain is biased… I don't want to just guess." [0:10:51–0:11:47]
- **Benchmark:** with a full research bank, "maybe a half hour to generate a stack". That means one new static ready for the ad account, not a fully produced UGC video. [0:11:47–0:12:06]

### Q3 (Darian James): We test emotional-bucket headlines on statics. Should heuristics be in those tests or come in once a headline hits? [0:12:06–0:15:48]
- Heuristics "apply to every human on the planet" and are "just a mental shortcut". Social proof = "a lot of people think this is safe, so if I… ingest it, it won't kill me." [0:12:23–0:12:45]
- **The 4–5 heuristics DTC uses today:** social proof, authority proof, urgency, scarcity, "and a little bit of like price-ish offer". [0:12:45–0:13:03]
- **Order:** "Prioritize your headline first, prioritize your core message… add a heuristic second. Heuristics for me are secondary characters, what we just call D2C best practices." [0:13:03–0:13:29]
- **0.2 seconds:** "Everybody thinks it's three seconds. It's not. It's 0.2 seconds is how fast the brain is processing what your message says and trying to decide whether it should waste energy to stop on the ad." Especially on statics. [0:13:29–0:13:46]
- Deploy heuristics "in like five or ten batches… the same creative, five different headlines". [0:13:46]
- **Darian's process (confirmed by Sarah):** stage 1 = minimal heuristics (headline + power image + maybe USPs), find what gets good CTR, narrow; stage 2 = push the winners into heuristic batches (goal gradient, social proof, expert). [0:14:12–0:14:42]
- **Sarah's iteration rule:** "I don't like to iterate the core message… I like to iterate the core message in different formats." Build a strong static (headline, subhead), then run "the same message in different formats": add social proof, authority proof, [goal-gradient effect](../../../concepts/heuristics/goal-gradient-effect.md), [affect heuristic](../../../concepts/heuristics/affect-heuristic.md), Von Restorff effect, "there's hundreds". [0:14:42–0:15:20]
- **Format sequence:** "I usually start with statics… the second format that I do is predominantly something that moves… a GIF, carousel, an animation," then duplicate the message into further video formats. *(Whisper: "expensive ads", probably "expansive" or video ads. Unclear.)* [0:15:20–0:15:48]
- "Heuristics just cut down on cognitive load." [0:15:48]

### Q4: Where do you start with ICPs, avatars, doc sheets? How far do you go? [0:16:05–0:21:30]
- Sarah's newest avatars mostly come from **pictorial (picture-based) or metaphorical surveys** that "pull out emotional motives underneath customer types". *(Whisper: "an odd survey". In the [June 4 call](2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) she calls these picture-based CIM / Core Identity Map surveys. That match is wiki interpretation.)* [0:16:11–0:16:50]
- **Demographic avatars are useless:** "She's Sarah, she's 29, she lives in Chicago with two cats… makes $150,000. That's just a snapshot… doesn't tell me anything about why she would buy from me." Hobbies, worries and frustrations go only a little deeper. [0:16:25–0:17:05]
- Avatar work "needs to start with identity, but then **behavior** is pretty important." Emotions are "like silly putty… slime", easy to push and pull because most marketers are good copywriters. "The difficult part… is which identity are we trying to activate and then what behavior are you trying to align with." [0:17:05–0:17:51]
- **Case: skincare client** *(brand not named. It is very likely Maelove: the May 14 Maelove scripts target "too many bottles on her countertop", and the June 4 Maelove static is "the last skincare switch you'll ever make". Wiki interpretation.)* [0:17:51–0:20:05]
  - Problem: every new ad "works for a minute and then it just freaking fatigues… we can never get that CPA below a specific amount."
  - Diagnosis: ads were all **use case** ("do you have wrinkles, sunspots, dry skin, oily skin?"). "I don't identify as somebody who has wrinkly skin. I actually identify as the opposite." Identities to test instead: fashionista, cares about interior health, "holistic natural kind of a girly".
  - **Behavioural research:** "What happens to this product as soon as it gets into somebody's house? Where is it stored, who's using it, how often do you think you use it compared to how often do you actually use it?" The pictorial survey surfaced an outlier: "I have 40 bottles… 30 bottles on my counter… 50… 15." They won't throw them away because they already paid (**sunk cost fallacy**).
  - **Message:** holistic identity + "you can throw away those 40 bottles… **this is the last time you're ever going to switch**." "That ad crushed and had lots of longevity because it was connected to who she thought she was and the experience she was actually having."
- **Acquisition rule:** "If you're struggling to get top of funnel customers in, it's predominantly because you're making ads to a middle of the funnel customer… just to get in one type of person." (She "hates the funnel" as an oversimplification.) [0:20:05–0:20:58]
- Extra methods: survey customers about hobbies, who they are outside the product, friends, family time; or paste lifestyle findings into ChatGPT: "give us a good ICP… so that we can start understanding identity and behavior". [0:20:58–0:21:30]

### Attendee (coaching-space agency; clients spend six to seven figures a month): turning hooks from "how to" into "how I" was a massive unlock. Why does it work? [0:21:30–0:26:16]
- **Their pattern:** "instead of *this is how to achieve [dream outcome]*… *this is how I achieved [dream outcome] in [time frame]*." [0:21:48–0:22:04]
- **Sarah:** "How I activates the **authority proof** heuristic. How I says I actually went through this experience and I now know all of the speed bumps that you're about to hit, so I'm going to teach you how to avoid them… somebody else went and did the hard work for me." You're also "activating **risk reduction** behavior". [0:22:12–0:22:46]
- **Against aspirational everything:** "We think aspirational everything… it's all about the transformation… stop doing that, primarily because some people don't actually want to change their life. They want to reduce the risk of going through the process of changing their life… fear is pretty big for humans." [0:22:46–0:23:06]
- **Ethics:** "Psychology is amazingly powerful, please don't use it for evil." [0:23:06]
- **Flip:** from "this is amazing, you could change your life" to "you don't have to change all of you, you just have to be careful about these three things that always come up for people when they go through this process. Risk reduction is huge in marketing… and often works a whole lot better." [0:23:06–0:23:45]
- **Attendee's case:** a consultant client spending **~$90k/day on Facebook ads**, teaching how to make six figures from home. ~80% of spend reached women, so the identity chosen was **stay-at-home mothers**. "Make six figures from your sofa" became **"Turn your organization skills into the college fund."** In the attendee's words, they went "from high valence high intensity to high valence low intensity, and the switch has just completely changed" results. The client said "you've just articulated exactly what we were thinking." [0:24:05–0:25:43]
- **Sarah's read (her generalisation):** "Make six figures from your sofa" probably works for a male audience, guys trying to increase income who want to "go up". "Most of these women don't want to [go up]… [they want to] take what I have and just use it better, apply it better." [0:24:52–0:25:12]
- The valence-intensity model "is a really old psychology model". Sarah built the course to explain why ads weren't working: "Oftentimes you're saying the right thing, we're just not saying it in the right way… It's not what you're saying, it's how you're saying it." See [valence-intensity module](../03-valence-intensity-model/README.md). [0:25:43–0:26:16]

### Q: Do you have an example of a pictorial survey? [0:26:31–0:29:20]
- Sarah can't show hers (brand-specific). Think of the **"which Harry Potter house are you in" quizzes**: you click images and get bucketed. [0:26:31–0:26:48]
- She uses **only four images** and chooses them very carefully, because images "could weight answers very easily". It took her months to learn. [0:26:48–0:27:20]
- "Pictures often elicit a better emotional response than logical surveys." She has "my own beef with **post-purchase surveys**". [0:27:20]
- **Leading questions:** "What's your favorite color, yellow or blue? Because there's only two choices I have to pick one, even though my favorite color might be purple." [0:27:20–0:27:50]
- **Cashmere case:** a brand (consulted last month) used pictures to decide its next product, a good start, but wrote the fabric name under each image (cashmere, cotton, polyester, silk). "Almost everybody said cashmere, so they went and produced a cashmere sweater. None of them sold." Cashmere is a signal: people "want to be someone who could possibly afford cashmere… that's who their identity was becoming… not who they were currently." *(Wiki tags: [aspirational identity](../../../concepts/heuristics/aspirational-identity.md), [status signaling](../../../concepts/heuristics/status-signaling.md), [ideal self](../../../concepts/ideal-self.md) vs [actual self](../../../concepts/actual-self.md).)* [0:27:50–0:29:00]

### Q (Walker LeVan): If you can't get a client's customer interviews or surveys, how do you determine identity? [0:29:20–0:32:06]
- Start "from the industry up": how does global behaviour in the category morph? Supplements are ingested, so buyers are "very careful about not poisoning themselves… very skeptical about the claims". [0:29:25–0:30:00]
- Find obvious identities (bodybuilders, heavy gym-goers) and **smaller viable subgroups**, e.g. 70–80-year-olds ("more silent gen") who take protein shakes to keep muscle mass. [0:30:00–0:30:31]

[0:30:31–0:32:06]

### Q: How do you convince your team to use psychology? [0:32:06–0:34:02]
- Her long-running struggle: transferring the skill set so she doesn't have to explain "this is why we're doing this" every time. [0:32:06–0:32:39]
- **Apply psychology to the metric they're moving.** Creative strategists and media buyers "only speak in terms of metrics… not trained to go look for psychology". So: "how can I apply psychology to **CPA**… cpa can actually be changed just by targeting a different identity. So today we're going to run some ads that are actually just targeting different identities so we can work on CPA", or CTR. [0:32:39–0:33:21]
- Teams lack "brain space and time", so build frameworks yourself and apply them to whatever the team is focused on. [0:33:21–0:34:02]

### Sarah's FAQ #1: "Why are my ads not converting?" [0:34:02–0:38:29]
- What people mean: "Which piece can I go mess with… product, landing page, creative, creator, UGC, format? Give me the one thing." That comes from a marketer mental model of tinkering until it fixes itself. [0:34:24–0:35:11]
- **Ecosystem first:** "If dog food sales are down there's nothing my ads can do to convince more people to buy." [0:35:11–0:35:37]
- **Real reason:** "a very weak **message-to-market match**… usually an emotional disconnect, sometimes a UX problem on the website… most of the time… our message is just not strong enough to get people to move." That's why valence was one of her first courses: "sometimes you're actually not using enough emotion." [0:35:37–0:36:00]
- **Cultural history (Sarah's view):** 80s–90s marketing and long-form sales gurus were harsh ("your life sucks… you should probably change that"). From ~2005–2015 millennial culture (positivity, inclusivity, "don't be offensive", especially in the US) taught marketers "I can't activate any negative emotion ever," which she disagrees with. Help people see "you don't have to stay like this, you don't have to suffer anymore. To do that you actually have to pull out a little bit of a negative emotion." [0:36:00–0:37:44]
- **Personal-trainer analogy:** "Your personal trainer is not sitting here going *you can do it, everything's fine, you look wonderful*. Most personal trainers are like *yeah this sucks, it's gonna hurt… do you want a six pack or do you want to stay the way you are?*" [0:37:44–0:38:00]
- Verdict: "We're either being **too soft** with our messages or we just have entirely the **wrong message**." [0:38:00–0:38:29]

### FAQ #2: "What type of ad should I run?" [0:38:29–0:40:48]
- What people really ask is how to pick the format, and **format is step four**: identity → emotion → behavior → draft the core message → choose the format that communicates it best. "This comes down to storytelling." [0:38:29–0:39:00]
- **Platforms are a lottery:** "If you're running ads you're gambling… competitors might be able to outspend you but they cannot out-create you… if you're more creative than your competitors you're going to do better." [0:39:00–0:39:20]
- **Algorithm = library (Sarah's metaphor):** the pixel watches site behaviour, combines it with ad behaviour, and builds "books on books" of every ad, conversion and behaviour ("here's our library of the Liquid Death brand"). It finds people who like the books already in the library, "which is not great for acquisition". [0:39:20–0:40:00]
- **Rule:** "Run the ads that have worked… rinse and repeat because you still need people to convert, but you've got to branch out… tell the algorithm, go find people who you've never seen in this library before." "Format… is just a vehicle, a mechanism to deliver the core message." [0:40:00–0:40:48]

### FAQ #3: "How much should I spend?" → the core-emotion case [0:40:48–0:43:43]
- Depends 100% on business model. Balance risk and reward: "We're paying to learn." She doesn't love the **volume game**: "I think it's kind of a crutch for not actually knowing who your customers are." [0:40:48–0:41:36]
- **Case: mushroom gummies** with "like 18 million use cases" (brain fog, ADHD, focus). Ads "sometimes work and then sometimes fatigue… I can't keep running millions of ads to all these different use cases." [0:41:36–0:41:58]
- **Find the one core emotion everybody shares:** "It's so critical you have a core emotion that applies to everyone… they're just using that core emotion for their specific problem." The repeated customer phrase was **"I know I can go back to this over and over and it will solve my problem"** → they want something **reliable**. And every use case wanted to "quiet the noise in the mind" → **calm**. [0:41:58–0:43:12]
- **Brand message:** "**a reliable piece of calm**". "Now the brand has one message… that they can now broadcast to every use case on the planet… we sell reliable calm." [0:43:12–0:43:43]

### Q: Is "core emotion" interchangeable with mindstates? [0:43:43–0:45:16]
- "Yes… you can use the mindstates to get to these." *(Whisper: "mind states". This is Will Leach's* Marketing to Mindstates *model; see the [June 4](2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) and [July 9](2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) calls.)* [0:43:43–0:43:58]
- For brand-core work Sarah uses the **BPE, the Tether Brand Personality Engine**. It identifies the core emotion you solve across all customer data, plus **what identity/enemy to go after "to show people who you are not"**. She offered to build it for the community. [0:43:58–0:45:16]
- "Most of the time brands want to be… everything to everyone, and this is how we just stagnate." **Liquid Death** "set down an enemy… corporate… big water basically is not telling you the truth… you should never drink water out of a plastic bottle." Other brands said the same, but Liquid Death said it "so definitively" and never deviated, so it took market share fast. [0:44:14–0:45:10]

### Q (Walker LeVan): My client's chocolate drink is bought for being sugar-free *and* for de-stress/brain fog. I built two avatars. Which do I lead with? [0:45:16–0:51:56]
- "Customers will come in and purchase from an **emotional reason** and then they will **justify it with logic** later. Logic is almost always attached to features." Sugar-free / tastes good = the logical reason. The deeper reason: "I brain fog constantly and I just can't get over it… it makes my day a lot better if I can have this as a go-to resource." [0:45:54–0:46:46]
- **Features-first exceptions:** "Fashion in particular, features first always… and home decor." People "don't get very deeply emotional" about those without heavy branding. Info products/education also lean on features. [0:46:46–0:47:20]
- **Her message for the client:** "**reliable source of calm in a delicious format**": reliable source first, delicious format second. Leonardo Larocca suggested **"delicious piece of calm"**. Sarah: "spot on… I would run that in an ad today." [0:47:20–0:48:09]
- **Brand vs performance:** "Brand comes from the internal and performance comes from external. We get to decide what we want to say, but the customer is going to tell us how they want us to communicate it." [0:47:47–0:48:09]
- **Repeat it forever:** "Find that core… message… and then never stop saying it. It's going to get boring as hell." Sarah started on Twitter in 2020 (COVID, iOS 14, "at home with two babies") and repeated **"psychology-based creative"** for ~5 years. "I accidentally built a brand on it… if you say it a lot it becomes yours." Competitors who say it get told "that's Sarah". "Get an elevator pitch for your brand… repeat it over and over." [0:48:09–0:50:21]
- **Examples:** **Dr. Squatch** went after relationship humour ("you stink and that's why people don't like you… get this soap and you'll smell amazing"). **Liquid Death:** skull-and-crossbones rebellion, "**murder your thirst**" for years. **Dasani, Fiji, Aquafina** "never had a tagline", or only emotionally neutral ones. "Pick your word or phrase, repeat it over and over." [0:50:21–0:51:00]
- Sarah has tested other ways to say "psychology-based creative" and "it always comes back to that because it's the easiest way for people to process it" ([processing fluency](../../../concepts/heuristics/processing-fluency.md)). Branding education is "the most vague thing on the planet". [0:51:00–0:51:56]

### Q: Do most ads use the same core emotion with differences per profile? [0:51:56–0:53:39]
- "Once you choose that core emotion of the brand, try not to deviate too much from the message. You can pick different emotions and I think you should." Test risk reduction, aspirational ("you can do it"), and **controversial/contrary**: "yeah, you can stay that way, go ahead… stay stagnant if you want." (See [reverse psychology](../../../concepts/heuristics/reverse-psychology.md).) [0:52:00–0:52:50]
- **Upcoming:** a training on "**psychological modifiers for chat**", because "chat is not built to emote… you have to actually tell it how to build you ads and headlines that are emotional." [0:52:50–0:53:10]
- Metaphor: core message = **vanilla ice cream**, "we're just going to put a lot of flavoring on top of it." [0:53:10–0:53:39]

### FAQ #4: "How do I know if my ad is working?" [0:53:39–0:55:37]
- Not just purchases, CVR or spend. "The best way to know is how people respond to it in the **comments**… once they align emotionally with it or identity-wise they will actively seek out a way to get involved." [0:53:44–0:54:20]
- "The best ads that I've seen… have like **thousands and thousands of comments** on them and they run for a very, very long time." [0:54:20–0:54:40]
- Read the **emotional weight** of comments: neutral ("yes I love this product") vs intense ("I will fight to the death to get this thing, it was the best product I've ever had"). [0:54:40–0:55:04]
- Second signal: **content made about your brand that has nothing to do with your ads** (TikTok, Reels, Shorts). [0:55:04–0:55:37]

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** apply psychology in order: identity → emotion → behaviour/tactics (heuristics, arrows, buttons, colours, format). [0:06:14–0:09:40]
- **DO** write the core message before choosing a heuristic or format. Format is step four. [0:13:03; 0:38:29]
- **DO** run heuristics as 5–10 headline variants on the same winning creative. [0:13:46]
- **DO** assume a **0.2-second** read on statics. Make the message instantly processable. [0:13:29]
- **DON'T** change the core message when iterating. **DO** carry the winning static's message into GIFs, carousels, animation, then video. [0:14:42–0:15:48]
- **IF** ads fatigue fast and CPA won't drop **THEN** check whether you're selling a use case instead of an identity. [0:17:51–0:18:40]
- **DO** research behaviour after purchase: where the product lives, who uses it, perceived vs actual use frequency. [0:18:40–0:19:22]
- **IF** a brand has many use cases **THEN** find the one core emotion they share in review language and make it the brand message. [0:41:58–0:43:43]
- **DO** repeat the brand's core phrase everywhere until you're sick of it. [0:48:09–0:50:59]
- **IF** the product is fashion or home decor **THEN** lead with features. **ELSE** lead with emotion and justify with features. [0:46:46–0:47:20]
- **IF** a top-of-funnel acquisition problem **THEN** you're probably writing for a mid-funnel buyer. Widen identities. [0:20:05–0:20:34]
- **DO** rinse and repeat winners but keep adding ads that pull people the algorithm "has never seen in this library". [0:40:00–0:40:26]
- **DON'T** default to all-positive. Pull "a little bit of a negative emotion" to show people they don't have to stay stuck, kindly. [0:36:43–0:38:00]
- **DO** vary emotions (risk reduction, aspirational, contrary) around one core message ("vanilla + flavourings"). [0:52:00–0:53:20]
- **DON'T** use ChatGPT as the source of customer types. **DO** validate each type on Reddit, then TikTok. [0:30:31–0:31:34]
- **DON'T** offer forced either/or survey answers or labels that signal status. **DO** use ~4 carefully chosen images. [0:26:48–0:28:46]
- **DO** prune research monthly/weekly. Don't carry Black Friday data into spring. [0:10:26–0:10:51]
- **DO** read comment volume and emotional intensity, plus unprompted organic content, to judge whether an ad hit identity/emotion. [0:53:44–0:55:37]
- **DO** sell psychology to media buyers as a lever on CPA or CTR ("today we test identities to work on CPA"). [0:33:05–0:33:21]

## How to apply
- [ ] For a competitor ad, tag identity → emotion → tactic, in that order. If identity is missing, the tactics don't matter.
- [ ] Mine reviews for one repeated phrase that cuts across use cases ("I know I can go back to this"). Turn it into a 3–5-word brand line.
- [ ] Rewrite every "how to" hook as "how I [outcome] in [time]", and one aspirational hook as risk reduction ("be careful about these three things").
- [ ] Write one ad on post-purchase behaviour (where the product sits, what piles up, what gets thrown away).
- [ ] Generate customer types with ChatGPT, keep only those with real Reddit threads, then check TikTok.
- [ ] Test headlines on one static first, then 5–10 heuristic variants, then a GIF/carousel version of the winner.
- [ ] When reviewing a competitor's winner, read its comments: count them and grade their intensity.
