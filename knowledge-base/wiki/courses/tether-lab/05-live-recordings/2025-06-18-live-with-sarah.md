# Live With Sarah! (June 18, 2025)
> Open Q&A from Germany with no screen share. Topics: where the [ought self](../../../concepts/ought-self.md) belongs in a coaching funnel (back end, not the ads); attracting affluent buyers (elevated language, "you're 70% of the way there"); luxury strategy (never bundle or discount, price up, gate access, compare yourself to something far pricier, as Maserati did at boat and private-jet shows); Sarah's ladder for applying psychology (heuristics → mental models → science of belief); a gelato brand's "burnt dinner" static that cut CPA from ~$22 to ~$6 and how to scale and iterate it; running evergreen ads during a sale; her precision testing cadence (3–5 new ads a week); creative diversity and the static → GIF → video ladder; and getting Gen Z to switch from matcha to a turmeric tea.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** choosing which self each funnel stage speaks to; writing hooks for high-ticket, luxury or premium products; iterating a winning static into a test batch; planning sale-period vs evergreen creative; setting test volume and format mix for a sub-8-figure brand; writing switching hooks that pull a younger audience off a competitor category.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a winning ad or funnel + customer type + budget → the self per stage, 2 iteration threads of 3 statics each, a format ladder plan (statics → GIF/carousel → UGC/pro video), a weekly test count, and "question the attachment" switching hooks.
- **Rules:**
  - DO open with [actual](../../../concepts/actual-self.md) / [ideal](../../../concepts/ideal-self.md) self in front-end ads ("I see you"). DON'T push ought-self "you should" messages until after the prospect has qualified; it reads as accusatory from a stranger. [0:08:25–0:10:29]
  - DO write actual-self hooks as **situations**: "You've checked the dashboard 20 times today and you still don't know what to do. That's okay." [0:16:42–0:17:43]
  - DO elevate language for higher-income buyers: "scale your brand", not "grow your business". [0:15:40]
  - IF the product is luxury THEN don't bundle, don't discount; price up, restrict access, and show it next to something far more expensive. [0:11:31–0:12:31; 0:19:49; 0:29:21–0:30:27]
  - DON'T say "limited spaces" to recruit premium buyers (it attracts "lack-mode" shoppers). DO frame scarcity as time ("this only opens once every six months") or as qualification ("see if you qualify"). [0:18:44–0:20:52]
  - IF a static wins THEN keep its core situation and test two threads: the same joke on more universal moments, and the deeper lived experience, ~3 statics each. Scale budget 10–15% a day while CPA holds. [0:38:03–0:41:19]
  - DO test new concepts as ~10 statics (one person, one emotion; or 3 emotions × 3) → GIF / animation / carousel → only then UGC, pro or "fake podcast" video. [0:59:15–1:01:19]
  - DO put many **formats** of the same core message in one ad set. Your ads compete with organic creators. [0:56:08–0:59:15]
  - IF you want switching behavior THEN make people question their attachment to the current choice or trend ("Is your matcha really doing what you think it is?"), rather than listing benefits. [1:03:25–1:06:33]

## TL;DR
- **Ought self goes at the back of the funnel.** Jamie's coaching data: the ads (actual → ideal) scale, but buyers who pay in full use ought-self language on sales calls. Sarah: don't move ought into the ads. "You're basically trying to be a very good friend with somebody when you haven't really made that relationship yet." Front end = "I see you". Back end = "you know you should be doing this". [0:05:15–0:10:29]
- **Luxury: don't bundle, don't discount, price up, gate access.** Affluent buyers "want it to be hard to access". Maserati, in the late-2000s slump, showed its cars at **boat shows and private-aircraft shows**: next to a jet, "this car looks like a steal". "Compare your product to something that's way more expensive than you." [0:11:31–0:12:31; 0:19:49; 0:25:06–0:30:27]
- **Sarah's psychology ladder:** heuristics change micro behavior (CTR, CVR; "you're the architect of a Disney World"), so use them to boost an account. **Mental models** (self-discrepancy, valence-intensity) shape whole campaigns. **Science of belief / core beliefs** (generation, upbringing, nostalgia) are for disrupting an industry. [0:20:52–0:25:06]
- **Situations beat demographics.** Ahmed's gelato headline "You already burned dinner, but it's okay, because we've got [gelato]" took CPA from ~$22 to ~$6 in four days. Scale it 10–15% a day while metrics hold, and test whether it works because people *lived* it or because it's *funny*. [0:35:53–0:41:19]
- **Precision over volume below 8 figures:** "I don't want to inject more than maybe 50 ads a month… I typically run somewhere between three to five a week as a new test." "~20 a month is very healthy… you can win with 20 ads a month." Volume testing works for 8-figure-plus brands that have production and analytics staff. [0:49:55–0:54:59]
- **Core message is everything.** Pick ~4 core messages for the brand and say each many ways and in many formats. "If you're a little fuzzy on it, people can feel it through the screen." [0:57:11–1:01:19]

## Key ideas

### 1. Which self belongs where in the funnel [0:05:15–0:10:29]
- Jamie (mostly coaching, a little DTC): the ad framework that scaled a client "quite massively" starts with the **availability heuristic**: "we were there too, and this is how we fixed it, come and fix it". In 3-selves terms, the ads move **actual → ideal**. From transcribed sales calls, the buyers who **pay in full** use much more **ought-self** language. Should the ideal-self CTAs switch to ought? [0:05:15–0:06:20]
- Funnel: ads → free lead magnet → upsell with a "triage" strategy call → second strategy call = sales call for the high-ticket program. On the first call, prospects say "I should be doing better". Jamie's AI analysis line: "most PIF buyers weren't dreamers, they were doers on the edge of fatigue". [0:06:20–0:08:25]
- Sarah: "My gut tells me no." "Ought is a weird marketing tactic. When you speak to the ought self… you're often telling people something that they already know… 'you should want this, you know it's good for you, why aren't you doing it?' Oftentimes that's something a close friend would do for you." It works at step 3 because prospects "are kind of ready to hear from an expert". In an ad it "feels too accusatory… 'well I know I should, but don't tell me'". [0:08:25–0:09:27]
- Front end: "create this relationship of care: **I see you**." After they get past "whatever wall you've put up just to qualify people", say "I see you, and also, girl, you know you should be doing that". "Then you can start to push a little bit." [0:09:27–0:10:29]
- Fireflies notetaker summary on screen: "Change the ads to focus on actual self instead o[f]…" (action item for Jamie). [~0:54:45]

### 2. Attracting affluent buyers: language, actual self, situations [0:13:34–0:17:43]
- Product: business coaching for network marketers, a **$5k** program ("mid-market"). These buyers are "very sensitive to ought, because they've been through enough battles in business to think 'I should know how to do this, and it's kind of embarrassing that I don't'". (Sarah to everyone: "Every business is completely different… give yourself some love.") [0:14:37]
- Target people for whom "a $5,000 test on a business coach is not going to be that big of a deal this month". Two changes up front: [0:15:40]
  1. **Elevate the language**: "I don't want to hear anything like 'grow your business', I want to hear things like '**scale your brand**'. 'Scale' in particular draws in a different customer type than 'grow'." Go "from high school level up to college kids".
  2. **Actual self first**, ideal as a second test layer. Prompt ChatGPT to "go after the person they are today and see if we can just align our products with who they are", instead of "you can level up". Say: "**You're 70% of the way there right now. You're just missing about 30% of it, and that's what we're going to teach.**" [0:15:40–0:16:42]
- **Actual self = situational awareness:** "You've checked the dashboard 20 times today and you still don't know what to do, but that's okay, because we're going to teach you how." "You've already gone to the fridge 18 times and there's nothing in there to eat, but that's okay, because you could just fill it with delicious gelato." "This is what's happening to you right now, and it's okay, because this is just the tiny missing piece. **Don't change everything, just change one thing.**" [0:16:42–0:17:43] *(Whisper: "salado" = gelato.)*

### 3. Luxury, scarcity framing and qualification [0:10:29–0:12:31; 0:17:43–0:20:52; 0:25:06–0:35:53]
- **Athleisure story:** a high-end brand "similar to Lululemon". "Every single person, including some big names", told them to bundle and discount. Sarah: "**Do not bundle, dear God, do not bundle.**" [0:10:29–0:11:31]
- Why: "Luxury-focused or exclusivity-focused customers, people who like to get things that not everybody can get, **want it to be hard to access**." They're often "the type of people that want things to be hard in other areas of their life". For high-ticket coaching, make it hard to access too. "Luxury is a totally different strategy… don't bundle, do not discount… because ought self gets activated differently for luxury products." [0:11:31–0:12:31]
- **"Very limited spaces" (Brian):** it "activates this part of the brain where it says this is going to go away real quick", which brings in "**lack shopping mode**" customers who "probably don't have the cash to continue". "This only opens up **once every six months**" is "a lack of time, not a lack of spaces". It recruits "I can't wait another six months, my business needs to move forward today… I'm going to dedicate time and money", a different, higher-level customer. "This is a little bit of [framing](../../../concepts/heuristics/framing-effect.md). How you frame the offer… is basically everything." [0:17:43–0:19:49]
- "**Maserati doesn't do limited anything.** They just jack that price up so that you can't get access to it unless you're a very specific type of customer." For luxury: "you need to out-price yourself… price yourself upwards in the market until you're so high that only a very select few can afford you." [0:19:49]
- Gabriela: for Jamie, a CTA like "**see if you qualify**", a qualification process ("not just because you can pay… we need to make sure you have the right mindset… your success reflects on us")? Sarah: "Yes, I love that." [0:19:49–0:20:52]
- **Luxury pricing (Gavin's follow-up):** luxury brands "jack that price way high up and then use **longevity of brand** to signal that they're more exclusive": Coach, Lacoste, Burberry. Lamborghini became "almost a commoditized luxury" after drop-shippers in ~2010–2015 bought, rented and photographed Lambos. Raising prices alone can crash a brand when people "have them categorized in a different way": "you have to know as a marketer which category do I fit in and can I go up-market easily." [0:25:06–0:27:17]
- **Sarah's own case:** she sold services at ~$7–10k to few clients (a part-time mom, ~20 hours a week). After tariffs / iOS 14 hit, clients wouldn't pay that. Going up-market meant DTC → B2B, which would take a 9–12-month rebrand. So she went **horizontal**: kept her prices, then added a "school setting", courses, YouTube underneath. [0:27:17–0:29:21]
- **Maserati:** when buyers dried up (Sarah: "probably five to seven years in the mid-2000s… everything's crashing in 2008"), "instead of taking their cars to other car shows… they took them to **boat shows** and… a **private aircraft show**, because next to a $2 million G6 this car looks like a steal… $200,000 to $500,000, I can afford that." "**For your product, if you're trying to go up-market, the best thing you could do is compare your product to something that's way more expensive than you.**" [0:29:21–0:30:27] It also filtered for buyers: someone shopping for a $15M yacht "can definitely afford" the car. "You want somebody in your ecosystem who has enough cash flow to be like, oh, this is nothing." [0:31:29–0:32:35] *(This is [anchoring](../../../concepts/heuristics/anchoring-effect.md) by context. The anchoring label is a wiki tag. The historical details are as Sarah tells them, not verified.)*
- **What are you qualifying for?** "Is it always price, or is it a particular type of person?" For Gavin (same-day, spontaneous events), qualify into a situation: "you didn't get to have fun as a kid because maybe you were raising your [family]… but now you have the time and the energy and the money to come in and just have fun with the rest of us". "Some people are fun and can't afford you… we want somebody fun who can afford what you're providing." [0:30:27–0:34:41]
- Sarah has a **qualification cheat sheet** (~30+ questions for the marketer: what do you know about your customers, what is this person to you, AOV/LTV/cost targets, who do you actually want) "to weed out bias". She'll drop it in the group. [0:33:40–0:35:53]

### 4. The psychology ladder: heuristics → mental models → science of belief [0:20:52–0:25:06]
Gabriela: "How do you decide when and how to apply a psychology concept… how do I determine if it will fit?"
1. **Heuristics** change "small baby behavior": conversion rates, clicks, where people go. "You're an architect… it's your job to build basically a Disney World for your customers to walk through. You don't want them to walk over in this direction, so we put a bush there, a tree there, a wall there." Social proof "helps… but it's just a hack, because it's a shortcut." [0:21:55–0:23:02] → [Heuristics](../../../concepts/heuristics.md)
2. **Mental models**: "self-discrepancy theory… valence and intensity, that's a mental model". "More about how people operate instead of just the short hacky stuff"; they "affect the entirety of your marketing campaign". [0:22:55–0:23:02]
3. **Science of belief / core beliefs**: "what kind of childhood did you have, did you have siblings, where did you grow up, what language did you speak… did you watch a lot of SpongeBob… did you see a lot of ads". Harder to apply, "but very much attached to **generations**". E.g. Gen X: "nostalgia is huge… I could probably make an **MTV** reference in the US and we'll bring in a lot of Gen Xers." [0:23:02–0:24:04]
- **When to use which:** "If I'm trying to just boost a little bit of what the account is doing, I'll use heuristics. If I'm trying to create completely new campaigns, I'll use mental models. If I'm really trying to understand our customers to actually disrupt an entire industry, then I'll go after science of belief." [0:24:04]

### 5. Scaling and iterating a situational winner (Ahmed's gelato) [0:35:53–0:42:22]
- Update: in an earlier hook brainstorm Ahmed's CPA was ~$22. The new headline "**You already burned dinner, but it's okay, because we've got [gelato]**" (transcribed "dessert") is running at **~$6 CPA**. A second ad is at ~$12–15. Launched four days ago, together with a new menu. [0:35:53–0:37:01]
- Sarah: "Pick a situation and just highlight the weird situation. It sounds weird… 'you want me to just talk about the dinner they burnt?' Yeah, talk about the dinner they burnt." [0:36:01]
- **Scaling:** "Sarah's a little bit more conservative… I usually up spend by **10 to 15% a day** as long as we're holding with our metric", meaning CPA stable and conversion rate holding. "Keep adding logs to that fire." Very conservative: +10% every other day. "The only way you know if this is a core message is if we feed it more fuel and it continues to hold." Worry that it won't hold = not yet sure it's the right message. [0:38:03–0:39:07] *(Whisper first says "10 to 50 percent", then "10 to 15 a day". 10–15% is the consistent reading.)*
- **Iterate with a hypothesis:** is it working because people *experienced* burning dinner or because they *find it funny*? Humor "works super well for your customer type". Build **three more statics** per thread: [0:39:07–0:41:19]
  - Thread A (universal moments, tests "funny"): "You skipped breakfast, so it's gelato time." "You just had coffee, so it's gelato time." "You just ate lunch, so it's gelato time."
  - Thread B (lived experience, tests "experienced"): "You spent three hours on that dinner and it kind of sucks, but it's okay, because you have gelato." "You spent three hours on dinner and he hated it, but it's okay, because you bought him gelato."
  - Sarah's bet: "I have a feeling for your customer type it's just funny." If thread A wins too, "we just need to scale a bunch of funny food-related things." New savory flavors (strawberry basil, olive oil…) could follow the same logic (gelato "like a salad"). [0:41:19]
- **Proof-graphic prompt:** Ahmed asks about a "proof graphic" prompt Sarah tweeted (transcribed "the vulnerable effect"). Sarah: it's in draft. Her admin is adding the Twitter prompts that tested well to the course "in a couple hours". [0:42:22–0:43:24] *(Prompt text not read out. The effect name is unclear in the audio.)*

### 6. Sales periods vs evergreen ads [0:43:24–0:48:51; 0:54:59–0:56:08]
- Yana: Australian brands' big sale ("second to Black Friday", likely EOFY / Click Frenzy; not named). New ads were approved too late to launch before the sale. Launch mid-sale or after? [0:43:24]
- Sarah: put evergreen ads in **their own campaign** (not in the sale campaign, which gets turned off). Run them at a much lower budget during the sale (e.g. **$200–500/day** if the main budget is ~$1,000/day). "As soon as the sale turns off, then I'll increase budget." Often she waits until "maybe the last two days of the sale", loads the evergreen ads and ramps them while the sale ramps down. [0:44:32–0:45:35]
- Sarah's own view: "I struggle with sales. I don't typically like to discount… psychologically it's not always the best", but many brands see results. [0:44:32]
- **Sale stickers on evergreen winners:** "A lot of brands have a lot of success" taking evergreen winners and "slap kind of a sales sticker on it". Caveat: "you're technically competing with yourself… as soon as you pull that sale, the previous ad gets dumped back into the algorithm and goes after people who are not sale-sensitive"; "every time you run a new offer, even if it's the same evergreen ad, it's technically going towards a new person". Track the post-sale relaunch carefully. A big brand Sarah knew kept "a whole bucket of previous winners" in a campaign, refreshed the creative and switched it on only for sales. [0:45:35–0:48:51] Yana: evergreen often performs best even during the sale, without sale messaging, because the site shows the offer anyway.
- **Leslie (chat): a site banner so evergreen-ad traffic sees the promo?** Yes: "as soon as people land on the site they're in shopping mode anyway… you don't have to always reset your ads." [0:55:05]

### 7. Creative testing: precision, not volume [0:48:51–0:56:08]
- Leonardo: budget $100–1,200/day; how many new tests a week? He runs ~10 creatives in a $200/day ad set for 7–14 days; adding 10–30 new ads a week would "overshadow the ones really working". [0:48:51–0:50:56]
- Sarah: "I do not love volume-based testing… I personally feel it is kind of a crutch for not knowing enough about… humans in general." Volume works for "**eight figures and above**", where there is staff for production *and* analytics ("somebody's got to produce it, somebody's got to analyze it, and then you've got to know what to do next"). [0:50:56–0:51:57]
- **Below 8 figures:** "a more structured, precision-focused creative strategy… I don't want to inject more than maybe **50 ads a month**… I typically run somewhere between **three to five a week** as a new test" (≈20 a month). [0:51:57]
- Why small works: "The algorithm is trying to match message to customers… It doesn't matter if you're the smallest fish in the smallest pond with a whale… if your message is better", Meta serves your ad. Small brands that grew fast "were very careful about the message in the beginning". [0:51:57–0:52:58]
- The real skill is **prioritizing ideas** ("which ones are probably just an iteration of something I've thought before"). "You don't need to run 500 ads… it's just a wasteful way to build a business." "Somewhere in the 20 range [a month] is very healthy, as long as they're very carefully worded and work off the message really well… You can win with 20 ads a month. I've seen it in many, many brands." [0:52:58–0:54:59]

### 8. Creative diversity and the format ladder [0:56:08–1:01:19]
- Gavin: many ads per ad set and let Meta pick, or one ad per ad set so each gets spend? Sarah prefers **multiple ads in one ad set**. DCT (dynamic creative testing: three images × three headlines mixed by Meta) suits very focused precision work. But "**creative diversity** is something that is actually a thing… volume [isn't] necessarily necessary, but diversity is massive because this is a social platform." [0:56:08–0:57:11]
- Diversity = formats: "I need a GIF, a static, a UGC, a pro video, a founder video… a weird one where we're shaving a balloon." "**Your ads compete with organic more than they compete with basically anybody else.**" [0:57:11]
- Her ad set = **one concept, many formats** (e.g. "psychology-based creative" as a video, a static, a GIF). Variants should say it differently, "because you're going to get a different person": "psychology-based creative" vs "grow your business using psychology". "Everybody needs to **pick four messages for the brand**" and find many ways to say each. Even a color or picture change "would do something totally different to somebody's brain." [0:57:11–0:59:15]
- **New-concept ladder:** [0:59:15–1:01:19]
  1. ~**10 statics**, small subgroup, "all go after one type of person and then one emotion". (She used to test **9** = 3 emotions × 3 statics, because her NLP runs usually surfaced three emotions.)
  2. When one takes off, put its core message into "something that moves": GIF, animation, carousel.
  3. If it hits again, build the "expensive ads": UGC, pro video, "a fake podcast ad".
  - "I don't do expensive ads until those statics have run, and that's for new concepts." A proven concept gets one static plus a GIF, a carousel, a video and more in its ad set.
- "**Core message is everything.** If you don't know what the message is, or you're a little fuzzy on it, people can feel it through the screen." [1:01:19]

### 9. Switching a younger audience: question the attachment [1:01:19–1:06:33]
- Aazar Shad: a tea brand that "kills it" with **golden milk** (turmeric; reduces inflammation and joint pain) attracts older buyers. How to win Gen Z, who drink matcha, coffee, chai lattes? [1:01:19–1:03:25]
- Sarah: benefit lists ("reduces indigestion…") pull people already experiencing the problem, typically older. To switch younger buyers, "drill down into belief" and make them **question their current choice**: "**Is your matcha really doing what you think it is?**" or question their sense of self: "**Are you actually a matcha girl, or do you want to be the type that does A, B, C**" (tied to turmeric). [1:03:25–1:04:29]
- Landing page: A/B test so you "don't cut off the legs of the business". The B version says something like "**Matcha is out, turmeric is in**". Question the trend, not attack it: not "matcha sucks" but "**the girlies aren't doing matcha anymore**". Competitor example: brands like MUD\WTR say "the dusty tea… is a scam" or similar. Sarah avoids "scammy" versions. [1:04:29–1:05:31]

### Rules of thumb
- **DON'T** lead cold ads with ought-self "should" messaging; save it for prospects who've qualified. [0:08:25–0:10:29]
- **DO** write actual-self copy as present-moment situations and end with "that's okay, just change one thing". [0:16:42–0:17:43]
- **IF** targeting higher-income buyers **THEN** raise the reading level and verbs ("scale your brand"). [0:15:40]
- **IF** luxury or exclusive **THEN** no bundles, no discounts; raise price; restrict access; and **DON'T** use "limited spaces". [0:11:31–0:12:31; 0:18:44–0:19:49]
- **DO** frame scarcity as time or qualification rather than quantity when you want committed buyers. [0:18:44–0:20:52]
- **IF** going up-market **THEN** show the product next to something far more expensive. [0:30:27]
- **DO** decide what you're qualifying for (price, personality, situation) before writing qualifying copy; use the qualification question list to remove bias. [0:30:27–0:35:53]
- **IF** you want small conversion lifts **THEN** use heuristics; **IF** building new campaigns **THEN** use mental models; **IF** disrupting a category **THEN** work on core beliefs (generation, upbringing). [0:24:04]
- **DO** scale a winner 10–15% a day while CPA and CVR hold (or +10% every other day if cautious). [0:38:03–0:39:07]
- **IF** a situational ad wins **THEN** split iterations into two hypotheses (universal/funny vs lived experience), ~3 statics each. [0:39:07–0:41:19]
- **DO** run evergreen ads during a sale in their own low-budget campaign and ramp them in the sale's last ~2 days or after it. [0:44:32–0:45:35]
- **IF** you add a sale sticker to an evergreen winner **THEN** track its post-sale relaunch separately (it reaches new, non-sale-sensitive people). [0:45:35–0:47:45]
- **DO** keep a promo banner on site during sales; evergreen traffic still sees it. [0:55:05]
- **IF** under 8 figures **THEN** test 3–5 new ads a week (≤50 a month); ~20 well-worded ads a month can win. [0:51:57–0:54:59]
- **DO** fill an ad set with one concept in many formats (static, GIF, UGC, pro, founder, weird). **DON'T** rely on volume. [0:56:08–0:58:14]
- **DO** define ~4 core brand messages and phrase each many ways. [0:58:14]
- **DO** prove a new concept with ~10 statics before paying for UGC or pro video. [0:59:15–1:01:19]
- **IF** you want a new (younger) audience to switch **THEN** question their attachment and identity and A/B test a separate landing page instead of replacing the current one. [1:03:25–1:05:31]
- **IF** targeting Gen X **THEN** test nostalgia (e.g. an MTV reference in the US). [0:24:04]

## How to apply
- [ ] Map the funnel: tag each asset (ad, landing page, email, call) by self. Ads = actual/ideal; ought only after qualification.
- [ ] Rewrite 3 hooks as present-moment situations ("You've already ___ 20 times today…") ending in "that's okay, change one thing".
- [ ] For premium/luxury products: remove bundle/discount language, add a qualifying CTA, and find a pricier comparison object.
- [ ] For a winning static: write thread A (universal moments) and thread B (lived experience), 3 statics each. Scale the original +10–15%/day while CPA holds.
- [ ] Plan the month: 3–5 new concepts a week. Each new concept = ~10 statics → GIF/carousel → UGC/pro video only after a static wins.
- [ ] Write the brand's 4 core messages and 5+ phrasings each; assign formats.
- [ ] For switching campaigns: write "question the attachment" hooks plus an A/B landing page.
