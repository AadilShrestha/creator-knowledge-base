# Emotion, Clarity, Urgency

> Three less obvious but "wildly effective" shortcuts: the **affect heuristic** (feeling), **processing fluency** (clarity) and **temporal discounting** (now beats later).

Page description: "These next three heuristics hit hard when used right: Effect heuristic (feeling), Processing fluency (clarity), Temporal discounting (now beats later). This is where your copy finally stops rambling and starts converting."

> **Naming note:** Sarah says "effect heuristic" in the audio, and the page text and e-book also spell it "Effect Heuristic." Her slides say **Affect Heuristic**, which is the standard behavioral-science term (*affect* = emotion). This wiki uses "affect heuristic."

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing hooks (affect), cutting a script or static down (fluency), or writing the promise/benefit beat (temporal discounting). Also use it to tear down competitor ads that open on a scene, look ultra-simple, or promise instant results.
- **Inputs → outputs:** customer reviews, the core message, time-to-benefit → an experience-first hook, a simplified layout or script, and an immediacy line.
- **Rules:**
  - DO open with the experience and create "that good feeling before making any sort of pitch."
  - DO mine reviews for scene lines (the flask hook came from a review).
  - DO keep layouts clean, copy short and visuals familiar. Let the face carry meaning.
  - DON'T add bullet points under a clear headline. DON'T "over design your way into trying to convince people."
  - DO state how soon the benefit arrives ("one bite," "today," "first use").
  - DON'T treat temporal discounting as price, value or bundles.
- **See also:** [Affect heuristic](../../../concepts/heuristics/affect-heuristic.md), [Processing fluency](../../../concepts/heuristics/processing-fluency.md), [Temporal discounting](../../../concepts/heuristics/temporal-discounting.md), [Valence](../../../concepts/valence.md).

## TL;DR
- **[Affect heuristic](../../../concepts/heuristics/affect-heuristic.md):** people make snap judgments from how something *feels*. Drop them into an experience before any pitch: "He put up the tent, but you brought the flask" [00:23]–[02:20].
- **[Processing fluency](../../../concepts/heuristics/processing-fluency.md):** easy to understand = easier to believe. The brain burns less energy, so ease reads as trustworthy. Cut the bullet points. "Please don't over design your way into trying to convince people" [02:26]–[04:13].
- **[Temporal discounting](../../../concepts/heuristics/temporal-discounting.md):** we prefer immediate rewards. It has "nothing to do with price." Make the reward feel close: "One bite, and your mood, energy, and balance is restored" [04:13]–[05:59].
- Together these drive emotion, clarity and urgency "all without needing like super long copy" [05:59].

## Key ideas

### 4. Affect heuristic [00:23]
![Affect heuristic flask ad](../../../assets/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/03-affect-flask-0021.jpg)
*Affect Heuristic slide [00:21]: a flask ad Tether designed. Golden-hour field, two hands holding cups beside a steel flask. Headline: "He put up the tent, but you brought the flask." Sub-line: "Engineered for adventure. Perfect for sharing." Badge: "★★★★★ Adventure approved 1,464+ sold". Footer: "As seen in Vogue, Men's Journal, goop, Business Insider".*

- This and the next three heuristics are "a little less obvious, but they're wildly effective" [00:30]–[00:33].
- **What it is:** "We make snap judgments based upon how something emotionally feels" [00:38]–[00:43]. It's "all about how the ad makes you feel, not necessarily what it is trying to convey" [00:43]–[00:49].
  - You're *not* selling a bundle or a price, a perception, values or social proof [00:55]–[00:59].
  - "We're dropping a person into the experience that we are trying to call up in their mind" [01:04]–[01:09].
- **Why it works:** when something feels "really good, safe, rewarding, or just like nice to be in... our brain assumes that it's good, safe... something that we actually need to pay attention to" [01:15]–[01:27].
- **Example:** the phrase came out of **one of the brand's customer reviews**: "He put up the tent, but you brought the flask" [01:27]–[01:36].
  - It drops the customer "directly into a memory almost of possibly going on a camping trip with their husband where he brings all of the stuff... He puts up the tent. He makes the fire... but you brought the flask. So you contributed too" [01:42]–[01:58].
- **Application:** "use visuals and language that kind of create that good feeling **before making any sort of pitch**." Convey the experience of what you could do with the product "and nothing else" [02:03]–[02:20].

### 5. Processing fluency bias [02:26]
![Legends hoodie ad](../../../assets/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/03-fluency-legends-0230.jpg)
*Processing Fluency Bias slide [02:30]: a LEGENDS hoodie ad Tether designed. Textured tan background, a confident, proud-looking man in a black hoodie, headline "The hoodie that sets its own standards," sub-line "Because ordinary just doesn't cut it." No bullet points.*

- **What it is:** "If it's easy to understand something, we believe it a whole lot more. We're... more interested in it because it's quick for us to understand" [02:37]–[02:48].
- **Why it works:** "The brain burns a lot less energy when something is fluent or if it's easy" [02:48].
  - **PB&J analogy:** making a peanut butter and jelly sandwich takes no real thought, "especially if you made it a lot so that you've become fluent in making PB&J. The same goes for ads" [02:53]–[03:02].
  - "That ease becomes trustworthy to the brain" [03:02].
- **Example:** in the Legends ad, the headline plus the model's "definitive... casual... kind of pride" expression "does a lot for what we're trying to convey. We don't actually have to say a whole lot" [03:09]–[03:30].
- **Common mistake:** marketers "put a very clear message up top and then try and explain more beneath it... by adding bullet points. I see bullet points everywhere in static ads these days, you don't need them" [03:30]–[03:45].
  - "If you have a good message, you're already using the processing fluency bias" [03:45].
  - "**Please don't over design your way into trying to convince people to buy from you.**" Keep it clear, keep it clean, simplify [03:50]–[04:01].
- **Use:** clean layouts, short copy, familiar visuals [04:01]–[04:13].

### 6. Temporal discounting [04:13]
![Ankhway temporal discounting ad](../../../assets/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/03-temporal-ankhway-0430.jpg)
*Temporal Discounting slide [04:30]: an Ankhway mushroom gummies ad. Headline "The only (delicious) shortcut that actually works." with a "100% Natural" badge and the key line at the bottom: "One bite, and your mood, energy, and balance is restored."*

- "People get this one very confused all the time... it has **nothing to do with price**, nothing to do with price" [04:13]–[04:30].
- **What it is:** "We prefer immediate rewards over long-term rewards. That's all this one talks about." It has nothing to do with bundles or the value you're communicating. "Humans really want rewards upfront. We don't want to wait a long time" [04:30]–[04:48].
- **Why it works:** "The brain is prioritizing really what's going to help us right now. It's trying to keep us alive" [04:48]–[04:58].
- **Example:** "the only delicious shortcut that actually works." The word *shortcut* "speaks to it just slightly" (easier, quicker). The real work is done by the bottom phrase, **"one bite."** It tells the brain how long until they start to feel results [04:58]–[05:30].
- **Don't** use it to convey how cheap your product is. It's about "ease of benefits." Use words like "today," "now," "first use," "in the next hour... make the reward feel very close" [05:30]–[05:59].

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** "use visuals and language that... create that good feeling before making any sort of pitch" [02:03].
- **DON'T** sell a bundle, price, perception, values or social proof in the affect beat [00:55].
- **DON'T** add bullet points to statics [03:41]. **DON'T** over-design [03:50].
- **DO** use clean layouts, short copy and familiar visuals [04:01].
- **DO** use "today, now, first use, in the next hour... make the reward feel very close" [05:47].
- **DON'T** use temporal discounting to signal cheapness [05:36].

## How to apply
- [ ] Mine reviews for a lived moment (who, where, what happened) and use it as the headline.
- [ ] Remove bullet points from your static. If the headline needs explaining, rewrite the headline.
- [ ] Add a time-to-benefit phrase: "one bite," "first use," "today," "in the next hour."
