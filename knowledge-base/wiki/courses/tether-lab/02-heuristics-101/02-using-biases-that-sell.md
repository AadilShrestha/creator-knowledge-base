# Using Biases That Sell

> The three "social" shortcuts most DTC brands already use, scarcity, social proof and authority bias, and how to push each one past the category default.

Page description: "Scarcity, social proof, and authority bias are the shortcuts most brands already use — but usually not well. Learn how to apply them like a pro without sounding like everyone else in your category."

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** adding urgency or trust layers to a script or static, writing badges and CTAs, or tearing down competitor ads that lean on "selling out," review counts or doctors.
- **Inputs → outputs:** stock/limits, review and customer counts, tribe name, available experts, audience trust profile → a scarcity line, a humanised proof line, and an authority choice.
- **Rules:**
  - DO prefer quantity or per-customer scarcity over "limited time."
  - DO humanise social proof: name the tribe behind the number.
  - DO check the audience trusts the authority you choose. Boomers often prefer peers, nurses or functional practitioners.
  - DON'T stop at these three. They're "very expected in DTC." Stack them with the lesser-known heuristics (L3–L4).
- **See also:** [Scarcity](../../../concepts/heuristics/scarcity-heuristic.md), [Social proof](../../../concepts/heuristics/social-proof.md), [Authority bias](../../../concepts/heuristics/authority-bias.md), [Heuristic stacking](../../../concepts/heuristic-stacking.md).

## TL;DR
- The aim of every heuristic is to "make it easier for the brain to run through its process of checks [and] balances" so it moves forward to the sale [00:02]–[00:23].
- **[Scarcity](../../../concepts/heuristics/scarcity-heuristic.md):** don't stop at "limited time." **Quantity limits** ("only 100," "4 per customer") are more powerful. The KFC 2014 example: dollar fries, limited to 4 packs per customer [01:26]–[02:24].
- **[Social proof](../../../concepts/heuristics/social-proof.md):** go beyond the stars and talk about the people. "6,000 sock lovers have adopted…" beats "6,000 five-star reviews" [03:24]–[04:02].
- **[Authority bias](../../../concepts/heuristics/authority-bias.md):** we trust *perceived* experts, and that can be anyone credible. Choose carefully: some audiences, often boomers, distrust doctor-level authority [04:16]–[06:03].
- All three are common and "very expected in DTC." The less obvious heuristics in the next lessons are "doing the biggest numbers in the ad account" [06:04]–[06:21].

## Key ideas

### Why heuristics speed up the sale [00:02]
- Heuristics "help the brain do a faster job, basically get to the end of its kind of like double check system... So you can get to the sale faster" [00:06]–[00:16].

### 1. Scarcity heuristic [00:27]
![Pela scarcity ad](../../../assets/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/02-scarcity-pela-0027.jpg)
*Scarcity slide [00:27]: a Pela phone-case ad. "If Plastic Could Protect, We Would Use It." with a badge "Our Best Styles Are Selling Out Quick!" and the line "4x stronger protection—from plants, not petroleum."*

- **What it is:** a lot of marketers know it, but "it goes in a lot of different directions more than what we actually use it for" [00:30]–[00:38]. It works off the idea "I'm going to miss out on something." When something feels limited (not here for long, limited stock or quantity), "we want it more" [00:40]–[00:51].
- **Why it works:** "It triggers kind of a survival instinct in us, a primal fear of missing out" [00:54].
- **The standard (correct) use:** buttons or badges like "our best stock is almost out," "our best styles are selling out quickly," "limited time offers," "limited stock," "this is never coming back" [01:00]–[01:25].
- **Branch out:**
  - Time limits are the default, but "it's even more powerful if you use **limited quantities**, like we only have 100 of these" [01:31]–[01:35].
  - Or limit quantity **per customer** [01:41].
  - **KFC example:** "KFC ran a very, very large campaign, I think this was in 2014," selling dollar fries "limited to four packs of fries per customer" [01:44]–[02:03].
  - **Why quantity scarcity works:** "it tells people exactly how much you expect them to buy, and it also kind of helps them make a judgment call as to how many packs of things other people are also buying" [02:04]–[02:15].
- Scarcity can be time limits, product limits or quantity limits [02:18]–[02:24].

### 2. Social proof [02:28]
![Crazy Compression social proof ad](../../../assets/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/02-social-proof-crazy-compression-0230.jpg)
*Social proof slide [02:30]: a Crazy Compression socks ad. "Comfort that won't compress your wallet! Say goodbye to heavy, achy legs" with a red "★★★★★ 6000+ 5 star reviews" badge. It's the "6,000 five-star reviews" Sarah suggests rewriting as "6,000 sock lovers."*

- DTC uses it constantly and is "very, very good, well-versed at it" [02:28].
- **What it is:** "If we assume that other people like it, it must be good, and I should like it too" [02:38].
- **Why it works:** people understand very quickly whether something (item, object, lead magnet, service) "is safe... If a lot of people have used it, I'm going to assume it's safe." "We have evolved to follow the crowd for safety" [02:43]–[03:02].
- **Examples:** "over 10,000 people trust us," "6,000 five-star reviews." People think: "a lot of people have tested this, so I probably don't need to worry" [03:04]–[03:15].
- **Apply:** reviews, testimonials, social media posts [03:17].
- **Push further:** "social proof can be all kinds of different things. It doesn't necessarily have to be five stars" [03:24]–[03:30].
  - Take your "6,000 five-star reviews" and say **who those people are**: "6,000 sock lovers have adopted [brand]" or "6,000 people who have tried it and loved it" [03:33]–[03:51].
  - "Talk about the people instead of the actual five-star review... you can get a lot better results just by injecting some humanity into your social proof" [03:53]–[04:02].

### 3. Authority bias [04:03]
![Maelove authority ad](../../../assets/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/02-authority-maelove-0400.jpg)
*Authority bias slide [04:00]: a Maelove serums ad Tether designed. "Transform your results, not your steps" with a quote card from Marisa Garshick, MD, Board Certified Dermatologist ("Maelove uses a simplified approach that makes it easy for anyone to incorporate these serums into their skincare routine."), a "It's the formula that matters ★★★★★" badge, and a "Shop the serums" CTA.*

- "Used sparingly in the industry, but I wish we would use it more because it's so, so, so, so powerful," especially for products that are "medicalized" or solve a medical issue [04:05]–[04:14].
- **What it is:** "We trust perceived experts, and I say that perceived on purpose" [04:16]. Experts "don't have to be doctors, lawyers, or... government officials." They can be someone who has read a lot or knows a lot about the topic, and they can be family members or friends [04:24]–[04:39].
- **Why it works:** "Following authority figures used to mean survival. If we follow people who are in a position of authority, we have a better chance of staying on the planet for a lot longer" [04:45]–[04:54].
- **Example, "Dr. Recommended":** an ad they designed where a dermatologist talks about her experience of the product. "We're basically borrowing authority from her because she has some sort of certification or award or expert status... She has a lot of credibility in her niche" [04:56]–[05:15].
- **Caution:** "Depending on the authority bias you choose, your audience might not be trusting of authority" [05:20]–[05:29].
  - "This happens often specifically with **boomers**." Many "tend to trust friend to friend or peer to peer or even nurse or... functional practitioners over doctor level authority, mostly out of... experience" [05:29]–[05:46].
  - If your customer type "has had bad experiences with authority, particular doctors, it might not be your best practice to use this" [05:46]–[05:52].
  - "Choose your authority bias wisely... be very careful with how you are framing this" [05:54]–[06:03].

### Transition [06:04]
- "These three social biases are obviously very common... They're also very expected in DTC." Next come "heuristics that marketers never think to use or possibly don't know because these are the ones that I see are doing the biggest numbers in the ad account" [06:04]–[06:21].

## Rules of thumb
- **DO** use limited quantities, which are "even more powerful" than time limits [01:31].
- **DO** limit quantity per customer to tell people how much to buy [02:04].
- **DO** "talk about the people instead of the actual five-star review" [03:53].
- **IF** selling something medicalized **THEN** use authority bias [04:09].
- **IF** the audience distrusts doctors (often boomers) **THEN** use peer, nurse or practitioner authority [05:29]–[05:52].
- **DON'T** rely only on scarcity, social proof and authority. They're "very expected in DTC" [06:09].

## How to apply
- [ ] Swap at least one time-based scarcity line for a **quantity** line ("Only 100 made," "Max 2 per customer").
- [ ] Rewrite your review count to name the tribe ("10,000 coffee lovers trust us").
- [ ] Before using a doctor or expert, check whether your audience trusts that kind of authority. Consider peers, nurses or practitioners instead.
