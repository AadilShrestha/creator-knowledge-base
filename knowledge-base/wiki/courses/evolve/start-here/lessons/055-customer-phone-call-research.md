# 55. Customer Phone Call Research
> Call recent big-basket customers, ask one open question about how they found the brand, then stay quiet and let them talk: the story you hear is the customer journey, the pain points and the raw language you can build ads from.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a human on the brand team can phone customers, and you need to prepare the call list, the opener and the note template, or turn call notes into research inputs (journeys, pain points, customer wording). You cannot place the calls yourself.
- **Inputs → outputs:** Shopify order list with phone numbers and order values → a prioritised call list, a call script, and a notes template. After the calls: raw notes → entries for the research doc (journey, problems before purchase, quotes, where they saw the ad).
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** call customers who placed a "higher than average purchase order" first. Example: AOV is $60 and someone bought "four or five of them". [00:00–01:34]
  - **DO** also call one-time buyers, repeat customers and people who just ordered. [00:37–01:34]
  - **DO** open casually, as a person, not as a survey: name, brand, "is now a bad time to talk?" [01:34–02:18]
  - **DO** ask one question, "how you got to our brand", then pause and say nothing else. [02:18–02:58]
  - **IF** they ask what you mean **THEN** clarify lightly ("how'd you find out about us?", "what were you looking for?") and go back to listening. [02:18–02:58]
  - **DO** let them talk "as long as possible" and write notes while they talk. [04:18]
  - **DON'T** fire a list of questions ("what's your favorite color", "what are you in pain about", "what are the benefits of our product"). The instructor calls these "dumb stuff". [03:37]
  - **DON'T** ask "do you mind if I ask you some questions?" [00:00–00:37]
  - **IF** they are busy **THEN** schedule a call-back. [Page text]
  - **DO** store notes in the research doc "or elsewhere". [04:18]
  - **DON'T** invent calls or call results. If no calls have been made, list this as a missing research input.

## Key ideas

### Why call customers at all [00:00–00:37]
- The topic "has come up quite a bit" in the community chat. A community member named Sean (unverified) says he still has brands doing "a few hundred thousand dollars a day" that call their customers (instructor claim, relayed). [00:00]
- "You can just simply have a phone call with someone and learn a little bit more about their journey." The instructor says this "can unlock a lot of gold". [00:00]
- Approach it "very casually", "just have a conversation", rather than bombarding the customer with questions. [00:00–00:37]

### Who to call and how to find them [00:37–01:34]
- First priority: any customer who placed a higher-than-average order. Worked example: AOV about $60 and someone buys four or five, so you can learn "why they're buying so many". [00:37–01:34]
- Also fine to call people who bought once, bought twice, are repeat customers, or have "just placed an order". [00:37–01:34]
- Where: Shopify admin, Orders, click the customer. The phone number should be collected at checkout; if not, "just Google how to collect phone numbers", which the instructor says is simple and does not demo. [00:37]

### The opener (warm call, not cold call) [01:34–02:18]
- Say: your name, "calling from" the brand, "is now a bad time to talk?" Instructor's example: "Hey Jack, it's Spencer calling from EZA, is now a bad time to talk?" (Whisper wrote "about time to talk"; the page text confirms "is now a bad time to talk"; "EZA" is as heard, brand name unverified.) [01:34]
- Two outcomes: "I'm in the middle of something, call me back" (schedule a time), or "no, I'm good, what's up?" [01:34]
- They will likely pick up and be curious because they just ordered. It's "kind of warm calling because they've already given you their credit card". [04:18–04:58]

### The one question, then silence [02:18–03:37]
- Script: "I saw you just placed an order with us and I wanted to one, just personally reach out and thank you for supporting our brand. But two, I just wanted to ask if you could explain a little bit more about how you got to our brand." [02:18]
- "You want to leave it there. You want to pause. You don't want to say anything else." The aim is to get them talking about how they arrived. [02:18]
- If they ask what you mean, rephrase: "how'd you find out about us?", "what were you looking for?" [02:18]
- The ideal answer is a story. Instructor's imagined answer: rebuilding a patio, frustrated with a paint finisher that kept drying out, tried in-person stores, searched online, saw one of the brand's Facebook ads, liked it, tried it. [02:58]
- From that one story you get several pain points and you can follow up ("you tried X, Y, Z; what about our brand specifically?"). [03:37]

### Why this works, per the instructor [03:37–04:18]
- "Once you understand the customer journey, you can ideally recreate it and you can know the different problems that they're going through." [03:37]
- They "automatically start talking about it" if you do not interrupt. [04:18]
- It is "not a word for word script", but it "will work well" and is "based off of basic cold calling principles". [04:18]

## Steps / framework
1. Pull customers from Shopify Orders; sort by order size and flag higher-than-average orders. [00:37]
2. Confirm phone numbers exist; if not, enable phone collection first. [00:37]
3. Call with the opener. If busy, book a call-back; if free, continue. [01:34]
4. Thank them, then ask how they got to the brand. Pause. [02:18]
5. Listen. Only clarify if asked; follow the story with light follow-ups. [02:18–03:37]
6. Take notes by hand or on a keyboard during the call. [04:18]
7. Save notes into the research doc. [04:18]

## Examples
- **Patio paint finisher story (imagined by the instructor):** problem (product drying out and "messing things up"), failed attempt (in-store shopping), discovery (Facebook ad), trigger to buy (liked the ad). Shows the shape of the answer you want: problem → failed attempts → discovery → purchase. [02:58]
- **Call example:** "Hey Jack, it's Spencer calling from EZA…" and the same pattern with a different brand ("Spencer from Aloe Yoga"). These are illustrative openers, not real campaigns. [01:34; 04:18]

## Prompts / templates (verbatim)
Script from the lesson page:

```text
Hey NAME, it's YOUR NAME calling from YOUR BRAND, is now a bad time to talk?

Yes = Make sure you schedule a time to call back
No = Move forward

I saw you just placed an order with us, and I wanted to, 1. personally thank you for supporting our brand, and 2. Just ask if you could explain a little bit more about how you got to our brand.

Let them talk for as long as possible.

You're looking into better understand their journey what they were looking for, and some of the things that they were potentially going through that ultimately made them purchase your product.
```

Notes template (derived, not from the course):

```text
Customer (first name / order value / orders to date):
How they found us (channel, ad, search, friend):
What they were trying to solve before us:
What they had tried that failed:
Exact phrases they used (verbatim):
What made them buy now:
Follow-up questions asked:
```

## How to apply
- [ ] Confirm the brand collects phone numbers; if not, flag it as a blocker.
- [ ] Build a list: highest-AOV orders first, then repeat buyers, then recent one-time buyers.
- [ ] Give the caller the script above and tell them to ask only the one question, then listen.
- [ ] Store every call in the notes template and copy verbatim phrases into the research doc.
- [ ] Mine the notes for problems, failed alternatives and discovery paths; feed them into the angle and desire work in [56](056-organizing-insights-overview-must-watch.md) onward.
- [ ] Never write copy that quotes a customer unless the quote was actually said and permission is confirmed (derived).
