# 67. Action Item: Find New Mechanisms
> The action-item page tells you to run the New Mechanism Finder prompt; the attached video (a YouTube-style screen share) teaches how to test the resulting concepts: test only fully congruent hooks on a brand-new concept, take bigger swings, and use granular variable testing only on proven winners.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** designing the first test of a new ad concept (a new mechanism, angle or claim), writing hook variations, or deciding whether to test one variable (messaging / imagery / hook / hold) or whole hooks.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a concept (angle + mechanism) and 2-3 candidate headlines/hook ideas → 3 fully congruent hook variants (image + headline, or clip + voiceover aligned), launched as a hook-only test; then, for a winner, a granular iteration plan.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** run the New Mechanism Finder prompt first (lesson [66](066-how-to-find-apply-new-mechanisms-part-2.md), prompt in full there) and attach the Market Sophistication doc. (Page text)
  - **DO** test only hooks on an initial concept. "The only thing that we are testing for brand new concepts is hooks. We don't test holds anymore." [05:49-06:47]
  - **DO** make each variant fully congruent: visual, text and (for video) voiceover support one idea. "You're testing fully congruent ads and you're just testing the beginning of it." [06:47]
  - **DON'T** isolate one variable (only messaging, or only imagery) on an unproven concept. It produces incongruent ads (or ads too similar for Meta to differentiate) and over-granular data. [01:56-03:23]
  - **DON'T** slap one headline on an unrelated image: "World's first stainless steel humidifier" on a plastic-humidifier image is incongruent and you'd write off a headline that was fine. [05:00]
  - **DO** take "bigger swings" in the first concept; use bolder claims (the winning set used "world's first/cleanest" claims). [08:50]
  - **DO** use variable-by-variable iteration ("messaging, imagery, hold") only after an ad wins. "This process does still apply, but it is for winning ads only." [09:35]
  - **DON'T** reuse a voiceover line that does not match the visual. The "Kids are unpredictable" hook would "make absolutely no sense" on an ad with no kids. [08:07]
  - **IF** a concept failed after a single-variable test **THEN** don't conclude the concept failed; the test may have been too granular and incongruent (derived from [05:00]).

## Key ideas
### Context: proof of results, then the correction [00:00-00:58]
- Opening claim: a brand "that just yesterday... February 13th" that did not exist the year before, scaled to "over $50,000 per day, extremely profitably", with Google Ads not managed by the agency. (instructor claim, with an Ads Manager screen shown; year implied 2025: "one of the biggest things that we have changed in 2025") [00:00]
- Topic: ad creative testing; "ad creative is what actually has the biggest impact on scaling". It updates an earlier video on the channel about initial ad-creative testing. [00:00-00:58]

### The old method (now called wrong for initial concepts) [00:58-02:49]
- Old advice: pick one variable and run three variations. Four variables: **messaging, imagery, hook, hold**. Example concept: "winter" for a humidifier brand. If you test messaging, you hold the image constant and vary the text; if hook, you vary visuals not text; etc. [00:58-01:56]
- Verdict: "this is the worst thing that you could do... Well, it's actually not the worst thing... but this should never be done on initial concepts." [01:56]
- Two mistakes: (1) **You are not building congruent ads.** One variation may be congruent, but with 3 (he uses three; "four or five, I really don't care") the others won't be, or "not going to be significantly enough different for Meta to even really do anything with it". (2) **You are too granular too early.** "We don't even know if this concept is going to work yet. So why are we going so micro?" [01:56-03:23]
- Most people also do not do the sequential follow-through (pick winning messaging, then move to imagery); they see one fail and move to the next concept. [02:49]

### The new method: test hooks only, fully congruent [03:23-06:47]
- "Hooks are hooks": a hook applies to images and videos; it is the first thing the audience sees. For images, call it "testing different images and different concepts". The key is that visuals and headlines align. [03:23]
- "Old Spencer is a bit of an idiot... what you're actually doing now is correct." [04:13]
- This is **not** iterating an existing ad; that is covered below. It is "the first ad concept that you're creating of whatever angle". [04:13]
- Concept used: a "superlative" ("I love this word... up to highest quality"). Three big claims, each with its own matching image:
  1. "The world's first stainless steel humidifier" (image showing stainless steel)
  2. "The first doctor designed humidifier" (image with a doctor; "some people think it's a toaster")
  3. "The world's cleanest humidifier" (tidy, made bed, plant: "background is all tidy... crisp")
  [04:13-05:49; 05:49-06:47]
- Why congruence matters: if "world's first stainless steel humidifier" sits on a plastic humidifier image, "that's not stainless steel that's plastic and you probably wouldn't click". The ad flops, you blame the headline, and you dig "deeper in the hole of never finding winning ad creatives". [05:00]
- Bigger swings: he says they went with the maximal claim ("the world's cleanest humidifier") rather than a milder one; the transcript garbles the contrast ("we didn't say the cleanest humidifier", likely a milder "a clean humidifier"). Taking bigger swings narrows "what is working congruently". [08:07-08:50]
- The old 4-variable system had a "hold"; "hold has kind of been transferred into the new version of hook". [05:49]

### Video example: the life-proof / "us versus them" concept [06:47-08:50]
- The winning ad concept is "life proof" (he first calls it "us versus them", then corrects). Three hooks, same idea, different clips and different voiceover:
  1. "That moment when your coffee becomes your worst enemy. We've all been there. That's why we..."
  2. "Well, kids are unpredictable, but your coffee cup shouldn't be. That's why we create..."
  3. "And this is why we have trust issues with takeaway cups. That's why we..."
- Hook 2 only works if its visuals show kids; "Kids are unpredictable, right? That would make absolutely no sense on this ad because there's no kids in the ad." Many people do this unknowingly in less obvious ways. [07:27-08:07]
- Billboard analogy: a billboard is one complete thing; you wouldn't run the same billboard with two unrelated headlines. [08:50]
- The brand of this video ad is not named (he says "this brand"; the product on screen is a drink cup, not a humidifier). Whether it is the same brand as the $50K/day brand is not stated.

### What to do once you have a winner [08:50-10:31]
- "This process is not fully written off... but this is for winning ads only." They found a winning ad from the humidifier batch (he doesn't say which) and looked at "what was winning about it" and "what do we think we could improve". [08:50-09:35]
- Questions to ask on a winner: Is it the **messaging** we can improve? Is there **clearer imagery**? Can the **hold** be better ("for an image... the complementary text around")? Then make more iterations. [09:35]
- The benefit claim: "By not doing that, you're going to increase your ad hit rate... finding winning ads... get data faster... overall better ads." [09:35-10:31]
- Testing a single headline or going very granular on an unproven concept "you're not giving this ad the best chance". See the whole ad "not just as a headline and not just as an image being stuck together". [10:31]

### Close / offer (non-operational) [10:31-12:13]
- Pitch for agency services and the Evolve program: "over 140 brands... more than 15 people doing a million dollars a month, and they're using these exact strategies" (instructor claim). [11:29]
- Encouragement to keep testing; "you could have the best product in the world, but if nobody knows about it...". [12:13]

## Steps / framework
**Initial-concept test (new concept, unproven):**
1. Pick one angle/concept and its mechanism (from [66](066-how-to-find-apply-new-mechanisms-part-2.md)).
2. Write 3 big claims (bigger swings), not three tweaks of one claim. [08:50]
3. Make each a complete, congruent ad: visual + text (+ voiceover for video) support the same claim. [05:00]
4. Test only the hook (first thing seen). Do not test hold. [05:49-06:47]
5. Compare which congruent hook wins.

**Winner iteration (proven ad only):** vary messaging, imagery, and hold/complementary text one at a time, based on "what's winning" and "what could improve". [09:35]

| Stage | What varies | What stays | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial concept | The hook: image/clip + headline/voiceover as a congruent pair | The concept (angle) | [06:47] |
| Winner iteration | Messaging, imagery, hold | The winning ad | [09:35] |

### Rules of thumb
- **DO** build ads like a billboard: one complete thing. [08:50]
- **DON'T** go granular before you know the concept works. [03:23]
- **IF** it is a winner **THEN** iterate on messaging, imagery, hold. [09:35]

## How to apply
- [ ] Run the finder prompt ([66](066-how-to-find-apply-new-mechanisms-part-2.md)) and shortlist 2-3 mechanisms.
- [ ] For each, write one bold claim and build a matching visual (or clip + voiceover).
- [ ] Check every variant: does the picture prove the words? Does the voiceover refer to anything not on screen?
- [ ] Launch only hook variants for the new concept; keep the rest of the ad identical in structure.
- [ ] Pick the winner and only then list improvements to messaging, imagery and hold.
- [ ] Log what won and why in the iteration notes ([Iteration playbook](../../copywriting/frameworks/iteration-playbook.md)).
