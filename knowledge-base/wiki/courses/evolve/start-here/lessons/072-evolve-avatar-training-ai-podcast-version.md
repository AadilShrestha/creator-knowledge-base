# 72. Evolve Avatar Training (AI Podcast Version)
> A spoken, audio-only recap of [lesson 71](071-the-evolve-avatar-training.md): stop writing broad "ghost profile" personas and build avatars by combining the Core 5 categories (desires, experiences, emotions, behaviors, demographics last), because creative specificity drives Meta/TikTok delivery, lower costs and higher conversion.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you need the Core 5 avatar method in one short pass (briefing an agent, refreshing the rules before building core or sub avatars), or want a quick check that your avatar is specific enough. Use [lesson 71](071-the-evolve-avatar-training.md) when you need the full worked examples.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a product, a list of researched desires and customer experiences/emotions/behaviors → avatars written as combinations ("desire + experience + emotion + behavior, demographic last") that can each become an ad angle.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** start every avatar with a surface-level desire and layer the other categories in this order: desires → experiences → emotions → behaviors → demographics. "Demographics LAST." [05:40]
  - **DO** run the desire checker: the desire must fit the sentence "I want X" or "I need Y". [07:28–08:19]
  - **DON'T** combine more than one primary desire in a single avatar. [05:40–06:36]
  - **DON'T** start from demographics ("age 35, female, lives in Texas"); it "tells us nothing about why she would buy a $200 kitchen gadget" and excludes people with identical pain. [05:40; 14:17]
  - **DO** isolate the experience: record only what happened, do not assume the emotion or behavior that followed. [09:09–09:55]
  - **DO** treat a secondary emotion (anxious, frustrated) as a prompt to find the primary emotion underneath (fear, sadness, joy). [09:55–10:41]
  - **IF** you find a belief in research **THEN** translate it: what happened (experience), how it feels (emotion), what they do because of it (behavior). Beliefs are not a sixth category. [14:17–15:16]
  - **IF** you are at $0–100K/month **THEN** build one desire-based core avatar and many sub-avatars around it; at $1M+/month expand to roughly 3, 5 or 10 core avatars, which needs high creative volume. [15:16–16:48]
  - **IF** the avatar is broad **THEN** you need a new mechanism, new information or the best offer to win. [02:29]

## Key ideas

### The problem: "ghost profiles" [00:00–00:56]
- Traditional personas (ICP, target audience) are "too broad". They "sit on a wall" for a month then get ignored. [00:00]
- Example of a ghost profile: "women 25 to 45 interested in fitness", after which you wonder why ad costs are "through the roof". [00:00–00:56]
- Goal of the episode: replace vague profiles with avatars combining the "core five categories" so the viewer thinks "wait, how do they know that about me?" [00:56–01:45]

### What an avatar is, and why broad ones fail [00:56–03:07]
- Definition: "a highly specific group of people" unified by similar things who are "the most likely to buy your product". [00:56–01:45]
- Broad, obvious avatars are "the riskiest to target" now: density and competition. Example: "young mothers looking for comfortable shoes" means competing with "a thousand other brands". [01:45–02:29]
- To win with a broad avatar you need one of three things: a genuinely new mechanism, completely new information, or "the absolute best offer on the market". [02:29] (The same three-way condition is in [71](071-the-evolve-avatar-training.md): "a great product with NEW mechanisms… NEW information… or you have the BEST offer".)

### Targeting is now the creative [02:29–03:56]
- Claim: targeting success is "99% dependent on the actual creative", not ad-manager audience buttons. (instructor claim, repeated from lesson 71's "Meta targeting is now done 99% by the actual creative") [02:29]
- The creative must speak so specifically that the algorithm "is forced to show it to more people just like them". [03:07]
- Analogy: a relatable meme you text to a friend with "this is so me". It captured "a specific internal experience", not an age or job title. That recognition is relevance, and relevance lowers cost and lifts conversion. [03:07–03:56]

### The specificity scale and blue oceans [03:56–04:51]
- Avatars sit on a spectrum from "one Pepe" (Whisper heard "Pepe"; lesson 71 gives "One Pepe (person)" and "All Pepes (People)") to "all Pepes". Sweet spot: "somewhere in the middle". [03:56]
- Combining traits creates near-infinite combinations, so "there will always be untapped avatar combinations" competitors have not thought of: "your blue ocean". Real wins are in "psychological and behavioral overlaps", not census data. [04:51]

### Rules of thumb for combining categories [05:40–06:36]
1. Avoid combining more than one primary desire in an avatar. [05:40]
2. More categories layered means a more specific and usually more effective avatar. [05:40–06:36]
3. The broader the avatar (fewer categories), the better your product, offer, brand and marketing skill must be. [06:36]
4. Flip side: the more specific the sub-avatar, "the less innovative or groundbreaking your product or your offer needs to be" to earn trust. "Specific messaging really earns you the right to be a little less polished in other areas." [06:36–07:28]

### The five categories, in order [06:36–14:17]
| # | Category | What it is | Spoken examples | Check / note | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Desires | Surface-level "I want X / I need Y"; the emotional pull that compels purchase. Not core desires (health, wealth, status): "way too broad" | Core "health" becomes "I need to lose this specific 10 pounds of baby fat". Core "control" becomes "I want my dog to stop pulling on the leash during our evening walks so I don't look bad". "I want to brush my teeth faster" | Desire checker: can you say "I want X" or "I need Y"? | [06:36–08:19] |
| 2 | Experiences | Situations people have been through or are in. Two types: situational and product-based | Situational: "my bedroom faces east and gets blasted with sunlight at 5:30 in the morning"; "I just had a baby and I am severely sleep deprived". Product-based: "I tried two different brands of blackout curtains and I still woke up tired"; "I spent $80 on a dog harness and my dog still pulls" | Isolate it: what happened, without the emotion or behavior. Cat example: wants cat healthy + "excessively licking its paws, leading to bald spots" | [08:19–09:55] |
| 3 | Emotions | How people feel about the desire or the experience; bypass logic | Six-pack + motivated/confident vs six-pack + defeated/ashamed. Dog pulling + embarrassed vs dog pulling + scared it runs into the street ("survival-based") | Six primary emotions (fear, joy, sadness…) vs secondary (anxious, frustrated, confident). Secondary signals a deeper primary: debt anxiety likely rooted in "fear of financial ruin". Use the secondary in copy; use the primary to craft the core appeal | [09:55–11:35] |
| 4 | Behaviors and habits | Predictable patterns people develop; ties to ego and self-identity | Tantrum-prone kid, parent "always brings a huge bag of snacks, water, toys"; wants to lose 20 lb and "meal preps every single Sunday"; insecure about skin and "constantly orders new skincare products" | Behavior checker: does this describe what the person does, physically or mentally? "Overthinking every financial decision counts" | [11:35–13:21] |
| 5 | Demographics | Static facts: age, gender, employment, location. Least important for motivation | Running gear: "wants to run a marathon but fears knee injury" filtered to 40+ because physical risk is higher | Useful for one extra layer of relatability or logistical accuracy; many are implied by other categories (caring for aging parents implies older) | [13:21–14:17] |

### Same psychology, different demographics [14:17–15:16]
- A 22-year-old woman and a 54-year-old man can share the same desire (find a partner), experience (ghosted on dating sites), emotion (defeated) and behavior (constantly checking the phone). Starting with "female, 20s" cuts a pool whose underlying pain is identical. "Starting with the core four, make sure you maximize your market for the pain you solve." [14:17]

### Beliefs are not a sixth category [14:17–15:16]
- Example belief: "I believe I can't lose weight because my genetics are against me." Translate: experience = trying and failing diets; emotion = hopelessness, anger; behavior = crash dieting every January. That yields "four distinct angles" instead of one generic genetics statement. [15:16]
- Lesson 71 states the same translation as three questions: "What happened to make them believe this?", "How does this belief make them feel?", "What do they do because of this belief?"

### How many avatars by stage [15:16–16:48]
- $0–100K/month: "focus intensely on one core avatar, preferably based on a really strong desire", then build "tons of sub-avatars" by layering experiences, emotions, behaviors. "You earn the right to go broader later." Goal: maximum resonance "in a small, deep pool". Sub-avatar examples: "wants to lose 10 pounds but feels defeated"; "wants to lose 10 pounds and meal preps every Sunday". [15:16–16:00]
- $1M+/month: "three, five, ten solid core avatars", which "requires world-class execution and just tremendous volume". [16:00]
- Named example (Whisper heard "comfort"; lesson 71 gives "Comfrt"): "they scaled by generating over 10,000 pieces of content a week" so every desire/experience/emotion/behavior combination has creative. (instructor claim, relayed from lesson 71) [16:00–16:48]

### Closing recap [16:48–18:19]
- Recap order: desires, experiences, emotions, behaviors, demographics for logistics. "The secret really is in the combinations." Specific enough sub-avatars "feel like they are literally reading the customer's mind", which lowers costs and lifts conversion (instructor claim). [16:48–17:45]
- Closing provocation: if you found "the single most unique ego-based behavior avatar in your market", how much weaker could your product be and still dominate that segment because the messaging was right? [17:45–18:19]

## Steps / framework
**Build an avatar (as summarised here):**
1. Pick one surface-level desire that passes the "I want X / I need Y" check. [07:28–08:19]
2. Add an isolated experience (situational or product-based). [08:19–09:55]
3. Add the emotion about the desire or experience (find the primary behind a secondary). [09:55–10:41]
4. Add the behavior (what they do, physically or mentally). [11:35–12:25]
5. Add demographics only for logistics or one extra relatability layer. [13:21–14:17]
6. Check the rules: one primary desire, more layers = more specific, broader = needs stronger product/offer. [05:40–07:28]

## Examples
- **Cat avatar:** Core avatar 1 = wants cat healthy (desire, very broad). Core avatar 2 = cat excessively licking paws, bald spots (experience). Combined: a sub-avatar who "knows exactly what you're talking about". [09:09–09:55]
- **Blackout curtains (before → after):** generic "a new curtain" ad "might get ignored"; "tired of wasting money on blackout curtains that still let light bleed through the sides" "built instant credibility" by acknowledging past failures. [09:09]
- **Six-pack, two tones:** motivated person gets "optimization, speed, getting there faster"; defeated or ashamed person needs a supportive tone that "acknowledge[s] the failure without blaming them". [10:41]
- **Behavior callouts:** "If you still meal prep every Sunday, but haven't seen the results you want, then X." / "Tired of filling your bathroom cabinet with products you only use twice." [12:25]

### Rules of thumb
- **DO** use demographics last. [05:40]
- **DO** layer more categories for more specificity; use fewer categories only when product/offer/brand/marketing are strong. [06:36]
- **DON'T** assume emotion or behavior when recording an experience. [09:09–09:55]

## How to apply
- [ ] Rewrite any "women 25–45"-style target as a desire + experience + emotion + behavior combination; add demographics only if logistics demand it.
- [ ] Run the "I want X / I need Y" check on every candidate desire.
- [ ] For each avatar, write the experience as a bare fact before adding emotion or behavior.
- [ ] Translate every belief found in research into experience, emotion and behavior.
- [ ] Match the number of core avatars to revenue stage; if under $100K/month use one core avatar and many sub-avatars.
