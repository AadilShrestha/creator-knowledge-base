# Market desire
> Desire is demand that already exists in the market ("the public spread of a private want"); you can only channel it onto your product. Classify it (instinct or tech problem, shaped by a trend or new education), score it (scope, urgency, staying power), pick the strongest one your product can credibly deliver, and only move it up the hierarchy when the bridge is authentic.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** choosing the desire a batch, angle, headline or product will target; diagnosing an ad that "did nothing" despite good execution; ranking candidate angles; picking a product.
- **Inputs → outputs:** product overview, customer language ("I want / I need / I wish"), current trends and newly learned facts in the niche → a ranked desire list (instinct or tech problem + force of change + scope/urgency/staying-power read), one channel headline per desire, and a pick for the next test.
- **Rules:**
  - **DON'T** try to create a desire: "You cannot create desire… It is something that just exists in the market." [L9 00:00–00:54](../lessons/009-market-desires.md); "Never fight desires." [L61 57:02](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
  - **DO** find the desire behind any benefit by asking "why?" until it stops getting deeper (the "Why Train"). [L56 02:44](../lessons/056-organizing-insights-overview-must-watch.md); [L14 09:26–11:35](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
  - **DO** classify each desire as one of six mass instincts or six mass technological problems, then name the style trend or mass education that shapes it now. [L61 05:20–20:23](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
  - **DO** score scope, urgency and staying power; "understand scope more than anything". [L61 36:31–40:39](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
  - **DO** write desires in the customer's words; check the word actually appears in threads/reviews ("hydrated" → "smoother and less dry skin"). [L63 04:00–04:53](../lessons/063-how-to-find-desires-part-3.md)
  - **DON'T** target a desire the product cannot live up to, or one the market already knows it fails at. [L57 03:47–04:42](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
  - **DO** sell the functional product (outcome), not the physical one (specs). [L9 12:19–14:06](../lessons/009-market-desires.md)
  - **IF** an ad gets no traction **THEN** suspect the desire before the execution ("Find your style" did nothing; "look wealthy" spent $20K+, instructor claim). [L9 14:53–15:44](../lessons/009-market-desires.md)
  - **DON'T** iterate wording only; state which desire a headline hits before iterating it. [L61 58:30–59:48](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
  - **DON'T** move a product down the hierarchy ("moving down = less emotional urgency = lower prices = weaker positioning"); move up only if it feels authentic. [L61 44:05–47:57](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
  - **DON'T** assume desire is enough: "Desires don't mean anything unless I can build belief." [L56 06:45](../lessons/056-organizing-insights-overview-must-watch.md); see [identification and belief](market-identification-and-belief.md).
- **See also:** [Desire hunt](../frameworks/desire-hunt.md) · [Desire calendar](desire-calendar.md) · [Product overview and mechanism](product-overview-and-mechanism.md) · [Core and sub avatars](core-and-sub-avatars.md) · [Evolve Copywriting: avatars and angles](../../copywriting/concepts/avatars-and-angles.md)

## Definition
- **Desire:** "strong feelings of wants or needs or wishes", "demand that already exists and is flowing through the market". Slide TL;DR: "Desire is the angle behind your ad and is the final outcome of what people get from buying your product." [L9 00:00–00:54; slide 9](../lessons/009-market-desires.md)
- **Mass desire:** "The public spread of a private want" (attributed to Schwartz): millions privately want the same thing, so advertising can harness it. [L61 00:00–01:22](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
- **Desire creates the avatar:** "desires essentially create avatars, or at least they create strong ones"; "A 22-year-old and a 45-year-old can share the same desire." [L61 00:00–02:40](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)

| L9 page text (Schwartz summary) | Physical / Material / Emotional-Social categories; permanent forces = mass instincts + tech problems; forces of change = style, education, culture | [L9 page text](../lessons/009-market-desires.md) | Same as L61 (older labels) |

Agent default: label with the L61 system (it is what the desire prompt and Growth Guide use) and add the L9 permanent/trending tag. (derived)

### Instinct power ranking (Desire Document)
| Rank | Instinct | Doc rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Health | "Still ultimate. Rich or poor, health scares move everyone" |
| 2 | Status | "Amplified massively by social media" |
| 3 | Sex | "Fundamental but more subtle in modern marketing (regulations/platforms)" |
| 4 | Comfort | "Elevated because technology made it possible + expected" |
| 5 | Control | "Boosted by modern complexity" |
| 6 | Belonging | "Less survival-urgent" |

"Health, status and sex are by far the big three." A lower rank "doesn't mean it's not powerful"; belonging and control "can flip flop". [L61 39:16–44:05](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)

### Technological problems (each with its feeling)
Complexity (feel dumb) · Overwhelm (anxious) · Fragility (frustrated) · Maintenance (tired) · Incompatibility (trapped) · Obsolescence (cheated). "The technology changes, but the problems persist." [L61 11:39–17:47](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)

## Why it matters
- "The amount of desire that your business can channel will determine how scalable your business is" (instructor claim). [L9 03:32](../lessons/009-market-desires.md)
- A desire shift alone moved a skate cut-resistant gear client from about $13.5K to about $201K revenue in one day after a hockey player's death made the risk real (on-screen dashboard; instructor's account). [L9 09:10–12:19](../lessons/009-market-desires.md)
- Product choice: "A winning product is a product that satisfies a mass desire"; pick the strongest desire magnitude (Shaun Eng). [L171 12:13–15:40](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
- Trending desires create roller-coaster performance; permanent desires stabilise it. [L121 07:31–12:50](../lessons/121-how-to-deal-with-roller-coaster-performance.md)

## How to use it (steps)
1. **Spot** desires: complaint patterns; "I want / I need / I wish I had / I was looking for"; cultural trends; where people spend money. [L61 34:21–36:31](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md); [L9 14:06](../lessons/009-market-desires.md) Full method: [desire hunt](../frameworks/desire-hunt.md).
2. **Go deeper** with the Why Train until you reach a feeling (desired, loved, safe, in control). [L14 10:28–11:35](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
3. **Classify:** instinct or tech problem + force of change (+ permanent/trending tag).
4. **Check fit:** can the product credibly deliver it? Is the desire already "met" by familiar products (stainless steel keeps water cold)? If met, you need a new mechanism or another desire. [L57 03:47–06:31](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
5. **Score** scope, urgency, staying power (1-5 each, derived scale); weight scope highest. [L61 36:31–40:39](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
6. **Channel:** write one headline per instinct/problem + trend/education pair. Don't test all 24 combinations; use the ranking. [L61 20:23–30:23](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
7. **Elevate (optional):** draft the same product one level up the ladder; keep only bridges that feel authentic. [L61 44:05–55:39](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
8. **Test** desires as separate cells; let spend decide. [L9 14:53](../lessons/009-market-desires.md)
9. **Label** every live ad's desire in the tracker (with awareness and sophistication). [L13 06:39](../lessons/013-action-item-practicing-the-core-3.md)

### Channel template (Desire Document)
```
<Product>:
Mass Instinct / Mass Tech Problem: <one of the twelve>
Style Trend / Mass Education: <specific trend or newly learned fact>
Channel: "<headline>"
```

## Common mistakes
- Demographics instead of desire ("female, 55+"). [L61 01:22–02:40](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
- Advertising "the tech" (motherboards) instead of the outcome. [L9 13:10](../lessons/009-market-desires.md)
- Surface desires ("I want to look better is not a desire"). [L14 09:26](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
- Building on trending desires alone (tariff ads, holiday promos) and riding the trend down. [L121 07:31](../lessons/121-how-to-deal-with-roller-coaster-performance.md)
- Choosing a "control" desire without checking sophistication: menopause buyers have heard "take control" "a zillion" times. [L63 40:52](../lessons/063-how-to-find-desires-part-3.md)
- Forced elevation (paper sold "to get girls"). [L61 46:42–47:57](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
- Analyst vocabulary instead of customer words. [L63 04:53](../lessons/063-how-to-find-desires-part-3.md)
- Treating the formula as a checklist ("Don't write like a mechanical robot"). [L61 57:02–58:30](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)

### Course conflicts and agent defaults
- **Desire-strength dimensions:** L9 video teaches two (intensity, scope) and says a third exists; L9 page text and L61 use scope, urgency, staying power. **Default:** score all three; urgency = intensity. [L9 03:32–04:28](../lessons/009-market-desires.md); [L61 36:31](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
- **Big vs small desire:** L9 says bigger desires take more skill and "sometimes it makes sense to start smaller" [L9 09:10](../lessons/009-market-desires.md); L61 says elevating up the hierarchy "can lead to more scale" [L61 44:05](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md) and, for health, consider a *lower* desire because "everybody in health is selling health" [L61 45:14](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md); L71 says at $0-100K/month pick "a medium-scope, strong desire" [L71 2:23:52–2:28:55](../lessons/071-the-evolve-avatar-training.md). **Default:** under $100K/month, pick a medium-scope, strong desire your product clearly serves; test elevation as a separate concept once a core desire wins.
