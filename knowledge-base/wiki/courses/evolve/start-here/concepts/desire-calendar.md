# Desire calendar
> A month-by-month map of which mass instincts peak for US buyers and why (from Spencer's Desire Calendar doc); use it as a lens for timing, framing and variations of existing winners, and as a diagnostic when a month underperforms, never as a replacement for your core desire.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** planning a month's or quarter's messaging; diagnosing a seasonal dip (January, February, July/August); writing variations of a winner or reviving a dead ad; timing gifting and holiday creative.
- **Inputs → outputs:** current month + next month, product category, current winners and dead ads → the month's top-3 instincts, 3+ framing tests (plus one inverse test), a launch date that respects buying lead time.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** open the calendar when performance is poor: "okay, what's going on with people?", then test ads against the rising desires. [L62 04:11](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
  - **DON'T** drop your core desires: "this does not mean that you stop going after all the other desires." [L62 04:11](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
  - **DO** launch seasonal messaging a month or more early: "people don't buy gifts on Valentine's Day, they buy gifts for Valentine's Day"; 67% of back-to-school shoppers start by early July (doc claim). [L62 07:32; 14:55](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
  - **DO** lead with the month's #1 instinct and layer #2 (September: control, then status). [L62 03:09–05:03](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
  - **DO** write at least one inverse test (people who resist the month's mood: "we missed summer too"). [L62 08:21](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
  - **DO** apply it to existing assets first: variations of current winners, revivals of ads that died. [L62 16:31](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
  - **IF** your niche peaks in the next month (supplements in January) **THEN** "don't take your foot off the pedal in December". [L62 13:48](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
  - **IF** late-summer performance dips **THEN** consider tightened spending after summer bills before changing the angle (instructor reasoning). [L62 05:53–06:41](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
  - **DON'T** assume a seasonal hook guarantees results ("back to school sale" does not mean "you're going to cook"). [L62 15:42](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
  - **DON'T** quote the doc's spend figures in ads or reports without checking the source; they are unverified doc claims with internal slips. [L64](../lessons/064-action-item-start-your-desire-hunt.md)
- **See also:** [Market desire](market-desire.md) · [Desire hunt](../frameworks/desire-hunt.md) · [Concept planning and growth guide](../frameworks/concept-planning-and-growth-guide.md) · [Tether Lab: triggering events](../../../../concepts/triggering-events.md)

## Definition
- A doc tab ("Desire Calendar", under Step 2: Desires of the Desire Document) giving, for each month: **top three desires** (ranked instincts for the period), **core desires** (named want → instinct), **opportunity** (messaging direction) and **data** ("the data for nerd section"). Built with AI deep research that Spencer "gave guidelines and corrected". [L62 02:26–03:09](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
- Rationale given: seasonal biology (summer sun → bigger purchases; winter → many smaller purchases and gifts), credited to a study the instructor names with a hedge (unverified). [L62 00:56–01:41](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
- It operationalises the **trending desire** idea (Valentine's Day spikes for florists) from [L9 02:36–03:32](../lessons/009-market-desires.md) and the "look at where people spend money" spotting rule from [L61 34:21–36:31](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md).

## Why it matters
- Explains the common pattern "December great, January sucks, February wrecked, July/August destroyed" (instructor's framing). [L62 00:00–00:56](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
- Timing changes results for the same ad: the CarePod product-aware static was "absolutely crushing" because it ran when people were in market for humidifiers. [L13 02:21–03:10](../lessons/013-action-item-practicing-the-core-3.md)
- Off-season needs a different awareness level: in summer "nobody is thinking of a humidifier", so the unaware hook is the one anyone stops for. [L121 15:36–16:57](../lessons/121-how-to-deal-with-roller-coaster-performance.md)
- "Timing is important: the same product can succeed or fail based on which desires are rising/falling." [L61 57:02](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)

## How to use it (steps)
1. Take the current and next month (creative needs lead time). [L62 14:55](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
2. Read the month's top-3 instincts and core desires (table below).
3. Map them to your product's existing desires; keep the core desire and change the *framing* (event, routine, gift, fresh start).
4. Write 3-6 lines: lead with #1, layer #2. Add one inverse line. [L62 03:09; 08:21](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
5. Apply first to current winners (variations) and dead ads (revivals). [L62 16:31](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
6. If a month is weak, check spending effects (post-vacation, post-holiday) before changing the product angle. [L62 06:41; 13:48](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)

## Examples
- **September (worked in the video):** routine/restart ("restart your routine"; "when the kids are gone, you now have time to focus on this"), fresh start ("September is the new January"; "be careful with that messaging"), parental success and empty-nesting moms (women ~40-55), inverse "we missed summer too". [L62 03:09–10:10](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
- **Evolve's own September line:** "you were side-questing the last two months, now it's time to lock in for Q4." [L62 09:07](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
- **Halloween for non-costume brands:** jewelry or health positioned as "look good for this specific event". [L62 10:10–11:57](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
- **Valentine's for non-romantic products:** Galentine's, friends, co-workers, "self appreciation"; can start mid-January. [L62 13:48–14:55](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
- **Back-to-school timing:** "if you're running back to school September 1st… you missed it." [L62 07:32](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)

## Common mistakes
- Running seasonal copy on the day itself instead of a month ahead. [L62 14:55](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
- Replacing core angles with seasonal ones. [L62 04:11](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)
- Treating seasonal promos as a strategy: product-aware ads + sophisticated market + trending desire = "roller coaster". [L121 07:31](../lessons/121-how-to-deal-with-roller-coaster-performance.md)
- Quoting the doc's "$127B in September" as a monthly number; it is a season total (derived from the doc's own figures). [L62 Prompts](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md)

### Course conflict and agent default
- **Seasonal/trending desires as a lever vs a liability.** L62 tells you to optimise ads toward the month's rising desires [L62 02:26; 16:31](../lessons/062-the-desire-calendar-part-2.md); L121 tells you to "rely less on trending desires" because trend-driven ads fall when the trend does [L121 07:31–12:50](../lessons/121-how-to-deal-with-roller-coaster-performance.md). **Default:** keep the core angle on a permanent desire; use the calendar only to reframe, time and vary it (never as the sole desire of a batch).
