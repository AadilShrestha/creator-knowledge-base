# Ad definitions (3:2:2, batch, concept, angle, variation, format, testing method)
> Evolve's shared vocabulary for planning a test: a **concept** is what *we* test, an **angle** is the customer's reason to buy (always written, even when the concept is a format), **variations** are the individual executions, **format** is how it is delivered, and every concept is loaded as one numbered 3:2:2 batch.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing a test brief or concept card; logging a batch in the Growth Guide; checking whether a teammate's "angle" is really a concept or format; tearing down a competitor ad into concept / angle / format; translating loose terms ("swing", "UGC test", "iteration") into this vocabulary.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a test idea + its sub-avatar(s) → one concept card: Batch #, Concept, Concept explanation, Angle(s) with sub-avatar, Variations (usually 3), Format, Testing method (Marksman/Sniper), Explanation; plus 2 headlines and 2 body copies for the 3:2:2.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** write the angle on every concept: "Every ad needs an angle, period." [L78 05:44](../lessons/078-understanding-ad-definitions.md)
  - **DO** test the angle with one question: "If the concept doesn't give the customer a reason to buy, it's not an angle yet." [L79 09:14](../lessons/079-how-to-come-up-with-ad-angles.md)
  - **DON'T** call Us vs Them, Before & After, Problem-Aware, Post-it note, "three reasons why", "UGC format" or "meme statics" an angle: "Comparing what? The what is the angle." [L79 02:00–05:27](../lessons/079-how-to-come-up-with-ad-angles.md); [L83 24:48](../lessons/083-putting-all-the-planning-together-examples.md)
  - **IF** the concept is "test the X angle" **THEN** still write the angle a second time "for tracking purposes". [L78 07:21–08:12](../lessons/078-understanding-ad-definitions.md)
  - **DO** load each concept as 3:2:2 (3 creatives, 2 body copies, 2 headlines) and give it a running batch number at the ad-set level. [L78 01:28–02:50](../lessons/078-understanding-ad-definitions.md); [L85 22:52–23:47](../lessons/085-the-3-0-evolve-growth-guide.md)
  - **DO** keep hooks direct until advanced: "the hook directly communicates the angle" ("14+ Hours. No Swelling"). [L79 20:19–22:14](../lessons/079-how-to-come-up-with-ad-angles.md)
  - **DO** derive the angle from a sub-avatar: desire → current behavior → gap. [L79 24:25–25:50](../lessons/079-how-to-come-up-with-ad-angles.md)
  - **IF** the variations carry different angles **THEN** it is a Marksman test; **IF** one angle **THEN** Sniper. [L81 10:58](../lessons/081-the-3-testing-methods-part-1.md)
  - **DON'T** get stuck in the "angle matrix": "just put down the angle that makes sense to you." [L83 02:16; 28:02](../lessons/083-putting-all-the-planning-together-examples.md)
  - **DON'T** reuse a competitor's lines as variations; copy the concept structure only (e.g. the "Nutella" template) and write your own angle. [L83 01:32–04:58](../lessons/083-putting-all-the-planning-together-examples.md); [L82 00:00](../lessons/082-the-3-testing-methods-part-2.md) ("ripping them, which is illegal")
- **See also:** [core-and-sub-avatars](core-and-sub-avatars.md) · [marksman-sniper-testing](marksman-sniper-testing.md) · [rule-of-one](rule-of-one.md) · [winning-ad-labels](winning-ad-labels.md) · [Angle generation prompt](../frameworks/angle-generation-prompt.md) · [Concept planning and growth guide](../frameworks/concept-planning-and-growth-guide.md) · [Campaign structure 3:2:2](../frameworks/campaign-structure-322.md) · Copywriting: [Avatars and angles](../../copywriting/concepts/avatars-and-angles.md), [Mini breakthrough memo and growth guide](../../copywriting/frameworks/mini-breakthrough-memo-and-growth-guide.md)

Recap line: "Concept again is your big idea. Angle is how you're choosing to position that big idea… variations is like how you execute it. And format is… how it actually comes out." [L78 15:40](../lessons/078-understanding-ad-definitions.md)

### Angle formula (L14) vs reason to buy (L78/L79)
- L14 (Shaun Eng) slide: "An angle is your chosen approach to connect with your avatar's current emotional reality. It's usually their dominant emotion + their daily struggle… Not about transformation, about connection." [L14 21:27–22:31](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
- L14 Skool **correction** (supersedes the slide): "Angle = Emotional Trigger + [Daily Struggle OR Ideal Outcome]". Problem/daily struggle = current pain + what makes it hell; aspirational/ideal = current emotion + dream state (e.g. fashion: show what the avatar aspires to be). [L14 page text](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
- L78/L79 (Spencer): angle = the main reason to buy, judged by "does it give the customer a reason to buy?"
- **Agent default (derived):** write the angle as a reason to buy (L79 test), then check it names the avatar's emotion plus a struggle or outcome (L14). A line that passes both is ready: "Stops tossing and turning" = reason to buy + daily struggle.

## Why it matters
- Clear terms let you "think less about terms and philosophies, and just focus on pure execution". [L78 00:41](../lessons/078-understanding-ad-definitions.md)
- Format-only tests produce no learning: "That's why your ads suck because you don't even have an angle. You're just thinking of like a format." [L78 06:39](../lessons/078-understanding-ad-definitions.md)
- Trending formats "are good hooks and good headlines… But if the angle sucks, no one's going to care"; templates fail "because your angle and… what you've put into it is not really working". [L79 04:29–06:11](../lessons/079-how-to-come-up-with-ad-angles.md)
- The batch number measures "how many ads it took you to kind of unlock that winner" and keeps reports usable. [L78 02:50](../lessons/078-understanding-ad-definitions.md); [L85 22:52–23:47](../lessons/085-the-3-0-evolve-growth-guide.md)
- Planning variables up front "makes learnings on the ads much easier since the variables are stated beforehand". [L14 32:17–33:16](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)

## How to use (steps)
1. Pick the sub-avatar(s) ([core-and-sub-avatars](core-and-sub-avatars.md)).
2. Write the **angle** for each: desire → behavior → gap → reason to buy ("better than nose strips"). Run the concept-or-angle test. [L79 09:14–11:09; 24:25](../lessons/079-how-to-come-up-with-ad-angles.md)
3. Write the **concept** (what we test) and a one-line concept explanation. [L83 00:00; 06:25](../lessons/083-putting-all-the-planning-together-examples.md)
4. Choose **variations**: 3 executions (2-4 allowed; "don't do more than four"; with two ideas run 2:2:2). [L83 00:39](../lessons/083-putting-all-the-planning-together-examples.md)
5. Choose **format** and **testing method** (one angle = Sniper; several = Marksman). [L81 10:58](../lessons/081-the-3-testing-methods-part-1.md)
6. Add 2 headlines + 2 body copies; assign the next **batch #**; put it in the Growth Guide with desire/core avatar and angle columns. [L85 05:00–09:57; 22:52](../lessons/085-the-3-0-evolve-growth-guide.md)
7. Plan 5-10 concepts as the first backlog. [L84 page text](../lessons/084-action-item-plan-your-5-10-first-concepts.md)

### Concept card template (L83 fields + L78 record; derived merge)
```
Batch #:
Concept (what we test):
Concept explanation (one sentence):
Angle(s) (reason to buy; repeat even if concept = angle):
  - Angle 1 → Sub-avatar: Desire / + Experience | Emotion | Behavior | Demographic
Variations (2-4): V1 / V2 / V3
Format:
Testing method: Marksman | Sniper
Explanation (why this structure, what we do with each answer):
Headlines (2): H1 / H2
Body copies (2): B1 / B2
```

## Common mistakes
- **Format as angle** ("testing UGC formats of what?"). [L78 06:39](../lessons/078-understanding-ad-definitions.md)
- **Product-first angles** ("our doctor designed it, let's tell people"): "people just don't care". [L82 14:36–16:13](../lessons/082-the-3-testing-methods-part-2.md)
- **Generic hold under a specific angle**: if the body "could be used on any angle", the angle is not executed (Career-ending injury swap test). [L83 09:49–11:23; 26:17](../lessons/083-putting-all-the-planning-together-examples.md)
- **Variations that "say the same thing very similarly"** in a Sniper test. [L83 24:48](../lessons/083-putting-all-the-planning-together-examples.md)
- **No batch number on ad sets**: reports become "a complete mess". [L85 22:52–23:47](../lessons/085-the-3-0-evolve-growth-guide.md)
- **Over-arguing terms**: "use the definitions that make the most amount of sense to you." [L78 00:41](../lessons/078-understanding-ad-definitions.md)

## Cross-course notes
- **Evolve Copywriting (same definitions):** concept vs angle vs hook, "the What is the angle", Marksman/Sniper and the "double sniper" variant are already in [Mini breakthrough memo and growth guide](../../copywriting/frameworks/mini-breakthrough-memo-and-growth-guide.md) and [Avatars and angles](../../copywriting/concepts/avatars-and-angles.md). Start Here adds the batch-number rule, the six-field concept card, the 2-4 creative range and the L14 emotional angle formula.
- **Evolve Copywriting (one main angle):** Copywriting says "focus on one main angle per ad"; Start Here allows several angles per *concept* (one per variation) in Marksman. Not a conflict: one angle per creative, several per concept.
- **Tether Lab (term clash):** Tether's [Iteration vs variation](../../../../concepts/iteration-vs-variation.md) uses "variation" for re-expressing a fixed core message across primitives and formats. Evolve's "variation" is any of the 3 executions in a concept, which may each be a different angle. When briefing, say which meaning you use.
- **Tether Lab (agrees on message first):** [Creative testing system](../../../../frameworks/creative-testing-system.md) tests the core message before formats, the same priority as "every ad needs an angle".
