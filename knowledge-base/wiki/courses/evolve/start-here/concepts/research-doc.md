# The research doc
> One document per brand holds all research: links, an ad-copy bank (headlines, hooks, CTAs, phrase bank, visual ideas), product, core problems and solutions, avatars, FAQ, competitors and an ad-account audit. Two versions exist (V2.0 shown in the videos, V4.0 to copy); each research module fills specific sections, and the finished doc feeds the product overview, desire hunt, avatars and the brand's AI project.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** starting research for a brand; deciding where a finding goes; checking whether research is "done"; building the context pack for an AI project or prompt.
- **Inputs → outputs:** brand onboarding answers, website, reviews, Reddit/YouTube/TikTok/Amazon/Google findings, competitor list, Ads Manager access → a filled V4.0 research doc with every section filled or marked "not found", plus a brain dump of ad ideas.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** copy the UPDATED (V4.0) doc; the V2.0 doc shown in lessons 22-36 is "(Optional)". [L21 page text](../lessons/021-action-item-copy-research-document.md)
  - **DO** run research one module at a time: "1. Watch Module → 2. Take Action → 3. Finish Research Step → Watch Next Module." [L17 page text](../lessons/017-overview-self-onboarding.md)
  - **DO** fill the self-onboarding form first as the "before" baseline; compare at the end. [L17 01:50–03:20](../lessons/017-overview-self-onboarding.md)
  - **DO** write every headline, visual or idea into the doc the moment it appears (brain dump / ad copy bank). [L19 04:11](../lessons/019-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-1.md); [L20 01:59–02:40](../lessons/020-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-2.md)
  - **DO** tally repeated phrases by adding "1" each time you see one (buzz/niche words). [L26 01:25–02:12](../lessons/026-google-research-part-2.md)
  - **DO** keep a phrase bank of positives (words for the main claim) and negatives (relatable problems). [L20 01:26–02:00](../lessons/020-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-2.md)
  - **DO** ask "who am I writing this for?" before writing any ad copy in the doc. [L21 doc, Ad Copy](../lessons/021-action-item-copy-research-document.md)
  - **DON'T** spend days on brand guidelines first: "not going to make or break your ads." [L19 01:51–02:35](../lessons/019-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-1.md)
  - **DON'T** build avatars from the doc's old demographic avatar block; the newer avatar training replaces it. [L20 page text](../lessons/020-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-2.md)
  - **DO** finish with a 10-15 minute clean-up (customer now/after, competitor notes, price range, creative audit, plus any missing section such as "Potential Competitive Disadvantages"). [L51 00:00–01:21](../lessons/051-cleaning-up-the-research-doc.md)
  - **DON'T** paste AI-returned "customer quotes" into the doc without opening the source. [L64](../lessons/064-action-item-start-your-desire-hunt.md)
- **See also:** [Research stack workflow](../frameworks/research-stack-workflow.md) · [Product overview and mechanism](product-overview-and-mechanism.md) · [Desire hunt](../frameworks/desire-hunt.md) · [Tether Lab: customer research stack](../../../../frameworks/customer-research-stack.md)

## Definition
A per-brand Google Doc that works like a "brand Bible": quick links at the top, then every research finding filed under fixed headings. "The whole goal is that we just have information somewhat organized" so the creative roadmap, avatar and desire work can pull from it quickly. [L20 00:36](../lessons/020-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-2.md); [L51 01:21–01:47](../lessons/051-cleaning-up-the-research-doc.md) "This is not like a do or die. You don't have to follow this exactly." [L19 04:11](../lessons/019-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-1.md)

### V2.0 Core Research questions (still useful as a checklist)
Who is the company · physical product · function · features · benefits · competitive advantages & claims · use cases · reviews · bad direct competitor reviews · buzz/niche words (tally) · wants/needs (surface) · what customers do not want · possible desires (rank by power) · customer now · customer AFTER our product · questions customers have · other solutions for main desires · day-to-day struggles · beliefs & objections · problems we have to solve while marketing. [L21 V2.0 template](../lessons/021-action-item-copy-research-document.md)

## Why it matters
- Spencer's agency creative strategy used to be "Twitter and whatever influencers were posting… and Foreplay"; the research process is what he credits with letting them "predictably grow" brands (instructor claim). [L17 00:00–00:54](../lessons/017-overview-self-onboarding.md)
- It is the input to everything after research: "taking your research and learning how to use it to build belief". [L56 06:45](../lessons/056-organizing-insights-overview-must-watch.md)
- It is one of three files the brand's Claude project is trained on (reviews, research doc, onboarding doc). [L59 01:22–02:39](../lessons/059-understanding-your-product-part-3.md)
- The Desire Hunting template says to fill it from the research doc first, and use a blank sheet only if none exists. [L64 page text](../lessons/064-action-item-start-your-desire-hunt.md)

## How to use it (steps)
1. Fill the self-onboarding form (10 questions; differentiator first). [L18](../lessons/018-action-item-self-onboarding.md)
2. Copy V4.0, rename for the brand, fill the Overview links. [L19 00:00–00:52](../lessons/019-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-1.md); [L21](../lessons/021-action-item-copy-research-document.md)
3. Work module by module in the order of the [research stack workflow](../frameworks/research-stack-workflow.md); after each, file findings under the schema above and dump ideas into Ad Copy.
4. Tally buzz words as you go; keep verbatim customer phrases in quotes with their source. [L26 01:25](../lessons/026-google-research-part-2.md)
5. Turn objections into "How do we…" problems with a candidate answer. [L49](../lessons/049-understanding-objections.md)
6. Clean up: every section filled or marked "not found"; add missing sections. [L51](../lessons/051-cleaning-up-the-research-doc.md); [L52](../lessons/052-action-item-clean-up-the-research-doc.md)
7. Hand off: Product Overview doc ([L57-60](product-overview-and-mechanism.md)) → desire hunt ([L64](../lessons/064-action-item-start-your-desire-hunt.md)) → avatars ([core and sub avatars](core-and-sub-avatars.md)); attach the doc to the brand's AI project.

## Examples
- **Laifen hairdryer (demo brand), clean-up page:** advantages & claims ("Award Winning Hair Dryer - Tech Advisor & Tech Radar; 100% Money Back Guarantee within 30 days; 2 YEAR WARRANTY; Free shipping; Air Speed performance…; less expensive alternative"), the added "Potential Competitive Disadvantages" heading, use cases ("For Professional hair stylist"). [L51 01:30](../lessons/051-cleaning-up-the-research-doc.md)
- **Visual ideas logged live:** "someone with like a bunch of foam in their hair", "someone who like has their hair on fire". [L20 01:59–02:40](../lessons/020-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-2.md)
- **Google research → objection:** big trusted brands fill the results → "competing against big brands and trusted sources online for shopping". [L25 00:37–01:36](../lessons/025-google-research-part-1.md)
- **Amazon tiers:** super budget about $10-$50, "medium" about $75-$200 ("that's us"). [L28 03:23](../lessons/028-amazon-research-part-1.md)

## Common mistakes
- Watching all 20+ research modules in a row: "your brain will be OVERWHELMED… and you will not do any research." [L17 page text](../lessons/017-overview-self-onboarding.md)
- Filling the doc from memory instead of the market. [L22 00:53](../lessons/022-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
- Blocking on brand guidelines. [L19 01:51](../lessons/019-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-1.md)
- Treating the V2.0 "Market Awareness" heading (price tiers, "who we're selling to") as the Schwartz awareness level. [L28 03:23](../lessons/028-amazon-research-part-1.md)
- Using the old demographic avatar block (name, age, income) as the avatar. [L20 page text](../lessons/020-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-2.md)
- Moving data between documents before the doc is clean. [L51 00:00](../lessons/051-cleaning-up-the-research-doc.md)

### Course conflict and agent default
- **Avatar block in the doc.** L20's walkthrough calls the demographic avatar block "the biggest thing that we've added" [L20 03:30](../lessons/020-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-2.md); its page text and the V4.0 template now point to desire-based core avatars from the newer training [L20 page text; L21 doc](../lessons/021-action-item-copy-research-document.md). **Default:** fill the V4.0 desire-based avatar fields; ignore name/age/income unless they change the message.
