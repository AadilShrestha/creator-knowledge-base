# Market identification and market belief
> Identification is the role your buyer wants to play (shown, mostly without words, through environment, people, story and expression); belief is the filter every claim must pass. Desire sets the ad's content, identity sets its visuals, belief sets how the ad is built, and without belief nobody buys.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** briefing visuals, locations and creator casting; writing for products with no clear "problem" (fashion, jewelry); writing scripts or pages that must be believed; choosing a proof device; diagnosing a good-looking ad that won't convert.
- **Inputs → outputs:** avatar research (language, environment, who they want to be), market beliefs, product beliefs, objections, too-good-to-be-true claims → (a) the four identification answers turned into a visual/casting brief; (b) one justifiable promise plus a chosen belief device (yes train, symptoms, authority, logic) and a draft.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** let desire set the content, identity set the visuals, and belief set the development of the ad: "If your customer does not have BELIEF… they will NOT buy." [L16 06:21–07:13](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)
  - **DO** answer the four identification questions before filming: where they want to be (environment), what they want to look like (model), what they want to feel (story), what self-image they want to portray (expression). [L15 16:53–17:53](../lessons/015-market-identification.md)
  - **DO** cast creators who embody the identity (outdoors creator grew 300 → ~15,000 followers; "another dude in a t-shirt in a storage room" did not, instructor claim). [L15 08:14–10:07](../lessons/015-market-identification.md)
  - **DON'T** stretch the role (billionaires selling mass deodorant; a Rolls-Royce driver in a minivan ad). [L15 15:56–16:53](../lessons/015-market-identification.md)
  - **DON'T** position against the buyer's self-image (Nescafé's time-saver clash). [L15 10:52–11:57](../lessons/015-market-identification.md)
  - **DON'T** violate established beliefs: "nothing you promise them, no matter how appealing, can save your ad." [L16 03:38–04:42](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)
  - **DON'T** stack promises; strengthen "one justifiable promise". [L16 04:42–06:21](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)
  - **IF** you must change a belief **THEN** acknowledge it bluntly and bring strong authority/evidence; never assert the new belief bare. [L16 12:06–13:48](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)
  - **IF** the product's effect is invisible on camera **THEN** build belief with a proxy the customer can check (nail growth for collagen) or education, in compliant wording. [L63 07:46–12:23](../lessons/063-how-to-find-desires-part-3.md)
  - **DON'T** fake endorsements or proof to manufacture identity or belief (repo rule; the course warns "you will get in trouble" for untrue celebrity claims). [L61 53:15](../lessons/061-understanding-applying-desire-part-1.md)
- **See also:** [Evolve Copywriting: beliefs](../../copywriting/concepts/beliefs.md) · [Evolve Copywriting: authority in ads](../../copywriting/concepts/authority-in-ads.md) · [Market sophistication levers](market-sophistication-levers.md) · [Tether Lab: identity stacking](../../../../concepts/identity-stacking.md) · [Tether Lab: three selves](../../../../concepts/three-selves.md)

## Definition
- **Identification:** "The desire of your customer to act out certain roles in their life… not for satisfaction, but for recognition & expression… Identification marketing is how we communicate the feelings our customer desires, without words." "We don't just buy objects, we buy roles." [L15 00:43–04:13](../lessons/015-market-identification.md) It can also mean "embrace who your customer already is". [L15 00:43–01:37](../lessons/015-market-identification.md)
- **Belief:** "Your customers mental picture of the world they live in… a filter through which your product must pass or be rejected… You leverage your CUSTOMERS logic, not your own." [L16 00:55–01:49](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)
- They are two of the five forces checked before any ad (identity, belief, desire, awareness, sophistication). [L8 00:40](../lessons/008-overview-of-psychology.md)

## Why it matters
- "If you can channel belief, it can be more powerful than the strongest of desires." [L16 06:21](../lessons/016-market-belief.md) "Desires don't mean anything unless I can build belief." [L56 06:45](../lessons/056-organizing-insights-overview-must-watch.md)
- "Just because someone wants this doesn't mean they believe it." [L63 33:53–35:06](../lessons/063-how-to-find-desires-part-3.md)
- Specificity creates belief: an ad that shows the avatar's exact scenarios "feels bespoke", so belief and conversion rise. [L14 00:00–00:59; 05:10](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
- "Challenging beliefs is the number one mistake… a lot of times you don't even realize that you're doing it." [L16 04:42](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)

## How to use it (steps)
### Identification brief
1. Write the role the product offers today and the role it should offer; keep both "at the same level". [L15 15:56–16:53](../lessons/015-market-identification.md)
2. Answer the four questions; convert each into a brief field:

| Question | Brief field | Course example |
|---|---|---|
| Where does my ideal customer want to be? | Environment / location | Dr. Squatch in nature; CarePod in a luxurious modern home |
| What do they want to look like? | Model / creator casting | Outdoors creator "JJ" in gear |
| What do they want to feel? | Story of the ad | Nostalgia, achievement, success (Rolex) |
| What self-image do they want to portray? | Expression / persona | "Loving, caring parent that is healthy and successful" |

3. Add credibility symbols that fit the role (doctor-created, family, home) only if true. [L15 14:22–16:53](../lessons/015-market-identification.md)
4. Cast for look, setting and language; test; if the identity clashes with the buyer's self-image, reframe the association. [L15 08:14–11:57](../lessons/015-market-identification.md)

### Belief build
1. Research: current market beliefs, beliefs about your product, most common objections, claims that sound too good to be true. [L16 16:27](../lessons/016-market-belief.md); objections → "How do we…" problems [L49 00:00–01:44](../lessons/049-understanding-objections.md)
2. Check the draft claim against those beliefs; rework anything that contradicts an established belief.
3. Cut to one justifiable promise.
4. Pick the device:

| Device | Job | Template | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yes train | Show you're talking about *them*; non-buyers self-exclude | 4-5 yes-questions about their struggles → "Then you've experienced what millions of others have" → "Which is why [product] allows you to [new direction]" | [L16 08:08–09:39](../lessons/016-market-belief.md) |
| Symptoms | "Prove that you have been there" | "[Daily frustration]. That's if [worse thing]. [Option A] supposedly [promise], but [failure]. And as soon as you try [option B], [new failure]. That's why [we] invented [product]." | [L16 09:39–12:06](../lessons/016-market-belief.md) |
| Authority | Loosen an old belief, install a new one | "I know you think X is true, but I'm going to show you why it is false. Forget everything you've [heard]… Because you are about to enter [new world]. [Evidence + authority]." | [L16 12:06–13:48](../lessons/016-market-belief.md) |
| Logic | Chain accepted facts to the product | [accepted fact] → [fact] → [however, problem] → [so you need X] → [to know X you must Y] → [too much / too little] → [which is why product does Y] | [L16 13:48–16:27](../lessons/016-market-belief.md) |

5. Prefer proof from the customer's own logic and from people like them ("there are people with similar beliefs who… are currently using our product"). [L16 01:49–02:47](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)

## Common mistakes
- Challenging a belief head-on without evidence (the "born on Kepler-452b" test). [L16 02:47–04:42](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)
- Promise piling, especially in supplements. [L16 05:31–06:21](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)
- A creator who doesn't look like the customer or the problem. [L14 07:09](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md); [L15 08:14](../lessons/015-market-identification.md)
- Believing your own claim is believable: the "I'm going to make you a millionaire in e-com" test shows how customers feel. [L56 03:30–04:24](../lessons/056-organizing-insights-overview-must-watch.md)
- Thinking an ad works because of its format ("us vs them") instead of the belief it builds. [L56 05:05](../lessons/056-organizing-insights-overview-must-watch.md)

### Course conflicts and agent defaults
- **Identity for everyone, or skip it?** L15: identification amplifies desire for every brand, "where you will start to really surpass all of your competition" [L15 00:00–00:43](../lessons/015-market-identification.md); L12 treats it as the stage-5 move [L12 10:56](../lessons/012-market-sophistication.md); L70: "99% of you should only be focusing on new information and/or new mechanisms" because brand-level identity takes a skill set Spencer has not built; for most, new identity = an underserved avatar [L70 00:00; 04:31](../lessons/070-how-to-find-apply-new-identities-part-4.md); L173: brand identity is "a whole other discussion" and not his specialty [L173 07:20–08:14](../lessons/173-how-brands-actually-scale-to-8-figures.md). **Default:** always apply identification at the brief level (environment, casting, expression); don't attempt a brand-identity repositioning as the strategic lever unless the brand already has that capability.
- **"Can't challenge beliefs" vs the authority device.** Rule 1 forbids violating established beliefs [L16 03:38](../lessons/016-market-belief.md), yet the authority technique is "I'm going to show you why this is false" [L16 12:06](../lessons/016-market-belief.md). **Default:** work with existing beliefs by default; only challenge one when you hold strong, checkable evidence and acknowledge the belief first.
