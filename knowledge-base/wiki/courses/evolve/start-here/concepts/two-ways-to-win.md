# Two ways to win
> If you are not first to market (you almost never are), there are only two ways to stand out: innovate on the product (a new or renamed mechanism, or a genuinely superior product) or innovate on the avatar (an underserved person nobody speaks to). At early stages, copy the desire your biggest competitor scales with and "out-avatar" them instead of trying to out-market them.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** go/no-go on a product or niche; writing the "why we will win" line of a brief; positioning against a saturated category; choosing what a new batch innovates on; auditing why a competent ad blends in.
- **Inputs → outputs:** product overview, competitor claims and avatars (ad library, Reddit, reviews), market sophistication read → one chosen lever (mechanism / superior product / underserved avatar) with evidence, or a "no-go" if neither exists.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** decide how you will stand out before testing: "Either you innovate on the product or you innovate on the avatar." If you don't know, "you're going to fail." [L171 19:15–19:46](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
  - **IF** you find neither a new mechanism nor an underserved avatar **THEN** don't run the product: "If I cannot find any of these two, then I wouldn't run it." [L172 23:00; 26:18](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md); [L171 17:13](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
  - **DON'T** fear saturation: "Saturation = big audience." [L171 18:14–18:44](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
  - **DO** at $0-100K/month, find the desire your biggest competitor scales with and "out-avatar" them rather than "out-market" them: "you're not that good at marketing yet." [L71 2:23:52–2:28:55](../lessons/071-the-evolve-avatar-training.md)
  - **DO** treat "new identity" at your stage as "finding an underserved new identity that you can market to… also known as developing a winning avatar." [L70 04:31](../lessons/070-how-to-find-apply-new-identities-part-4.md)
  - **DO** look for mechanisms in what you already have (ingredient, process, delivery form, unadvertised feature, renamed feature with a new purpose) before inventing products. [L65 22:38](../lessons/065-understanding-market-sophistication-part-1.md); [L171 32:05–33:38](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
  - **DON'T** claim a feature is unique or "first" if it is industry-standard; a rename must point to a new purpose, not a fake novelty. [L65 doc](../lessons/065-understanding-market-sophistication-part-1.md) (see conflict below)
  - **DO** make the creator and the ad visibly match the avatar; an ad that differentiates by neither product nor avatar is "knee pain, pull on the braces, bro". [L14 07:09–09:26](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
  - **IF** the product itself is clearly superior on something the market cares about **THEN** show it plainly; "The iPhone didn't even need to out-market BlackBerry." [L173 02:48–03:46](../lessons/173-how-brands-actually-scale-to-8-figures.md)
- **See also:** [Market sophistication levers](market-sophistication-levers.md) · [Product overview and mechanism](product-overview-and-mechanism.md) · [Core and sub avatars](core-and-sub-avatars.md) · [Product research screen](../frameworks/product-research-screen.md) · [Evolve Copywriting: positioning](../../copywriting/concepts/positioning.md)

## Why it matters
- First-to-market is "extremely rare"; copy-paste sellers only win when they are first. [L171 18:14–18:44](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
- In stage 3-5 markets a plain claim is ignored; differentiation is the only route to belief. See [market sophistication levers](market-sophistication-levers.md).
- An underserved avatar can make a stage-5 product feel new: Stanley's old market claims ("Keeps ice 2 hours longer than YETI") vs the Gen Z market ("Your emotional support water bottle"). [L171 27:08–28:36](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
- Product superiority can dwarf creative work: same strategy, a better Product B took spend ~5x with higher ROAS (instructor's case, Ads Manager screenshots). [L173 01:55–02:48](../lessons/173-how-brands-actually-scale-to-8-figures.md)

## How to use it (steps)
1. **Map the market:** competitor ads (Ad Library, keyword search), Reddit pros/cons, reviews; list each competitor's claim and avatar. [L172 07:50–19:26](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md)
2. **Product lane:** from the [product overview](product-overview-and-mechanism.md) and the [mechanism finder](../lessons/066-how-to-find-apply-new-mechanisms-part-2.md), list mechanisms no competitor talks about; check each against the product page and reality.
3. **Avatar lane:** list who the competitors target; find who they ignore (age, body type, situation, culture). Check that the avatar has the desire and the reach. [L171 29:08–31:35](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
4. **Pick one lever** (or both, True Classic style) and write it as one line: "Not another [category], but the first one that…" (mechanism) or "Not another [category], but for people who…" (avatar). [L65 Big 3 recap](../lessons/065-understanding-market-sophistication-part-1.md)
5. **No lever → no-go** (or keep researching). [L172 23:00](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md)
6. **Test** the lever as a new concept with three congruent hooks; when it wins, build sub-avatars around it before widening. [L71 2:23:52–2:28:55](../lessons/071-the-evolve-avatar-training.md)

## Examples
- **True Classic** (both levers): previous market stage 5 ("softest", "highest quality", "best fit"); dad-bod market "first to acknowledge this avatar exists"; mechanism = body-optimising fit that "hides your gut, enhances your shoulders". [L171 29:08–31:35](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
- **Stanley** (avatar): from construction workers to Gen Z girls. [L171 27:08–28:36](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
- **Astronaut Projector** (mechanism then avatar): astronaut form of an old starlight projector, then a Gen Z TikTok-trend store; "like 10 million in one year" (claim). [L171 23:27–25:06](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
- **Oversized hoodie brand** (product tweak): 10-20% longer so it covers the knees; "multi-nine-figure brand" (claim; brand name unverified; needs stock, not for beginners). [L172 36:39–38:48](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md)
- **Lip duo UGC** (neither): "amazing for your lips, nourishing, smells good" with no avatar specifics → blends in. [L14 07:09–09:26](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)

## Common mistakes
- Spam-testing products with no planned edge: "your entire success is based off luck." [L171 01:53–02:50](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
- Trying to out-market a bigger competitor with the same avatar before you have the skill. [L71 2:23:52](../lessons/071-the-evolve-avatar-training.md)
- Pivoting desire too early: change the core desire only after 2-4 weeks of failed sub-avatar tests. [L76 13:20](../lessons/076-how-to-build-sub-avatars.md)
- Selling a product you don't believe works (chin slimmers "largely ineffective"); "DON'T SELL BS GIMMICKY PRODUCTS". [L171 25:06–27:08](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
- Strengths the market doesn't care about. [L173 08:14–09:04](../lessons/173-how-brands-actually-scale-to-8-figures.md)
- Taking competitors' TikTok Shop videos as your own ads (L172 course tactic: **don't execute**; copy the mechanism, not the asset; make your own creative with the same structure). [L172 54:30–59:30](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md)

### Course conflicts and agent defaults
- **Does a new mechanism or new avatar reset sophistication to stage 1?** L171 video: Shilajit gummies, Stanley and True Classic reset to "level one" [L171 21:22; 28:36](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md); L171 page correction: same product + new mechanism = level 3 unless framed as something entirely different [L171 page text](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md). **Default:** assume stage 3 for a new mechanism; a truly unaddressed avatar can behave like an early-stage market, but still bring proof.
- **Renaming standard features vs false uniqueness.** L171 says name/rename features (Stanley's double-wall insulation) to create a mechanism [L171 32:05–33:38](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md); the L65 doc lists "claiming false uniqueness for industry-standard processes" as a mechanism mistake [L65 doc](../lessons/065-understanding-market-sophistication-part-1.md). **Default:** rename to give a feature a new purpose or clearer meaning; never call it "first", "only" or "patented" unless true.
- **Out-product vs test cheap first.** L173: "If you want to succeed on easy mode, just make the best product" [L173 03:46–04:36](../lessons/173-how-brands-actually-scale-to-8-figures.md); L171: beginners shouldn't buy stock or customise; dropship and test for traction first [L171 18:44–19:15](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md). **Default:** beginners win on avatar and messaging with existing products; invest in product superiority once traction proves demand.
