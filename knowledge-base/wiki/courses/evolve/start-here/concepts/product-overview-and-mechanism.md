# Product overview and mechanism
> Every product is two products: the physical one (materials, specs) and the functional one (what it does); people buy the functional one. Before desires, avatars or ads, fill the 7-step Product Overview doc (basics → how it works in sixth-grade words → features → assumed benefits → assumed desires → new mechanisms → hidden mechanisms) and name the mechanism: *how* the product creates the result.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** starting work on any brand or product; before desire, sophistication or avatar work; when an ad aimed at a real desire won't convert; when a product seems "the same as everyone else's".
- **Inputs → outputs:** product page, ingredients/materials, reviews, research doc, brand onboarding answers → a filled Product Overview doc (assumptions labelled), a list of candidate mechanisms with the desire each serves, and a superiority verdict (strengths/weaknesses/uniqueness vs alternatives).
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** start with the product: "Understanding your product is the first and most important thing." [L22 00:00](../lessons/022-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
  - **DO** split physical (materials, size, specs) from functional (benefits, results, experiences); "People buy what it does and not what it is." [L57 02:57–03:47; 11:22](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
  - **DON'T** advertise features and hope the viewer connects them to a benefit ("visible heat… I don't really know how that's a benefit for me yet"). [L22 03:16](../lessons/022-understanding-your-product-part-1.md); [L57 03:47](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
  - **DO** name the mechanism: "the specific way that your product creates the results. It's not what it does. It's how it does it." [L57 11:22](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
  - **DO** write "how it works" twice: technical, then sixth-grade; check it in a readability tool. [L58 03:13–05:44](../lessons/058-understanding-your-product-part-2.md)
  - **DO** label benefits, desires and mechanisms "assumed" until research validates them: "a lot of you think you have a unique mechanism that's actually not that unique." [L58 14:24; 20:40](../lessons/058-understanding-your-product-part-2.md)
  - **DON'T** target desires the product "cannot live up to". [L57 03:47–04:42](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
  - **DO** cold-read your own site in incognito as a stranger; anything you know that the site doesn't say, "communicate that right away". [L22 01:25–03:16](../lessons/022-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
  - **DON'T** trust an AI-filled overview: "AI in general makes educated guesses… it assumes"; ask what is fluff. [L59 04:06](../lessons/059-understanding-your-product-part-3.md)
  - **DO** judge superiority by the market's view: "Your opinion DOES NOT MATTER. The Markets Opinion is what matters." [L173 06:26](../lessons/173-how-brands-actually-scale-to-8-figures.md)
  - **DON'T** copy example-doc claims ("patented", "68%", "Ozempic") into a real brand's doc or ads. [L60](../lessons/060-action-item-complete-understanding-product-doc.md); [L59 compliance note](../lessons/059-understanding-your-product-part-3.md)
- **See also:** [Market sophistication levers](market-sophistication-levers.md) · [Market desire](market-desire.md) · [Research doc](research-doc.md) · [Evolve Copywriting: unique mechanism](../../copywriting/concepts/unique-mechanism.md) · [Evolve Copywriting: compression and plain language](../../copywriting/concepts/compression-and-plain-language.md)

## Definition
| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Physical product | "The materials, ingredients, and components you deliver; size, weight, construction, specifications; what you can touch, measure, and manufacture." It "justifies and documents the quality" and provides evidence | [L57 doc frame](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md); [L9 13:10–14:06](../lessons/009-market-desires.md) |
| Functional product | "What your product DOES for people; the series of benefits that solve problems or fulfill desires; the performances, results, and experiences it creates" | [L57 doc frame](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md) |
| Mechanism | "The specific WAY your product creates its results. Not what it does, but HOW it does it." Mechanisms "create believability in sophisticated markets", give "NEW hope", differentiate from "everything else they've tried" | [L57 11:22–13:52](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md) |
| New mechanism (USP) | "What makes your product unique? Does your product have any NEW mechanisms that separate it from the market?" | [L57 template step 6](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md) |
| Hidden mechanism | Something the product does or contains that you don't advertise, surfaced by four prompts | [L57 template step 7](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md); [L58 24:06–31:40](../lessons/058-understanding-your-product-part-2.md) |

"Every performance your product delivers is a potential avenue to a different avatar within your market." [L57 doc frame](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)

## Why it matters
- Ads fail for three reasons: you don't understand where the product fits, what the market wants, or you fail to execute. [L22 00:00](../lessons/022-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
- "Your product is what will create the majority of your success": same agency strategy, Product A → Product B took spend from $6,940 to $38,961 a month and ROAS from 3.39 to 4.44 (Ads Manager screenshots; instructor's case). [L173 00:58–03:46](../lessons/173-how-brands-actually-scale-to-8-figures.md)
- "Before you worry about who to target, what to say, how to scale… ABO, CBO, you have to master what your product does." [L57 14:44](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
- A mechanism the brand wasn't advertising (CarePod stainless steel; harness front D-ring) became the winning angle (instructor claims). [L66 03:13–07:10](../lessons/066-how-to-find-apply-new-mechanisms-part-2.md)

## How to use it (steps)
1. **Collect inputs:** brand onboarding answers (differentiator first; every enticing claim) [L18 doc Q1, Q9](../lessons/018-action-item-self-onboarding.md), product page, reviews, research doc.
2. **Cold-read** the site incognito; log each item as physical / function / feature / benefit / competitive advantage or claim / use case. [L22 02:18–04:13](../lessons/022-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
3. **Write objections as you see them** ("Dyson knockoff", "expensive"). [L23 00:57](../lessons/023-understanding-the-product-part-2.md)
4. **Fill the Product Overview doc** (template below). Route: no research yet → manual ([L58](../lessons/058-understanding-your-product-part-2.md)); research exists → Claude project with reviews + research doc + onboarding doc, then audit ([L59](../lessons/059-understanding-your-product-part-3.md)). [L60](../lessons/060-action-item-complete-understanding-product-doc.md)
5. **Map** feature → benefit → desire with "why does someone want that?"; allow many-to-many. [L58 07:41–20:40](../lessons/058-understanding-your-product-part-2.md)
6. **Mechanisms:** list what's new; run the hidden-mechanism prompts; then run the [New Mechanism Finder Prompt](../lessons/066-how-to-find-apply-new-mechanisms-part-2.md) in a clean chat and check each output against the product page. [L66 00:00–03:13](../lessons/066-how-to-find-apply-new-mechanisms-part-2.md)
7. **Desire fit check:** can the product credibly deliver each desire? Is the desire already met by familiar products? How big is the bridge (hat → back pain)? [L57 03:47–08:58](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
8. **Superiority audit** (research-based): weaknesses; strengths the market cares about; anything unique; after looking at other options, which is superior? [L173 05:29–09:04](../lessons/173-how-brands-actually-scale-to-8-figures.md)
9. **Keep it live:** reuse the doc as an attachment for the desire and new-information prompts. [L59 04:58](../lessons/059-understanding-your-product-part-3.md); [L63 28:04–30:06](../lessons/063-how-to-find-desires-part-3.md)

### Product Overview Document (verbatim template, L57/L60)
```text
Product Overview Document
Purpose: Identify what EXACTLY your product is and does to identify desires, build avatars, and create winning ads.

Step 1: Basics
Product Name:
Price Point: $

Step 2: Product In Action
HOW does your product work? Describe the process/method of what it does and the outcome it provides. Then provide a 6th-grade reading level version.
(Example: "The probiotics colonize the gut, which improves digestion, which reduces bloating")

Step 3: Features
1-5. Feature

Step 4: Assumed* Benefits
Feature N: → Benefit: It does X

Step 5: Assumed* Desires
Benefit N: → Desire: The person wants/needs X

Step 6: New Mechanisms (USPs)
1. New Mechanism

Step 7: Hidden Mechanisms (For AI Analysis)
1. What does your product do that you DON'T advertise?
2. What ingredients/components do you include that competitors don't?
3. If your product worked TOO well, what would be the "problem" customers would complain about
4. If someone was explaining this product to their friend, what would they say?
```
(Lists condensed from five numbered lines each; full text in [L57](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md).)

## Examples
- **Stainless steel cup:** physical = steel; functional = keeps water cold; that desire is "already met", so a feature-only ad fails; "perfect temperature" would be a new angle. [L57 01:33–06:31](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
- **Laifen hairdryer first pass:** benefit "fast drying", feature "visible heat", claim "celebrity hairstylist approved", features "110,000 RPM motor", "1,000 hours lifespan". [L22 03:16–04:13](../lessons/022-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
- **Dog harness (manual walkthrough):** ITW buckle → "dog won't get out" → "they want their dog to be safe"; front D-ring → "less pulling" → "feel in control", "less embarrassed on walks"; "too well" complaints → "I can't have a quick walk anymore - every dog owner wants to know where I got it". [L58 07:41–31:40](../lessons/058-understanding-your-product-part-2.md)
- **Collagen proof gap:** the benefit is invisible, so a customer-visible proxy (nail growth) becomes the belief device (untested idea). [L63 05:42–10:20](../lessons/063-how-to-find-desires-part-3.md)
- **Apple iPod:** "a thousand songs in your pocket" made a technical mechanism instantly legible. [L65 23:36–25:00](../lessons/065-understanding-market-sophistication-part-1.md)

## Common mistakes
- Thinking you know the product: "Most people think they know their product. They don't." [L57 doc frame](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
- Strengths the market doesn't care about ("I make it myself"). [L173 08:14–09:04](../lessons/173-how-brands-actually-scale-to-8-figures.md)
- Jargon and "fancy" terms ("choke free") instead of plain words. [L58 04:49](../lessons/058-understanding-your-product-part-2.md)
- Letting AI answer "what don't you advertise?"; it "has no idea" what you advertise. [L58 24:59](../lessons/058-understanding-your-product-part-2.md)
- Claiming false uniqueness for industry-standard processes; explaining a mechanism without customer value. [L65 doc](../lessons/065-understanding-market-sophistication-part-1.md)
- Keeping features that don't matter ("sturdy grab handle"). [L58 16:11](../lessons/058-understanding-your-product-part-2.md)

### Course conflicts and agent defaults
- **Product first or customer first?** The course order is product → desire → sophistication → avatars [L56 02:05–06:45](../lessons/056-organizing-insights-overview-must-watch.md), but L57's "9 Figure Tip" says the biggest brands "start with the customer and the desire first", then build the product (HexClad, Ridge, Grüns) [L57 08:58–10:39](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md). **Default:** for an existing product, do the overview first; for choosing or developing a product, start from the desire and the complaints (see [product research screen](../frameworks/product-research-screen.md)).
- **Manual vs AI first draft.** L58 says do it by hand to learn; L59 says use a Claude project if research exists and admits "you don't really learn a lot from doing this"; L60 settles it by route. **Default:** agents use the L59 route with all three inputs, then audit line by line against sources.
