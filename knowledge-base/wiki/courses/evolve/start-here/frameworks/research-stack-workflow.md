# Research stack workflow
> Build one research doc per brand by running 14 sources in a fixed order (one module, one action, one finished section at a time), turning every finding into a filed entry (phrase bank, buzz-word tally, objection, FAQ, segment, visual idea, creator), then cleaning the doc and handing it to the organize-insights chain (product → desire → sophistication → belief builders).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** starting work on a new brand or product; a brand's ads are stuck and nobody can say what the market wants; preparing inputs for the [desire hunt](desire-hunt.md), avatars or [angles](angle-generation-prompt.md).
- **Inputs → outputs:** brand name, site URL, target country, founder/brand answers (10-question intake), competitor list, ad-account read access (if any), reviews and order data (if any) → a filled research doc (Overview links, Ad Copy bank with phrase bank, Product, Core Research problems/solutions, Avatars seeds, FAQ, Competitors, Ad Account Audit) plus a "missing inputs" list.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** run research as a loop: "1. Watch Module → 2. Take Action → 3. Finish Research Step → Watch Next Module." Finish one source's section before opening the next. [L17 page text](../lessons/017-overview-self-onboarding.md)
  - **DO** fill the self-onboarding form first, then compare it with the finished doc. [L17 02:00–03:20](../lessons/017-overview-self-onboarding.md)
  - **DO** start with the product: "Understanding your product is the first and most important thing." [L22 00:00](../lessons/022-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
  - **DO** search from the target market (VPN to the selling country, incognito window). [L25 00:00](../lessons/025-google-research-part-1.md)
  - **DO** tally repeated claims and words with "+1" per sighting to see "which words are being overdone". [L26 01:25–02:12](../lessons/026-google-research-part-2.md)
  - **DO** keep a phrase bank with two lists: positives (claim/result words) and negatives (relatable problems). [L20 01:26–02:00](../lessons/020-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-2.md)
  - **DO** write visual ideas and ad ideas the moment they occur, into the brain-dump area, with the source. [L19 04:11](../lessons/019-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-1.md); [L20 01:59–02:40](../lessons/020-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-2.md)
  - **DO** read comments on every strong video, thread and long-running ad: "Comments are king." [L44 03:35–04:33](../lessons/044-tiktok-research.md); [L39 03:01](../lessons/039-facebook-research-part-2.md)
  - **DON'T** copy a big competitor's ad one-for-one: they "are going to be outspending us and outconverting us". [L38 02:23–03:23](../lessons/038-facebook-research-part-1.md) Copy the mechanism, not the asset.
  - **DON'T** let missing brand guidelines block research. [L19 01:51](../lessons/019-getting-ready-to-do-research-part-1.md)
  - **DON'T** treat a Reddit comment, an AI summary (Shulex) or a competitor's numeric claim as fact. Log it as an insight to test. [L28 04:22–05:10](../lessons/028-amazon-research-part-1.md); [L35 02:48](../lessons/035-competitor-research-part-1.md)
  - **IF** a source needs a human (phone calls, the Instagram ad-only feed on a phone, paid tool logins) **THEN** prepare the script/checklist and list the step as a missing input; never invent its results. [L55](../lessons/055-customer-phone-call-research.md); [L53](../lessons/053-ad-only-instagram-research.md)
  - **IF** most customer questions are about specs **THEN** treat the market as sophisticated (they know the outcome and are shopping for mechanism and features). [L29 01:45–02:43](../lessons/029-amazon-research-part-2.md)
- **See also:** [research doc schema](../concepts/research-doc.md) · [desire hunt](desire-hunt.md) · [research → first concepts playbook](../playbooks/research-to-first-concepts.md) · [product research screen](product-research-screen.md) · Tether: [customer research stack](../../../../frameworks/customer-research-stack.md), [competitor landscape audit](../../../../playbooks/competitor-landscape-audit.md)

## Definition
The Evolve research stack is the ordered set of research modules (lessons 17-56) whose combined output is a single **research doc**. Each source has a defined job (what to search, what to save, where to file it). Research ends with a clean-up and with the "organize insights" chain, whose goal is to turn research into belief: "Desires don't mean anything unless I can build belief." [L56 06:45](../lessons/056-organizing-insights-overview-must-watch.md)

## Why it matters
- "The effort that you put in prior to making the ad is the difference between the ad usually winning and the ad flopping on its face." [L56 01:18](../lessons/056-organizing-insights-overview-must-watch.md)
- Failure has three causes: you don't know where the product fits, you don't know what the market wants, or you fail to execute. Research fixes the first two. [L22 00:00](../lessons/022-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)
- "The best copywriters still use their customer's words to write more relatable copy." [L56 05:48](../lessons/056-organizing-insights-overview-must-watch.md)

### Step 15: clean up (L51-52)
Time-box "10, 15 minutes". Fill "potential customer right now" and "after our product", competitor notes, price range, the creative audit; add a mirrored "Potential Competitive Disadvantages" section; copy any heading you need and rename it. [L51 00:00–01:47](../lessons/051-cleaning-up-the-research-doc.md)

### Step 16: organize insights (L56 → L57-86)
Work in this order: product overview → desire (Why Train) → market sophistication → belief builders (new mechanism, new information, new identity/avatars). [L56 02:05–06:45](../lessons/056-organizing-insights-overview-must-watch.md) Continue with the [desire hunt](desire-hunt.md) and the [research → first concepts playbook](../playbooks/research-to-first-concepts.md).

## Converters: finding → doc entry
| Finding | Convert to | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated competitor claim ("no heat damage, fast drying") | +1 tally; if heavily repeated, mark "overdone", find a fresh way to say the desire | [L26 02:12](../lessons/026-google-research-part-2.md); [L49 03:28–04:07](../lessons/049-understanding-objections.md) |
| Listicle category ("best budget", "best for heat damage") | Segment + draft persona | [L26 02:12–03:59](../lessons/026-google-research-part-2.md) |
| Big trusted brands fill the SERP | Objection "competing against big brands and trusted sources"; avoid head-on premium comparisons that trigger "how does this do everything... for $300 less?" | [L25 00:37–02:25](../lessons/025-google-research-part-1.md) |
| Amazon use-case majority ("out of the shower") | Filming environment | [L29 00:00–00:55](../lessons/029-amazon-research-part-2.md) |
| Competitor fails ("it broke") | Risk-reversal message about our service, never an announcement of their failure; only if the client can deliver it | [L36 00:00–01:42](../lessons/036-competitor-research-part-2.md) |
| Upvoted "cheap = expensive" belief | Education angle ("here's the science behind it") | [L31 02:33–03:27](../lessons/031-reddit-research.md) |
| "Everyone says best for everyone" | Segment the message ("Do you have thick, heavy hair?") | [L31 01:47](../lessons/031-reddit-research.md) |
| Comment names a person or use (daughter, school, dog) | Test an ad on that use case | [L39 02:12–03:01](../lessons/039-facebook-research-part-2.md) |
| Comment complains about the landing page | Landing-page fix | [L39 01:33–02:12](../lessons/039-facebook-research-part-2.md) |
| Hook without a time frame | Add one ("How to eliminate frizzy hair overnight") | [L44 05:31–06:30](../lessons/044-tiktok-research.md) |
| Recurring word in long-running ads ("secret") | Test "the secret to [desire]" | [L42 05:36–06:08](../lessons/042-creative-research-part-2.md) |
| Unknown problem or term (hard water, "paddle eraser") | Research it; add to problems / possible product opening | [L42 03:49–04:43](../lessons/042-creative-research-part-2.md); [L54 02:50–03:43](../lessons/054-answer-the-public-research.md) |
| ATP "how to choose..." | "How to choose the right [product]" advertorial or VSL | [L54 07:46–08:34](../lessons/054-answer-the-public-research.md) |
| ATP comparison naming another product/sport | Avatar from that audience | [L54 06:03–06:55](../lessons/054-answer-the-public-research.md) |

| Popular alternative solution | Work with it (complement) or call out its flaw; educate, don't insult ("stop using cheap hair dryers" → you buy expensive shampoo yet use a $20 dryer) | [L48 03:21–04:19](../lessons/048-understanding-other-desires.md) |

| Cheaper for the same claims | "Too good to be true" objection; address it ("we don't make customers pay for a brand name") | [L49 00:52–01:44](../lessons/049-understanding-objections.md) |

## Templates

### Research-doc output skeleton (V4.0 headings, from L20-21)
```text
OVERVIEW: brand links | research process | onboarding doc | brand guidelines (or "none yet") | Atria board
AD COPY ("Ask yourself who you are writing this for before writing")
  Headlines | Hooks | CTAs | Phrase bank (+ positives / - negatives) | Visual ideas
PRODUCT: who is the company | what it is / does | features | benefits | potential competitive advantages & claims | potential competitive disadvantages | use cases | price range
CORE RESEARCH: problems we have to solve (as "How do we...?" → answer) | solutions | beliefs & objections | other solutions for main desires | day-to-day struggles | buzz words (+1 tallies)
AVATARS (seeds only; build with lessons 71-77): segments | draft personas | customer now / after
FAQ: questions customers have (source)
COMPETITOR RESEARCH (3-5): ad library | landing pages | main site | price range | active offers | notes (objective) | bad reviews
MEDIA BUYER: AD ACCOUNT AUDIT: top prospecting (30d / 6m) | top retargeting (or "no clear retargeting ads over the last 30 days") | top hook rate | top hold/CTR | winners to carry
CREATORS: name | platform | audience size | content trends
BRAIN DUMP: idea | source | date
MISSING INPUTS: (agent-added) source | why missing | who can supply
```
Full verbatim headings: [L21](../lessons/021-action-item-copy-research-document.md). Schema versions: [research doc](../concepts/research-doc.md).

### Self-onboarding intake (the 10 questions, condensed)
Differentiator (most important) · 2-3 things you wish everyone knew · best seller (URL, from sales data) · brand perception · what stops people buying · influencers/authorities (names, handles) · seasonal best/worst months · FAQs · enticing claims and guarantees (IMPORTANT) · content drive link. Verbatim form: [L18](../lessons/018-action-item-self-onboarding.md). Write "unknown" or "none" rather than guess.

### Phone-call script (human executes; from L55)
```text
Hi [name], this is [first name] from [brand]. Is now a bad time to talk?
(busy → book a call-back)
Thanks so much for ordering. I'm curious how you got to our brand?
(stay silent; if asked "what do you mean?": "How'd you find out about us? What were you looking for?")
Notes: where they first saw us | problem before purchase | what they tried before | exact phrases | why they chose us
```
Don't ask "do you mind if I ask you some questions?" or run a question list. [L55 00:00–03:37](../lessons/055-customer-phone-call-research.md)
