# Product research screen
> Any product can work if it clears a few gates: AOV $60+ after bundles, at least 3x markup on COGS + shipping, a trend that is not declining, no big-brand or trademark exposure, a price inside the market's expected range, a strong mass desire, a solution- or product-aware entry point, and a clear way to stand out (new mechanism or underserved avatar). If you cannot name the differentiator before testing, don't test.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** vetting a candidate product for a new brand or line extension; sanity-checking a product a human picked; writing the "why we will win" section of a launch brief; diagnosing a stuck brand whose ads are fine but results cap (product ceiling).
- **Inputs → outputs:** product, price (single unit and bundle), COGS + shipping quote, 5-year Google Trends curve for the target country, brand/trademark lookups, top competitor ads and TikTok Shop videos, reviews/Reddit → a go/no-go card with each gate scored, the chosen differentiator, the awareness entry point, a conservative revenue estimate of the leading store, and the risks.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** require AOV $60+ after bundles: "selling something... $10, but your cost to advertise is $20, there's zero chance." [L171 08:13–09:12](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
  - **DO** require at least 3x markup on COGS + shipping, "ideally more"; 5x is good. [L171 09:12–09:42](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
  - **DO** check Google Trends (target country, 5 years): reject "consistently down" and spike-then-fall; flat or rising is fine. "The money is made in stagnant and trending markets, but not in declining ones." [L171 09:42–10:47](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md); [L172 19:26; 32:55–34:07](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md)
  - **DO** drop products tied to a big brand, patent or active trademark you cannot work around; check the country's registry (US: USPTO) and the brand's size. [L172 02:53–07:50](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md)
  - **DO** require a differentiator: "If I cannot find any of these two [new mechanism, underserved avatar], then I wouldn't run it." [L172 23:00; 26:18](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md); [L171 17:13; 19:15–19:46](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
  - **DO** prefer solution- or product-aware entry points for a first product; don't go unaware "to scale". [L171 16:11–16:42](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
  - **DO** sell only products you believe work; sanity-check efficacy with an LLM and evidence. [L171 26:37–27:08](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
  - **DO** judge superiority by the market's view, from research: "Your Opinion DOES NOT MATTER. The Markets Opinion is what matters." [L173 06:26; slide 8](../lessons/173-how-brands-actually-scale-to-8-figures.md)
  - **DON'T** spam-test products: success becomes "based off luck"; record why each product is tested. [L171 01:53–02:50; 06:17](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
  - **DON'T** fear saturation: "Saturation = big audience." [L171 18:14–18:44](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
  - **DON'T** pick heavy/bulky goods, unverifiable battery products, a seasonal-only product (unless you accept the window) or a niche you are repelled by. [L172 26:18–27:19; 45:54; 47:39–49:53](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md)
  - **DON'T** rip TikTok Shop videos and strip metadata to run as ads, even though L172 teaches it [L172 54:30–59:30](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md). **Don't execute.** Compliant alternative: tear the top videos down for hook, format, angle and proof type, then brief original creative ([competitor ad teardown](../../../../playbooks/competitor-ad-teardown.md)).

## Definition
A go/no-go procedure for a product, taught as "a thinking exercise, not a spy-tool hunt": "A winning product is a product that satisfies a mass desire." Tools (Kalodata, SimilarWeb, Google Trends) only shortlist; the decision rests on criteria, desire, awareness, sophistication and a differentiator. [L171 12:13–13:14; 33:38–34:41](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)

## Why it matters
- "You can make any product work, but the difficulty of each product will vary." [L171 13:14](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
- Product sets the ceiling: same agency strategy, new product → 5x spend at a better ROAS (client case, instructor claim). "Apple did not even have to 'out market' the blackberry." [L173 01:55–02:48; slide 7](../lessons/173-how-brands-actually-scale-to-8-figures.md)
- The most common mistake is a desire the product "cannot live up to". [L57 03:47–04:42](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md)

## How to use it (steps)

### Stage A: shortlist (L172 method)
1. Kalodata, Product tab: last 7 days, average unit price > $15, sales growing rapidly; sort by revenue. [L172 00:58](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md)
2. Mark each of the top 20: big brand / patent / trademarked name / heavy / battery / seasonal / above $100. [L172 how to apply](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md)
3. Estimate the leading store conservatively: visitors (SimilarWeb) × CVR (assume ~1%) × AOV. Mark it an assumption; for TikTok-Shop-led stores site traffic understates sales. [L172 10:16; 14:47; 48:53](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md)

### Stage B: gates
| # | Gate | Pass | Fail → | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AOV after bundles | ≥ $60 | Bundle/upsell plan or drop | [L171 08:13](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md) |
| 2 | Markup on COGS + shipping | ≥ 3x (5x good) | Renegotiate or drop | [L171 09:12](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md) |
| 3 | Expected price range | 3x price still inside what buyers expect (Amazon, LLM check). Example: $20 cost, $60 price, $40-60 expected | Drop; "super premium prices but only... cheap low quality products... won't go well" | [L172 42:00–45:54; page step 10](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md) |
| 4 | Trend (5 years, target country) | Flat or rising | Declining or spike-then-fall → drop | [L171 09:42](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md); [L172 19:26](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md) |
| 5 | Brand / IP | Not a big brand's product; no blocking patent; trademarked names and phrases listed to avoid | Drop or rename product and brand | [L172 02:53–07:50; 32:55](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md) |
| 6 | Logistics | Lightweight (beginner); no unverifiable battery; seasonality accepted | Drop | [L171 10:47](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md); [L172 45:54; 47:39–49:53](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md) |
| 7 | Desire magnitude | Strong mass desire; existing solutions are expensive (price anchors listed) | Weak desire ("desk more organized") caps scale | [L171 13:44–15:40](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md) |
| 8 | Desire fit | Product credibly delivers; market hasn't already learned it fails | Pick another desire or drop | [L57 03:47–06:31](../lessons/057-understanding-your-product-part-1.md) |
| 9 | Awareness entry | Solution- or product-aware angles available | Unaware-only → skip as a first product | [L171 15:40–17:13](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md) |
| 10 | Sophistication read | Competitor claims listed (Ad Library, TikTok Shop top videos, Amazon) | — | [L172 07:50–08:55; 54:30](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md); [L65 12:20](../lessons/065-understanding-market-sophistication-part-1.md) |
| 11 | Differentiator | New mechanism (incl. a renamed feature with a new purpose) or underserved avatar, written down | Neither → no-go | [L171 19:15–19:46; 32:05–33:38](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md) |
| 12 | Conviction | Evidence it works; LLM check not "largely ineffective" | Drop gimmicks | [L171 25:06–27:08](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md) |
| 13 | Superiority audit | Strengths the market cares about, with evidence | Fix product from complaints, or reposition | [L173 05:29–09:04](../lessons/173-how-brands-actually-scale-to-8-figures.md) |

### Stage C: decide and launch
- IF the category is known (e.g. self-cleaning litter boxes) THEN stop educating; run product-aware ads: comparison, price, ease of use. [L172 16:59–26:18](../lessons/172-live-product-research-w-kalodata-pt-2.md)
- Cap setup: product + store in 8 hours, then launch. [L171 05:16–06:17](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
- Log every product and creative tested with the reason; review to lift hit rate (1% → 2% → 3%). [L171 06:17–08:13](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)
- Stick to 1-2 avatars per vertical so learning compounds. [L171 35:45–36:16](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)

## Templates

### Go/no-go card
```yaml
product: ""
unit_price: 0; bundle_aov: 0; cogs_plus_shipping: 0; markup: 0      # gates 1-2
expected_price_range: "" ; price_source: ""                          # gate 3
trend_5y: flat|rising|declining|spike_fall ; country: ""             # gate 4
ip_check: {big_brand: no, patent: no, trademarked_terms_to_avoid: []}   # gate 5
logistics: {weight: light|heavy, battery: no, seasonal_window: ""}   # gate 6
desire: {statement: "I want...", magnitude: weak|strong, price_anchors_of_existing_solutions: []}  # 7
desire_fit: yes|no|partly                                            # 8
awareness_entry: solution_aware|product_aware|unaware_only           # 9
competitor_claims: []                                                # 10
differentiator: {type: new_mechanism|renamed_feature|underserved_avatar|none, statement: ""}  # 11
conviction_evidence: ""                                              # 12
superiority_audit: {strengths_with_evidence: [], weaknesses: [], unique: "", superior_alternative: ""}  # 13
leader_revenue_estimate: "visitors x 1% x AOV = ... (assumption)"
verdict: go|no_go ; reasons: []
```

### Product-superiority audit (L173 slide 8, verbatim)
```text
Ask Yourself BASED ON my research…
What are the weaknesses of my product?
What are the strengths of my product?
Is there anything that really makes my product unique?
After looking at other options on the market, what is the superior product?
Your Opinion DOES NOT MATTER.
The Markets Opinion is what matters.
```

### Old market vs new market claims (avatar move check)
| Old market claims | New market claims | Check |
|---|---|---|
| (what everyone says now) | (what we'd say to the new avatar) | Are the new claims genuinely unaddressed? |
Model: Stanley, stage 5 "Keeps ice 2 hours longer than YETI" → new avatar "The cup that makes you drink more water", "Your emotional support water bottle". [L171 27:08–28:36](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md)

| Mistake | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|

| Testing without knowing how you'll stand out | Write the differentiator first | [L171 19:15–19:46](../lessons/171-how-to-make-any-product-work-brand-building-pt-1.md) |
