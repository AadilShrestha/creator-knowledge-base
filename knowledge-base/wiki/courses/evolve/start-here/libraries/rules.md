# Rules library (Evolve Start Here)
> The canonical rules of the Evolve Start Here program: 1,576 harvested bullets from all 175 lessons, de-duplicated into one ID'd list grouped by pipeline job. Every lesson citation is kept, and where lessons disagree, both positions sit side by side with an agent default.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you are making a decision inside any pipeline step (research, desire hunt, avatars, angles, concept planning, campaign setup, reading KPIs, labelling winners, scaling, learnings, iterations, creator program) and need the course's rule, or you are QA-ing a plan, brief or ad against it.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the job you're on → the section in the [table below](#sections-and-counts) → grep the ID prefix or a keyword (e.g. `MB`, `3:2:2`, `zombie`, `Insense`) → the rule plus its citations → apply it, after checking [conflicts](#conflicts-at-a-glance) and the [⛔ index](#-dont-execute-index).
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** cite the rule ID (e.g. "MB23") in plans and reviews, and open the lesson link before quoting an instructor or a result.
  - **DO** take numbers from [kpi-thresholds](kpi-thresholds.md), where every numeric rule sits next to its conflicting values. The rules here give the logic.
  - **DO** treat the §19 rules (X01–X10) as hard constraints: they override any course tactic.
  - **DON'T** read "(instructor claim)" results as evidence. Rules marked "(derived)" are the lesson page's inference, not the instructor's words.
  - **IF** two rules disagree **THEN** use the agent default in the "Conflict:" line next to them (also in [conflicts at a glance](#conflicts-at-a-glance)).
  - **IF** you need writing-craft detail **THEN** use the [Copywriting rules](../../copywriting/libraries/rules.md). This page holds the program, research, media-buying and creator rules.

## 2. Psychology: desire, awareness, sophistication, rule of one, identification, belief (PS) — L8–16

### The five checks
- **PS01** **DO** check all five before making ads: identities the buyer wants to resonate with, whether they "actually believe us", their desires, their awareness level and how sophisticated the market is. [L008 00:40](../lessons/008-overview-of-psychology.md)
- **PS02** **DON'T** copy creatives without understanding what drives the buyer: "We used to make ads and just copy creatives from what everybody else was talking about, but until we actually understood what makes people tick…". [L008 00:00–00:40](../lessons/008-overview-of-psychology.md)

### Desire
- **PS03** **DO** "channel" existing desire; never "create" it: "You cannot create desire, okay? It is something that just exists in the market." [L009 00:00–00:54](../lessons/009-market-desires.md) (repeated in DS01)
- **PS04** **DO** classify each desire as permanent (health, wealth, relationships) or trending (crypto, Valentine's Day, AI); build long-term results on permanent desires, use trending ones for short-term gain, and write down the permanent root behind every trend (derived). [L009 00:54–03:32](../lessons/009-market-desires.md)
- **PS05** **DO** rate desire strength on intensity ("minor headache versus severe migraine"), scope ("total amount of people that actually share this desire") and staying power. [L009 03:32–04:28](../lessons/009-market-desires.md) [L009 page text](../lessons/009-market-desires.md) (scored in DS08)
- **PS06** **DO** sell the functional product (what it does for the buyer), not the physical one; the physical parts "justify and document" quality. Don't advertise motherboards; advertise the outcome. [L009 12:19–14:06](../lessons/009-market-desires.md) (expanded in PR01–PR03)
- **PS07** **DO** find desire in research language ("I wanted", "I was looking for", "I was in need"), then test each desire in creative and message. [L009 14:06–15:44](../lessons/009-market-desires.md)
- **PS08** **IF** an ad gets no traction **THEN** suspect the desire before the execution: "Find your style" "did absolutely nothing" while "I want to look wealthy" spent over $20,000 (instructor claim). [L009 14:53–15:44](../lessons/009-market-desires.md)
- **PS09** **DON'T** pick a niche desire when a bigger adjacent desire exists unless you are starting small on purpose: "going after a desire that's a lot bigger is going to take a lot more skill … sometimes it makes sense to start smaller." [L009 09:10](../lessons/009-market-desires.md) Conflict: see AV09 (revenue-stage desire pick); agent default = AV09.
- **PS10** **IF** a market event spikes concern (news story, holiday) **THEN** expect a desire spike and have matching creative ready. [L009 10:08–11:43](../lessons/009-market-desires.md)

### Market awareness
- **PS11** **DO** decide the awareness level before every new ad or idea; it is how the instructor judges whether it will scale. [L010 00:00–00:36](../lessons/010-market-awareness.md)
- **PS12** **DO** at **most aware**: "get out of the way, make a great offer, reduce risk & make it easy for them to purchase." [L010 03:41–04:34](../lessons/010-market-awareness.md)
- **PS13** **DO** at **product aware**: build trust, share benefits and features, show how yours solves the desire best, use future pacing; label an ad product aware when it shows why *my* product beats others in a category the viewer already knows. [L010 05:32–06:23](../lessons/010-market-awareness.md) [L013 02:21](../lessons/013-action-item-practicing-the-core-3.md)
- **PS14** **DO** at **solution aware**: present your solution as superior and compare against other solution types, not the same product type. [L010 06:23–08:48](../lessons/010-market-awareness.md)
- **PS15** **IF** you run us-vs-them **THEN** pick the comparison to set the level: same product type = product aware; a different type of solution = solution aware; in crowded markets compare against other solution types (derived). [L010 07:16–08:48](../lessons/010-market-awareness.md)
- **PS16** **DO** at **problem aware**: articulate the problem so clearly "they say 'YES THAT'S THE PROBLEM I'M HAVING'", then introduce the solution; **DON'T** stay in the problem too long ("I don't want to relive my problem"); test how long you stay there. [L010 09:35–11:49](../lessons/010-market-awareness.md)
- **PS17** **DO** at **unaware**: call out the core emotion, educate on the problem causing it, then solution, product and offer; "the goal is to educate the unaware prospect to become problem aware." [L010 13:28–14:22](../lessons/010-market-awareness.md)
- **PS18** **DON'T** make unaware your first move: it "is the hardest level of market awareness to market at. And for most brands, you actually don't really need to tap into unaware … until you're at some serious scale" (instructor claim). [L010 13:28](../lessons/010-market-awareness.md)
- **PS19** **IF** you start an ad at a level **THEN** carry the viewer through every later level to most aware (offer). [L010 16:54–17:52](../lessons/010-market-awareness.md)
- **PS20** **DO** write the awareness level next to every ad in your tracker; at scale you reach all five stages, so don't assume everyone is problem aware (derived). [L010 17:52](../lessons/010-market-awareness.md)
- **PS21** **DON'T** call a headline unaware if it names the problem: mentioning exhaustion means the reader already knows they are tired = problem aware. [L011 01:08–01:57](../lessons/011-understanding-unaware-awareness-levels.md) [L011 03:28–04:29](../lessons/011-understanding-unaware-awareness-levels.md)
- **PS22** **DO** write unaware ads with no problem attached (curiosity, aspiration, "interesting information" anyone could watch), educate or entertain first, then walk the viewer through the stages: "That is how you create true unaware ads." [L011 05:54–07:16](../lessons/011-understanding-unaware-awareness-levels.md)
- **PS23** **DON'T** assume a problem-naming ad reaches everyone: a person tired most days may see it on a day they are not tired. [L011 06:27](../lessons/011-understanding-unaware-awareness-levels.md)
- **PS24** **DO** run the awareness-level prompt with your product and angle swapped in and audit every line: "You also can't blindly trust AI." **IF** it labels a problem-aware line unaware **THEN** paste the "Prompt For Mistake", ask for 3–5 new headlines and make it explain the error. [L011 00:00–00:37](../lessons/011-understanding-unaware-awareness-levels.md) [L011 03:28–04:29](../lessons/011-understanding-unaware-awareness-levels.md) See [prompts](prompts.md).
- **PS25** **DO** prefer Claude over GPT for script writing (instructor opinion). [L011 05:17](../lessons/011-understanding-unaware-awareness-levels.md)

### Market sophistication (stage model)
- **PS26** **DO** define sophistication as "how many similar products has the market been told about before? How many claims have they been told? And how many of them have actually tried those products". [L012 04:13–04:45](../lessons/012-market-sophistication.md)
- **PS27** **IF** stage 1 (brand-new market) **THEN** state the claim simply and prove it; **IF** competitors already make the claim (stage 2) **THEN** enlarge it; **IF** the market has heard every claim (stage 3) **THEN** introduce a new mechanism; **IF** the mechanism is no longer new (stage 4) **THEN** enlarge the mechanism (easier, faster, overcomes its limits); **IF** "the market doesn't believe you anymore" (stage 5) **THEN** sell identification. [L012 05:16–10:56](../lessons/012-market-sophistication.md) Enlarged claims need real proof (repo rule).
- **PS28** **IF** you reach stage 4 **THEN** choose between stage 5, a return to stage 3, or a new product at stage 1; keep re-checking, mature markets cycle back to stage 3 or 1. [L012 13:23](../lessons/012-market-sophistication.md) [L012 18:47](../lessons/012-market-sophistication.md)
- **PS29** **DON'T** launch a claim without checking who else makes it: seven competitors all claimed "fast hair drying without heat damage", so the ad failed. [L012 17:57–18:47](../lessons/012-market-sophistication.md)
- **PS30** **DO** build an ad-library board of competitor and substitute-solution ads and look for the one that stands out. [L012 15:55–17:20](../lessons/012-market-sophistication.md)
- **PS31** **DO** practise labelling reference ads on awareness and sophistication only ("you can just ignore the hook and the hold"), recording spend, conversion value, saved link and start date; label enlarged-mechanism ads stage 4 and brand-new mechanisms stage 3. [L013 00:35–04:04](../lessons/013-action-item-practicing-the-core-3.md) Conflict: see DS33 (don't label the exact stage); agent default = DS33.
- **PS32** **DO** use the label to choose the next test: another awareness level against the same desire. [L013 06:39](../lessons/013-action-item-practicing-the-core-3.md)

### Rule of one
- **PS33** **DO** make every ad or page appeal to "one avatar, one desire, and one message". "If you try to target everyone, you try to get no one." Don't mix avatars (post-surgery woman vs golfer vs gardener). [L014 00:00–00:59](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md) [L014 04:09](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
- **PS34** **DON'T** call a demographic an avatar ("Woman 35 plus who struggles with knee pain... is not an avatar. This is a demographic."); define it by "emotional pains, past failed attempts, specific desired goals, and day-to-day habits", plus content consumed and hobbies the problem restricts. [L014 02:01–04:09](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
- **PS35** **DON'T** go too specific (exact age, kids, degree): "We care about commonalities." [L014 05:10–06:07](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
- **PS36** **DO** keep asking "why" until you reach the deep desire, then show the same avatar and desire in every segment of the ad. [L014 09:26–15:48](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md) [L014 14:41](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md) [L013 06:05](../lessons/013-action-item-practicing-the-core-3.md)
- **PS37** **DO** make the hook call out the avatar, the problem or the solution. [L014 07:09](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md) [L014 22:31](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
- **PS38** **DO** define the angle as the avatar's dominant emotion plus daily struggle (or ideal outcome), the "meeting point"; **DON'T** treat format (image, video, UGC) as an angle. [L014 21:27–22:31](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md) Conflict: see AD03 (angle = reason to buy); agent default = write the reason to buy, voiced through emotion + struggle.
- **PS39** **IF** an avatar and desire win **THEN** test different angles around them rather than new avatars; **IF** the avatar works but the desire doesn't **THEN** test desires; **IF** the desire works but not the avatar **THEN** test avatars. [L014 29:03](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md) [L014 33:16](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
- **PS40** **DO** match the landing page to the ad (same avatar, desire, emotion): "Congruence Across Funnel = Zero Friction." [L014 30:09–32:17](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)
- **PS41** **DO** accept a "worse-looking" ad if its appeal is more specific. [L014 19:10](../lessons/014-rule-of-1-1-desire-avatar-message.md)

### Identification
- **PS42** **DO** use identification to "communicate the feelings our customer desires, without words"; "we don't just buy objects, we buy roles". Show the move from current identity to dream identity. [L015 01:37](../lessons/015-market-identification.md) [L015 04:13](../lessons/015-market-identification.md) [L015 11:57–13:36](../lessons/015-market-identification.md)
- **PS43** **DO** cast creators who embody the identity (look, gear, setting, language): the right creator grew from 300 to about 15,000 followers; "another dude in a t-shirt in a storage room" did not (instructor claim). [L015 08:14–10:07](../lessons/015-market-identification.md)
- **PS44** **DON'T** stretch the role (billionaire endorsing mass-market deodorant; a Rolls-Royce driver in a minivan ad) or position against the buyer's identity (Nescafé's time-saver pitch clashed with homemakers' self-image). [L015 10:52–11:57](../lessons/015-market-identification.md) [L015 15:56–16:53](../lessons/015-market-identification.md)

### Belief
- **PS46** **DON'T** violate established beliefs: "If you violate your ideal customers established beliefs in the slightest, then nothing you promise them, no matter how appealing, can save your ad." [L016 03:38–04:42](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)
- **PS47** **DON'T** stack promises; strengthen "one justifiable promise". [L016 05:31](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)
- **PS48** **DO** let desire set the ad's content, identity its visuals and belief how it is built; use customers' logic, not yours, and show people with the same beliefs using the product. [L016 06:21–07:13](../lessons/016-market-belief.md) [L016 01:49](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)
- **PS49** **DO** pick a belief device: the **yes train** (questions they answer "yes" to before the product appears), **symptoms** ("this guy just gets it"), **authority** (acknowledge the belief bluntly, show strong evidence) or **logic** (each statement builds on the last). [L016 08:08–16:27](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)
- **PS50** **DO** research current market beliefs, beliefs about your product, common objections and claims that sound too good to be true. [L016 16:27](../lessons/016-market-belief.md)

## 11. KPIs, columns and targets (KP) — L100–102
- **KP01** **DO** sort ads by Amount spent and look only at ads with spend AND results: "We only care about ads that are getting spend and ads that are getting results." Then read ROAS/CPA. ## 13. Scaling, attribution, turn-off, roller coasters (SC) — L115–121
### Scale up / down protocol
- **SC01** **DO** start with "are we hitting target KPI?" for the campaign **and** the whole ad account, read on **7-day click** data. [L115 00:00](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md) [L115 09:41](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md)
- **SC02** **IF** KPI is hit on a limited-time promo with a hard end date **THEN** go straight into scaling protocol. [L115 01:01](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md)
- **SC03** **IF** KPI is hit on a regular campaign **THEN** require **48–72 hours** of consistently above-target KPI, or a "new reason to be scaling" (new winning ad, offer or landing page); otherwise wait 24 hours. In practice the team often waits 36–48 h. [L115 01:01–03:23](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md) [L115 07:13](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md)
- **SC04** **DO** require click-based proof: **≥60%** of purchases click-based, or click-based data alone meeting KPI; if not, wait 24 h and consider 7-day-click-only. [L115 03:23–05:51](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md) [L115 07:13–08:23](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md)
- **SC05** **DO** increase budget **20% flat**; smaller steps (15/10/5%) at $10k+/day; more aggressive under ~$1k/day (or $2–3k). **IF** consistently 50–100% above KPI **THEN** raise by that percentage, "at your own risk". [L115 05:51–07:13](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md)
- **SC06** **DON'T** scale recklessly off one good day; people "scale up and then all of a sudden your ROAS just tanks". [L115 03:23](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md) [L115 08:23](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md)
- **SC07** **IF** below break-even KPI for 24–48 h **THEN** cut 20% (if above minimum daily spend); **IF** between break-even and target for 48–72 h **THEN** cut 20%, else wait 24 h. Cut 20%, not 10%. [L115 08:23–09:41](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md)
- **SC08** **IF** you are at minimum daily spend or cutting **THEN** work outside the ad account: new DCTs, funnel bug checks, learnings, deep research for new avatars and mechanisms, offer tests. - **SC09** **IF** plateaued for months **THEN** test new offers inside the existing campaign. [L115 14:32](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md)
- **SC10** **IF** you cannot scale **THEN** "Make a more Ads"; budget moves alone are not the fix. [L119 page image](../lessons/119-action-item-scale-your-ads.md)

[L115 05:51–07:13](../lessons/115-how-to-scale-facebook-ads-up-down.md) [L097 07:02](../lessons/097-how-to-set-up-abo-scaling-campaign.md) [L118 05:48–06:48](../lessons/118-advanced-surf-scaling-method.md)
