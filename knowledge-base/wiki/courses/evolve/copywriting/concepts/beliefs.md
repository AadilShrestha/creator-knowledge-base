# Beliefs (the customer's certainties, and belief in your claim)
> A belief is "a feeling of certainty about what something means"; beliefs are "as strong as desires", so a great claim fails if it violates what the prospect is already certain of. Research beliefs from actions, behaviours, experiences and emotions, then decide for each one whether to work with it or shift it (with an authority, a reassigned objection, or a story), and make sure every ad builds enough belief in its own claim before it pitches.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** an angle or claim "should" work but doesn't; mining reviews/Reddit for an avatar; choosing between aligning with or changing a belief; diagnosing losers and spend winners ("belief issue"); checking an ad before the pitch.
- **Inputs → outputs:** voice-of-customer text (reviews, Reddit, comments, surveys) → a belief list (first-person certainty statements, tagged by source type and helps/hurts) → per belief: align / reassign / shift with authority or story → the script lines that do it, plus a belief check (relatability, mechanism, proof, authority, objections handled).
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** record beliefs as their own field in avatar research, next to desires, experiences, behaviours, emotions. "What you haven't dug into is their beliefs." [L15 00:34–01:13](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
  - **DO** reverse-engineer beliefs from behaviour: "we look at actions, we look at behaviors, we look at experiences, and we look at emotions." [L15 10:29–12:02](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
  - **DO** write each belief as a statement the person is certain of ("It's not about what you eat, it's about how much"), not a topic. [L15 12:46](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
  - **DON'T** write a claim that contradicts an established belief: "if you violate your customers established beliefs, even in the slightest, nothing you promise them will matter." [L15 slide 04:00](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
  - **DON'T** assume your strongest claim is your best headline: "Your most powerful claim, is not always your most powerful headline." [L15 slide 04:00](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
  - **IF** a good claim isn't converting **THEN** suspect a missing or violated belief before changing the claim. [L15 01:13; 14:03](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md); "A majority of the time ads fail because they are lacking trust, aka belief." [L48 16:50](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)
  - **IF** a belief was formed by an authority (a trainer, a doctor) or by experience **THEN** changing it needs a matching or stronger authority or a new experience; experiential beliefs are strongest. [L15 05:30–08:49; 14:03–14:53](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
  - **DON'T** tell people their objection is wrong; reassign the blame to a new target ("The capsules were the issue, not the probiotics"). [L28 15:48–18:50](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
  - **DO** shift beliefs through a character like the viewer who goes through the shift, so the viewer "convince[s] themselves"; education alone rarely does it. [L39 04:03–05:32](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md)
  - **DO** run the belief check before pitching: relatability, new mechanism, proof, authorities, objections handled; more of it in sophisticated (stage 4-5) markets. [L29 06:08–07:43](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)

## Definition (two senses the course uses)
1. **The customer's existing beliefs.** Dictionary: "An acceptance that a statement is true or that something exists." Tony Robbins (instructor's preferred): "a feeling of certainty about what something means"; you can be certain of things that are untrue, and most beliefs are not conscious. [L15 01:13–03:09](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) Schwartz (per the instructor): "beliefs build the mental picture of the world that your potential customer lives in." [L15 03:56](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
2. **Belief in your ad's claim (trust/believability).** In the iteration audit the instructor renamed the "authority" component to **belief**: "What are we doing to build trust? Clear authority or strategic copy?"; "this belief component is a huge piece of why so many ads fail." [L46 10:58](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md) Spend winners usually lack "trust (belief)". [L50 02:02–02:38](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)

Sense 1 tells you what the claim must not violate and what must be shifted; sense 2 is whether the ad has done enough (mechanism, authority, facts, objection handling) to be believed.

## Why it matters
- **Beliefs are as strong as desires** (slide "Eugene Beliefs"). "The effectiveness of your copy is as much determined by the willingness of your customers to believe what it says, as it is by the promises it makes." [L15 04:00](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
- **The missing piece behind "great claim, no sales"**: "the number one reason why most people feel like they're making great claims… but they just… are missing something. And that something is usually belief." [L15 01:13](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
- **Burned buyers carry category beliefs**: after a failed pee pad, "I now have a biased belief that pee pads don't solve this problem" and scroll past "Try this pee pad". [L21 11:15–12:42](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **Nobody buys before believing**: "If you pitch someone that doesn't believe you, they're not going to buy." [L29 06:08–07:43](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
- **Positions must be acceptable**: positioning is "also about what the customer is willing to accept". [L56 01:13](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)

## Where beliefs come from (four types)
| Type | Slide definition (verbatim) | Strength | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Experiential | "Formed based on personal experiences" | Strongest, hardest to move | [L15 05:30–06:22](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) |
| Influenced / adopted | "Acquired from others, such as parents, friends, or culture, and reinforced by marketing or social circles" | Stronger than felt | [L15 06:22–07:06](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) |
| Intuitive / felt | "Internal, often non-conscious, feelings of what is true that guide behavior" | Starting point | [L15 07:06–07:55](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) |
| Memory-based | "Based on recalled events or facts" | Least important (instructor) | [L15 07:55–08:49](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) |

**The belief cycle:** Beliefs → drive thoughts / feelings / behaviour / action (or lack of) → determine experience → which proves the belief. Diet example: belief "I have no willpower" → "I'll just fail" → defeated → keeps eating the same → never starts → gains weight → belief strengthened. "The conversion from your ad will live or die based on belief." [L15 08:49–11:22](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)

## How to use it
1. **Pick the avatar and desire**, then collect raw voice-of-customer text. [L15 10:29–12:02](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
2. **Walk the cycle backwards** from an experience or behaviour: "what must this person be certain of for this to make sense?" Write it first-person. [L15 12:02–14:03](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
3. **Tag** each belief by source type and who formed it (name the authority), and helps / hurts our product (tagging derived from "some help us, some hurt us" [L15 07:55–08:49](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)).
4. **Write "why do they care"** as "They want [desire]. But they have the belief [belief]." [L17 page; 01:42–02:20](../lessons/17-being-aware-of-avatars.md)
5. **Decide per belief:**
   | Belief is… | Do | Source |
   |---|---|---|
   | Helpful | Align with it (write copy "that goes with them") | [L15 14:03](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) |
   | Harmful, about the category ("X doesn't work") | Reassign the blame to a specific villain; bring a new mechanism | [L28 15:48–19:57](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md); [L21 12:42–14:32](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md) |
   | Harmful, about themselves ("I'm not X enough", "I have no willpower") | Reversal ("even if you cheat") or reassign ("You're not broken, the villain is Y") | [L28 09:03–11:54; 22:41–23:49](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
   | Formed by an authority | Use a stronger or better-fitting authority | [L15 14:03–14:53](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) |
   | Deep, identity-level | Story with a relatable character who shifts; don't lecture | [L39 04:03–06:14](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md) |
   | "Solidified" and won't move | Don't fight it in this ad; pick another angle (instructor: some people won't accept pee pads can work) | [L21 12:42–13:35](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md); [L15 03:09–03:56](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) |
6. **Check the claim** against the belief list; flag any hook that contradicts a held belief. [L15 how to apply](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
7. **Build belief in the claim** (sense 2): mechanism ("the mechanism is what builds belief" [L14 05:08–05:59](../lessons/14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md)), authority before the pitch [L20 00:00](../lessons/20-being-aware-of-authorities.md), facts, vulnerability [L50 07:04–07:40](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md), objections prevented [L28](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md). Avoid "wonder drug" claims: "you're going to run into belief issues." [L67 20:54](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md)

## Common mistakes
- **Only researching desires and pains.** "There's a deeper cause, which are the beliefs." [L15 14:53–15:32](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
- **Trying to change set-in-stone beliefs head-on** ("no chance at all"). [L15 03:09–03:56](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
- **Lecturing instead of letting them convince themselves.** [L39 05:32–06:14](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md)
- **Blaming the buyer.** Blame the industry, a hidden ingredient or "they": "the buyer feels better about themselves and trusts you more." [L40 25:35; 27:56–28:52](../lessons/40-what-makes-a-great-story.md)
- **Over-claiming** so the claim itself breaks belief. [L67 20:54](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md)

## Cross-course notes
- **Agreement (Tether Lab):** [Heuristics](../../../../concepts/heuristics.md) says pick heuristics "from the customer's belief system and the season"; [hidden truth](../../../../concepts/heuristics/hidden-truth.md) and [reframing](../../../../concepts/heuristics/reframing.md) shift beliefs by making people "second-guess" them ("people just say it's genetics. It's not."), which matches Evolve's reassigning.
- **Agreement:** Tether's [validation over authority](../../../../concepts/validation-over-authority.md) handles distrust of experts by validating the buyer's own belief ("your gut feeling is right"); Evolve's equivalent is relatability authority plus aligning with helpful beliefs.
- **Method difference:** Tether's [customer research stack](../../../../frameworks/customer-research-stack.md) mines reviews for hidden motivators and warns that reviews alone re-recruit existing buyers; Evolve mines Reddit and reviews specifically for beliefs. Default: use both, and look for the belief behind each outlier.
