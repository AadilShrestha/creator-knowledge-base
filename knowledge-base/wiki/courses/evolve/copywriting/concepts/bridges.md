# Bridges (keeping attention after the hook)
> The hook's job is to stop the scroll; the bridge's job is to **keep** the viewer, and it is where they decide to stay or drop. Write one bridge per hook using one of six patterns (introduce the mechanism, escalate the problem, escalate the desire, reposition the problem, allude to new information, set the timeline), show the escalation rather than say it, and test cutting the hook so the bridge becomes the opener ("in medias res").

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a hook exists and the next ~3-8 seconds need writing; a hook stops people but retention drops; a broad or label hook needs to funnel to the avatar; making a "hookless" variation of a finished script; remaking a winner's bridge.
- **Inputs → outputs:** hook + mechanism + avatar problem/desire + authority + customer language → one bridge per hook, labelled with its pattern, written as visible escalation, plus an optional hook-cut variant; for iterations, a remade bridge with more authority/stakes/visuals.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** give the bridge one goal: "keep their attention". [L27 00:00–00:31](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
  - **DO** write a custom bridge for each hook: "We're definitely not just writing a hook and then having everything go to the same bridge." [L27 23:06–23:52](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
  - **IF** you can't bridge a hook (it "doesn't make any sense") **THEN** the hook isn't doing its job; rewrite the hook. [L27 00:31–01:00](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
  - **DO** choose the pattern by what the hook sets up (table below). [L27 02:15–05:01](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
  - **DO** show the escalation, don't announce it: not "now the problem is even worse" but "now they're not eating their greens at all". [L27 08:34–09:02](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
  - **DO** use facts or story in the bridge, not opinions: viewers are "looking for a reason to scroll off… they want a reason to object because they know it's an ad". [L48 14:51–16:50](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)
  - **DON'T** reassure in a bridge where you need urgency ("It sounds gross, but it's actually extremely common" makes the viewer think "I'm not as worried. Scroll"). [L52 08:08–10:40](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
  - **DO** use hedged, compliant wording on reposition bridges ("it could be something living inside of you", not "you have"). [L27 15:47–16:12](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
  - **DO** write the whole ad, cut the hook, play it; if it still makes sense, test it as an extra variation (3-2-2 becomes 4-2-2). [L27 20:13–20:59; 23:06–24:05](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
  - **DO** make sure a bridge used as an opener carries context: "Ideally your bridge has some kind of context… otherwise this will likely not be effective." [L27 page; 22:51–23:06](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
  - **DO** (video prompt) write bridges in spoken language with "exact customer frustration language", reference market gaps from the new-information doc, and end on an open loop into the solution. [L72 Section B](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)
- **See also:** [Hooks](hooks.md) · [Open loops](open-loops.md) · [Unique mechanism](unique-mechanism.md) · [Raising the stakes](raising-the-stakes.md) · [Authority in ads](authority-in-ads.md) · [Video ad script workflow](../frameworks/video-ad-script-workflow.md) · [Templates library](../libraries/templates.md)

## Definition
- "Once your hook has 1 goal, grabbed their attention. Your bridge also has 1 goal, keep their attention." "The bridge is the most important part to decide whether or not someone keeps watching or they drop off." [L27 page; 00:00–01:19](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
- In the course's script structure: Hook (0-3 s) → Bridge (3-8 s; "Pain intensification") → Hold (8-35 s) → CTA (35-45 s). [L72 rule digest](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md) In Milanote it is the "HOLD / BRIDGE" card, labelled per hook ("hook one bridge"). [L05 05:35–06:54](../lessons/05-why-you-need-to-write-in-the-environment.md)
- After the bridge, "the rest of your ad… is just building belief". [L29 06:08–06:52](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)

## Why it matters
- "Even if you're just grabbing someone's attention doesn't mean that it's going to convert." Most people read the hook, then "either they keep watching because it leads into something interesting, which is essentially the bridge, or we lose them." [L27 00:11–01:19](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
- "The bridge section is usually a make or break on how much of that attention stays on the ad." [L48 14:51](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md); "that bridge section is a make or break on whether or not they actually stay." [L52 07:24–08:08](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
- **Bridges can be breakthroughs**: "One of the $1,000,000 ads we made (single ad spent $1M) was actually just an in-media res hook (aka a bridge hook)" [L27 page; 01:47–02:15](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md); a breakthrough "that has spent over two million dollars… goes straight in the action", and of three cuts of one ad, one was a breakthrough and two "completely flopped" (instructor claims). [L52 11:25–12:22](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
- **Broad and label hooks depend on it**: after a label call-out "the bridge becomes important"; broad hooks need a bridge to funnel to the product. [L34 07:36–08:08](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md); [L24 how to apply](../lessons/24-the-2-types-of-hooks.md)

## The six bridge patterns (choose by what the hook sets up)
| # | Pattern | Use when the hook… | Formula / example | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce the mechanism, tie back to the promise (P-B-P) | Names a problem/product; product-aware market; visual product | "First, this [PRODUCT] has a [NEW MECHANISM]. And the [NEW MECHANISM] is what actually stops [PROBLEM] and causes [VISUAL SOLUTION OF PROBLEM]" / "The X MECHANISM helped me get DESIRE (CONTROL). And the Y MECHANISM helps with COMMON PROBLEM." ("the exact strategy that we use to make the million dollar ad") | [L27 02:15–07:10](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md) |
| 2 | Escalate the problem | Opens a loop about a pain | "There's an uncomfortable truth about those embarrassing white flakes on your shoulders, and it's way grosser than you think."; "Only wanting chicken nuggets? Red flag. Sugar cravings? Red flag. Low appetite? Red flag."; "Are you one of the 96% of dog owners who don't clean their dog's teeth daily? Well, by age 3, you could be setting up your dog for a lifetime of dental issues" | [L27 07:10–11:04](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md) |
| 3 | Escalate the desire | Promises a result | "You're probably dreading tomorrow's nail battle as much as your dog is, but what if I showed you dogs actually falling asleep during trims."; "If you smell like this... I'm definitely coming over."; "Nobody believes I'm 54 anymore. They're convinced I got some expensive treatment" | [L27 11:04–13:37](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md) |
| 4 | Reposition the problem (ideally with an authority) | Says "you don't need X" / "it's not what you think" | "Mittens control the scratching, but what's triggering the itch? Remove what's causing it."; "The problem isn't skin related. Be careful because it could be something living inside of you." | [L27 13:37–16:17](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md) |
| 5 | Allude to new information (ideally from an authority) | Promises insider knowledge | "So why do most compression socks feel so uncomfortable? It turns out we've been using the wrong materials"; "My cat was trying to teach me something for years, but I ignored him…" | [L27 16:17–18:22](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md) |
| 6 | Set the timeline | Claims long experience or fast results | "But I'll teach it to you in about one minute"; "Here's what happens inside your body in the first seven days" | [L27 18:22–19:44](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md) |
| + | In medias res (cut the hook) | Finished ad still makes sense from the bridge/action | Ryze doctor "drink alcohol if you want to damage your liver…"; The Groovd "The unique groove design of these books"; Based Supplies "Yes, we make skincare from beef fat…" | [L27 19:44–24:24](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md) |

Patterns can be stacked (dandruff: escalate the problem, then "give me the next 90 seconds and I'll explain this"). [L27 07:55](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)

## How to use it
1. **Name what the hook set up** (problem, result, "you don't need X", secret, experience/time, product).
2. **Pick the matching pattern**; for escalation, add a relatable first line so the viewer nods ("Are you one of…", "You're probably dreading…"), then escalate. [L27 08:10–11:04](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
3. **Call out the avatar** inside the bridge ("I thought I was being a [GOOD PARENT], but after [Y MONTHS], [PROBLEM] was still happening, and now [PROBLEM IS EVEN WORSE]"). [L27 08:10–08:34](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
4. **Write it as visuals**: what do we *see* getting worse or better? Brief music too ("Your audio also plays into the valence and intensity factor"). [L27 08:34–09:02](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md); [L52 09:46–10:40](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
5. **Use customer language and specific behaviour** ("This one enzyme is why you have to avoid walking past the kitchen pantry, even after you just ate"; from Reddit). [L71 04:49–06:50](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
6. **Keep the authority frame where it helps** ("Scientists call it DPP-4."). [L71 16:01](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
7. **Time it**: the prompt allots 3-8 s; the course now extends hook + bridge to about 8 s per variant "to try to add more congruence… and in essentially allowing meta to see that they are more different". [L72](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md); [L70 15:59–16:39](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md)
8. **Cut-the-hook test**, and look for an action shot later in the ad you could start on. [L27 20:13–24:24](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
9. **AI review**: name the fix ("the bridge here is lacking the stickiness that is needed to keep the person watching… Let's re-optimize the bridge"). [L36 01:25–02:19](../lessons/36-what-about-ai.md)

## Common mistakes
- **One shared bridge for all hooks.** [L27 23:06–23:52](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
- **Announcing the agenda** ("in this video I'm going to explain…") unless you are deliberately setting a timeline. [L27 24:05–24:24](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
- **Telling escalation** instead of showing it. [L27 08:34–09:02](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
- **Opinion bridges** and **reassuring bridges**. [L48 14:51–16:50](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md); [L52 08:08](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
- **Overclaiming causes** ("root cause", "you have parasites"); use "could be". [L27 15:47–16:12](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
- **Hookless cuts with no context** (the mushroom cut has less context "until I see the mushroom come in"). [L27 22:25–22:51](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)

## Cross-course notes
- **Agreement (Tether Lab):** Tether's [UGC script writing playbook](../../../../playbooks/ugc-script-writing.md) spends "~2/3 of the runtime on setup (problem + lived experience)"; Evolve's bridge patterns 2-3 (escalate problem/desire) are that setup in compressed form.
- **Agreement:** Tether's [suspense](../../../../concepts/heuristics/suspense.md) and [micro-story](../../../../concepts/heuristics/micro-story.md) pages support holding attention with a small story beat, like Evolve's story bridges (deck refresh).
- **Different structure:** Tether's playbook tests "3 hooks × 2 bodies"; Evolve tests three hook+bridge pairs over one identical hold. Default: if testing hooks, follow Evolve (bridge travels with its hook; hold constant); test bodies separately.
