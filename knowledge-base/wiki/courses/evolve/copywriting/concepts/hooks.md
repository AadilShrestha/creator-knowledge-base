# Hooks (the writer's number-one job)
> A hook stops the scroll **and keeps people** ("sticky"); before writing, make two choices (targeted vs broad; promise vs open loop), then build in emotion, a curiosity gap and high stakes, start in the action with zero backstory and zero chaos, make it work without sound, and judge it by retention and sales, not hook rate alone.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing the first 0-3 s of a UGC/voiceover script or a static headline; generating hook variants for a test; classifying a competitor hook; diagnosing a loser with a low hook rate or a spend winner that doesn't convert.
- **Inputs → outputs:** avatar + angle + mechanism + awareness level + context (is the core fact interesting?) → 3+ hooks, each labelled targeted/broad, promise/open-loop/combined, opener type, emotion, gap, stakes, visual, and a matched bridge → after testing, a hook-rate/hold-rate/CTR read and the next iteration.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** treat the hook as suspect #1 when an ad underperforms: "it is 98%, probably 99% of the time due to your hook" (instructor claim). [L23 00:00–00:20](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md); "the majority of time when you create a losing ad, it's due to the fact that the hook just simply did not grab the person's attention." [L48 00:00–00:39](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)
  - **DO** make it sticky: a hook that gets bites but loses the fish "is a terrible hook… you don't want bites. You want sales." [L23 00:48–01:37](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
  - **DO** ask first "Am I going to write a targeted hook or a broad hook?" and "Am I going to make a promise or am I going to open a loop?" [L24 00:00–00:27](../lessons/24-the-2-types-of-hooks.md); [L25 00:18–00:41](../lessons/25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md); checklist Q3-4 [L58](../lessons/58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md)
  - **DO** aim for the three must-haves: **emotion, curiosity gap, high stakes** (text or visual). [L23 02:56–06:59](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
  - **DO** start in the action ("even for 'yap sessions'"); **DON'T** use backstory or in-jokes; **DON'T** add chaos ("a confused brain will scroll… Curious NOT confused"); make it "Understandable Without Sound". [L23 06:59–10:04; page](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
  - **DO** open the loop in the first 3 seconds and close it as late as possible. [L26 00:27–01:35](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
  - **DO** write a custom bridge for every hook and test hook + bridge together (the hold stays identical). [L27 23:06–23:52](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md); [L70 15:59–16:39](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md)
  - **DO** deliver whatever the hook promised or implied; "If you don't… you've broken my trust and I'm just going to scroll." [L25 04:17; 12:01–12:30](../lessons/25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md)
  - **DON'T** let a hook mislead: a hook can be "the reason why it's not working" if it can't be tied back to the rest of the ad. [L50 03:29–04:46](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)
  - **DO** copy hook *mechanisms* across niches (reduce to slots), never the words: "you can also look across categories and sometimes those are the most powerful ones." [L57 00:54–01:39](../lessons/57-100-hook-templates-form-winning-ads-deck.md)
- **See also:** [Open loops](open-loops.md) · [Bridges](bridges.md) · [Unaware ads](unaware-ads.md) · [Raising the stakes](raising-the-stakes.md) · [Labels and customer language](labels-and-customer-language.md) · [Write in the environment](write-in-the-environment.md) · [Hooks library](../libraries/hooks.md) · [Templates library](../libraries/templates.md)

## Definition
- "Hook is the #1 job of the writer. Your hook has to be STICKY." [L23 page](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
- The hook's one goal is to grab attention; the bridge's goal is to keep it. [L27 00:00–00:31](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
- For unaware ads, the hook's "entire job is just to get people to watch the video" (it "sells the remainder of the ad itself"). [L35 03:42–04:40](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
- Hook/headline = "how the angle is communicated". [L67 angle-prompt doc](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md)

## Decision 1: targeted or broad?
| | Targeted | Broad |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | "You go SPECIFIC to call out your audience" | "You go VERY BROAD to call out everyone to reach a NEW audience… and then narrow down from there" |
| Use when | Large product awareness; beginner writers; brands under ~$100-200K/month or weak DR copy [L48 08:31–09:17](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md) | You have the skill to grab everyone and funnel; scaling past a saturated audience |
| Attention driver | Relevance ("that's me") | A universal, relatable thing + curiosity gap |
| Distribution | Big specific group | Shares ("I might know someone who is") |
| Risk | Non-avatars scroll | No curiosity ("here's how I make a million dollars"); weak funnel |
| Examples | "Don't brush your dog's teeth? Food builds up quickly causing issues"; "Your toes will never be cold again" | "This isn't a phone case."; "Who has a better life, cats or dogs?"; "When you send us your shirts, you're trusting us with your memories." |
| Source | [L24 00:27–02:26](../lessons/24-the-2-types-of-hooks.md) | [L24 02:26–07:50](../lessons/24-the-2-types-of-hooks.md) |

## Decision 2: promise or open loop?
| | Promise | Open loop |
|---|---|---|
| Rule | "Leave in the context -> Make a Promise (context = the idea is really good and interesting)" | "Take out the context -> Open Loop"; "delete the product" |
| Viewer thinks | "I know exactly what this is… do I want it?" | "Wait, what's going to happen?" |
| Use when | The meat of the ad is interesting on its own | The context is boring ("a lot of times… our context is essentially the product") |
| Must | "better deliver it" | "better be worth it" |
| Examples | "He covered his floor with 100,000 pennies"; "Lighter VS Lollipop"; MrBeast "$1 vs $100,000,000 Car!" | "You'll never believe what he covered his floor with"; "Which shoes will protect your feet the most?"; "Watch out for these ingredients when buying shampoo" |
| Source | [L25 00:41–09:53](../lessons/25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md) | [L25 09:53–12:30](../lessons/25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md) |

Combined hooks are common ("most open loops have some form of a promise inside of them"): "Here's how to bathe your dog without water", "How ice cubes clean a scorching pan", "Hands down the coolest kids craft". [L25 12:30–15:00](../lessons/25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md) Test: "If you have a lot of questions after reading a hook, that's more of an open loop." [L25 14:59–15:29](../lessons/25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md) Write both versions and test them. See [open loops](open-loops.md) for the three loop types.

## Metrics and thresholds (as stated)
| Metric | Threshold / benchmark | Who / caveat | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6-second retention | Content creators aim for "90% retention for the first 6 seconds" | Organic creators compete in "the auction" | [L23 01:37–03:25](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md) |
| Same, for ads | "If I could even just get 45% of people, that's going to probably be a banging ad" | Instructor's rule of thumb, no data | [L23 02:56–03:25](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md) |
| Hook rate (loser diagnosis) | "less than 30%" = "it just didn't grab a person's attention"; but "we have had breakthroughs with 30% hook rates" | Soft metric, not "the end all be all" | [L48 00:39–01:27](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md) |
| Hook rate / hold rate / CTR (prompt feedback) | Low = hook rate < 25%, hold rate < 5%, CTR < 1% | The video prompt's examples | [L70 06:31 (prompt)](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md); [L72](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md) |
| Data sufficiency | Count hook/hold rate only with significant spend: "$1k in spend minimum after 7 days" (page); "$2 and a 70% hook rate… needs more data" | Recommendation | [L46 06:33–08:00](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md) |
| Hook-to-hold | "% of people who got hooked watched 15 seconds or more" | Spend-winner diagnosis | [L50 01:15](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md) |
| Timing | Hook 0-3 s; hook + bridge extended to ~8 s | Video prompt; "something that we started doing as of late" | [L72](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md); [L70 15:59–16:39](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md) |
| Static headline | 6-8 words max (static prompt) | Prompt's own examples often exceed it | [L65 prompt](../lessons/65-banger-static-ads-prompt.md) |

Rule (derived from the above): low hook rate → rewrite the hook (and its visual); decent hook rate but poor spend/conversion → look at bridge, claims, belief, offer, or a misleading hook, not more hook variants. [L48 rules](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md); [L50 03:29–04:46](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)

## How to write one (step order)
1. Avatar, angle, awareness and mechanism are set ([avatars and angles](avatars-and-angles.md), [market awareness](market-awareness.md)).
2. Choose targeted/broad, then promise/open loop (both decisions above).
3. Draft 3 hooks from different families; the video prompt suggests (1) discovery-led from the new-mechanism doc, (2) contrarian/"what they're not telling you" from new information, (3) pain-point validation from customer data. [L72 rule digest](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)
4. Make at least one lower-valence "self-check" hook ("give people a reason to watch to make sure that they're not doing something wrong"). [L71 01:24–04:13](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
5. Use customer words and labels ("feels like torture"; "SAHM"). [L71 17:32](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md); [L34 02:21–07:36](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
6. Put stakes in the visual if the line can't hold them; brief the editor. [L23 05:39–06:59](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
7. Lay out on a hook card; shorten. [L05 02:51–04:42](../lessons/05-why-you-need-to-write-in-the-environment.md)
8. Write a bridge per hook; check the hook still makes sense without set-up, and test a hookless (bridge-first) cut as an extra variation. [L27 19:44–24:24](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
9. Run the build checklist; then test 3 hook+bridge variants with one hold.

## Common mistakes
- **Optimising hook rate alone** ("I used to be so obsessed with high hook rates"). [L23 01:13–01:37](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
- **The obvious hook** ("do you have low testosterone?"). [L48 01:27](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)
- **Broad with no curiosity** or with weak DR copy. [L24 03:51–04:21](../lessons/24-the-2-types-of-hooks.md); [L48 08:31–10:49](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)
- **Excluding viewers with needless details** (location, demographic, purpose). [L25 17:07–17:59](../lessons/25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md)
- **Breaking the promise** (unless it is a deliberate, escalated, funny rage-bait). [L25 06:46–08:59](../lessons/25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md)
- **Fake danger in fear hooks**: "DON'T. BE. A. PIECE. OF. SHIT." [L26 page](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
- **Pasting a review straight in as a hook** (works "probably 999 times out of 1000" not at all); find the interesting part and add a gap. [L44 05:34–06:20](../lessons/44-prompts-to-help-you-storytell.md)
- **Rewriting a winning hook and losing what it communicated.** [L09 08:49–09:37](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
- **Summarising hooks in learnings**: record them verbatim (on-screen and voiceover). [L46 06:33–08:00](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)

## Tensions (agent defaults; logged in the contradictions notes)
- **Hook length:** video prompt "~5 words (3-7), max 2 lines" vs Spencer's final hooks of 13-17 words with hook+bridge stretched to ~8 s [L71 limitations](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md). Default: keep the first on-screen line ≤ 2 mobile lines; let the spoken hook run longer only if every word adds stakes or a gap.
- **Hook-rate threshold:** "< 30%" [L48](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md) vs "< 25%" [L72](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md). Default: < 25% = clear hook problem; 25-30% = borderline, judge with hold rate and spend.
