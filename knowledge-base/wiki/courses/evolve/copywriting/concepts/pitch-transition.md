# Pitch transition (pitching without rejection)
> Only introduce the product after the ad has (1) paid off what the hook promised and (2) built enough belief. Then move into the product with a transition line and pitch it as a recommendation (or a founder's real reason), urgent or non-urgent, never as a cold "buy my product".

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing the product reveal / CTA beat of a UGC script or static; step 4 of the course's hook → bridge → objections → pitch writing order; diagnosing an ad that gets attention but does not convert; tearing down where and how a competitor ad introduces its product.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a drafted body (hook promise, bridge, mechanism, proof, objection handling) + the ad's awareness level + true offer facts (guarantee, sale, sell-out history) → a payoff check, a belief check, one transition line, one pitch line (voice + energy chosen), and a CTA that closes the hook's loop.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** run the payoff check first: every promise in the hook ("4 reasons why", a curiosity gap, a claim) is delivered before the pitch. Pitch before the payoff and "people will not buy. It just doesn't make sense." [L29 00:00–01:44](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
  - **DO** run the belief check second: relatability, new mechanism, proof, authorities and handled objections are present. "If you pitch someone that doesn't believe you, they're not going to buy." [L29 06:08–07:43](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
  - **DO** signal authority before the pitch, not during it. [L20 00:00–00:34](../lessons/20-being-aware-of-authorities.md)
  - **IF** the market is sophisticated (late stage 4 / stage 5) **THEN** build more belief and more identity ("show them it's for them") before pitching. [L29 06:52–07:43](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
  - **DO** default to a non-company "recommendation" voice for unaware, solution-aware and product-aware ads: "people are very skeptical of buying things online. They want to be recommended things online." [L29 08:27–09:00](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
  - **IF** the brand has a real purpose and a founder who can say it **THEN** pitch in company voice ("We started X because of Y"): "people don't want to buy from companies, they want to buy from people." [L29 08:27–09:00](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
  - **IF** the ad is an offer / most-aware ad (e.g. BOGO) **THEN** pitch the offer directly and skip the transition pattern. [L29 09:00–09:51](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
  - **DO** use one transition line ("Now here's the thing...", "The one caveat is...") and one pitch line; do not stack many. [L29 09:51–10:49](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
  - **DON'T** use "I did research" as the pitch: "nobody really buys that". Use experience-based authority ("I've bought three or four of these and... the ones that I found from this company over here is the best one"). [L29 10:49–11:40](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
  - **DON'T** lead with the discount in a static ("SAVE UP TO 25%" is "a very empty claim and everybody does this"); give a reason (vulnerability) instead. [L54 03:02–03:53](../lessons/54-iteration-examples-breakdowns-walkthrough.md)
  - **DO** give the viewer a true reason to act now ("WHY DOES THE CONSUMER NEED TO BUY THIS NOW"), not only "sale ends tonight". [L50 07:40–08:35](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)
  - **DON'T** fabricate urgency or proof: use "sold out X times", "10,000 people switched" only with real numbers (the lesson's numbers are placeholders) [L29 10:49–12:58](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md); the video prompt asks for "real urgency (not fake scarcity)" [L72 docx Section D](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md).
  - **IF** testing hooks **THEN** keep one universal CTA identical across the three hook/bridge variants so the test isolates the opening. [L70 16:39](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md)

## Definition
In this course a "pitch" is "essentially a CTA": the moment the product is introduced "after you've built it up" [L29 00:00–00:51](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md). The **pitch transition** is the move from the body (education, story, mechanism, proof, objection handling) into that product moment. It has three parts:
1. **Gate:** two checks that must pass first (payoff delivered, belief established).
2. **Transition line:** a pattern sentence that shifts from education to recommendation.
3. **Pitch line:** who is speaking (a third party recommending vs the company) and with what energy (non-urgent vs urgent).

The course places it fourth in the writing sequence: hooks, bridges, objections, pitch. "Basically, the rest of your ad after the bridge is just building belief." [L29 06:08–06:52](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)

## Why it matters
- **An unpaid promise kills the sale.** The page defines "delivered" as "You've given your 4 reasons why", "You've filled the curiosity gap", "You've delivered on your promise." [L29 page text "#1"](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
- **Great payoffs get shared** (instructor claim, no data): people share when they "got value", "discovered something new (they're first)", or "it could help someone they know". [L29 01:44–02:32](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
- **Viewers need different amounts of belief.** Some click after relatability + mechanism; others need more proof, authority or objections answered, which is why people click through at different points in a video. [L29 06:52–07:43](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
- **Spend winners usually fail after the hook, not at it.** In the iteration section, ads that Meta spends on but that do not convert most often lack belief, urgency or stakes, or a strong CTA ("no reason for cold people to act now"). [L50 page list #4; 07:40–12:15](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)
- **Going straight to the product loses trust.** Dog dental powder: "goes straight to the product, no earning of trust" (#8 belief issue). [L54 slide table](../lessons/54-iteration-examples-breakdowns-walkthrough.md)

## How to use it (steps)
1. **List the hook's promises.** Number of reasons, the curiosity gap, the claim. Mark where each is paid off in the body. Fix gaps before writing any CTA. [L29 00:00–01:44](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
2. **Run the belief checklist:** relatability, new mechanism, proof, authorities, objections prevented/handled. Add what is missing; add identity cues for sophisticated markets. [L29 06:08–07:43](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md); place the authority before the pitch [L20 00:00–00:34](../lessons/20-being-aware-of-authorities.md).
3. **Pick the voice** with the table below.
4. **Pick the energy** (non-urgent vs urgent) using only true facts.
5. **Write one transition line, then one pitch line**, then the action. Close the hook's loop in the CTA where possible (L71's final CTA echoes the hook: "Your body has been fighting you. Metaglow helps you fight back.") [L71 12:47–13:45](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md).
6. **Optional hook move (flash forward):** put the payoff/desired outcome in the first seconds and let the body explain how you got there. Credited to Adley (Viralish). [L29 02:32–05:23](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md) The Claude Code iteration list calls the same move "In media res: ... start with the ideal outcome, then start explaining how we got there". [L47 iteration table](../lessons/47-claude-code-10-step-iteration-process.md)

### Decision table: voice and energy

| Brand with a real founder purpose | Company / founder | Non-urgent | "We started X brand because of Y issue" · "If you want to try our products, we do have X guarantee" [L29 11:40–12:58](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md) |
| Company voice with a real sale / sell-out record | Company | Urgent | "We do have a sale active right now, while supplies last" · "We have sold out X times now, so if you want to try X, link is below" [L29 12:25](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md) |

### CTA slot in the course's video structure
The video prompt fixes the CTA at **35–45 s** of a 30–45 s script: "summarize transformation, price with value framing, bonuses, risk reversal with pattern interrupt, real urgency (not fake scarcity), clear CTA". Problem-aware scripts: "CTA makes it easy to try"; product-aware scripts: "CTA is risk reversal". ## Common mistakes
- **Pitching before the payoff** ("four reasons" hook, three delivered; curiosity gap never filled). [L29 00:00–01:44](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
- **Pitching to someone who doesn't believe yet**: no mechanism, proof or authority before the product. [L29 06:52–07:43](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md); [L54](../lessons/54-iteration-examples-breakdowns-walkthrough.md)
- **Authority dropped in during the pitch** instead of before it. [L20 00:00–00:34](../lessons/20-being-aware-of-authorities.md)
- **"I did research" as the credential.** [L29 10:49–11:40](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
- **Brand-voice slogans in a UGC script** where a recommendation would be trusted more. [L29 08:27–09:00](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
- **Discount as the headline** ("everybody does this"). [L54 03:02–03:53](../lessons/54-iteration-examples-breakdowns-walkthrough.md)
- **Transactional urgency only** ("sale ends tonight") with no reason the problem matters now. [L50 07:40–08:35](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)
- **Varying the CTA across hook variants** in a hook test, which muddies the read. [L72 docx "Testing Reminder"](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)
- **Shipping AI-drafted price or offer lines unchecked** (the "per jar" error). [L70 17:45](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md)

## Cross-course notes
- **Evolve (Spencer Pawliw) vs Tether Lab on CTA wording.** Tether Lab's [active CTA language](../../../../concepts/active-cta-language.md) says write definitive buy verbs ("Get it", "Order yours now", "See how it works") and avoid browse verbs ("Shop Now", "Learn More") because they "encourage browsing behavior". Evolve's non-urgent recommendation lines are deliberately soft ("If you're curious, link below", "I found these from X company"). Agent default (derived): use Evolve's recommendation voice for the *transition and pitch line*, then end on one definitive action verb ("grab yours below", "see how it works") in the final CTA line. Logged in the contradictions notes.
- **Agreement on risk reversal.** Tether Lab's [risk-reduction hooks](../../../../concepts/risk-reduction-hooks.md) ("Anytime you put guarantees anywhere in your ad, that speaks to security") matches Evolve's company-voice line "we do have X guarantee" [L29 11:40–12:25](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md) and the product-aware "CTA is risk reversal" rule [L72](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md).
- **Agreement on placement.** Tether Lab puts buy prompts "where the visitor is convinced (after the first product video, after the fit section)" ([active CTA language](../../../../concepts/active-cta-language.md)); Evolve's belief gate is the ad-script version of the same idea.
- **Native/recommendation framing.** Tether Lab's [native advertising](../../../../concepts/native-advertising.md) job is "introduce the product naturally → substantiate the promise → offer a clear next step", consistent with Evolve's order (payoff, belief, recommendation).
