# Unaware ads
> Unaware ads speak to people who don't yet see a problem or a new way to satisfy a desire: the hook is product-free and identity-driven (hidden fear, hidden desire, "did you know", or debate), the opening feels like content, and the ad walks the viewer from unaware to an offer in one piece. Hardest to write, potentially the most scalable.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing cold-traffic, top-of-funnel hooks; scaling past a saturated problem-aware angle; iterating a winner "down" one awareness level; tearing down competitor ads whose first seconds never mention the product; checking whether a draft accidentally reads unaware.
- **Inputs → outputs:** avatar identity + the product's problem/desire + true surprising facts or stats → 3–10 product-free hooks each tagged with an opener type, the full path (hook → problem → solution types → product → offer), and the bridge that carries the viewer from hook to problem.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** forget the product in the hook: price, product name and benefits "mean nothing to them yet"; "concentrate on the prospect's identity". [L35 02:58–04:40](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
  - **DO** make the hook's only job to "sell the remainder of the ad itself". [L35 03:42–04:40](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
  - **DO** pick one opener: hidden fear, hidden desire, did you know, debate ("The big three are hidden fear, hidden desire, and did you know"). [L35 06:44–08:00](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
  - **DO** walk the full sequence in one ad: identity hook (unaware) → problem or future problem (problem aware) → types of solutions (solution aware) → your product as the best fit (product aware) → an offer that makes impulse buying easy (most aware). [L35 04:40–05:55](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
  - **DON'T** introduce the product too early; it muddies the sequence. [L35 04:40–05:55](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
  - **DO** make the opening feel like content (education, entertainment, a story, information people want). [L35 02:06–02:58; 20:02–20:38](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
  - **DON'T** equate camouflage with unaware: "Not every single ad that is camouflaged... is unaware." [L35 02:58–03:42](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
  - **DON'T** confuse unaware with problem aware: naming a problem the viewer didn't know they had is unaware-level work; problem-aware copy makes a known problem feel "worth solving". [L21 04:45–05:30](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md); [L35 01:26–02:06](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
  - **DON'T** try to appeal to everyone: unaware only has "to resonate with a specific identity" (pets, cats over 7, chocolate eaters). [L35 13:28–14:25; 19:32–20:38](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
  - **IF** the hook is a shock visual **THEN** follow it with an authority or story that makes the unconscious fear concrete (choking footage, then a fireman). [L35 14:25–14:59](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
  - **DO** plan the longest copy for unaware ("Unaware... is the longest form copy, because you have to take people through each customer journey"). [L21 15:45–17:23](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
  - **IF** an unaware ad loses **THEN** suspect execution: unaware is "too much of a wild card ... a really big swing". [L48 01:27](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)
  - **IF** iterating a winner **THEN** test one level up or down ("solution aware → try unaware"). [L52 13:01–13:43](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
  - **DO** pitch with a recommendation voice, not a company slogan, at the end of an unaware ad. [L29 08:27–09:51](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
  - **DON'T** fear-monger: one unaware fear static is "scaring people" but about "a real thing that happens"; slide: "I am not recommending fear-mongering at all." [L67 ref slide 16](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md)

## Definition
Unaware ads target people "not actively seeking a solution" because they (1) do not recognise a problem or (2) do not realise a new way to satisfy a desire; by identifying with the prospect in the hook, they become aware as they watch. [L35 01:26–02:58](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md) Schwartz (quoted): "logical prospects for your product and yet in their own minds are hundreds of miles away from accepting that product." [L35 02:06](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md) The awareness lesson: unaware "is still a very real category. And it's why it can dominate on social platforms"; also "identity marketing is used for unaware audiences". [L21 02:57–03:51](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)

Three characteristics [L35 02:06–05:55](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md):
1. **Camouflage:** it "doesn't look, feel, or sound like an ad" (but camouflaged ads can start at any awareness level).
2. **Identity over product:** the hook is "their headline, their problem, their state of mind".
3. **The sequence:** all five awareness stages in one ad.

## Why it matters
- "Unaware ads appeal to the largest market but are the hardest to create... when you crack them, they can become the most scalable ads in your account and convert the largest percentage of the market." [L35 00:00–00:38](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md) (instructor claim)
- The Buyer's Pyramid: 3% ready to buy, 7% open, 30% not thinking about it, 30% think they're not interested, 30% definitely not interested; "60% or more" sit in unaware depending on grouping, and "these percentages will change". [L35 00:38–01:26](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
- Broad hooks that are "technically unaware" are how you "create ads that have this hyper scalability", and they travel by shares. [L24 06:59–07:50](../lessons/24-the-2-types-of-hooks.md)
- They are "hyper scalable because they don't feel like an ad when they start". [L35 19:32–20:38](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
- Spencer's own tweet: "Creating unaware ads on Meta is the easiest way to legally print money" (used as a juxtaposition exercise). [L40 02:12–03:13](../lessons/40-what-makes-a-great-story.md)

## How to use it (steps)
1. **Choose the identity**, not the product (pet owners, parents, people who walk, chocolate eaters). [L35 steps](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
2. **List raw material** for the avatar: 5 hidden fears, 5 hidden desires, 5 true surprising facts/stats/stories, 2 debate questions. [L35 checklist](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md) The research sheet's awareness tab gives one question per level, e.g. Unaware: "Exercise and health are important to me - besides doing cardio, how else can I improve my cardiovascular health?" [L22 Tab 9](../lessons/22-how-to-finalize-your-ad-ideas.md)
3. **Write product-free hooks** with the opener table below; use identity call-outs ("If you have [X]...") or a concrete visual.
4. **Draft the path**: hook → story/problem → why old solutions fail → solution category → our product → offer, with a bridge that keeps attention ("the goal of the bridge [is] to keep the person there"). [L35 04:40–05:55; 14:59–15:56](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md) Problem compounding ("one problem leads to another") is "important for unaware and problem-aware ads". [L52 13:01](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
5. **Check the opening reads like content**, then check the finished ad still reads at the planned level; mismatches are common ("planned problem aware, reads unaware"). [L46 09:20](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md); [L47 05:12–05:55](../lessons/47-claude-code-10-step-iteration-process.md)
6. **Tag every ad with its opener type** so iterations can test opener against opener with the same body. [L35 checklist](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)

### The four openers
| Opener | Core move | Structure (page text) | Real hook | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hidden fear | A fear everyone shares but doesn't talk about daily | Lead with the fear, tell a story around it, bridge to your solution | "If you have pets, your washer is growing something inside of it." | [L35 06:44; 15:56–16:38](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md) |
| Hidden desire | A desire everyone shares but doesn't think about daily; "imagine if" rather than "this product will deliver" | Lead with the desire, tell a story, bridge | "3 signs your hair is actually wavy" | [L35 06:44–07:42; 10:32–11:12](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md) |
| Did you know | Genuine curiosity ("people just want to learn things"; associated with Hormozi) | Lead with a question, stat or story that can be bridged to the problem | "What is the number one ingredient in most chocolate bars? I'll give you a hint. It is not cacao." | [L35 07:42; 18:08–19:32](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md) |
| Debate | The urge to take sides; "like if you were gonna do a poll" (Spencer has "only one example") | Lead with an open-ended question or comparison, then bridge | "Who has a better life, cats or dogs?" | [L35 07:42; 09:33–10:32](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md) |

Schwartz's seven unaware headline types (hidden dream, hidden fear, accepted image into an unacceptable problem, common objection, ultimate triumph, result of a problem, result of an accomplishment) are on [lesson 35](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md); none mention a product.

## Common mistakes
- **Mentioning the product, price or benefit in the hook.** [L35 02:58–04:40](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
- **Calling a camouflaged problem-aware ad "unaware"**, or calling "Are you tired?" problem-aware when it's surfacing a problem. [L35 02:58–03:42](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md); [L21 04:45–05:30](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **Skipping stages** (jumping from hook to product) so the viewer never becomes problem- or solution-aware. [L35 04:40–05:55](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md); [L21 16:24–17:23](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **Too broad and too weak**: broad hooks need strong DR copy and visuals ("For men who struggle with hair loss you need this!" is weak broad). [L48 08:31–10:49](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)
- **Throwing out an angle that was never executed at the planned level.** [L46 09:20](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)
- **Trusting AI's awareness labels**: "Claude will struggle with awareness levels." [L70 19:26–20:20](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md); the Claude Code agent's call needed human review [L47 05:12–05:55](../lessons/47-claude-code-10-step-iteration-process.md)
- **Using the course's video prompt for unaware scripts**: it is built only for problem- and product-aware scripts (see Cross-course / internal notes below). [L72 docx length rules](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)

## Cross-course notes
- **Inside Evolve: where scale comes from, and whether the video prompt allows unaware.** L35 says unaware ads reach "the largest market" and "can become the most scalable ads in your account" [L35 00:00–00:38](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md). L21 says "Product aware is biggest it has ever been" and that winning product-aware buyers unlocks "massive scale" [L21 10:20–13:35](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md). And the course's own video prompt says "Do not write Unaware hooks or Most-Aware retargeting scripts" ("too educational, too long, wrong audience") with a 45 s cap [L72 docx](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md), while L21 calls unaware copy "the longest form". Agent default (derived): use the L72 prompt only for problem/product-aware scripts; write unaware concepts by hand with the L35 sequence, allowing longer runtimes (VSL expansion [L53](../lessons/53-the-iteration-playbook-formats-styles.md)); treat both "biggest market" claims as instructor opinion and let tests decide. Logged in the contradictions notes.
- **Evolve (Spencer Pawliw) vs Tether Lab on camouflage.** Evolve's unaware ad "doesn't look, feel, or sound like an ad" [L35 02:06–02:58](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md). Tether Lab's [native advertising](../../../../concepts/native-advertising.md) rule: "Match the placement without hiding the commercial relationship"; a sales message "should be recognizable as such". Agent default (derived): content-first *openings* are fine, but never fake an editorial source, a news outlet or a real customer; disclose per platform rules. Logged in the contradictions notes.
- **Evolve vs Tether Lab on journey stages.** Evolve packs every awareness stage into one unaware ad [L35 04:40–05:55](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md). Tether Lab's [TEEP journey](../../../../frameworks/teep-journey.md) writes separate ads per stage (trigger, exploration, evaluation, purchase) and keeps stages apart in the account. Agent default (derived): an Evolve unaware ad maps to TEEP's trigger/exploration slot as its entry point; tag it there, and still give it a full path to the offer. Logged in the contradictions notes.
- **Agreement.** Tether Lab's [hidden truth](../../../../concepts/heuristics/hidden-truth.md) ("something important being overlooked") is the heuristic behind Evolve's "did you know" and hidden-fear openers; both forbid fabricated "they don't want you to know" claims.
