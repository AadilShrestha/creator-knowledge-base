# Compression and plain language (less fluff, speak it, 6th-grade level)
> Three editing passes the course runs on every script: **compress** (delete until removing anything else would collapse the message), **speak it** (would a door-to-door salesperson say this?), and **simplify** (grade 6 or below in the Hemingway app, using formatting as well as word choice).

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** editing any hook, headline, body ("hold") or UGC/voiceover script; reviewing LLM first drafts; QA before a brief ships (checklist Q11-13).
- **Inputs → outputs:** a draft → (1) a deletion pass, (2) a read-aloud pass, (3) a readability pass with grade score → a shorter, spoken, grade-6-ish version with the meaning unchanged.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** "write with the delete key" and stop only when "there's nothing left to remove" from the hook/headline/lead/hold. [L11 03:20–03:53; slide 07:50](../lessons/11-why-you-need-to-write-less-fluff.md)
  - **DO** test each sentence: "what happens if I delete this?" If the flow still works, "it probably shouldn't have been there in the first place." [L11 07:12–07:43](../lessons/11-why-you-need-to-write-less-fluff.md)
  - **DON'T** compress into emptiness: compress "without losing meaning"; "nothing said well is still nothing". [L11 01:06–02:14](../lessons/11-why-you-need-to-write-less-fluff.md)
  - **DON'T** ship first drafts or raw LLM output ("Claude will just give you it and you'll just ship it"). [L11 06:07–06:37](../lessons/11-why-you-need-to-write-less-fluff.md); [L02 09:29](../lessons/02-the-current-state-of-copywriting.md)
  - **DO** ask Claude Hopkins' question of every line: "Would this help a salesman sell the goods?" Imagine saying it at a stranger's door. [L12 01:04–01:35](../lessons/12-why-you-need-to-speak-copy-not-write-it.md)
  - **DO** write video lines to be spoken (creator, AI voice, AI actor) and treat on-screen text as speech too: it is "read in the consumer's own voice". [L12 01:35–02:38](../lessons/12-why-you-need-to-speak-copy-not-write-it.md)
  - **DO** paste every brief/script into the Hemingway app and aim for "grade 6 or below". [L13 00:31](../lessons/13-why-you-need-to-write-at-a-6th-grade-level.md)
  - **DO** fix formatting, not only words: the same ad text scored grade 13 as a block and grade 5 as short lines and bullets. [L13 01:38–02:11](../lessons/13-why-you-need-to-write-at-a-6th-grade-level.md)
  - **DON'T** "try to sound smarter than you actually are"; use customer quotes, not a thesaurus. [L13 00:31](../lessons/13-why-you-need-to-write-at-a-6th-grade-level.md); [L34 00:00–00:53](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
  - **IF** a technical word is needed for credibility ("clinically proven", "GLP-1") **THEN** keep it and gloss it in plain words ("fullness hormone"); bending the grade is acceptable then. [L71 21:28–24:24](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md); [L68 35:41–36:28](../lessons/68-how-to-write-image-ads-part-2.md)
- **See also:** [Write in the environment](write-in-the-environment.md) · [Show, don't tell](show-dont-tell.md) · [Labels and customer language](labels-and-customer-language.md) · [Three copy checks](three-copy-checks.md) · [Copy checklist scorecard](../rubrics/copy-checklist-scorecard.md) · [Video ad script workflow](../frameworks/video-ad-script-workflow.md)

## Definition
| Pass | Course rule | Test | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compress (less fluff) | "A great sentence is a good sentence made shorter" (Harry Dry); "If a word isn't working for you, it's actually working against you" (Kaplan's Law of Words graphic) | Jenga: remove blocks until one more removal collapses the message | [L11 00:32–05:31](../lessons/11-why-you-need-to-write-less-fluff.md) |
| Speak it | "When writing imagine you're a door-to-door sales person" | Would you say it to someone's face? | [L12 01:00–01:35](../lessons/12-why-you-need-to-speak-copy-not-write-it.md) |
| Simplify | "Your customers don't want complicated language. They want easy to understand language." | Hemingway grade ≤ 6; no "very hard to read" sentences | [L13 page text; 00:00–00:31](../lessons/13-why-you-need-to-write-at-a-6th-grade-level.md) |

These are checklist items 11 (6th grade), 12 (condensed further without losing meaning) and 13 (door-to-door) [L01 03:00](../lessons/01-overview-of-copywriting-program.md); [L58](../lessons/58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md).

## Why it matters
- **Compression is "the direct response copywriter's number one tool."** "A lot of people are great at writing thoughts down but very few are good at compacting it." [L11 00:00–00:32](../lessons/11-why-you-need-to-write-less-fluff.md)
- **Respect and impact:** "Using fewer words lets you be more impactful with the words you keep"; less fluff "shows you also respect your customers time". [L11 slide 07:50](../lessons/11-why-you-need-to-write-less-fluff.md)
- **Every ad is already spoken**, by a creator, AI voice, or the viewer's inner voice reading on-screen text. [L12 01:35–02:38](../lessons/12-why-you-need-to-speak-copy-not-write-it.md)
- **Confusion scrolls:** "a confused brain will scroll… When in doubt, dumb it down." [L23 09:23–10:04](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md) "Nobody is going to be mad if something is easy to read." [L13 02:43](../lessons/13-why-you-need-to-write-at-a-6th-grade-level.md)
- **Clear writing takes time:** "Clear writing requires more time, effort, and thought than writing a long, unedited draft." [L11 05:30 slide](../lessons/11-why-you-need-to-write-less-fluff.md)

## How to use it (edit order)
1. **Draft without editing**, then take a break (an hour; or next day for headlines). [L11 08:13](../lessons/11-why-you-need-to-write-less-fluff.md); [L07 00:00–00:53](../lessons/07-why-great-copywriting-requires-boredom.md)
2. **Deletion pass**: for every sentence and word ask "what happens if I delete this?"; restore anything whose loss changes the meaning; stop when nothing more can go. [L11 03:20–07:43](../lessons/11-why-you-need-to-write-less-fluff.md)
3. **Lay it out where it will appear** and shorten the hook again on the card ("a great hook is just a good hook made shorter"). Example: "You struggle with back pain for years" → "Years of back pain" → "Years of back pain ends here". [L05 03:45–04:42](../lessons/05-why-you-need-to-write-in-the-environment.md) See [write in the environment](write-in-the-environment.md).
4. **Read aloud**: would you say it at a door? Replace "revolutionary fitness experience" with what it does; where you can show it, write the demonstration ("this is how thin the MacBook Air is" + envelope). [L12 00:00–03:24](../lessons/12-why-you-need-to-speak-copy-not-write-it.md)
5. **Readability pass**: Hemingway; replace "simpler alternative" words (e.g. "alleviate"), remove weakeners ("are designed"), break blocks into short lines and bullets. [L13 01:05–02:43](../lessons/13-why-you-need-to-write-at-a-6th-grade-level.md)
6. **Gloss jargon** you must keep, and use the customer's term ("fullness hormone" for emotion, "GLP-1" for clinical credibility). [L71 21:28–22:13](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
7. **For LLM drafts**, run a second pass instructed only to delete and tighten, then compare against the message line (derived from [L11 how to apply](../lessons/11-why-you-need-to-write-less-fluff.md)).

## Common mistakes
- **Shipping drafts** under time pressure. [L11 06:07](../lessons/11-why-you-need-to-write-less-fluff.md)
- **Compact but empty** lines ("fuel your progress"; the instructor "doesn't know what it means"). [L13 02:11](../lessons/13-why-you-need-to-write-at-a-6th-grade-level.md)
- **Letting the editor decide line breaks** on a long hook ("your hook is like four lines long and you're like, oh crap"). [L05 02:51](../lessons/05-why-you-need-to-write-in-the-environment.md)
- **Trusting AI simplification blindly**: "use Claude with caution… actually analyze it." [L13 00:31–01:05](../lessons/13-why-you-need-to-write-at-a-6th-grade-level.md)
- **Lecture-heavy objection handling** with stats the viewer cannot parse. [L28 20:22–21:09](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
- **Thesaurus words** instead of customer words. [L34 00:00–00:53](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)

## Cross-course notes
- **Agreement (Tether Lab):** [Processing fluency](../../../../concepts/heuristics/processing-fluency.md) ("Simple = safe. Complicated = risky"; the "7-year-old in 3 seconds" test) and [cognitive load](../../../../concepts/heuristics/cognitive-load.md) back the same edit passes.
- **Disagreement on register:** Evolve says grade 6 or below for everyone [L13](../lessons/13-why-you-need-to-write-at-a-6th-grade-level.md); Tether's live sessions say match the buyer's education and raise the register for affluent buyers, while a Tether CreativeOS lesson says "first-grade level" ([contradictions row 20](../../../../contradictions.md)). Agent default: simple words and short sentences always (both agree); keep Evolve's grade-5-7 target for mass-market DTC; allow a higher register only for premium/affluent audiences, as in the Tether default. Logged in the contradictions notes.
- **Disagreement on bullets:** Tether: "DON'T add bullet points under a clear headline ('you don't need them')" ([processing fluency](../../../../concepts/heuristics/processing-fluency.md)); Evolve formats body copy into bullets to drop the grade [L13 01:38](../lessons/13-why-you-need-to-write-at-a-6th-grade-level.md) and builds headline + 3-4 bullet statics [L67](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md). Agent default: if the headline alone carries the message, test it without bullets; if body copy is needed, use 3-4 short bullets rather than a paragraph (Tether's [cognitive load](../../../../concepts/heuristics/cognitive-load.md) also caps bullets at 3-5).
- **Note on the brief's suggested link:** Tether's [fluency mechanism](../../../../concepts/fluency-mechanism.md) is about recurring brand characters (recall), not plain language; the plain-language counterpart is processing fluency.
