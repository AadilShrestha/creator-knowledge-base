# Raising the stakes
> People pay more attention when more can be gained or lost, so after drafting, ask "how can we have more at stake?" and apply one of five levers (bigger, witness, urgent, permanent, costs more) to the hook, the bridge or the body, using only true, visual, concrete consequences.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a hook or problem line reads flat; a bridge reassures instead of pulling the viewer in; an ad is a spend winner that doesn't convert (stakes issue #10); a script is "boring"; tearing down what creates tension in a competitor ad.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a flat line ("This DNA kit tells you your heritage") + the product's true consequences (health, money, time, relationships, status) → five rewrites (one per lever), the strongest placed in the hook, bridge or first escalation beat, with a visual that carries it.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** run the stakes check after the draft: "how can we have more at stake? How can we create more drama? How can we make the ad feel like a big deal?" [L30 01:27–02:26](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)
  - **DO** test by feel: if you "can feel that emotion", the stakes are raised. [L30 01:27–02:26](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)
  - **DO** try all five levers on the same line: bigger, witness, urgent, permanent, costs more. [L30 02:26–09:20](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)
  - **DO** go visual and extreme: "the Kardashians" beats "the internet"; "run through a wall" beats "more energy". [L30 03:13–04:45](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)
  - **DO** check every hook for the three must-haves, "emotion, curiosity gap, high stakes" (not all winners have all three, "but a lot of great hooks do"). [L23 02:56–03:25](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md); checklist Q4 [L58](../lessons/58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md)
  - **IF** the words can't carry the stakes **THEN** put them in the visual; the visual "does not have to relate to the spoken line" (a man throwing four cats into a boat). Brief the editor. [L23 05:39–06:59](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
  - **DON'T** assume stakes must live in the hook: "a lot of this happens throughout the rest of the video as well." [L30 10:58–11:44](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)
  - **DO** make the bridge higher-stakes and visual rather than reassuring; "it's extremely common" lowers urgency ("I'm not as worried. Scroll"). [L52 07:24–10:40](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
  - **IF** a spend winner gets attention but doesn't convert **THEN** check stakes (#10) after belief (#7) and urgency (#9): raising stakes "can actually increase the urgency massively as well (2 for 1)". [L50 10:58–12:15](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)
  - **IF** a losing ad is "boring" (very common) **THEN** crank up intensity and raise the stakes; **IF** it is "super intense" **THEN** dial it down. [L48 10:49](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)
  - **IF** the lever is "make it cost more" **THEN** expect negative valence; the gain flip is possible but less common. [L30 07:59–08:45](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)
  - **IF** the product has no inherent drama (a fishing koozie) **THEN** create stakes with a scene ("Cop found us under a bridge again"), not with product claims. [L30 12:15–13:22](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)
  - **DON'T** escalate past the truth: the script playbook adds "BE ETHICAL" [L52 page #2](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md); power-word swaps must not break the truth [L63 page](../lessons/63-power-words.md); Red Bull's "wings" brought legal trouble and warnings [L30 03:13–03:58](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md).
- **See also:** [hooks](hooks.md) · [bridges](bridges.md) · [open-loops](open-loops.md) · [story-building-blocks](story-building-blocks.md) · [show-dont-tell](show-dont-tell.md) · [comparisons](comparisons.md) · [iteration-outcomes](iteration-outcomes.md) · [iteration-issue-codes](../frameworks/iteration-issue-codes.md) · [word-lists library](../libraries/word-lists.md) · [rewrites library](../libraries/rewrites.md) · Tether Lab: [intensity](../../../../concepts/intensity.md), [emotional zones](../../../../concepts/emotional-zones.md), [loss aversion](../../../../concepts/heuristics/loss-aversion.md)

## Definition
"Stake" = "what you stand to gain or to lose"; "the bigger the potential gain or loss, the more people pay attention" (game show for $10,000 vs $5 million; proposing on an empty beach vs in a 50,000-seat stadium). [L30 00:00–01:27](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md) In the storytelling section it becomes building block #3, **stakes + drama**: "Every great story has stakes, it has drama... A lot of the time, the more DRAMA your story has, the BETTER the story will perform." [L40 17:07](../lessons/40-what-makes-a-great-story.md) The five levers are the instructor's own ("This is just the way that I've created"); the idea of stakes came from Adley (Viralish). [L30 01:27–02:26](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)

## Why it matters
- **Attention and drama:** high stakes make the ad "feel like a big deal". [L30 01:27–02:26](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)
- **Hooks:** high stakes is one of three hook must-haves. [L23 02:56–03:25](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
- **Retention after the hook:** the bridge is "make or break"; adding authority and/or raising the stakes is how you fix it. [L52 07:24–08:08](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
- **Conversion:** "A lot of the best breakthroughs will have high stakes and will make it feel much more important." [L50 10:58](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md) Stakes also feed urgency: "That word lifetime creates massive urgency". [L50 10:04](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)
- **Stories:** "People are interested in abnormal things, not normal things." [L40 18:37](../lessons/40-what-makes-a-great-story.md)

## How to use it (steps)
1. Draft the ad. Mark each line in the hook, bridge and first ~20 s as low or high stakes. [L30 checklist](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)
2. For each low-stakes line, write one rewrite per lever (table below), using the lever's question.
3. Keep the rewrites you can *feel* and that are concrete/visual; swap abstract nouns for concrete ones.
4. Place one lever in the hook (text or visual) and hold others for the bridge and body; the bridge should escalate, not reassure. [L27 09:36–10:32](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md); [L52 07:24–10:40](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
5. Run a power-word pass over the draft (Mistake → Catastrophe, Issue → Threat, Help → Save) only where it stays true. [L63 page](../lessons/63-power-words.md)
6. Verify every consequence is real and substantiated; log the lever used per variant so results can be read by lever. [L30 checklist](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)

### The five levers
| # | Lever | Question to ask | Flat → stakes example | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make it bigger (escalation) | Can I make the problem or solution more extreme? | Dog pees in house → dog ruins $10,000 hardwood floors; "the internet is raising my kids" → "I don't want the Kardashians raising my kids" | [L30 02:26–04:45](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md) |
| 2 | Add a witness | Who's watching? Who will see if this goes right or wrong? | Fishing under a bridge → "cop found us fishing under a bridge"; dog pees in front of our landlord | [L30 04:45–05:43](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md) |
| 3 | Make it urgent | Why does this matter right now? | Improve your teeth → the damage is happening right now; "now is the time" → "you're at the perfect age to do this, every year it gets harder" | [L30 05:43–06:24](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md) |
| 4 | Make it permanent | What's lost or gained forever if they don't act? | Dental issues → "lifetime of problems" / "lifetime of people complimenting you on your smile"; wallet → "your grandkids will use this wallet" | [L30 06:24–07:59](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md) |
| 5 | Make it cost more | What is this really costing them? (money, relationships, time) | Snoring → "sleep divorce"; coffee crashes → "every morning coffee is robbing you of three hours of your day" | [L30 07:59–09:20](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md) |

All five work on the gain side too (permanent compliments, grandkids' wallet). [L30 07:09–07:59](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)

### Where stakes go (decision table)
| Symptom | Where to raise stakes | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hook stops nobody / reads flat | Hook text, or a high-stakes visual if the text can't carry it | [L23 05:39–06:59](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md) |
| Open loop is weak | Add a booster: juxtaposition, a danger word ("watch out"), or a high-stakes visual (barefoot jump onto nails) | [L25 10:10–14:18](../lessons/25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md) |
| Viewers leave right after the hook | Bridge: escalate the problem (diagnosis, red flags, compounding problems) | [L27 09:36–10:32](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md); [L52 13:01](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md) |
| Breakthrough needs more urgency | Emotional hook test: usually more intensity / lower valence | [L52 05:51–07:24](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md) |
| Spend winner, CTR fine, CVR low | Stakes #10 after belief #7 and urgency #9 | [L50 10:58–12:15](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md) |
| Price objection | Compare up and extend the cost beyond dollars (time, relationships, stress) | [L28 34:49–35:34](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| Format iteration (street interview) | Raise the prize ($20 cash guess; $10,000 mentioned) | [L53 13:47–14:22](../lessons/53-the-iteration-playbook-formats-styles.md) |

## Common mistakes
- **Abstract escalation** ("the internet", "more energy") instead of a concrete, visual one. [L30 03:58–04:45](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)
- **Reassuring bridge** ("it's actually extremely common") that releases the tension the hook built. [L52 08:08–10:40](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
- **Regret where a real threat exists** (wallet). [L50 11:34–12:15](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)
- **Stakes the visual doesn't explain**: the wallet ad showed a scanner "but doesn't explain what is happening". [L52 slide](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
- **A hook that only bites**: shocking visual, then viewers leave once the tension resolves (four-cats caveat). [L23 06:08–06:59](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
- **Overclaiming to escalate** (health, legal, money claims you can't back). [L30 checklist; 03:13–03:58](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md); [L52 page #2](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
- **Asking AI to "make it better"** instead of "I need to raise the stakes here". [L36 01:25–02:19](../lessons/36-what-about-ai.md)
- **Prompts without the principles:** the story prompts "don't mean anything" without conflict, stakes and the rest. [L44 07:18](../lessons/44-prompts-to-help-you-storytell.md)

## Cross-course notes
- **Evolve (Spencer Pawliw) and Tether Lab agree that flat ads fail.** Tether Lab's [intensity](../../../../concepts/intensity.md): "Sometimes ads underperform not really because they're too emotional, but because they're not emotional enough." Evolve's levers are one concrete way to turn intensity up in copy; Tether's levers (zoom, colour, light, verbs) are the visual counterpart.
- **Tether Lab adds guardrails Evolve states less often.** Tether Lab warns against using Zone 4 (low valence, high intensity) manipulatively ([emotional zones](../../../../concepts/emotional-zones.md)), against high-intensity styling on luxury/high-trust products ([intensity](../../../../concepts/intensity.md)), against shock for Zone 1 buyers ([shock factor](../../../../concepts/heuristics/shock-factor.md)), and against shaming the buyer ([pain-point amplification](../../../../concepts/heuristics/pain-point-amplification.md)). Evolve's own rules partly match ("BE ETHICAL" [L52](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md); "super intense → dial down" [L48](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md); blame the industry, not the buyer [L40 27:56–28:52](../lessons/40-what-makes-a-great-story.md)). Agent default (derived): raise stakes with true consequences; for luxury, wellness-intimacy or trust-led brands prefer the gain-side and permanence levers over fear and shock. The broader "how much drama" disagreement is logged under story-building-blocks in the contradictions notes.
- **Loss framing.** Tether Lab's [loss aversion](../../../../concepts/heuristics/loss-aversion.md) list (money, time, growth, peace of mind...) overlaps Evolve's "make it cost more"; Tether adds: pair the loss with the reason it happens, and don't stack loss + scarcity + countdown + pain in one ad.
