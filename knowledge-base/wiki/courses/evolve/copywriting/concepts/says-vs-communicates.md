# Says vs communicates (subtext over literal words)
> Two lines can state the same fact while one sells and the other fails, because what matters is what the copy **communicates** (the feeling, desire and position it makes the reader infer), not what it literally **says**; tear down winners for what they communicate, then rebuild that communication with new words.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** tearing down a competitor hook or ad; writing variations or iterations of a winner; reviewing a draft that is factually right but flat; checking item 10 of the course checklist ("What is this copy actually communicating?").
- **Inputs → outputs:** a hook/ad → two lines: **SAYS** (literal content) and **COMMUNICATES** (feeling + desire + position + who it is about) → 3-5 variations with new words and the same communication, each re-checked.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** ask of every line "what is this actually communicating?" and write the answer as a short phrase. [L09 00:00–01:29](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md); checklist Q10 [L58](../lessons/58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md)
  - **DO** name the feeling and the desire before copying or iterating: "What is the feeling that it communicates and what is the desire that it's communicating?" [L09 08:49](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
  - **DO** build variations by "executing it with different words to communicate the same thing". [L09 08:49](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
  - **DON'T** copy a winning hook word for word: "find the desire" and write it your way. [L09 08:19–08:49](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
  - **DON'T** rewrite a hook so the facts stay but the feeling changes: "you lose what your original hook was communicating." [L09 08:49–09:37](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
  - **DON'T** turn customer-focused copy into a brag: it "removes the focus from the customer, and puts the focus on Avis". [L09 06:42–07:34](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
  - **IF** a variation underperforms **THEN** check whether it still communicates the original feeling, or something else, or nothing. [L09 11:16](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
  - **DO** prefer lines that let the reader make the claim in their own head (facts, comparisons, shown behaviour) over stated claims: "telling will trigger the logical brain". [L32 00:00–02:35](../lessons/32-how-to-write-ads-that-show-and-dont-tell.md); [L33 01:17–02:45](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
- **See also:** [Three copy checks](three-copy-checks.md) · [Facts over opinions](facts-over-opinions.md) · [Show, don't tell](show-dont-tell.md) · [Comparisons](comparisons.md) · [Iteration playbook](../frameworks/iteration-playbook.md) · [Ad teardowns library](../libraries/ad-teardowns.md)

## Definition
- "It's not about what your copy says… it's about what your copy is actually communicating. And the problem with most of you guys is that your copy actually doesn't communicate anything." [L09 00:00](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
- Communication = "what it makes the customer feel and what it makes the customer think." [L09 07:34–08:19](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
- The instructor calls this "one of the most important modules" for writing iterations and variations and for analysing copy "the right way". [L09 00:00](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)

## Why it matters
- **Direct response has no room to over-explain**, so the subtext must do the work. [L09 01:29](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
- **Facts communicate more than they say.** "He scored 1,600 on the SAT" communicates "smart" without saying it; "what your writing communicates is far more important than what it actually says." [L08 10:05](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md); facts "are usually going to communicate something more" [L10 01:06–01:41](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
- **Implied claims meet less resistance.** "When you simply state facts that imply a claim, you allow the prospect to create their own assumption." [L28 06:14–07:06](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) A metaphor ("a glove for your foot") makes the reader conclude "they must fit really good". [L33 01:17–02:45](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
- **Variations fail when communication drifts.** "A lot of you guys create variations that communicate something different or… communicate nothing at all." [L09 11:16](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
- **Single words can carry the whole subtext.** In the Korean-cream iteration, "some MILF" is "the authority. That's what builds the trust." [L48 17:23–18:22](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)

## How to use it
**Teardown (competitor or own winner)**
1. Write **SAYS**: the literal content in one line.
2. Write **COMMUNICATES**: feeling, desire, position, and whose interest it centres (customer vs brand). The instructor reads the piano ad as "people thought I was a loser… I proved them all wrong… I had the last laugh" with status/recognition as the core desire. [L09 02:19–04:04](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
3. Check: is the communication believable? Is it about the customer? [L09 04:59–07:34](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
4. Log both lines in the teardown table (derived; the [ad teardowns library](../libraries/ad-teardowns.md) format).

**Variation / iteration**
5. Keep the COMMUNICATES line fixed. Write 3-5 new hooks with different words (Avis: "can't afford X" series). [L09 09:37–11:16](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
6. To reach a sub-avatar, extend the same message to the group who feels it (Avis "working like a dog to get to the top? Shake hands with Avis" speaks to underdogs; "they're not even talking about rental cars"). [L09 10:19–11:16](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
7. Re-state what each variant communicates; discard any whose answer changed. (derived from [L09 08:49–11:16])
8. (derived, UGC) Do the same on spoken hooks and on the visual: the creator's first line and first shot should communicate the same feeling.

**Reading practice**
9. Read one piece of copy per session and write what each line communicates; bad copy is useful too ("this copy communicates nothing"). [L03 04:10–04:40](../lessons/03-how-to-guarantee-you-get-better-at-copywriting.md)

## Common mistakes
- **Variations that change the words and lose the feeling** (Avis "number one"). [L09 09:37](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
- **Brag lines** that shift focus to the brand ("we will be the best"). [L09 06:42](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
- **Copying a winner word for word** instead of naming its desire. [L09 08:19](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
- **Corporate empathy** ("Many people struggle with weight management") that communicates distance; the video prompt asks for peer-level validation in customer language instead. [L72 Core Rule 6](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)
- **Generic copywriter words** that communicate "you're selling me" rather than "you're one of us": "You're speaking their dialect. So you're one of them, not selling them." [L34 00:53–02:21](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
