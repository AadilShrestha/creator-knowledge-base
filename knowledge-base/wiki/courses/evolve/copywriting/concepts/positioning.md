# Positioning
> Positioning is the slot your brand holds in the prospect's mind; Spencer's rule is to win by being *different* (not "better"): first in a (sub)category, in contrast to the leader, against a named enemy, by saying no to something competitors can't, and visibly so, then make every ad reinforce that slot.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** before writing angles, hooks, scripts or briefs for our brand (what slot must every ad reinforce?); tearing down a competitor (what position does their ad imply, and is it taken?); auditing a draft for "me too" or "we're better" claims; diagnosing why product-aware ads or breakthroughs fail.
- **Inputs → outputs:** category, competitor list and what the leader stands for, competitor reviews (complaints), our real product differences and mechanism, revenue stage → a position statement (new category / contrast / values / enemy / "no" / visual difference), plus the lines and visual devices ads must use to reinforce it.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** answer "What position do you own?" before writing; if you can't, "that is likely your problem". [L55 00:37-01:33](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
  - **DON'T** claim "better": "you should never, ever say that you're better, because if you were, you'd be number one." Claim a difference and let the viewer ask why. [L55 19:00-19:32](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
  - **DO** make the difference visible: "seeing is believing" (Kind Bar packaging, Halo Top's 280 on the label). [L55 13:42; 19:32-20:26](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
  - **IF** you can be first **THEN** name a new (sub)category ("first prebiotic soda"); **IF** not **THEN** reposition the leader by contrast (who it's not for, what it won't do). [L55 20:26-25:47](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
  - **DO** name a strategic enemy (competitor, approach or belief) and something you say "no" to. [L55 25:47-33:49](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
  - **DON'T** pick a position someone already owns: "Own is the key word." [L56 00:00-00:34](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
  - **DO** check the customer will accept it: "it's not just about what you want. It's also about what the customer is willing to accept." [L56 01:13](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
  - **IF** the brand is small (under about $100K/month) **THEN** go narrower: "it's better to be more narrow than it is to be more broad." [L56 05:41-06:24](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
  - **DON'T** write an ad "a competitor can sign"; ask "What is something that I can say that nobody else can say?" [L08 14:09-15:04](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
  - **DON'T** call the product what it *is* ("like Ozempic but natural", "best massage gun"); describe what it uniquely does ("the first/only X that [mechanism]"). [L72 docx "Categorization = Death"](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)
  - **DO** track competitors "not so you can copy their ads" but to separate yourself. [L55 07:48](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
  - **DON'T** quote the lesson's valuations or sales figures in copy; they are instructor/slide claims (one sales screenshot was AI-generated). [L55 03:42-05:20](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)

## Definition
- "Positioning is how your customers will perceive your brand and your product in their mind", prospects included. [L55 06:50](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
- Thesis credited to Jack Trout and Al Ries: "Great marketing is not a battle of products. It's a battle of perceptions in the mind." [L55 00:00-00:37](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
- "Actionable" version quoted from April Dunford (as transcribed): a position "defines how your product is the best in the world at delivering a differentiated value that a well-defined set of customers care a lot about". [L55 07:48-08:40](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
- It is about differentiation: "Nobody cares about better. They care about why are you different?" [L55 09:31-10:24](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
- Copywriting itself is defined by position: a copywriter "writes something that reinforces a position in the user mind & can influence a behaviour". [L02 02:40; 03:05-03:37](../lessons/02-the-current-state-of-copywriting.md)
- Position vs story (slide "Definitions"): "Positioning = WE do X. Story = WHY we do X. Storytelling = getting that WHY inside the customer's head." [L40 05:50-06:52](../lessons/40-what-makes-a-great-story.md)

## Why it matters
- **Hard work can't fix a bad slot:** "A company stuck with a losing position is not going to benefit much from hard work"; "a lot of brute force is not going to fix a terrible position." [L55 05:20-06:13](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
- **Product-aware is the biggest, most competitive level, and positioning is how you win it:** "You likely struggle with product aware because you have no positioning..." and "that's why positioning is more important than ever." [L21 13:35-14:32; 17:23](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **"Me too" is the default failure:** if every competitor says "we're the best X", find a point of difference (mechanism, position) before writing. [L02 08:57-09:29](../lessons/02-the-current-state-of-copywriting.md)
- **Breakthroughs last longer when the position is real.** The iteration audit asks "What are we doing that's different?"; if you do the same as competitors, "this breakthrough ad might not last that long because I'm just gonna get copied back". [L46 11:51](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md) Without product differentiation, "breakthrough ads will die faster than usual". [L51 14:45-15:28](../lessons/51-how-to-iterate-on-breakthroughs.md)
- **A unique mechanism makes you "the only place"** that has it, which "creates this bias that they have to now buy from you." [L14 00:49-01:41](../lessons/14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md)

## How to use it

### The five moves (L55) and the worksheet (L56)
Full procedure: [Positioning exercise](../frameworks/positioning-exercise.md). Summary:

1. **Map the landscape** (research the prospect's mind): category, competitors, the leader and what it stands for, positions already owned. [L56 02:04](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
2. **Path A: new (sub)category.** "If you cannot be first in a new category, be first in a subcategory." Not so narrow it collapses into an owned slot ("glacier water in a can" still means Liquid Death). [L55 20:26-23:16](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
3. **Path B: the obvious hole (creneau).** Redefine who it's for; find what competitors won't do/say/show; take the inverse position (timeless vs trendy, one size vs inclusive); call out what others won't (Halo Top). [L56 02:35](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md); [L55 33:49-39:31](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
4. **Make it concrete:** define the strategic enemy (competitor / approach / belief), what you'll say no to, and how you'll look visually different ("Different beats better"). [L56 02:35-03:23](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
5. **Fill the four tables:** who you compete against → how you're different; what (features) you compete against → how you're different; product problems from competitor reviews → how you solve them; your differences → the problem each could solve. "Different one solves this problem. Boom, now we're onto something." [L56 03:23-05:41](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
6. **Balance:** "a unique position with an appeal that is not too narrow." [L56 05:41](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
7. **Reinforce it in every ad** with a recurring device (David Cod, Halo Top label); "in modern day marketing, it's very rare to see a brand actually reinforcing position." [L55 15:13-16:46](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)

### Turning the position into ad copy
- **Mechanism as position:** even "we're number two" can be the mechanism (Avis "We try harder"). [L19 03:04-03:45](../lessons/19-being-aware-of-mechanisms.md) Copied products still scale by positioning something as new: a newness mechanism or "we've built this for this new avatar" (instructor claim). [L14 03:37-04:26](../lessons/14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md)
- **Category-of-one hooks:** a genetic-testing kit positioned as "how you're going to die"; "positioning is everything", the brand "made themselves a category of 1". [L23 03:47-04:48](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
- **Label-pointing:** David Protein "just points at the label" (20g protein, 150 calories, zero sugar). [L08 07:23-08:20](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
- **Only-we-can-say-it authority:** Solawave "40+ patents protect what copycats can't replicate" ("really strong positioning... only we can say this"). [L16 10:28-12:06](../lessons/16-why-you-need-to-understand-authority.md)
- **Negative framing:** position by what the product will *not* do relative to other solutions. [L22 03:09-03:42](../lessons/22-how-to-finalize-your-ad-ideas.md)
- **Identity objection** ("This isn't for me"): lean into identity marketing and positioning. [L28 05:15-06:14](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
- **Enemy in the story:** enemies "matter in your positioning ... they matter in your storytelling"; done well, the story "will reinforce your positioning". [L42 00:00-00:50; 04:34](../lessons/42-the-three-enemies-of-storytelling.md)
- **First/only line in the script:** "Metaglow is the first and only collagen with Dyglomera." [L71 23:47](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
- **Audit field:** "Positioning → What are we doing that's different? How have we separated ourselves?" (component 8 of the iteration audit). [L46 11:51](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md) Worked answer for Uproot Clean: "The enemy is surface level washing machine cleaners. Their positioning is they are the first-and-only to create an enzyme based cleaning tablet." [L52 DOCX](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)

### Position statement template (derived)
`We are the [first/only (sub)category | contrast to leader] for [who], against [enemy]; we say no to [thing competitors can't drop]; you can see it in [visual difference].`
Evolve's own fill (slide "Finding Your Position"): new category = high-ticket coaching for a monthly subscription; contrast = everyone promises fast results, "we do it slowly"; values = "building real skills to win long term"; enemy = gurus teaching copy-paste strategies; say no to = copy-paste strategies, hacks, trends. [L55 41:56-44:05](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)

## Common mistakes
- **Not knowing your position at all:** "most people in e-com nowadays, they don't even know what positioning is. I was one of those people." [L55 00:37-01:33](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
- **"We're better than our competitors"** presented as repositioning; it isn't. [L55 23:16](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
- **Advertising "as if competitor positions do not already exist".** [L55 06:50-07:48](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
- **Wanting a slot someone else owns.** [L56 00:00-00:34](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
- **Too narrow a sub-category** (or one already implied by the leader). [L55 22:23-23:16](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md); [L56 05:41](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
- **The "everybody trap":** selling to everyone sells nothing; an enemy is "not about hate" but about acknowledging a choice. [L55 27:18](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
- **Copy-paste of a trending template** ("native/camouflage ad" wave) instead of a position: it "is going to die fast." [L02 10:01](../lessons/02-the-current-state-of-copywriting.md)
- **Filling the worksheet from imagination** (or letting AI fill it): the value is real competitor, review and product facts written down. [L56 03:23; 04:46-05:41](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
- **Skipping the positioning check during iteration** (the Claude demo skipped it "as an advanced item"). [L47 07:22-08:14](../lessons/47-claude-code-10-step-iteration-process.md)

### Course-internal tensions
- **Contrast vs "never compare".** L55 teaches repositioning *by contrast with the leader* (Tylenol vs aspirin, Scope vs Listerine) and naming an enemy [L55 23:16-28:49](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md); the L72 prompt says "Never compare yourself to existing categories or solutions. This is non-negotiable." ([L72 docx](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)). Agent default: never write "like X", "X alternative" or "better than X"; contrast is fine when it states a difference the leader cannot claim (who it's for, what it avoids) or names an enemy *approach*, and the claim itself is "the first/only [mechanism]".
- **"Never say better" vs "demonstrate superiority".** L55: never claim better [L55 19:00-19:32](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md); the L72 prompt's objective: "Demonstrate clear product superiority through NEW/ONLY positioning". Agent default: show superiority only through a specific, provable difference; no "better/best" wording.
- **"Never categorize" vs the final script.** The prompt bans naming what the product is, yet Spencer's polished hold says "first and only collagen" (L71 page notes this tension). Default: naming the category is acceptable only inside a "first/only [category] with [mechanism]" construction.

- **Category: create vs fit (tension).** Evolve says create or narrow into a category you can be first in, look visually different, and "never categorize" the product in copy [L55 20:26-23:16](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md); [L72 docx](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md). Tether Lab's [Category perception](../../../../concepts/category-perception.md) says the brain files every brand into an existing category, and for everyday products familiar category cues (Amazon-style icons on PDP images) can help recognition. Agent default: position (and write ads) as different; use familiar category cues only for functional page elements of everyday products, never for elevated brands (Tether agrees on that exception).
- **Luxury (tension).** The Evolve video-ad prompt's luxury adaptation says "lead with over-delivery positioning; maximize perceived value of bonuses; money-back guarantees and exclusive access" [L72 docx "Product-type adaptations"](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md). Tether Lab's [Luxury positioning](../../../../concepts/luxury-positioning.md) says never bundle or discount for exclusivity buyers and gate access instead. Agent default: for true luxury/exclusivity buyers follow Tether Lab (no bundle/bonus stacks, time- or qualification-based scarcity); use Evolve's over-delivery stack for "high perceived value" mass-premium products. Both agree on exclusivity and status outcomes. Evolve's "say no" cases (Brandy Melville one size) fit Tether's restricted-access logic.
- **Agreement on narrowness:** Evolve: small brands go narrow [L56 05:41-06:24](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md); Tether Lab's category perception: split unrelated categories so "the brain doesn't have to think".
