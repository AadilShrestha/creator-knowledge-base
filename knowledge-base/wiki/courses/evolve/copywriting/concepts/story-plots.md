# Story plots (the nine arcs for ads)
> Evolve teaches nine plots for ads. Seven are Christopher Booker's; Spencer adds In Media Res and The Two Paths, and Two Paths replaces Tragedy. A plot only sets the order of the beats. It is not what makes a story work, so use plots mainly to **iterate a winning angle into new story shapes**.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a winning ad needs new story versions of the same angle and avatar; choosing the plot for a UGC, voiceover or talking-head script; labelling the arc of a competitor's story ad during a teardown.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a proven angle, avatar and proof, plus the story-block check ([Story building blocks](story-building-blocks.md)) → the arc of the current winner, 2+ alternative arcs written as 4-line mini stories and beat outlines, each logged as an iteration.
- **Rules:**
  - **DON'T** treat the plot as the story: "Just by writing a story a specific way is not what actually makes it work." Change, withheld information, cause and effect, tension and stakes come first. [L43 00:00–01:40](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
  - **DO** use arcs to iterate: they are "great for iterations on angles of winning ads" and show "what specific 'plots' might be resonating with your avatar". [L43 15:44–16:29](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
  - **IF** the winner is "problem, new problem, bigger problem, solution" **THEN** test Confusion, In Media Res or Two Paths on the same angle. [L43 15:44](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
  - **IF** buyers are skeptical **THEN** try Voyage & Return ("I tried the extreme path so you don't have to"). [L43 10:17](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
  - **IF** buyers are "slightly unaware" and accept their situation **THEN** try Rebirth. [L43 11:12–11:58](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
  - **IF** the market is sophisticated or heavy with objections **THEN** try Confusion. [L43 11:58–12:50](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
  - **IF** the ad is an explainer, voiceover or talking head meant "to shift direction and not really dramatize pain" **THEN** use The Two Paths. [L43 13:45–15:44](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
  - **DON'T** run Tragedy in DTC: its goal is "fear, awareness, or behavior prevention", not conversion. [L43 12:50–13:45](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
  - **DO** make the hero relatable: "The other person needs to see themselves as the 'same person' as the hero." [L43 04:15–06:09](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
  - **DO** write Overcoming the Monster as a Fichtean curve (escalating problems) when you can: "no downtime which means no boredom". [L43 02:35–04:15](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
  - **DO** test an In Media Res cut of any long winner: cut off the hook, start at the action or mechanism, and "see if the ad still makes sense". [L27 20:13–20:59](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md); [L52 11:25–12:22](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
- **See also:** [Story building blocks](story-building-blocks.md) · [Strategic enemies](strategic-enemies.md) · [Iteration playbook](../frameworks/iteration-playbook.md) · [Iteration outcomes](iteration-outcomes.md) · [Bridges](bridges.md) · [Market awareness](market-awareness.md) · [Templates library](../libraries/templates.md) · [Story frameworks for ads (Tether Lab)](../../../../frameworks/story-frameworks-for-ads.md)

## Definition
A **plot (arc)** is the order of beats a story follows. Booker sorted all plots into seven (*The Seven Basic Plots*). "In typical Spencer fashion" Evolve adds two for ads. [L43 00:00–00:58](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) Spencer calls plots "kind of a controversial thing ... not something that is really needed". They help beginners, but "it comes down to you having excessive knowledge on your category and knowing what actually goes into making a great story". [L43 00:00–00:58](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)

| # | Arc | Beat chain (lesson page) | When to use / Spencer's note | First line of the page's mini story (energy-supplement example) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Overcoming the Monster** (= PAS) | Problem → fight → win. Stronger variant, the **Fichtean curve**: Problem → New problem → Bigger problem → Even bigger problem → Solution to all | The enemy is the focus; the win is "removal of pain, not chasing a dream". "Very typical" and "doesn't work all the time" without the story principles. Creed analogy: problem, fight, lose... then win | "For years, my energy would crash by 2 p.m." | [L43 01:40–04:15](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| 2 | **Rags to Riches** (hero's journey) | Low point → struggle → breakthrough → success | "Becoming a new person". The hero must be relatable (Spider-Man starts with a family death), or at a lower point than the viewer (Humby chemo ad) | "I used to wake up exhausted no matter how much I slept." | [L43 04:15–07:08](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| 3 | **In Media Res** (Evolve addition; credited to "Sarah") | Important moment/action → discovery → reinforcing win | Start in the action. Spencer says an in-media-res ad spent "over a million" (instructor claim) | "It's 3pm and I just crushed my biggest task of the day with no brain fog, no caffeine crash, nothing." | [L43 06:09–08:47](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| 4 | **The Quest** | Goal → obstacles → discovery → win | Hook is "aspirational, not desperate"; the product helps reach a desired state | "I've always wanted steady energy all day — no crashes, no burnout." | [L43 08:47–10:17](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| 5 | **Voyage & Return** | Normal → chaos → realization → better normal | "Very powerful for skeptical buyers"; builds authority through the chaos | "I went all-in trying to fix my energy." | [L43 10:17–11:12](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| 6 | **Rebirth** | Stuck → wake-up → transformation | "No enemy. No grind." A moment of awareness; for slightly unaware buyers | "I thought feeling tired all the time was just part of adulthood." | [L43 11:12–11:58](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| 7 | **Confusion** (aka Comedy) | Misunderstanding → insight → clarity | "I misunderstood the problem ... and now I fixed it." For sophisticated markets and objections | "I kept buying products for energy." | [L43 11:58–12:50](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| 8 | **Tragedy** | Downward spiral, "decline without redemption" | **Not for DTC.** Used in anti-smoking, anti-drunk-driving, environmental and charity ads | — | [L43 12:50–13:45](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| 9 | **The Two Paths** (Evolve's replacement for Tragedy) | Consequences of the current path → a way forward | Explainers, voiceovers, talking heads. "Future pacing". Spencer: "also known as the Pixar framework" | "Most people keep pushing through fatigue the same way every day." | [L43 13:45–15:44](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |

## Why it matters
- **A fast source of iterations.** Changing the arc gives "different ways of saying the same thing", so you "squeeze the most out of the angle and the avatar that I have working". [L43 15:44–16:29](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
- **Each arc carries a belief shift in a different shape.** L39 says the way to shift beliefs is a story in which a character like the viewer goes through the shift [L39 04:50](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md); the arcs give that shift different shapes (derived; the L39 page's derived steps point to these arcs).
- **In Media Res has the strongest spend claims in the course:** a single in-medias-res/hookless ad that "spent over a million dollars" [L27 01:47–02:15](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md); one of their biggest breakthroughs, "spent over two million dollars", which "goes straight in the action"; and one test where "three different cuts. One of them was a breakthrough. The other two completely flopped." [L52 11:25–12:22](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md) (instructor claims; no metrics shown)

## How to use it
### A. Iterate a winner into new arcs (L43 procedure)
1. **Label the winner's arc.** For example, problem → new problem → bigger problem → solution is Fichtean. [L43 15:44](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
2. **Keep the angle, avatar, mechanism and proof fixed.** Change only the arc (one variable).
3. **Pick a new arc** with the decision table below.
4. **Rewrite the beats in the new order.** Re-run the five-block check and the laws ([Story building blocks](story-building-blocks.md)). Re-check the enemy; Rebirth has none by design.
5. **Log it as a script iteration of the same angle** and read results per arc ([Iteration playbook](../frameworks/iteration-playbook.md); [Iteration outcomes](iteration-outcomes.md)).

### B. Arc decision table
| Situation | Try | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Winner is a problem-escalation story | Confusion, In Media Res, Two Paths | [L43 15:44](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| Winner looks like Rags to Riches | Start it as Two Paths | [L43 16:29](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| Skeptical buyers who have "tried everything" | Voyage & Return | [L43 10:17](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| Slightly unaware, resigned buyers | Rebirth | [L43 11:12–11:58](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| Sophisticated market, misconceptions, objections | Confusion | [L43 11:58–12:50](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| Aspirational desire, not escaping pain | Quest | [L43 08:47–10:17](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| Clear enemy or pain to remove | Overcoming the Monster / Fichtean | [L43 01:40–04:15](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| Real customer at an extreme low point (with permission and real proof) | Rags to Riches | [L43 05:14–06:09](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| Explainer, voiceover or talking head; shift direction without dramatising pain | Two Paths | [L43 13:45–15:44](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |
| Long winner with several strong middle moments | In Media Res cut | [L52 11:25–12:22](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md) |
| Prevention or awareness goal, not conversion | Tragedy, but outside DTC only | [L43 12:50–13:45](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md) |

### C. In Media Res, the arc the course uses most
- **Edit test:** write hook, bridge and ad; find where you "get to the point"; cut there and start. [L43 07:08–08:47](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
- **Pro-tip:** take the three hooks of a 3-2-2 test, cut them off, play the ad from the bridge and test it as a variation ("4-2-2"). [L27 20:13–20:59](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
- **Claude Code iteration list (#3 "In media res"):** "Start the ad with the unique mechanism (show don't tell). Get straight to the point in the first 5 seconds of the ad, start with the ideal outcome, then start explaining how we got there." Priority: Medium. [L47](../lessons/47-claude-code-10-step-iteration-process.md) (DOCX table)
- **Hookless examples:** Ryze doctor ("Drink alcohol..."), The Groovd ("The unique groove design of these books"), Based Supplies ("Yes, we make skincare from beef fat. Everyone thought we were crazy until their kid's eczema finally healed."). [L27 20:59–22:25](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
- **Used in the course's own script:** Bridge 2 of the MetaGlow ad re-uses the hook-1 line and bridge as an in-media-res variant. [L71 07:13–08:10](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)

## Common mistakes
- Thinking "problem, fight, win" *is* storytelling. It is only the order the words follow. [L43 00:58–01:40](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
- Using an arc with an unrelatable hero. [L43 04:15–06:09](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
- Shipping Tragedy (decline with no redemption) as a DTC conversion ad. [L43 12:50–13:45](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
- Forgetting conflict, enemies and the laws when switching arcs; the arcs are "an extra piece of information". [L43 16:29](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md)
- Opening an In Media Res cut on a moment that lacks context. In the Ryze example "I don't have as much context until I see the mushroom come in." [L27 22:25–22:51](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)
- Mixing up the three "Pixar" meanings across courses (see below).

## Cross-course notes
Compared with **Tether Lab's** [Story frameworks for ads](../../../../frameworks/story-frameworks-for-ads.md) (Sarah's "Storytelling Loadout"):
- **Shared:** both list Booker's Seven Basic Plots, In Medias Res, the Fichtean Curve and PAS. Evolve credits "Sarah" for In Media Res, plausibly Tether Lab's Sarah Levinger (not confirmed on screen) [L43 06:09](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md); [L52 11:25](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md).
- **Role of the framework:** Tether Lab hands ChatGPT a named framework *as the main lever* ("write me a UGC script" without one gives flat scripts) and picks it by funnel job. **Evolve (Spencer Pawliw)** treats plots as optional, applied *after* the story blocks and mainly for iterating winners. Default: pick a structure from either course, then audit with the Evolve blocks.
- **"Pixar" naming mismatch:** Tether Lab's **Pixar framework** is the six-sentence beat chain ("Once upon a time... Every day... One day... Because of that... Because of that... Until finally..."), Sarah's #1. Evolve calls **The Two Paths** (consequences of the current path → a better way; future pacing) "also known as the Pixar framework" [L43 13:45–15:44](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md). Evolve also uses "Pixar" for an **AI animation style** [L53 08:03](../lessons/53-the-iteration-playbook-formats-styles.md); [L54 07:18–08:49](../lessons/54-iteration-examples-breakdowns-walkthrough.md). **Agent default:** in briefs, never write "Pixar" alone. Write "Pixar six-sentence framework (Tether Lab)", "Two Paths / future pacing (Evolve)" or "Pixar-style AI animation (format)". Logged in the contradictions notes.
- **Fichtean curve defined differently:** Evolve's version is one narrator's *compounding problems* ("Problem → New Problem → Bigger problem → Even Bigger problem → Solution to all") [L43 01:40–04:15](../lessons/43-the-storytelling-arcs-plots.md). Tether Lab's is *multiple crises or characters*, each resolved quickly ("Game of Thrones"). Name which one you mean in the brief. Logged.
- **PAS:** Evolve equates PAS with Overcoming the Monster, "very typical", and prefers the Fichtean escalation. Sarah says she steers away from PAS "unless I'm really trying to sell hard at the bottom of the funnel". These are compatible: use PAS/Fichtean for direct-response pushes, not as the default for every ad.
- **In Medias Res funnel placement:** Tether Lab files it under Marketing & Sales (retargeting, evaluation stage, fake podcasts). Evolve uses it as a cold-traffic cut of a winner and attributes its $1M and $2M ads to it. A difference in emphasis, not a contradiction: test it at both stages.
- **Tragedy:** Evolve bans it for DTC. On the Tether Lab page, the UGC skeleton for Tragedy ("What happens if you don't, use sparingly and end on hope") is wiki-derived, not Sarah's instruction. Evolve's Two Paths is the safe version of the same "what happens if you don't" move.
- **Seven Basic Plots:** Sarah says "I need to test it more" (untested). Spencer uses them as an iteration tool. Neither course gives performance data per plot.
