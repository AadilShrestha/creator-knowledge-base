# Comparisons: metaphors, similes and analogies
> Compare the product, mechanism or problem to something the customer already knows, so their own brain supplies the context and credibility: a metaphor for a softer, less claim-like line, a simile for clarity, an analogy ("X is like Y") for severity or to reframe why old solutions fail.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a claim is abstract, technical or easy to doubt; explaining a mechanism or ingredient in one beat; reframing why the customer's current solution fails; writing a bridge or static headline that must land instantly; tearing down classic or competitor headlines.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a plain claim/mechanism ("fits well", "this enzyme does X", "pee pads don't work") + the avatar's familiar world → a metaphor, a simile and an analogy variant, one picked, each with the right feeling (positive for the product, extreme for the problem) and, where possible, a literal visual.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** use a comparison to "allow the customer to get context from their own brain and from their own experiences". [L33 00:00–01:17](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
  - **DO** use comparisons for three jobs: give the brain context, make complex science simple ("this little enzyme is like..."), reframe why old solutions failed. [L33 page text; 12:00–13:29](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
  - **IF** you want less skepticism **THEN** use a metaphor ("A glove for your foot"): the reader makes the claim in their own mind. **IF** you want clarity **THEN** use a simile ("Fits your foot like a glove"), accepting it "becomes more of a claim". [L33 01:17–03:27](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
  - **DON'T** write the poet's analogy ("A is to B as C is to D"): "Nobody would ever say this." Use the practical form "X is like Y". [L33 03:27–04:15](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
  - **DO** pick the compared thing for its feeling: a million dollars is great; flying a plane without a license is severe. "We want to create a good association." [L33 04:15–07:15](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
  - **DO** escalate the comparison when attacking the old way ("using traditional pee pads, that's like walking in the Amazon barefoot"). [L33 12:44–13:29](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
  - **DO** make the customer feel smart: "You don't want to talk to your customer like they're dumb." [L33 00:41–01:17](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
  - **DO** pair the line with a literal visual when you can (Heinz tomato bottle; Goli "YOUR GUT" glass). [L33 09:26–10:18](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md); [L31 04:45–05:18](../lessons/31-how-to-create-ads-that-show-dont-tell.md)
  - **DON'T** let a metaphor become a literal health or performance claim you can't back: Red Bull "had to add disclaimers" after "wings". [L33 05:36–06:29](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md); [L30 03:13–03:58](../lessons/30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)
  - **IF** you research comparison ads **THEN** study big brands' older ads from when they were "on the come up", not today's ads that "just reinforce position". [L33 07:15–08:06](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
- **See also:** [show-dont-tell](show-dont-tell.md) · [unique-mechanism](unique-mechanism.md) · [says-vs-communicates](says-vs-communicates.md) · [raising-the-stakes](raising-the-stakes.md) · [objection-handling](objection-handling.md) · [positioning](positioning.md) · [templates library](../libraries/templates.md) · [ad-teardowns library](../libraries/ad-teardowns.md) · Tether Lab: [reframing](../../../../concepts/heuristics/reframing.md), [processing fluency](../../../../concepts/heuristics/processing-fluency.md), [anthropomorphism](../../../../concepts/heuristics/anthropomorphism.md)

## Definition
The instructor's working definitions (he says he uses them "the logic way", not as a poet) [L33 00:00; 01:17–04:15](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md):

| Form | Definition | Lesson example | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metaphor | Symbolic comparison: one thing *is* another | "A glove for your foot" (shoe ad, credit Sarah Levinger); "Red Bull gives you wings"; "Test drive a Macintosh" | "A little bit more brainpower"; the reader makes the claim; less skepticism |
| Simile | Direct comparison with "like" or "as" | "Fits your foot like a glove"; "Tastes like a million dollars"; "It can sing like Caruso..." | Clearer, but reads more like a claim |
| Analogy | Metaphor + simile combined into one statement | "This shoe fits your foot like a glove fits your hand"; "...like trying to fly a plane without a license"; "like hospitals without doctors" | Borrows severity or authority |

Summary line (page text and spoken): "The metaphor version requires an extra mental step, but all versions allow our brain to compare to something it knows and is familiar with to create an association with your product." [L33 05:36](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)

## Why it matters
- "Some of the best marketing that has ever been done has come from leveraging metaphors and... similes and analogies." They "borrow credibility in a weird way just by comparing it to something", and "very complex things sound simple". [L33 00:00–00:41](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
- **What the copy communicates beats what it says:** "A glove for your foot" says nothing about fit but communicates "they must fit really good". [L33 01:17–02:45](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md) (See [says-vs-communicates](says-vs-communicates.md).)
- **Severity transfers:** "just like how you need a license to fly a plane, you need copywriting fundamentals to make winning ads". [L33 04:15–05:36](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
- **Visual stakes in bridges:** the winning dandruff bridge "it's feeding on your scalp like a buffet" is "so visual" and "higher stakes" than "it's actually extremely common". [L52 08:08–10:40](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
- **Eugene Schwartz lists it as a headline move:** "4. Metaphorize the claim" ("BANISHES CORNS!", "MELTS AWAY UGLY FAT!"), alongside sensitize, demonstrate, dramatize and symbolize. [L64 page](../lessons/64-eugenes-headlines.md)

## How to use it (steps)
1. **Pick the claim to land**: fit, energy, quality, importance, difficulty, or why the old way fails. [L33 steps](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
2. **Find a familiar thing whose known feeling matches** (glove = comfort, wings = energy, a million dollars = great value, a hospital without doctors = disaster, a test drive = premium purchase). Use the avatar's world, not yours.
3. **Choose the form** with the decision table below.
4. **For problems or old solutions, escalate**; for the product, keep the association positive. [L33 12:44–13:29](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
5. **Add a literal visual** where possible. [L33 09:26–10:18](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
6. **Check what it communicates** and whether it implies a claim you can't substantiate. [L33 01:51; 05:36–06:29](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)

## Common mistakes
- **Poetic analogies** nobody would say out loud. [L33 03:27–04:15](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
- **Wrong valence**: comparing your product to something with a bad or neutral feeling. [L33 05:36](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
- **Talking down** to the customer instead of letting them connect the dots. [L33 00:41–01:17](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
- **Unfamiliar comparison points** for the avatar (derived from "context from their own brain"). [L33 00:00–01:17](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
- **Metaphors that become literal claims** (Red Bull disclaimers). [L33 05:36–06:29](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
- **Studying today's big-brand ads** that only maintain position. [L33 07:15–08:06](../lessons/33-how-to-use-metaphors-similes-analogies.md)
- **Copying another brand's visual metaphor 1:1** (Goli "has already kind of got this"). [L31 04:45–06:10](../lessons/31-how-to-create-ads-that-show-dont-tell.md)

## Cross-course notes
- **Agreement (Tether Lab).** Tether Lab's [static ad creation playbook](../../../../playbooks/static-ad-creation.md) says: "Metaphor? Give it a literal visual (light switch set to ON)", matching Evolve's Heinz and Goli examples. Tether's [reframing](../../../../concepts/heuristics/reframing.md) names "visual metaphor carousel" as a format and "What if your problem isn't what you think it is?" as the move, which is Evolve's "reframe why old solutions failed" use. Tether's [anthropomorphism](../../../../concepts/heuristics/anthropomorphism.md) is the personification cousin of the metaphor (see Ryze in [show-dont-tell](show-dont-tell.md)).
- **Fluency (Tether Lab).** Tether's [processing fluency](../../../../concepts/heuristics/processing-fluency.md) ("That ease becomes trustworthy to the brain") explains why Evolve's simile is clearer but more claim-like and the metaphor costs "a little bit more brainpower": choose the metaphor when skepticism is the bottleneck, the simile when comprehension is. (derived)
- **Comparative claims vs positioning.** Evolve's Citroen example borrows authority from rivals with a joke; Evolve's positioning lesson says "never, ever say that you're better" (see [positioning](positioning.md) and its contradictions entry). Agent default (derived): use comparisons to famous *categories or experiences*, not direct "better than [competitor]" claims, unless they can be substantiated.
