# The three copy checks (visual, falsifiable, nobody else can say it)
> Run every hook, headline and claim through Harry Dry's three questions, as taught by Spencer Pawliw: **Can I see it? Can I say it's true or false? Am I the only one who can say it?** Three yeses usually means strong copy; three noes usually means rewrite from scratch.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** auditing any hook, headline, static, script line or competitor ad; rewriting copy that is vague, generic or swappable with a competitor's; deciding which competitor line is worth remixing (copy the mechanism that earns the pass, not the words).
- **Inputs → outputs:** a line (or ad) + product facts list + the top competitor's name → a yes/no per check with the failing words quoted → a rewrite that is visual, checkable and ownable.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** score each line on all three checks and quote the words that fail. "If you have three no's, you probably wrote something that is not that great… if you have three yeses, you're probably scaling" (instructor claim). [L08 00:41–01:29](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
  - **DO** treat the checks as pattern recognition, not law: "not every successful ad follows this, but a lot of them do." [L08 00:00–00:41](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
  - **DO** replace abstract words with pictures and let the reader draw the abstract conclusion: "Don't tell people it's the softest. Tell them that it feels like cashmere." [L08 04:01](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
  - **DO** make the claim checkable: a count, an ingredient, a test, a time, a first. "He scored 1,600 on the SAT", not "He's intelligent". [L08 08:20–10:05](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
  - **DON'T** write an ad a competitor could sign: "Never write an ad that a competitor can sign." [L08 13:32](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
  - **IF** you can paste the biggest competitor's product name into your ad and it still makes sense **THEN** it fails check 3; rewrite around a claim, mechanism, name or history only you have. [L08 11:22–13:21](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
  - **IF** you are stuck on check 2 **THEN** run the "point at it" exercise: if you could only sell the product by pointing, what would you point at? [L08 05:34–07:23](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
  - **DO** run the Google exact-match test on the headline (search it "in quotes") as an extra uniqueness check. [L58 Q9](../lessons/58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md)
  - **DON'T** fill a failed check with an invented fact. Only true, verifiable facts pass check 2; a made-up fact "re-sophisticates the market". [L28 08:28–09:03](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)

## Definition
Three yes/no questions the instructor takes from Harry Dry ("all from Harry Dry, I believe") [L08 00:00](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md). The recap wording [L08 16:51](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md):

| # | Check | Question | Passes when | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Visual | "Can I see it?" | A concrete object, scene, person or comparison appears in the reader's mind | Abstract nouns/adjectives: "best practice", "value proposition", "softest" |
| 2 | Falsifiable | "Can I say that it's true or false?" | A number, ingredient, test, time or event someone could check | "simple", "smart", "high quality", "unmatched" |
| 3 | Nobody else | "Can I be the only one who says it?" | Swapping in a competitor's name makes it nonsense | The swap still reads fine |

The same three questions are items 7, 8 and 9 of the course's 13-question checklist [L01 03:00](../lessons/01-overview-of-copywriting-program.md), [L58](../lessons/58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md).

## Why it matters
- **Visual copy is remembered and understood.** Harry's memory test (optimized process, dumpster fire, dirty dog, value proposition, best practice, wet newspaper): you remember the visual ones. If a line creates no picture, "the user won't clearly understand your message because they can't see it." [L08 01:29–03:06](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
- **Falsifiable copy communicates more than it says.** "Just saying he scored 1600 in the SAT doesn't say 'oh, he's smart', but it communicates that." [L08 10:05](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md) Facts also kill objections because they are not arguable claims ("Can they prove you wrong?"). [L28 06:14–07:58](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
- **Ownable copy creates exclusivity.** If a consumer feels "you're the brand that does that thing", they "have to buy from you". Check 3 "forces you to look deeper at what you're actually selling" and is the instructor's prompt for breakthroughs: "What is something that I can say that nobody else can say?" [L08 14:09–15:04](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
- **Sameness gets filtered.** The lizard brain categorises and ignores messages that are "the same as everything else" [L14 01:41–03:37](../lessons/14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md); "me too" copy that says "we're the best X" is the trap the instructor warns about in [L02 08:57–09:29](../lessons/02-the-current-state-of-copywriting.md).

## How to use it
1. Write the line. List every claim word in it.
2. **Check 1 (visual).** Circle abstract words. For each, ask "what would a camera show?" Replace with a familiar object, scene or comparison ("feels like cashmere and it's priced like cotton"). For a full pass, run the Visual Words prompt over the draft [L59](../lessons/59-visual-words.md).
3. **Check 2 (falsifiable).** For each claim ask "could a reader say true or false?" If not, swap in the fact behind it: ingredient count, a first, a test result, a time, a physical behaviour. Pull it from the [facts list](../lessons/60-facts-list.md). If you can only make it falsifiable with a number you don't have, drop the claim.
4. **Check 3 (nobody else).** Take your last three ads (or this draft), open the biggest competitor, swap their product name in. If it "still makes sense", it fails. Rewrite around your unique mechanism, name, history or positioning. [L08 10:59–12:50](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md) Then search the headline in quotes on Google [L58 Q9](../lessons/58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md).
5. Score: 3/3 ship candidate; 2/3 fix the failing check; 0-1/3 rewrite from the fact or mechanism up.
6. (derived, UGC) Apply check 1 to the visual plan too: the line the creator says should point at something the camera can show ([show, don't tell](show-dont-tell.md)).

## Common mistakes
- **Abstract superlatives as the headline** ("softest", "unmatched power and performance", "revolutionary"). [L08 03:06](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md); [L10 02:16](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
- **Thinking falsifiable means a statistic.** Facts can be firsts, counts, physical behaviour ("melts in your mouth, not in your hand"). See [facts over opinions](facts-over-opinions.md).
- **Passing check 2 with a fabricated number.** Never; see the compliance rules in [L72](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md) ("Compliance Check - Utmost Important").
- **Skipping check 3 because it is "the hardest one"**; the instructor admits you never fully achieve it ("people will copy you no matter what") but being first lets you own the line. [L08 12:50–14:09](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
- **Copying a competitor's winning line word for word.** It fails check 3 by definition; copy what earns its pass (the visual device, the self-made test), not the words (derived; consistent with [L57 00:00](../lessons/57-100-hook-templates-form-winning-ads-deck.md)).
