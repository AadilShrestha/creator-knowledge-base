# Open loops (juxtaposition, ethical fear, do the opposite)
> An open loop is an information gap the viewer wants to close: you withhold just enough to create an itch in the first 3 seconds and don't scratch it until as late as possible; build it with juxtaposition (hot + cold), ethical fear (loss aversion) or doing the opposite of the category (pattern interrupt), and always pay it off honestly before you pitch.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing hooks and opening beats; a hook "just says the product is good"; planning where the payoff lands; adding forward pull between lines of a script; diagnosing ads that intrigue but don't sell.
- **Inputs → outputs:** claim + the category's norms + competitors' positioning + true dangers/facts → hook candidates per loop type (juxtaposition / fear / opposite), each with its payoff line and the second where it closes, plus a check that every loop opened is closed before the CTA.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** open the loop in the first 3 seconds and "do NOT scratch it/close that loop until the final 3 seconds" (or as late as you can). [L26 00:27–01:35; page](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
  - **DON'T** close it in the next line; extend it with suspense and context "in a way that isn't boring". [L26 01:06–01:35](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
  - **DO** remember the goal: "Our goal is not watch time. Our goal is sales." Each line's job is to get the next one read (Schwartz). [L26 01:47–02:22](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
  - **DO** build juxtaposition from a hot and a cold: "two things together that should not go together"; "Great Copywriting is just a search for juxtaposition" (Schwartz, per the page). [L26 02:22–03:33](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
  - **DON'T** write one-sided superlatives ("The strongest coffee": "There is no HOT & COLD… Everybody says this"). [L26 08:11–08:45](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
  - **DO** use ethical fear ("Watch out for X", "Red flags your X", "What if everything you knew about X is wrong?"), and **DON'T** fake danger: "DON'T. BE. A. PIECE. OF. SHIT."; scaring people for no reason can bring "very serious legal charges". [L26 09:15–12:48](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
  - **IF** everyone in the category positions the same way **THEN** do the opposite; list what competitors say, do and target, then invert. [L26 12:48–15:10](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
  - **DO** connect the clash: if an image/clip clashes with the line, the ad must explain the connection (suitcase water, goat on the rug). [L26 05:08–07:29](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
  - **DO** fill every loop before pitching: "you have delivered your payoff… If a curiosity gap was opened, it is filled." [L29 00:00–01:44](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md) Failing to close loops is a listed reason spend winners don't convert. [L50 04:46](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)
  - **DO** aim for the middle of the curiosity curve: tell about 80%, leave 20%; don't leave so much unresolved that people give up. [L40 14:00–15:39](../lessons/40-what-makes-a-great-story.md)
- **See also:** [Hooks](hooks.md) · [Bridges](bridges.md) · [Raising the stakes](raising-the-stakes.md) · [Story building blocks](story-building-blocks.md) · [Unaware ads](unaware-ads.md) · [Hooks library](../libraries/hooks.md)

## Definition
- "An open loop is an information gap that the reader or watcher wants to fill in their heads. You create this open task in their mind and they want to close that task." You withhold "to create intrigue". [L26 00:00–00:27](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
- Image (credit Adley): "create an itch that you don't scratch". [L26 00:27–01:06](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
- As a hook choice, an open loop "is more focused on WITHHOLDING information… so they have to keep watching", versus a promise that gives "all of the information they need". [L25 page; 01:47–03:20](../lessons/25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md)
- The course calls open loops "the most powerful one" of the strategies (overview). [L26 summary](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md); juxtaposition is "the number one way". [L26 02:22](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)

## Why it matters
- **Unfamiliar pairings force attention**: "we have to pay attention to things that are unfamiliar… a goat doesn't belong in the house." "Juxtapositions create space. That space is what the reader or watcher wants to continue to watch for." [L26 07:29–09:15](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
- **Danger triggers the subconscious**: like a loud bang, "potential danger ahead" makes people stop; "We're more motivated by fear than we are by outcomes" (instructor claim). [L26 10:07–12:48](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
- **Sameness gets ignored**: "Everybody is always copying each other"; a multi-nine-figure owner told him "I just look at what everybody's doing in Evolve and I just do the opposite" (instructor anecdote). [L26 14:41–15:57](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
- **Loops carry information**: open loops are "a phenomenal way to… give a little bit of information while withholding some", so viewers watch long enough to have "enough information to actually purchase". [L26 16:26–17:08](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)

## The three loop types
| Type | Slide alias | Mechanism | Patterns | Risk | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Juxtaposition | "Curiosity gap" | Two things that shouldn't go together; Point A → Point Z | "How a homeless person became a millionaire"; "I quit the gym and lost 20 lbs"; "The natural deodorant that performs like a toxic one"; "Remember everything, organize nothing"; "14+ hours, no swelling"; "Live with a death wish" | Superlatives with no contrast are flat | [L26 02:22–09:15](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md) |
| Through fear, ethically | "Loss aversion" | Subconscious danger check | "Watch out for these ingredients when buying shampoo"; "3 red flags of vitamin deficiency in kids"; "What if everything you knew about dandruff is wrong?"; "Watch how X stops negative thing" | Legal and trust risk if exaggerated | [L26 09:15–12:48](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md) |
| Do the opposite | "Pattern interrupt" | Break the category convention | Old Spice targeting women ("the man your man could smell like"); Nexgen CEO "Let me tell you why my product STINKS" | Must still be true to the brand | [L26 12:48–15:57](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md) |

**Juxtaposition formats:** image + copy (sapling photo + "My husband was on a waiting list to receive a heart"), clip + copy (water poured into a suitcase + "This is how I'm keeping my valuables SAFE"), imagery alone (goat on a hallway rug + "Washable rugs. Made for the whole family to enjoy"). [L26 05:08–07:29](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)

## How to use it
1. **Write the claim and its "cold"** (natural vs toxic; quit gym vs lose weight; 14 hours vs no swelling; hard thing vs easy, as in "Eating 1,200 calories is easy": "two things that don't go together… now you're curious"). [L26 02:47–05:08](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md); [L67 05:01](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md)
2. **Pick the loop type**; write one of each per angle and test them against each other. [L26 how to apply](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
3. **Decide the visual**: does an image/clip clash with the line, or carry the stakes? [L26 05:08–07:29](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md); [L23 05:39–06:59](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
4. **Plant and pay off**: Adley's pattern: destroy or twist something people associate with the category, "do something intentional like setting this aside to anchor something that will make sense later as a retention tactic", then reveal. [L23 11:07–11:34](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
5. **Keep the pull going between lines** (the prompt's "slippery slope"): end lines with intrigue ("Regular sheets trap heat. But that's not the real problem..."), create "but wait" moments, use pattern interrupts ("It's actually the opposite..."). [L72 Core Rule 4](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)
6. **Mark the payoff second** in the script (open by 0:03, close late), and close loops before the pitch. [L26 00:55–01:35](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md); [L29 00:00–01:44](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
7. **Close the big loop in the CTA** by echoing the hook ("Your body has been fighting you. Metaglow helps you fight back."). [L71 final PDF](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md); prompt Section D "loop closure to the hook" [L70 docx](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md)
8. **Fear check**: is the danger real, and does the video help the viewer avoid it? [L26 09:15–09:42](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)

## Common mistakes
- **Closing the loop immediately.** [L26 01:06](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
- **Confusion instead of curiosity** (too much withheld, chaos): "Curious NOT confused." [L23 09:23–10:04](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md); [L40 14:00](../lessons/40-what-makes-a-great-story.md)
- **A clash never explained** (goat with no rug payoff). [L26 05:53–07:29](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md)
- **Bait loops** that the product can't pay off; breaking the promise loses trust. [L25 04:17](../lessons/25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md)
- **Leaving loops open at the pitch.** [L50 04:46](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)
- **Dramatised conspiracy loops** ("something the pet industry doesn't want you to know") without a real secret; the instructor notes the tick ad is dramatised. [L27 17:53–18:22](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md)

## Cross-course notes
- **Agreement (Tether Lab):** [Open loop](../../../../concepts/heuristics/open-loop.md) ("make sure the loop *can* be closed honestly… in the last 3 seconds"; "DON'T bait") and [curiosity gap](../../../../concepts/heuristics/curiosity-gap.md) ("*almost* closable") match Evolve's rules; Tether's [suspense](../../../../concepts/heuristics/suspense.md) and [pattern disruption](../../../../concepts/heuristics/pattern-disruption.md) cover the same ground as Evolve's loop types.
- **Disagreement (how many loops):** Tether: "DON'T open more than one loop in a short ad. One question, one payoff." ([open loop](../../../../concepts/heuristics/open-loop.md)); Evolve's video prompt: "Every sentence should create an open loop that pulls them to the next sentence" (slippery slope) [L72 Core Rule 4](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md). Agent default: one *main* loop from hook to payoff; line-to-line micro-curiosity ("But that's not the real problem…") is fine if each one closes within a line or two and never competes with the main question. Logged in the contradictions notes.
- **Agreement on ethics:** Tether's [hidden truth](../../../../concepts/heuristics/hidden-truth.md) ("DON'T fabricate or exaggerate a 'they don't want you to know' claim") matches Evolve's "don't be a piece of shit" rule for fear loops.
