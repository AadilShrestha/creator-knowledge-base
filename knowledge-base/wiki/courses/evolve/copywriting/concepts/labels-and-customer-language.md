# Labels and customer language
> Write in the customer's dialect, not "copywriter language": call the audience out with a label they use for themselves (or one you coin and get adopted), and swap polished generic words for the rough, specific words customers actually use. It earns trust ("you're one of them") and attention ("their brain lights up").

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing hooks and bridges for a defined avatar; proofing any draft (especially AI output) for generic phrases; building a customer-language bank from reviews, Reddit, comments and surveys; naming an avatar or mechanism; tearing down a competitor's call-out.
- **Inputs → outputs:** avatar/niche + raw customer research (reviews, Reddit threads, comments, survey answers) → (a) a list of self-identifying labels, found and coined, with where each appears; (b) a customer-language bank (generic copywriter phrase beside the customer's phrase); (c) a rewritten hook/bridge/hold using them.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** use customer quotes, not a thesaurus: "You shouldn't use a thesaurus to sound smarter. You should use customer quotes to sound more like them." [L34 00:00–00:53](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
  - **DO** apply the door-to-door test: you wouldn't say "the revolutionary all-in-one", you'd say "plug this thing in and put it at the sky and it'll give you internet". [L34 00:00–00:53](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
  - **DO** call out the avatar with a label, ideally in the hook; labels also work inside the copy. [L34 02:21; 06:51–07:36](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
  - **DO** check a label is a term people use "about themselves, not a term outsiders use to describe them". [L34 Prompt For Labels](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
  - **IF** you can coin a label from your mechanism (Creamunchy, Labubu) **THEN** test it, knowing it "requires adoption, but when done correctly, it can crush". [L34 04:11–05:07](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
  - **DO** run a word-swap pass after drafting: find "the generic and polished copywriter words" and replace them with "rough, specific language and customer quotes". [L34 05:07–06:51](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
  - **DO** keep a running customer-words bank per avatar and pull from it when stuck. [L34 06:51](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
  - **DO** jot down any term you don't recognise during research: "If you don't recognize something, probably means you should jot it down." [L34 08:08–08:57](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
  - **DO** research while you edit: replace generic lines with specific ones from the research doc and Reddit ("kitchen pantry", "something crunchy", "feels like torture"). [L71 06:18–06:50; 17:32; 19:33–22:13](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
  - **DO** ask what word customers actually say for a technical thing ("I'm full hormone" doesn't exist; people say "fullness hormone"; keep "GLP-1" for clinical credibility). [L71 21:28–22:13](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
  - **DON'T** fear repelling people: labels "will definitely repel a lot of people but you'll also attract that specific group". [L34 07:36–08:08](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
  - **IF** a label stops the scroll **THEN** write a strong bridge right after it to carry them into the ad. [L34 07:36–08:08](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
  - **IF** a label's group is too small (the "TAM issue") **THEN** widen it ("men considering TRT", not "men on TRT not happy"), or run the niche angle in its own campaign. [L48 06:43–08:31](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)
  - **DON'T** call out the avatar every time: "You don't always need to go all in on your avatar all the time." Broad hooks are the scalable alternative. [L24 03:28–03:51](../lessons/24-the-2-types-of-hooks.md)

## Definition
Two tools from one principle ("One of the biggest mistakes that I feel like copywriters make is writing in copywriter language instead of customer language") [L34 00:00](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md):
1. **Labels:** "the names that prospects had given themselves or basically the names that they identify as"; ask "what are the terms that these people own?" Either **found** (SAHM, ecom bro, night owl, grinder, wasband) or **created** (Creamunchy peanut butter, credited to Sarah Levinger; Labubu and the knock-off "Lafufu"). [L34 02:21–05:07](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
2. **Word swapping (customer language):** after writing, replace polished copywriter words with the customer's own rough, specific words. [L34 05:07–06:51](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)

## Why it matters
- **Trust:** "You're speaking their dialect. So you're one of them, not selling them." [L34 00:53–02:21](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
- **Attention:** "Their brain lights up when they see familiar language" ("those are my people"). [L34 00:53–01:38](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
- **Why labels work:** identity/ego, community ("these are my people"), and the brain likes labels because they make communication easy. [L34 02:21–03:17](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
- **Conversion:** "When words feel native and not salesy, they're just going to convert better." [L34 06:00–06:51](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
- **AI drafts need it most:** Claude gets you "70, 80% of the way there"; the last 20–30% is customer language and research. [L70 24:58](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md) The video prompt's bridge spec asks for "exact customer language". [L72 docx Section B](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)

## How to use it (steps)
1. **Define the avatar and mechanism** (see [avatars-and-angles](avatars-and-angles.md), [unique-mechanism](unique-mechanism.md)).
2. **Collect language:** run "Prompt For Labels" (Urban Dictionary; prioritise high-upvote terms) and "Prompt For Internet" (Reddit, forums, reviews, comments → labels + a Customer Language Bank with generic vs real versions, sources cited). Both prompts are verbatim on [lesson 34](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md) and in the [prompts library](../libraries/prompts.md). You can also paste Facebook comments into Claude and ask it to identify customer language or labels. [L34 08:08–09:25](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
3. **Add manual notes** from your own research and avatar building; jot down unfamiliar terms. [L34 08:08–08:57](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md) Keep them in the "Customer Language Snippets" field of the research template. [L67 template](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md)
4. **Choose the call-out mode** with the decision table below.
5. **Draft, then word-swap**: list generic phrases, replace each from the bank. Optionally ask Claude, with your brief, for "the generic copywriter words" and replacements that "sound more like our customer" (spoken suggestion, no written prompt). [L34 06:00–06:51](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
6. **Fact-check the words**: customer wording must not smuggle in claims you can't support (Spencer dropped "rare" from "rare African fruit"). [L71 21:28](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
7. **Bridge after the label**, then save new phrases to the bank for the next brief. [L34 07:36–08:08](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)

| Want scale beyond the avatar; strong copy skills | Broad hook, then funnel down (identity still present: "if you have pets") | "If you have pets, your washer is growing something inside of it." | [L24 02:26–07:50](../lessons/24-the-2-types-of-hooks.md); [L35 15:56–16:38; 19:32–20:38](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md) |
| Unaware ad | Identity call-out, product-free | "If you have a cat older than 7, you'll want to hear this." | [L35 14:59–15:56](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md) |
| Label group is tiny (losing ad) | Widen the label's population | "I was 4 months into TRT" → "I almost started testosterone injections until I found this." | [L48 06:43–08:31](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md) |

## Common mistakes
- **Thesaurus copy** ("revolutionary all-in-one"). [L34 00:00–00:53](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
- **Outsider labels** the audience wouldn't use about themselves. [L34 Prompt For Labels](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
- **A label with no bridge**: the call-out stops them, nothing keeps them. [L34 07:36–08:08](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
- **A label so specific the market is tiny** (TAM issue #2). [L48 06:43–08:31](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)
- **Shipping AI's generic words** without a research pass. [L71 06:18–06:50](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md); [L36](../lessons/36-what-about-ai.md)
- **Trusting AI for terminology**: it called DPP4 a hormone. [L71 03:40](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
- **Coining a label once** and expecting adoption; it needs repetition across ads (derived from "requires adoption"). [L34 04:11–05:07](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)

## Cross-course notes
- **Agreement (Tether Lab).** Tether Lab's [tribal belonging](../../../../concepts/heuristics/tribal-belonging.md) and [in-group bias](../../../../concepts/heuristics/in-group-bias.md): "name the tribe with a noun the buyer would use about themselves" ("10,000+ customers" → "Why 10,000 Chai Lovers are Switching..."), and put it "mostly in hooks". Same rule as Evolve's labels. Tether also types social proof with the label ("350,000 tea drinkers"), a use Evolve doesn't mention. Tether's [customer research stack](../../../../frameworks/customer-research-stack.md) sorts research into "customer language (headlines)", matching Evolve's bank.
- **Disagreement on coined labels.** Tether Lab: "DON'T invent a tribe the audience doesn't recognise. Use their words, not brand words" ([tribal belonging](../../../../concepts/heuristics/tribal-belonging.md)). Evolve: create your own label ("Creamunchy", Labubu) because, though it "requires adoption", "when done correctly, it can crush" [L34 04:11–05:07](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md). Agent default (derived): call-out hooks use found labels; test coined labels for the *mechanism or product* (Creamunchy names the texture, not the tribe) as separate variants. Logged in the contradictions notes.
- **How specific a call-out should be.** Tether Lab's [specificity](../../../../concepts/heuristics/specificity.md): call out "one hyper-specific situation"; "Generic call-outs let Meta find the cheapest, not the right, buyer." Evolve warns that a too-niche label shrinks the TAM (#2) and that broad hooks scale [L48 06:43–08:31](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md); [L24](../lessons/24-the-2-types-of-hooks.md), while also flagging "too broad and copy not strong enough" (#2.5). Agent default (derived): use a specific *situation or visual* to signal the audience (Tether) attached to a large population label (Evolve: "men considering TRT"). Logged in the contradictions notes.
