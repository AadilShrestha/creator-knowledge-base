# High-information vs low-information buyers
> Evolve sorts buyers by how much information they need before buying, not by "logical vs emotional". Most of the market needs a lot of information, so the ad has to carry more of it. That information only lands when it is wrapped in a story that shifts a belief; a lecture won't do it.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** briefing any cold-traffic ad; deciding how much education, mechanism and proof a script must carry; diagnosing a competitor ad that is "all facts" or "all vibe"; choosing between a short hook-and-offer ad and a longer story or VSL-style ad.
- **Inputs → outputs:** avatar, angle, awareness level, the facts, proof and belief shifts the buyer still needs → a label (high or low information, default high), a list of the information the ad must carry, and the story vehicle that will carry it.
- **Rules:**
  - **DON'T** label an audience "logical" or "emotional". Spencer calls the split "too vague and not actionable"; "nobody just buys out of pure emotion and nobody buys out of pure logic." [L38 02:32](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)
  - **DO** classify by information need: high-information buyers "need a lot more information and likely need a lot more convincing"; low-information buyers already have what they need or don't need much. [L38 02:32–03:23](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)
  - **DO** assume high information by default: "the majority of market is high information". To scale "to a larger side of the market", find a way to communicate more information. [L38 03:23–04:01](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)
  - **DON'T** send information without a wrapper. If you just blast information at someone, "nobody is going to care". [L38 04:45](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)
  - **DO** wrap the information in a story: "stories are what actually make information more interesting." [L38 04:45](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)
  - **DON'T** treat education as differentiation. "Education alone is the least effective way to differentiate yourself" (Jason Fladlien, quoted). [L39 00:00–00:47](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md)
  - **DON'T** lead with basic information the avatar already knows, such as sleep 8 hours, hydrate, 10,000 steps. People already know it and still don't act. [L39 01:39–02:32](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md)
  - **IF** you have *new* information (e.g. "it's not overeating, it's cortisol") **THEN** use it as an objection re-assignment inside a story, not as the whole ad: "If knowledge was enough, they would have already taken action." [L39 02:32–04:03](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md)
  - **DO** aim to "get them to convince themselves": show a character like the viewer going through the belief shift. [L39 04:50–05:32](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md)
  - **DON'T** slip into "content writing" or "lecture" mode. [L39 06:14](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md); [L16 17:12–18:07](../lessons/16-why-you-need-to-understand-authority.md)
  - **IF** a story interests you because you're at its centre **THEN** test it from the prospect's seat. Spencer's picture: telling a homeless man your Ferrari paddle shifter got stuck. [L38 06:17–06:57](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)
- **See also:** [Story building blocks](story-building-blocks.md) · [Story plots](story-plots.md) · [Beliefs](beliefs.md) · [Objection handling](objection-handling.md) · [Market awareness](market-awareness.md) · [Unaware ads](unaware-ads.md) · [Write a video ad script](../playbooks/write-a-video-ad-script.md) · [Iteration playbook](../frameworks/iteration-playbook.md)

## Definition
The framing comes from L38. The lesson page credits Hormozi; Whisper heard "Mosey", so treat the attribution as likely but unconfirmed. [L38 03:23](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)

| Type | Who they are (instructor's words) | What the ad must do |
|---|---|---|
| **Low-information buyer** | Already has the information they need, or doesn't need much to decide. "Probably more emotional" in the old language. [L38 02:32](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md) | Grab attention, state the offer and close. Little education is needed (derived). |
| **High-information buyer** | Needs "a lot more information" and "a lot more convincing" before buying. Most of the market. [L38 02:32–03:23](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md) | Deliver the missing information *inside the ad*. Without ads, these buyers soak it up "a little bit here, a little bit there" over time; direct response wants them to "buy today". [L38 04:01](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md) |

The classification is based on "natural decision-making tendencies with purchases" and past purchase experience. [L38 02:32–03:23](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)

## Why it matters
- **Scale lives in the high-information majority.** An ad that only works on people who are already convinced caps out. To scale you must carry more information. [L38 03:23–04:01](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)
- **The job is two-part: grab, then keep.** Keeping attention matters "even more so for those who are high information". [L38 05:33](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)
- **Information alone doesn't move behaviour.** Basic facts are already known; new facts give "new hope" but people "will STILL hear this 'new' information and STILL not take action". What blocks them is psychological: how they "think ... feel ... believe ... behave". [L39 02:32–04:03](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md)
- **Stories lower defences.** When the brand states facts, even with percentages, the viewer thinks "you're saying that because you want me to buy". Inside a story "the mental defenses are down". [L39 07:11–08:01](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md) (instructor claim; the lesson's summary slide was generated with Google and cites no research)
- **It sets the boundary between content writing and copywriting.** A content writer "give[s] information on a topic". A copywriter "reinforces a position ... & can influence a behaviour". [L02 02:40; 03:05](../lessons/02-the-current-state-of-copywriting.md)

## How to use it
1. **Label the buyer.** For each avatar and angle, write "high" or "low" information. Default to high. [L38 03:23](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)
2. **List what has to travel.** Write down the facts, mechanism steps, proof and belief shifts this buyer still lacks. Cross-check with the awareness level: the lower the awareness, the longer the copy and the more education it carries. The L21 page's level guide (derived from the lesson) rates unaware as "extremely long" and most aware as "short". [L21 02:57–03:51; 16:24–17:23](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
3. **Sort each item as basic, new or belief.**

   | Item type | Decision | Source |
   |---|---|---|
   | Basic information the avatar already knows | Cut it or shrink it; it earns "no shit, Sherlock" | [L39 01:39–02:32](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md) |
   | New information (new cause, new mechanism) | Keep it, but use it as an objection re-assignment inside a story beat | [L39 02:32–04:03](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md); [Objection handling](objection-handling.md) |
   | Blocking belief | Build the story around a character who holds this belief and then drops it | [L39 04:03–05:32](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md); [Beliefs](beliefs.md) |
4. **Choose the wrapper.** Pick a story vehicle and run the five-block check on it ([Story building blocks](story-building-blocks.md)). Pick an arc ([Story plots](story-plots.md)) and an enemy ([Strategic enemies](strategic-enemies.md)).
5. **Choose the length and format.** If the information load is heavy and the angle is proven, the course's iteration options include **VSL expansion**: "go deeper on education, mechanism and proof", often 2-4 minutes. Spencer calls it "much more labor intensive". [L53 10:17–11:13](../lessons/53-the-iteration-playbook-formats-styles.md) Another option is **AI-animated ingredients or solutions**, so complicated mechanisms become "interesting and easier to visualize". [L53 05:36](../lessons/53-the-iteration-playbook-formats-styles.md)
6. **Test from the prospect's seat.** Can the avatar see themselves in the story? If they can't, rewrite. [L38 06:57](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)

## Common mistakes
- Calling a buyer "emotional" or "logical" and writing for that label. [L38 02:32](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)
- Writing a fact list or "studies show" hook and calling it an ad. [L39 01:39–04:03](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md)
- Filling the weekly creative quota with "educational or entertaining words". [L02 02:32](../lessons/02-the-current-state-of-copywriting.md)
- Assuming a story is interesting because it is a story ("Cool story, bro"). [L38 05:33–06:17](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)
- Using raw stats in language that's hard to read, so the ad gets "very lecture heavy". [L28 20:22–21:09](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
- (derived) Inventing a customer story to carry the information. The course's own brief prompt says "Don't make up specific customer names/stories" ([L72](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md), "Borrowed Proof Protocol" in the brief prompt). Use real reviews ([L44](../lessons/44-prompts-to-help-you-storytell.md)).

## Cross-course notes
- **Evolve (Spencer Pawliw):** rejects the logical/emotional split. Buyers differ in how much information they need, and most need a lot, so the fix is *more* information wrapped in a story. [L38 02:32–04:45](../lessons/38-why-storytelling-is-so-powerful.md)
- **Tether Lab:** puts emotion first. The subconscious "makes most buying decisions" and the conscious mind "mostly signs off" ([Subconscious vs conscious mind](../../../../concepts/subconscious-vs-conscious-mind.md)). An ad that "speaks to logic, not emotion" is flagged as under-emotional ([Valence-intensity diagnostic](../../../../frameworks/valence-intensity-diagnostic.md); [Diagnose why your ads aren't working e-book](../../../../resources/diagnose-why-your-ads-arent-working-ebook.md)).
- **Tension and default:** the courses agree that a bare fact dump fails. They differ on the diagnosis: Tether Lab says the ad lacks emotion, Evolve says the information has no story wrapper. **Agent default:** carry the information the high-information buyer needs, but deliver it through a character's emotional belief shift. Then score the draft with both lenses: emotion zone (Tether Lab) and information carried plus story blocks (Evolve). Logged in the contradictions notes.
- Both courses favour story vehicles. See [Story frameworks for ads (Tether Lab)](../../../../frameworks/story-frameworks-for-ads.md) and [Native advertising](../../../../concepts/native-advertising.md).
