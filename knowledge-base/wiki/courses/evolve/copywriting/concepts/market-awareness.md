# Market awareness (Schwartz's five levels, redefined for today)
> Schwartz's five levels (unaware, problem, solution, product, most aware) still apply, but people now move through them in minutes, so the course redefines each level's job: unaware ads surface a problem the viewer isn't thinking about, problem-aware ads make it worth solving, solution-aware ads compare solution types, product-aware ads (now the biggest, most sophisticated level) prove why yours is different, and most-aware ads push with an offer.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** choosing what an ad must do first for a given audience; labelling a competitor ad; picking script length/structure; diagnosing a loser whose execution drifted from its planned level; writing an "awareness-level test" iteration.
- **Inputs → outputs:** avatar + product + what the audience has already tried → one awareness level, the job at that level, structure and length, proof to bring; for teardowns, a level label and the gap to exploit; for iterations, the same idea rewritten one level up or down.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** decide the level before writing: "am I writing more of an unaware ad? … a problem aware ad? Am I starting at the solution level? … at the product-aware level?" [L21 17:23–18:21](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
  - **DON'T** assume people sit in problem awareness; they Google, TikTok and ChatGPT and can go "from literally problem aware to buying a product and being most aware in 10 minutes." [L21 00:45–02:15](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
  - **DO** at problem aware, make them see a problem *worth solving* ("You dramatize the need vividly…" - Eugene); naming a problem they didn't know they had is unaware-level work. [L21 04:45–06:59](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
  - **DO** at product aware, find out whether they tried your category and what they believe because of it, then speak to it ("I know you've tried pee pads and here's why they failed"). [L21 13:35–14:32; 18:21](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
  - **IF** you struggle at product aware **THEN** suspect "no positioning… new mechanisms… authority… [shallow] customer knowledge". [L21 13:35–14:32](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
  - **DO** keep copy shortest for most aware and longest for unaware: "you have to take people through each customer journey." [L21 15:45–17:23](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
  - **DO** check after writing that the copy actually sits at the planned level; mismatches make teams wrongly throw out angles. [L46 09:20](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)
  - **IF** an ad crushes at one level **THEN** iterate it one level up or down ("you can go up or down both ways"). [L52 13:01–14:30](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
  - **DON'T** trust an AI's awareness label blindly: "Claude will struggle with awareness levels." [L70 19:26–20:20](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md); [L47 05:12–05:55](../lessons/47-claude-code-10-step-iteration-process.md)
  - **IF** the ad is an offer / most-aware ad **THEN** pitch directly; use recommendation-style transitions for product-, solution- and unaware ads. [L29 08:27–09:51](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
- **See also:** [Unaware ads](unaware-ads.md) · [Hooks](hooks.md) · [Beliefs](beliefs.md) · [Unique mechanism](unique-mechanism.md) · [Objection handling](objection-handling.md) · [Positioning](positioning.md) · [Iteration playbook](../frameworks/iteration-playbook.md)

## The five levels as redefined
| Level | Page definition (verbatim where quoted) | Ad's first job | Structure | Length (page image) | Example from the course | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | "Is still a very real category… Your goal is to still make people aware of a problem they aren't consciously thinking about." | Surface the problem (or a new way to satisfy a desire) | Problem → amplify → solutions → products → why ours → brand → offer | Extremely long | "Do you sit and work for 12 hours a day?… for two or three years?" | [L21 02:57–03:51; 16:24–17:23](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md) |
| **Problem aware** | "your copy is not to identify the problem (that's unaware) it's to make the prospect understand that they have a problem worth solving." | Dramatise the need | Agitate, quantify the consequence | Very long | "your bank account literally has one hundred and seventy four dollars in it… and you're still spending" | [L21 04:45–06:59](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md) |
| **Solution aware** | "aware of the solutions that exist… just aren't convinced which one will solve it the best." | Compare solution types; why this type | Name options; defer the product | Long | Dog peeing indoors: pee pads, meds, outside, trainer | [L21 07:50–09:25](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md) |
| **Product aware** | "aware of your types of product & its category, but they aren't completely aware of all YOUR product does, they aren't convinced it does it well, and they aren't convinced it's right for them." | Be the right product, not just the category | Acknowledge past failures → why they failed → new mechanism, positioning, authority | Medium | "Three reasons why most pee pads fail" | [L21 09:25–15:11](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md) |
| **Most aware** | "likely seen your ads or heard of your brand… may not trust that your brand specifically can actually deliver… or haven't got the right offer" | Push over the edge | Offer, reassurance, guarantee | Short | "40% off pee pads" | [L21 15:11–15:45](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md) |

Page-image principle: "The less they know, the more you must teach. The more you must teach, the longer your copy." [L21 page image](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)

## Why it matters
- **Speed changed the market.** Schwartz wrote in 1966, "before cell phones, the internet"; what changed is "how fast people progress through these stages nowadays". [L21 00:00–02:57](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **Product aware is the scale lever.** "Product aware is biggest it has ever been… But it's also a lot more sophisticated"; "if you can help someone who has had a poor experience with pee pads actually buy yours… you will unlock massive scale." [L21 10:20–13:35](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md) "That's why positioning is more important than ever." [L21 17:23](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **Unaware is the biggest pool and hardest ad.** About "60% or more" of the market (Buyer's Pyramid); "when you crack them, they can become the most scalable ads in your account." [L35 00:00–01:26](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
- **"We don't give people enough credit for how aware they are."** "Are you tired?" to people with common problems hits buyers already aware of "your pillow". [L21 04:45–05:30](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **Losers fail differently by level** (page list): unaware = "too much of a wild card"; problem/solution aware = hook not relatable, problem not painful enough, copy "super basic" ("do you have low testosterone?"); product/most aware = offer not competitive, differentiation unclear. [L48 01:27](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)

## How to use it
1. **Diagnose the audience**: has the viewer tried the category? Which products? What do they now believe? [L21 18:21](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
2. **Write one real customer question per level** for the product (Growth Guide "Awareness" tab). Air purifier example: Most aware "What's the least expensive air purifier that does the best job?"; Product aware "How much space can an air purifier really clean? Is it worth it?"; Solution aware "Is ultraviolet cleaning a better long term way to keep the air in my home clean?"; Problem aware "I feel congested at home but not at work. What in the world is going on?"; Unaware "Exercise and health are important to me - besides doing cardio, how else can I improve my cardiovascular health?" [L22 Awareness tab](../lessons/22-how-to-finalize-your-ad-ideas.md)
3. **Pick the level and log it** (Ad Roadmap "AWARENESS LEVEL" column). [L22](../lessons/22-how-to-finalize-your-ad-ideas.md)
4. **Match hook, bridge, hold and length** to the level (table below for short video).
5. **After writing, re-label** the finished copy and fix drift (e.g. a planned problem-aware washer ad whose hook "your washer is growing something inside of it" reads unaware). [L47 05:12–05:55](../lessons/47-claude-code-10-step-iteration-process.md)
6. **Iterate across levels** with the awareness ladder (below).

## Awareness vs sophistication (don't mix them up)
Awareness = how much the prospect knows about problem, solutions and you. **Sophistication** (Schwartz stages, used in [L28](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)) = how many similar claims the market has heard: stage 1-2 "Does this even work?" → direct proof + clear mechanism; stage 3-4 "How is yours different?" → new mechanism + authority, reframe past failures; stage 5 "This isn't for me" → identity ("Make the copy about WHO they are"). [L28 03:20–06:14](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) A product-aware audience in a stage-4 market needs both: a "why yours" structure and a new mechanism. (derived combination)

## Common mistakes
- **Treating "Are you tired?" as a problem-aware hook** for an audience that already knows every product. [L21 04:45–05:30](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **"Try this [category]" to people the category failed.** [L21 12:06–12:42](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **Introducing the product too early in unaware ads** ("muddied waters"). [L35 04:40–05:55](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
- **Offer-heavy accounts** = mostly reaching most-aware/product-aware viewers; if offer ads underperform, check whether the audience needs belief handling first. [L21 15:45](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **Planning one level and executing another.** [L46 09:20](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)

## Tension inside the course
The video-ad prompt says "ONLY writing for Problem-Aware or Product-Aware… Do not write unaware hooks (too educational, too long, wrong audience) or most-aware retargeting" [L72](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md), while L21/L35 call unaware ads the largest and potentially most scalable. Default: use the prompt for problem/product-aware scripts; write unaware ads with the [L35 process](unaware-ads.md) as a separate, harder concept. Logged in the contradictions notes.

## Cross-course notes
- **Agreement on non-linear journeys (Tether Lab):** Tether's [TEEP journey](../../../../frameworks/teep-journey.md) says "there is no step-by-step down the funnel… people kind of morph in and out"; Evolve says people jump from problem to most aware in minutes. Rough mapping (derived): Trigger ≈ unaware/problem aware; Exploration ≈ solution aware; Evaluation ≈ product aware (us vs them, ingredients, founder ads); Purchase ≈ most aware (offers).
- **Diagnostic overlap:** Tether reads the winning TEEP stage as a measure of market education ("IF trigger-stage ads convert most THEN the market is low-educated", [triggering events](../../../../concepts/triggering-events.md)); Evolve reads a dominance of offer winners as "mostly reaching most-aware and product-aware viewers" [L21 15:45](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md). Compatible.
- **Self by stage:** Tether's [three selves](../../../../concepts/three-selves.md) keeps cold ads on Actual/Ideal and saves Ought for qualified/warm audiences; apply that inside Evolve's levels (Ought lines only for most-aware/retargeting) (derived).
