# Objection handling (prevent first, then handle)
> Sort every objection into one of three buckets (product/market, claim, avatar/belief), pick the technique that works for that bucket (facts, reversals, "because", reassigning, gradualization), match the depth to the market's sophistication, and never defend price, only reframe it; the best objection handling stops the objection forming at all.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing the hold/body after hook and bridge; mining reviews and ad comments for doubts; reviewing a competitor ad for how it disarms skepticism; writing a price/offer section; iterating a winner whose comments repeat one objection.
- **Inputs → outputs:** objections from research (reviews, Reddit, ad comments) + the claims + sophistication stage + mechanism/proof → an objection table (objection, bucket, technique, written line, script slot) and a hold that runs Objection → Claim → Proof → Benefit once, in customer language.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** aim to prevent, not just handle: "write a copy that's so good that you can just prevent them from happening in the first place." [L28 00:00–00:29](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
  - **DO** keep a running objections list in four buckets: product, angles, claims, and objections "rooted from your customers beliefs". [L61 page](../lessons/61-objections-list.md); [L01 04:08](../lessons/01-overview-of-copywriting-program.md)
  - **DO** classify each objection before choosing a technique (table below). [L28 00:29–03:20; 21:09–23:49](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
  - **DO** write facts that imply the claim; test "Can they prove you wrong?" [L28 06:14–07:58](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
  - **DON'T** invent facts ("sold out five times"); that "re-sophisticates the market." [L28 08:28–09:03](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
  - **DON'T** tell people their objection is wrong: "You can't tell people that their objections are wrong. You just have to break it down so we're on the same team." [L28 16:53–17:52](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
  - **DON'T** lecture or stack stats: "if your customer is confused and they can tell you're trying too hard to sell them, then you lose them." [L28 20:22–21:09](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
  - **DON'T** write generic objection lines: "I know what you're thinking. You've tried supplements before. They didn't work" is "a terrible line… not specific enough"; name what they actually tried. [L70 23:07–24:04](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md); [L71 08:47](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
  - **DO** run Objection → Claim → Proof → Benefit ONCE in the hold: "Don't try to cycle multiple times." [L72 Core Rule 5](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)
  - **DON'T** defend price: "The number one rule is never defend price. You always reframe what they're buying." [L28 33:17–34:09](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
  - **IF** a winning ad's comments repeat one objection **THEN** answer it in the script or in a comment-reply hook. [L52 14:30–16:05](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
- **See also:** [Beliefs](beliefs.md) · [Facts over opinions](facts-over-opinions.md) · [Authority in ads](authority-in-ads.md) · [Unique mechanism](unique-mechanism.md) · [Market awareness](market-awareness.md) · [Pitch transition](pitch-transition.md) · [Value words (Big 4)](value-words-big-4.md) · [Rewrites library](../libraries/rewrites.md)

## Definition: three buckets
| Bucket | What the viewer thinks | Examples (page) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Product / market | "I've tried this before and it didn't work" / "this category doesn't work" | "Tried before, didn't work" / "Category doesn't work" | [L28 00:29–01:27](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| 2. Claim | "I don't believe your promise" / "too good to be true" | "Don't believe your promise" / "Sounds too good to be true" | [L28 01:27–02:26](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| 3. Avatar (belief) | "I'm not that type of person / this won't work for me" | "I'm not X enough" / "This won't work for ME" | [L28 02:26–03:20](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |

Avatar objections are the customer's beliefs about themselves (see [beliefs](beliefs.md)); e.g. someone may believe a car-fixing product works but think "I shouldn't be anywhere near fixing a car". [L28 02:55–03:20](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)

## Why it matters
- **Belief decides the sale**: "If you pitch someone that doesn't believe you, they're not going to buy"; "You've prevented/handled objections" is a belief check before every pitch. [L29 06:08–07:43](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
- **Spend winners stall on doubt**: "You grabbed attention, they like your claims, but do not believe them" and "People do not see the value in your offer" are listed reasons. [L50 04:46](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)
- **Burned buyers generalise**: after one failed product "our brain likes to categorize": "gut health supplements don't work". [L28 16:15–16:44](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md); [L21 11:15–12:42](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)

## Technique by bucket (most → least effective, per the page)
| Bucket | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th / doesn't work | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product / market | **Reassigning** ("Blame the real villain, not the category") | Facts ("Specific differentiation from what failed") | Because ("Explain why this time is different") | Reversals ("Position as the reason you made it") | [L28 21:09–22:12](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| Claim | **Facts** ("Visual, falsifiable, ownable proof") | Because ("Mechanism that validates the claim") | Reassigning ("Only if explaining why past claims failed") | Reversals ✗ ("Doesn't address skepticism") | [L28 22:12–22:41](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| Avatar | **Reversals** ("You don't have to be X") | Because ("Works because it doesn't require X") | Reassigning ("You're not broken, the villain is Y") | Facts ✗ ("Doesn't address internal belief") | [L28 22:41–23:49](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |

### Depth by sophistication (Schwartz stages)
| Stage | Market objection | Handle with | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | "Does this even work?" | Direct proof + clear mechanism explanation (+ authority) | [L28 03:50–04:18](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| 3-4 | "How is yours different?" ("They've tried shit before") | New mechanism + authority that reframes why past failures weren't their fault (because / reassigning) | [L28 04:18–05:15](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| 5 | "This isn't for me" | Identity: "Make the copy about WHO they are, not what you're selling. Describe them so accurately they can't object." | [L28 05:15–06:14](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |

## The five techniques with lines
| Technique (credit) | Pattern | Example from the course | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facts (Harry Dry / Hopkins) | State facts that imply the claim | "Cookies that have sold out 43 times"; "Worn by supermodels in London and dads in Ohio"; CarePod "sold out seven or eight different times" (instructor claim) | [L28 06:14–09:03](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| Reversals (Caples) | Make the objection the selling point | "Kids know their greens and still love them!"; "Tightens even the most damaged skin."; Ryze "Lose Weight Even If You Cheat" | [L28 09:03–11:54](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| Because (Cialdini) | "[Claim] because [reason]": "a mini proof bridge" | "It's so easy to clean because it's made of stainless steel so you can just throw it right in the dishwasher." | [L28 11:54–15:48](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| Reassigning | "This objection you have is valid, but it actually is valid on just this thing specifically" | "Most probiotics fail because they die in your stomach acid… These survive because of the delayed-release capsule" (New target: "The capsules were the issue, not the probiotics"); "a lot still use grain fillers that dogs can't digest" (page examples; numbers illustrative) | [L28 15:48–21:09](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| Gradualization (Schwartz) | A "stream of acceptances": Promise-Belief-Promise, Paragraph Parallelism, Repetition of Proof | Grüns script ("You know greens are good for you. You also know you should be taking them daily. But here's what stops you: the taste. The texture…"); CarePod "If you have a kid, you probably have a humidifier in their room. And if you have a humidifier, you probably hate cleaning it every few days." | [L28 23:49–33:17](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |

## How to use it
1. **Collect objections** in the customer's words during research (reviews, Reddit, ad comments), in the four buckets. [L61](../lessons/61-objections-list.md); [L52 15:11](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
2. **Classify** each (product/market, claim, avatar) and note the sophistication stage. [L28 00:29–06:14](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
3. **Pick the technique** from the table; write the line.
4. **Order the hold as a stream of acceptances**: accepted truth → build on it → name their real objection before they think it → proof (not their fault) → promise → mechanism → specific proof at the doubt moment → restate the solved problem. [L28 27:37–30:12](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
5. **Let authorities carry the claims** (repetition of proof: "I consulted this expert…"; "Every claim was not made from the person in the voiceover. It was from the authority."). [L28 25:35–27:37](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
6. **In a 30-45 s video**, compress to one cycle: Objection (8-11 s) → Claim (11-16 s) → Proof (16-25 s) → Benefit (25-32 s, shown not listed). [L72 rule digest](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)
7. **Make the objection specific** (Spencer's final: "You've tried GLP-1 supplements, appetite suppressants, and natural alternatives. But the problem is, none of those work long-term… They only BOOST your fullness hormone. NONE of them PROTECT it."). [L71 final PDF](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
8. **Handle price last, by reframing** (below).
9. **Re-read for lecturing**; cut stats the viewer can't parse. [L28 20:22–21:09](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)

## Price objections: three reframes
| Method | Structure | Example | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily breakdown + comparison | "Not $X, it's $Y per day" / "the same as a cup of coffee" | "$59/month = $1.97/day. Less than a bottle of kombucha." (attacking a habit people enjoy "sometimes works, sometimes it doesn't") | [L28 34:09–34:49](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| Expensive cost comparison (compare up) | "Compared to [expensive alternative], this is nothing" | "Transplants cost $15,000 and don't stop future loss. This is $49/month and addresses the enzyme causing it." | [L28 34:49–35:34](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |
| Desire reframe | "You're not buying [product]. You're buying [outcome/identity]." (later in the copy, not the hook) | "You're not buying a powder. You're buying the ability to wear the clothes in the back of your closet again." | [L28 35:34–37:00](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) |

Spencer's final video CTA uses the daily frame plus risk reversal: "try it for 60 days 100% risk-free… Try it risk free for less than $2/day". [L71 final PDF](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md) An offer that is simply not competitive is "an iteration outside of the ad" (#8 offer issue). [L48 18:22–18:56](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)

## Other objection moves in the course
- **Transparency / do the opposite**: Nexgen CEO names the #1 complaint ("half the size… almost twice as much! And you know what? They're right.") then answers it. [L26 15:10–15:40](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md); [L16 33:36–36:48](../lessons/16-why-you-need-to-understand-authority.md)
- **Vulnerability**: "if your phone is an iPhone 12 or below… we can't help you" (hypothetical) [L50 07:04–07:40](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md); "this might not be for everyone" before the product [L54 03:53–04:46](../lessons/54-iteration-examples-breakdowns-walkthrough.md); offer vulnerability "Too good not to share. Even if it hurts our margins." instead of "save up to 25%" [L54 03:02–03:53](../lessons/54-iteration-examples-breakdowns-walkthrough.md).
- **Comment-reply hooks / script patches**: comment "Does it work for X type of person, like me?" → add "it even works for X"; comment "I've tried X before and this is the same thing" → put it on screen: "I used to think X was the same as this, but here's how it's actually different." [L52 15:11–16:05](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
- **New information as reassigning**: "it's not overeating, it's cortisol from constant stress"; useful but can still fail to trigger action without a story. [L39 02:32–04:03](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md)
- **Neutralise an outrageous claim** with an acknowledgement ("that would be an exaggeration") while keeping the vivid outcome. [L32 03:33–04:31](../lessons/32-how-to-write-ads-that-show-and-dont-tell.md)
- **Value words answer doubts**: new/only ("Is this new?"), easy ("Can I actually do this?"), safe ("What if I try this and it doesn't work for me?"), big ("Will this actually change my life?"). [L62 image](../lessons/62-value-words.md)

## Common mistakes
- **Fighting the objection** instead of agreeing and solving. [L28 09:03–09:27](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
- **Using reversals on claim skepticism** or **facts on avatar beliefs** (marked ✗ on the page). [L28 page](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
- **Generic "you've tried before" lines.** [L70 23:07–24:04](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md)
- **Several objection cycles** in a short video. [L72 Core Rule 5](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)
- **Defensive price lines** ("it's worth it"). [L28 33:17](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
- **Using illustrative page stats** (74%, 89%, 8x, 80% real meat) as real data. [L28 limitations](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)

## Cross-course notes
- **Agreement (Tether Lab):** Tether's [risk-reduction hooks](../../../../concepts/risk-reduction-hooks.md) (guarantees, "$0 trial", "you don't have to change all of you") and [skeptic format](../../../../frameworks/skeptic-format.md) (claim → doubt → test on camera → concession) are complementary objection tools; the skeptic format is a video version of Evolve's "name the objection, then prove it".

- **Price framing:** Tether's [perception management](../../../../concepts/perception-management.md) ("give a frame of reference in the buyer's own units", "$0 trial" not "free trial") extends Evolve's daily-breakdown reframe.
