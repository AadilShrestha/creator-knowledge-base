# Facts over opinions (turn matters of opinion into matters of fact)
> Facts are unbiased, communicate more than opinions and are hard to argue with, so turn every matter of opinion in a script into a matter of fact (not necessarily a statistic), build hooks from a true fact about the product, and never invent one.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing or auditing headlines, bullets, bridges and claims; a hook gets attention but the ad does not convert (empty claims, opinion bridges); building the facts list before writing; checking a competitor ad's proof.
- **Inputs → outputs:** draft line(s) + product doc/facts list + sources → each claim labelled **F** (checkable) or **O** (opinion) → opinions replaced or backed by the fact behind them → one hook written *from* the strongest non-statistical fact.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** ask of every statement: "is this an opinion? If it is an opinion, just try to turn it into a fact." [L10 01:06](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
  - **DO** "when in doubt, just give a fact"; facts "are usually going to communicate something more". [L10 01:41](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
  - **DO** run the test "Can they prove you wrong?": "Delicious cookies" (yes: "says who?") vs "Cookies that have sold out 43 times". [L28 07:35–07:58](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
  - **DO** look beyond statistics: a first ("first stainless steel humidifier"), a count, a physical behaviour ("won't melt in your hand"), a position ("we're number two"). "This does not need to be a statistic." [L10 08:35–09:36; 12:49](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
  - **DO** show the fact and let the customer conclude (six ingredients vs a long list; "less is more" becomes unnecessary). [L10 05:26–06:58](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
  - **DO** attach the factual reason to a slightly opinionated word ("dog-proof" because it's washable). [L10 06:58–07:32](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
  - **DON'T** invent facts: a made-up "sold out five times" "re-sophisticates the market"; use facts "100% true" and checkable. [L28 08:28–09:03](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) The video prompt marks compliance "Utmost Important": claims must be real and based on data you have. [L72 Critical Reminders](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)
  - **DO** verify facts against the product page; AI fills gaps and is "not always right" (DPP-4 called a hormone; "rare" African fruit dropped because unverifiable). [L71 03:40; 21:28](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
  - **IF** the hook works but the bridge loses people **THEN** check the bridge for opinions and replace them with facts. "Someone's looking for a reason to scroll off… they want a reason to object." [L48 14:51–16:50](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)
  - **IF** you have a fact **THEN** ask "how can you write something that comes from that fact?" (M&M's). [L10 10:36–12:17](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
- **See also:** [Three copy checks](three-copy-checks.md) · [Says vs communicates](says-vs-communicates.md) · [Objection handling](objection-handling.md) · [Authority in ads](authority-in-ads.md) · [Bridges](bridges.md) · [Facts list](../lessons/60-facts-list.md) · [Rules library](../libraries/rules.md)

## Definition
- "Facts are unbiased." "I'm five foot 10" is a fact; "if you're five foot 10, you're short" is an opinion people will argue. [L10 00:00–00:32](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
- Harry Dry's line: "you want to turn a matter of opinion into a matter of fact." [L10 01:06](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
- A falsifiable line is "a CONCRETE thing that we can say is TRUE or FALSE". [L08 08:51 slide](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)

## Why it matters
- **Credibility:** "When you are rooted in facts, you have much more credibility… People will believe you" (credited to David Perell). A run of facts can even carry an opinion after them. [L10 13:20](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
- **Objections die:** "When you write with matters of fact over matters of opinion, objections die because you're not making arguable claims." [L28 06:14–07:06](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) Facts are the top technique for claim objections, but they "don't address internal belief" (avatar objections). [L28 22:12–23:49](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
- **Empty claims lose in sophisticated markets:** claims with zero proof/facts/authority "will not be believed even if it is exactly what people want". [L48 12:57–14:51](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)
- **Spend winners need facts to convert:** the fix for belief issues is to "add authority, use more facts or show vulnerability". [L50 07:04](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)
- **Every fact says something:** "When you say a fact, it guarantees that you say something" ("unmatched power and performance" vs "1100 horsepower"). [L10 11:46–12:17](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)

## How to use it
1. **Build the facts list before writing**: brand, product, and interesting market facts, each with its source. [L60 page](../lessons/60-facts-list.md); [L01 04:08](../lessons/01-overview-of-copywriting-program.md)
2. **Label** every claim in the draft F or O. [L10 01:06](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
3. **Replace or back each O**: what is in it, what is it first to do, how does it behave, what causes the problem? (Teen supplement: outcome promise → "vitamin deficiencies can create chemical imbalances that may lead to increased irritability in teens".) [L10 04:25–05:26](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
4. **Prefer non-statistical facts** for hooks; keep one statistic-led variant. [L10 08:02–09:36](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
5. **Write from the fact**: turn the fact into the line (M&M's "melts in your mouth, not in your hand"). [L10 10:36–11:46](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
6. **Lead bullets with a hard product-doc fact** when one exists ("now we're leading with a fact"). [L67 24:37–26:09](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md)
7. **Gloss jargon** so the fact stays readable: "(hunger hormone)", "(stress hormone)". [L68 35:41–36:28](../lessons/68-how-to-write-image-ads-part-2.md)
8. **Verify** every number against the product page/study and fix mismatches before shipping (the course's own ads show 56% vs 68% for the same stat; the final video script uses the website's 56%). [L67 limitations](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md); [L71 25:12](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
9. **Check the opposite direction**: if you removed the opinion words, does the ad still work? (Papa Bear "Less is more" still works because the lists carry it.) [L10 05:26–06:58](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)

## Facts as authority
Research facts are one of the ten authority types: "DID YOU KNOW? we lose 150 strands of hair per day" (Divi) and "Most kids start school with 40g of sugar" make the advertiser seem knowledgeable, as long as you educate "without boring someone to sleep". [L16 15:39–18:07](../lessons/16-why-you-need-to-understand-authority.md) The instructor also uses a fact to make a problem real before the pitch ("1L of bottle water can contain 240,000+ plastic particles"; his own illustrative figure, unverified). [L20 00:34–01:18](../lessons/20-being-aware-of-authorities.md) See [authority in ads](authority-in-ads.md).

## Common mistakes
- **Equating "fact" with "number".** Non-numerical facts are harder but more distinctive. [L10 09:06; 12:49](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
- **Opinion hooks with no curiosity** ("chocolate stopped feeling like chocolate"). [L54 01:18–02:09](../lessons/54-iteration-examples-breakdowns-walkthrough.md)
- **Filling templates with numbers you can't substantiate** ("4 weeks", "30-cent"). [L57 rules](../lessons/57-100-hook-templates-form-winning-ads-deck.md) (derived note)
- **Stacking stats until it lectures**: "stats, stats, stats… language that's hard to read". [L28 20:22–21:09](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
- **Treating basic information as a selling fact.** Facts the avatar already knows ("sleep 8 hours, hydrate") get "no shit, Sherlock"; education alone "is the least effective way to differentiate yourself" (Jason Fladlien, per the instructor). [L39 00:00–02:32](../lessons/39-why-you-cant-just-rely-on-education.md)
- **Letting AI supply "facts".** [L71 03:40; 21:28](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md); [L68 30:59–34:44](../lessons/68-how-to-write-image-ads-part-2.md)

Default: state the specific fact; skip the "they" conspiracy unless it is true.
