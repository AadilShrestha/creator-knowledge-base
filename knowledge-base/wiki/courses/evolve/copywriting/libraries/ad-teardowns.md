# Ad teardowns library (Evolve Copywriting)
> Every real ad, landing page, brand campaign and course mock-up that Spencer Pawliw shows or critiques in Evolve Copywriting, merged across lessons and grouped by niche then format, with the course's verdict (what works, what fails), the fix, the principle and the iteration issue code (#1–#14) where a lesson assigns one.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** tearing down a competitor ad (find the closest precedent and Spencer's verdict); QA-ing our draft against known failure modes; picking a proven mechanism in our niche to remix; diagnosing why a winner stalls (issue codes).
- **Inputs → outputs:** the competitor ad (screenshot or script, niche, format, outcome if known) → [issue-code index](#issue-code-index) and niche section → 2–4 analogous rows → (1) the competitor's mechanism named in course terms, (2) the likely failure and fix, (3) a remix brief: **same mechanism, our avatar, our proof, new words and visuals**.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** write "what it says" and "what it communicates" as two lines in every teardown, then name the feeling and desire before copying anything. [L09 00:00-01:29](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md) [L09 08:49](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
  - **DO** break a winner into components (desire, avatar, angle, mechanism, authority, awareness, valence/intensity, positioning) and mark each working / improvable / missing. [L52 00:00-01:37](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md) [L47 DOCX identify block](../lessons/47-claude-code-10-step-iteration-process.md)
  - **DO** copy the mechanism, not the asset. When a competitor already owns a visual (Goli's pour test), "find your own visual, don't copy 1:1". [L31 04:45-06:10](../lessons/31-how-to-create-ads-that-show-dont-tell.md)
  - **DON'T** treat view counts, "crushing", "$1M spent" or "3X'd the account" as verified results; they are Spencer's claims or on-screen numbers.
  - **DON'T** reproduce a borrowed-authority trick that relies on an invented claim (BoomBoom's "banned episode"). [L16 24:55-26:42](../lessons/16-why-you-need-to-understand-authority.md)
  - **IF** a competitor ad is a native story or an easy AI UGC ad **THEN** expect copycats (#13) and plan your own differentiation, not a copy. [L51 12:19-14:45](../lessons/51-how-to-iterate-on-breakthroughs.md)
  - **IF** you can't tell whether the competitor ad is winning **THEN** look for repetition (many creators running the same concept, "700 plus ads running") and say it is an inference. [L31 04:45-06:10](../lessons/31-how-to-create-ads-that-show-dont-tell.md) [L35 18:08-19:32](../lessons/35-how-to-write-unaware-ads-hooks.md)
- **See also:** [Hooks](hooks.md) · [Rewrites](rewrites.md) · [Angles](angles.md) · [Rules → Iteration](rules.md#10-iteration-and-testing) · [Iteration issue codes](../frameworks/iteration-issue-codes.md) · [Nine-component audit](../frameworks/nine-component-audit.md) · [Positioning](../concepts/positioning.md) · Tether Lab's [ad-teardowns library](../../../../libraries/ad-teardowns.md) (psychology-tagged, failure patterns F1–F15)

## How to read the table
- **What works / What fails / Fix suggested:** Spencer's verdict as recorded on the lesson pages; "—" = not critiqued; "(derived)" = the lesson page's inference, not his words.
- **Principle:** the lesson's teaching point the ad illustrates.
- **Issue code:** the iteration-playbook number the lesson assigns (see the index); "—" when none is assigned.
- **NOT A REAL AD / PAGE** marks Spencer's mock-ups; *hypothetical* marks a spoken example. They teach a pattern, they are not market evidence.
