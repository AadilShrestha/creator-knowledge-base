# Hooks library (Evolve Copywriting)
> Every hook, headline and opening line in the Evolve Copywriting course (Spencer Pawliw), merged across lessons. Rows are grouped by hook type and tagged with niche, so an agent can find a precedent for a competitor's opener or a mechanism to remix.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you are naming the hook type of a competitor ad, writing the first line or first 3 s of a UGC script or a static headline, or producing hook variants for a 3-hook test.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the job (targeted or broad? promise or open loop? awareness level?) and the niche → filter this page by section and the [niche index](#quick-index-by-niche) → 3–5 precedent rows → the **mechanism** (column "Why it works") rewritten with our avatar, our proof and our words. Fill-in slot versions are in [templates](templates.md#1-hook-templates).
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** decide targeted vs broad first, then promise vs open loop, before you write a word. [L24 00:00-00:27](../lessons/24-the-2-types-of-hooks.md) [L25 00:18-00:41](../lessons/25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md)
  - **DO** check every hook for the three must-haves: emotion, curiosity gap, high stakes. Not every winner has all three, "but a lot of great hooks do". [L23 02:56-03:25](../lessons/23-the-framework-behind-great-hooks.md)
  - **DO** copy the mechanism (the slot structure, the juxtaposition, the fear check), never the line. Spencer is "obviously not the biggest fan of templates" and does not "believe in copy and pasting". [L57 00:00-00:54](../lessons/57-100-hook-templates-form-winning-ads-deck.md)
  - **DO** look across categories as well as at direct competitors; "sometimes those are the most powerful ones". [L57 00:54-01:39](../lessons/57-100-hook-templates-form-winning-ads-deck.md)
  - **DON'T** reuse a hook whose proof you don't have (sell-out counts, review counts, "doctor recommended", study numbers). Facts must be "100% true". [L28 08:28-09:03](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
  - **DON'T** treat rows tagged *prompt-output* or *hypothetical* as tested winners. They show a pattern, not evidence.
  - **IF** a hook stops the scroll but buyers don't follow **THEN** suspect the hook itself, not the hold: "just because your hook is stopping people ... doesn't mean that it's going to get them to purchase". [L50 03:29-04:46](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md)
- **See also:** [Templates](templates.md) · [Rewrites](rewrites.md) · [Ad teardowns](ad-teardowns.md) · [Rules → Hooks](rules.md#3-hooks) · [Hooks concept](../concepts/hooks.md) · [Open loops](../concepts/open-loops.md) · [Unaware ads](../concepts/unaware-ads.md) · [Labels](../concepts/labels-and-customer-language.md) · Tether Lab's psychology-tagged [hooks library](../../../../libraries/hooks.md)

## How to read the table
- **Hook:** verbatim or near-verbatim as shown or spoken; "/" separates variants of the same hook. Square brackets are the source's own placeholders or bleeps.
- **Heuristic(s) / Emotion / zone / Self targeted / Why it works:** from the lesson pages. Evolve does not use Tether Lab's three-selves model, so **Self targeted** holds the avatar or identity the hook calls out; "—" means the lesson names none. "(derived)" marks a tag added by the lesson page, not said by Spencer.
- **Niche · brand / format:** the niche first (used by the [niche index](#quick-index-by-niche)), then brand and format when known.
- **Source:** every lesson that shows or discusses the hook, with timestamp, sheet row, slide or doc section.
- Provenance tags inside cells: *hypothetical* = Spencer's illustration, not a live ad; *prompt-output* = an AI draft shown in a lesson; *not used* = written and rejected; *untested* = no result given.
