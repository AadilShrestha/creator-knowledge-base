# Word lists (Evolve Copywriting)
> The word-level toolkit of Evolve Copywriting in one place: the Big 4 value words, the 33 power-word swaps, the visual-words check, labels and customer-language swaps, Eugene's 38 headline moves (names, with a link to the examples), the Four U's, and the facts- and objections-list habits that feed them.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a line is accurate but flat, abstract, polished or generic; when tagging which value a competitor hook sells; when you need many headline structures from one claim; when setting up research docs before writing.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a draft line or claim + our facts list, objections list and customer-language bank → the matching list below → a swapped or restructured line that is more concrete, more intense or more "theirs", and still true.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** swap words only where the new word is still true of the product and the avatar's situation; one or two power words per line *(derived)*. [L63 page](../lessons/63-power-words.md)
  - **DO** prefer the customer's word over a thesaurus word: "You should use customer quotes to sound more like them." [L34 00:00-00:53](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md)
  - **DO** back every value word with proof: "safe", "easy" and "fast" need a fact behind them *(derived)*. [L62 image](../lessons/62-value-words.md) [L60 page](../lessons/60-facts-list.md)
  - **DON'T** use a swap or headline move that implies a claim you can't substantiate (Deadly, Toxic, Fatal, Save; "remove limitations", "accuse the claim of being too good"). [L63 page](../lessons/63-power-words.md) [L64 page](../lessons/64-eugenes-headlines.md)
  - **DON'T** reproduce the classic example headlines for our product; copy the move. [L64 page](../lessons/64-eugenes-headlines.md)
- **See also:** [Value words: Big 4](../concepts/value-words-big-4.md) · [Labels and customer language](../concepts/labels-and-customer-language.md) · [Facts over opinions](../concepts/facts-over-opinions.md) · [Objection handling](../concepts/objection-handling.md) · [Rewrites](rewrites.md) · [Templates](templates.md) · [Prompts](prompts.md)

## 1. Value words: the Big 4
The lesson 62 table (credit Kyle Milligan), transcribed: four groups, what the copy must say, and the reader's silent question. [L62 image](../lessons/62-value-words.md) The video-script prompt uses the same four as "the Big 4 emotions": hit 2–3 per section, never force all four ("Quality Over Quantity"). [L72 docx Core Rule 1](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md) [L70 docx Core Rule 1](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md)

| Value words | What you need to say | What they're thinking | Signals the video prompt lists | Course hook examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **NEW / ONLY** | "This is something they've never seen before. It's different from everything else." | "Is this new? Or is it the same old stuff I already tried?" | Discovery language, "first time", contrarian angle, "viral", new mechanism, "first and only" | "Here's why I stopped using regular sheets."; "The viral sheets that regulate body temperature." [L70 docx](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md) |
| **EASY / ANYBODY** | "This is simple to do. It doesn't take a lot of work. Anyone can do this." | "Can I actually do this? Is it hard?" | "without trying", "automatically", "just X", "easiest way", "$1.57 per day", "Just tap below", "Starts working in 2 weeks" | "The easiest way to get rid of dandruff." [L70 docx](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md) |
| **SAFE / PREDICTABLE** | "This is safe. Other people tried it and it worked. You won't lose money if it doesn't work." | "What if I try this and it doesn't work for me?" | Social-proof numbers (real only), "not your fault", scientific validation, "60-day money-back guarantee", "Keep the first jar anyway" | "Can't stop thinking about food? Not your fault." [L70 15:05](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md) |
| **BIG / FAST** | "You'll see big results. It happens fast. You won't have to wait forever." | "Will this actually change my life? Or is it just a small difference?" | Specific timelines and percentages ("24% less, week one", "by day 10"), "Save 20%", bonus stacking, "Less than a coffee" | "After 40, your body makes 3x more of this enzyme." [L70 15:05](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md); "Your toes will never be cold again" [L70 docx](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md) |

How to use it: tag each claim in a draft or competitor ad with a group; for a missing group, check whether its doubt is real for this avatar (objections list), then add one proof-backed line. Pick one lead value per hook. *(derived from the table)* [L62 page](../lessons/62-value-words.md) Spencer's checklist and overview also point to value words "to add perceived value". [L01 04:39](../lessons/01-overview-of-copywriting-program.md)

## 3. Visual words: abstract → concrete

## 5. Headline moves (Eugene's 38)
One claim, 38 ways to state it. The page lists the moves with 62 classic example headlines (Viceroy, Bayer, Rolls Royce, Alka-Seltzer...); the examples stay on [lesson 64](../lessons/64-eugenes-headlines.md#templates). Copy the move, never the headline; check uniqueness with the exact-match search. [L64 page](../lessons/64-eugenes-headlines.md) [L58 page Q9](../lessons/58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md)

| Group (lesson page's grouping) | Moves (number: name as on the page) |
|---|---|
| Quantify, compare, specify the work | 1 Measure the size of the claim · 2 Measure the speed of the claim · 3 Compare the claim · 11 Show how much work, in detail, the claim does · 30 Show how easy the claim is to accomplish by imposing a universally-overcome limitation · 31 State the difference in the headline |
| Make it vivid | 4 Metaphorize the claim · 5 Sensitize the claim (feel, smell, touch, see, hear) · 6 Demonstrate the claim by showing a prime example · 7 Dramatize the claim, or its result · 15 Before-and-after the claim · 21 Symbolize the claim |
| Surprise and challenge beliefs | 8 State the claim as a paradox · 9 Remove limitations from the claim · 23 Startle the reader by contradicting the way he thinks the mechanism should work · 32 Surprise your reader into reading that former limitations have now been overcome · 36 Accuse the claim of being too good · 37 Challenge the prospect's present limiting beliefs |
| Borrow authority, identity, case history | 10 Associate the claim with value or people the prospect wishes to be identified with · 14 Tie authority into the claim · 19 State the claim as a case-history quotation · 26 Turn the claim or the need into a case history |
| Question and address the reader | 12 State the claim as a question · 18 Turn the claim into a challenge for the reader · 33 Address the people who can't buy your product · 34 Address your prospect directly · 38 Turn the claim into a question and answer |
| Information, mechanism, craft | 13 Offer information about how to accomplish the claim · 20 Condense the claim (interchange your product and the product it replaces) · 22 Connect the mechanism to the claim · 24 Connect the need and the claim · 25 Offer information in the ad itself · 27 Give a name to the problem or need · 35 Dramatize how hard it was to produce the claim |
| Newness, exclusivity, emphasis, warning | 16 Stress the newness of the claim · 17 Stress the exclusivity of the claim · 28 Warn the reader about possible pitfalls if he doesn't use the product · 29 Emphasize the claim by its phraseology (two sentences, repetition) |

Lesson-page picks *(derived)*: for curiosity try 8, 18, 23, 25, 33; moves 1–6, 11 and 30 need a real number, speed, comparison or demonstration first; 9, 21 and 36 need proof before use. [L64 page](../lessons/64-eugenes-headlines.md)

## 6. The Four U's (hooks)
From the video prompt (Caples order): **UNIQUE** (most important), **USEFUL**, **URGENT**, **ULTRA-SPECIFIC**. Hit at least three per hook; UNIQUE almost always. Pair with one hook framework: "This is the Biggest Thing Happening Now" or "This is Quirky". [L72 docx The Four U's Checklist](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md) [L72 docx Section A](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)

## 7. Facts list habits
- **Three lists:** all the facts around your **brand**, around your **product**, and the **interesting** facts in your **market**. "Keep this list close by when writing." "Whenever researching, jot down all of the 'FACTS'." [L60 page](../lessons/60-facts-list.md) [L01 04:08](../lessons/01-overview-of-copywriting-program.md)
- **A fact isn't only a number:** a first ("the first stainless steel humidifier"), a count ("four ingredients"), a physical property ("melts in your mouth, not in your hand"), a mechanism ("it's toasted"). Non-numerical facts are harder but more distinctive. [L10 08:35-09:36](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md) [L10 12:49](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
- **Record the source** with each fact so the claim can be substantiated *(derived)*. [L60 page](../lessons/60-facts-list.md)
- **Use it:** when a line is an opinion ("our best formula yet"), find the listed fact that replaces it; when you have a fact, ask "how can you write something that comes from that fact?" [L10 01:06](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md) [L10 10:36-12:17](../lessons/10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md)
- **Never invent** a fact to fill a list ("sold out five times" when it isn't). [L28 08:28-09:03](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)

## 8. Objections list habits
- **Four buckets:** potential objections around your **product**, your **angles**, your **claims**, and those rooted in your **customers' beliefs**. Keep the list close; add to it whenever researching. [L61 page](../lessons/61-objections-list.md) [L01 04:08](../lessons/01-overview-of-copywriting-program.md)
- **Classify before answering** (lesson 28): product / market ("tried it before, this category doesn't work"), claim ("too good to be true"), avatar / belief ("not for me", "I'm not X enough"). [L28 00:29-03:20](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
- **Sources:** reviews, Reddit threads, the ad's own comments ("Does it work for X type of person, like me?"). [L52 15:11-16:05](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md) [L68 00:00-03:32](../lessons/68-how-to-write-image-ads-part-2.md)
- **Use it:** before finalising a script, mark every listed objection as prevented, handled or consciously ignored *(derived)*; pick the technique (fact, reversal, "because", reassign, stream of acceptances, price reframe) from [templates §6](templates.md#6-objection-belief-and-price-templates). [L61 page](../lessons/61-objections-list.md) [L28 07:06-37:00](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
- **Make objection lines specific:** "You've tried supplements before" is "a terrible line"; name what they actually tried. [L70 23:07-24:04](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md)
