# Rulebook (Evolve Copywriting)
> The 720 rule bullets harvested from the 72 Evolve Copywriting lessons, merged into one canonical DO / DON'T / IF…THEN rule per idea, grouped by the job you're doing (research, writing craft, hook, bridge, body, pitch, story, static, video script, iteration, AI use, ethics), each with every lesson that teaches it.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing or QA-ing any asset, settling a creative decision ("do we put the offer in the hook?"), briefing a creator, or deciding what to do with a test result.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the job you're on → the matching section → the applicable rule IDs → a checklist for the draft, or a decision with its citation.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** treat [§12 Ethics and compliance](#12-ethics-and-compliance) as hard constraints that override any other rule or prompt output.
  - **DO** cite rule IDs (e.g. "H14, B4") in briefs and reviews so a human can trace the reason.
  - **DO** read Spencer's rules as pattern recognition, not law: "not every single ad follows this, and not every successful ad follows this, but a lot of them do." [L08 00:00-00:41](../lessons/08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md)
  - **DON'T** treat rules marked *(derived)* as Spencer's words; they are the lesson page's inference.
  - **IF** two rules conflict **THEN** use [Conflicts at a glance](#conflicts-at-a-glance) and the course [contradictions page](../contradictions.md).

## 1. Research and strategy
### Avatars and beliefs
- **RS1** **DO** build avatars from a desire first, then experiences, behaviours and emotions; add demographics only if needed. Experiences and behaviours reveal beliefs and pain points. **DON'T** rely on demographics alone. — [L17 00:00-00:58](../lessons/17-being-aware-of-avatars.md) · [L15 00:34-01:13](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
- **RS2** **DO** answer "Who am I writing this for?" and "Why do they care?" (as "they want [desire] but they believe [obstacle]") before writing, and re-check the finished headline: "who did I write this for, and are they going to care?" — [L17 00:00-02:20](../lessons/17-being-aware-of-avatars.md) · [L17 01:42](../lessons/17-being-aware-of-avatars.md) · [L17 04:26](../lessons/17-being-aware-of-avatars.md)
- **RS3** **DON'T** reuse one headline across avatars ("Never overcook chicken again" fails for a pro chef). **IF** the audience changes **THEN** change the headline and the angle, not just the visual *(derived)*. — [L17 02:57-04:26](../lessons/17-being-aware-of-avatars.md)
- **RS4** **DO** record beliefs as their own field in avatar research, co-equal with desires, and keep a running list of beliefs while researching: "when you can identify them, you're able to write copy that goes with them." — [L15 00:34-01:13](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) · [L15 03:56-04:40](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) · [L15 14:03-15:32](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
- **RS5** **DO** write each belief as a statement the person is certain of ("it's not about what you eat, it's about how much"), reverse-engineered from actions, behaviours, experiences and emotions. — [L15 10:29-12:46](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
- **RS6** **DON'T** write a claim that violates an established belief: "if you violate your customer's established beliefs, even in the slightest, nothing you promise them will matter." Your most powerful claim is not always your most powerful headline. — [L15 03:56-05:30](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
- **RS7** **IF** a good claim isn't converting **THEN** suspect a missing or violated belief before changing the claim. — [L15 01:13](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) · [L15 14:03](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)
- **RS8** **IF** you need to change a belief **THEN** find what formed it (experience vs influence) and bring a matching or stronger authority or experience; experience-formed beliefs are hardest to move. — [L15 06:22-08:49](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) · [L15 14:03-14:53](../lessons/15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)

### Research lists and inputs
- **RS9** **DO** keep three running fact lists while researching (brand, product, interesting market facts), each fact with its source, and keep them beside you while writing. — [L01 04:08](../lessons/01-overview-of-copywriting-program.md) · [L60 page](../lessons/60-facts-list.md)
- **RS10** **DO** keep an objection list in four buckets (product, angles, claims, customer beliefs), add to it whenever you research, and check every claim and angle against it. — [L01 04:08](../lessons/01-overview-of-copywriting-program.md) · [L61 page](../lessons/61-objections-list.md) · [L28 03:20-03:50](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md)
- **RS11** **DO** build a customer-language bank: exact quotes, sayings, labels; jot down any term you don't recognise; search the sub-avatar's behaviour + "reddit" and copy the quotes, emotions and behaviours into the doc. — [L34 06:51-09:25](../lessons/34-how-to-write-with-labels-customer-language.md) · [L68 00:00-03:32](../lessons/68-how-to-write-image-ads-part-2.md)
- **RS12** **DO** keep one master document (Product, Research, Avatars, Angles/Ideas, New Information, New Mechanisms) and feed it to the model: "the more context you give it about who your customer is, the better." — [L67 00:00](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md) · [L67 23:40](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md) · [L69 page](../lessons/69-action-item-write-your-image-ad-briefs.md)
- **RS13** **DO** produce at least one new ad idea per working session from one of five sources: customer research, another brand's ads, our own ads, a marketing book, an ad that stopped your scroll. — [L03 05:10-06:11](../lessons/03-how-to-guarantee-you-get-better-at-copywriting.md)
- **RS14** **DO** read top ads actively: state what each line *communicates*, not just what it says. — [L03 04:10-04:40](../lessons/03-how-to-guarantee-you-get-better-at-copywriting.md) · [L09 00:00-01:29](../lessons/09-the-importance-of-how-you-say-it.md)
- **RS15** **DO** track competitors "not so you can copy their ads" but to separate yourself. — [L55 07:48](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)

### Angles and desire
- **RS16** **DO** write every angle as a plain reason to buy, list two or three per product, and define it even when the concept is a format or sub-avatar test. — [L18 00:00-03:42](../lessons/18-being-aware-of-angles.md) · [L18 page text](../lessons/18-being-aware-of-angles.md) · [L67 Angle-prompt doc, Knowledge](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md)
- **RS17** **DON'T** pack several angles into one ad; keep the main angle in the hook and push secondary angles into sub-headlines or body copy. — [L18 03:00-04:32](../lessons/18-being-aware-of-angles.md)
- **RS18** **DO** remember "we can not create desires, we can only channel them." — [L22 DesiresCore Avatar A2](../lessons/22-how-to-finalize-your-ad-ideas.md)

### Mechanisms
- **RS19** **DO** attach a mechanism to every claim ("What is the mechanism that can prove it?"), preferring visual mechanisms people can "put two and two together" on. — [L19 00:00-00:46](../lessons/19-being-aware-of-mechanisms.md) · [L19 02:15-04:23](../lessons/19-being-aware-of-mechanisms.md) · [L14 04:26-05:08](../lessons/14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md)
- **RS20** **DO** advertise the angle or feeling, not the mechanism's name ("a hoodie that feels like a hug", not "a weighted hoodie"); the mechanism explains why they believe it. — [L19 01:43-02:15](../lessons/19-being-aware-of-mechanisms.md)
- **RS21** **DO** write the mechanism as the agent of the result ("this mechanism is what allows you to get X"), explain how it works enough to feel believable, back it with a person who got Y using it, and don't over-explain past the desire. — [L14 04:26-05:59](../lessons/14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md)
- **RS22** **DO** search a product for mechanisms in order: truly new → new ones we could add → unspoken (useful, but nobody says it) → worn out but still valued (rename it). **IF** nothing is unique **THEN** pick the unspoken or renamed one that is useful and makes sense ("toasted" explains taste; "made in Wyoming" may not). — [L14 09:26-13:54](../lessons/14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md) · [L14 12:20](../lessons/14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md) · [L14 15:36-16:31](../lessons/14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md)
- **RS23** **DON'T** add a feature just because it's new; it must be useful (stainless steel beats LED mood lights). It "doesn't have to be better. It just has to be new", but long term the product should be better too. — [L14 02:58-04:26](../lessons/14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md) · [L14 06:49-09:26](../lessons/14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md)
- **RS24** **IF** the market is sophisticated about a mechanism **THEN** refresh or rename it. **DON'T** expect every great ad to state its mechanism, but "a lot of the best ads do". — [L19 02:15-03:45](../lessons/19-being-aware-of-mechanisms.md)

### Awareness
- **RS25** **DO** decide the awareness level before writing: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware or most aware. — [L21 17:23-18:21](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **RS26** **DO** match the job to the level: unaware → surface a problem or desire they aren't thinking about; problem aware → dramatise the need vividly; solution aware → discuss the other options; product aware → speak to what they believe after trying the category; most aware → push with an offer or reassurance. — [L21 02:57-08:33](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md) · [L21 13:35-15:45](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **RS27** **DO** write the shortest copy for most aware and the longest for unaware: you must walk people through every stage you skip. **IF** offers dominate your account **THEN** you're mostly reaching most- and product-aware viewers. — [L21 15:45-16:24](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **RS28** **DON'T** underestimate awareness: people search, ask ChatGPT and buy "in 10 minutes". Naming a problem to someone who didn't know they had it is unaware work, not problem aware. — [L21 00:45-02:15](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md) · [L21 04:45-05:30](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md) · [L21 11:15](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)
- **RS29** **IF** the audience tried the category and it failed **THEN** address the belief that failure created ("pee pads don't work") or bring a new mechanism; **DON'T** lead with "try this [category]". **IF** you struggle at product aware **THEN** check positioning, mechanism, authority and customer knowledge. — [L21 11:15-14:32](../lessons/21-being-aware-of-market-awareness.md)

### Positioning
- **RS30** **DO** make every piece reinforce or establish a position and get an action, not just inform; write the position before the ad ("What position do you own?" — if you can't answer, that's likely the problem). — [L02 03:05-04:08](../lessons/02-the-current-state-of-copywriting.md) · [L55 00:37-01:33](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md) · [L56 00:00-00:34](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
- **RS31** **DON'T** claim to be better: "you should never, ever say that you're better, because if you were, you'd be number one." Claim a difference; fill the tables with "How are you different?", not "Why are you better?" **IF** everyone says "we're the best X" **THEN** that's the "me too" trap. — [L55 19:00-19:32](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md) · [L55 23:16](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md) · [L56 03:23-04:01](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md) · [L02 08:57-09:29](../lessons/02-the-current-state-of-copywriting.md) · [L42 00:50](../lessons/42-the-three-enemies-of-storytelling.md)
- **RS32** **IF** you can be first **THEN** name a new category or sub-category; **IF** not **THEN** reposition the leader by contrast (who it's for, what it avoids) or take the inverse position (timeless vs trendy). — [L55 20:26-24:15](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md) · [L56 02:35](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
- **RS33** **DO** name a strategic enemy (an approach, a belief or a competitor) and say "no" to something competitors won't give up. — [L55 25:04-31:33](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md) · [L42 03:40](../lessons/42-the-three-enemies-of-storytelling.md)
- **RS34** **DO** research the prospect's mind (category, leader, owned positions, review complaints) to find the obvious-but-missed hole, and check the customer is willing to accept the position. **DON'T** pick a position someone already owns. — [L55 33:49-39:31](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md) · [L56 00:00-02:04](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md) · [L56 04:01-04:46](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
- **RS35** **DO** keep the position narrow enough to be real and broad enough to have demand; **IF** you're small **THEN** "it's better to be more narrow". — [L56 05:41-06:24](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
- **RS36** **DO** make the difference visible ("seeing is believing"), and fill the positioning exercise from real competitor, review and product facts even if you run it through Claude. — [L55 13:42](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md) · [L55 19:32-20:26](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md) · [L56 03:23-05:41](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
- **RS37** **DON'T** build a strategy on copy-pasting a trending template (the native / camouflage wave): "is going to die fast." — [L02 10:01](../lessons/02-the-current-state-of-copywriting.md)

### Idea memo
- **RS38** **DO** write each idea as a memo: WHY (evidence from our own account), WHAT (structure and a fill-in line), HOW (a reference ad link). — [L22 00:50-03:42](../lessons/22-how-to-finalize-your-ad-ideas.md)

## 12. Ethics and compliance
Hard constraints. They override any rule, template or prompt output.
- **X1** **DON'T** invent facts: sell-out counts, review counts, "X people switched", study numbers. Only state facts that are "100% true" and verifiable; invented facts "re-sophisticate the market". — [L28 08:28-09:03](../lessons/28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) · [L29 10:49](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
- **X2** **DON'T** fabricate social proof: use the tier that matches real volume or attributable borrowed proof; never claim your own 10,000 reviews, invent customer names or stories, or attribute made-up stats to the brand. — [L70 12:01-12:44](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md) · [L70 docx Borrowed Proof Protocol](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md) · [L72 docx Social Proof Scaling System](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md)
- **X3** **DON'T** claim an endorsement you don't have: borrowed authority says "we noticed X about Navy SEALs", not "Navy SEALs endorse us"; say "experts say" unless compliance clears a name. — [L16 21:13-23:57](../lessons/16-why-you-need-to-understand-authority.md) · [L16 25:45](../lessons/16-why-you-need-to-understand-authority.md)
- **X4** **DON'T** fake or exaggerate danger: "my number one rule is just please don't be a piece of shit with this"; scaring people for no reason is short-term and can bring "very serious legal charges". Fear must be real, "not malicious". — [L26 09:15-09:42](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md) · [L26 12:30-12:48](../lessons/26-how-to-write-open-loops.md) · [L67 Angle doc, ref slide 16](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md)
- **X5** **DO** keep facts true in every iteration: "always take the facts and tell the truth"; don't invent authority stories (the grandpa is an illustration, not a template to fake). — [L52 02:19](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
- **X6** **DON'T** pretend AI UGC is a real person or give an AI avatar a human first-person experience. — [L53 08:51-09:37](../lessons/53-the-iteration-playbook-formats-styles.md) · [L71 16:01](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
- **X7** **IF** you can't confirm a claim on the product page **THEN** soften or drop it ("rare African fruit" → "African fruit"): "Some claims you can push, some claims you don't want to push." — [L71 21:28](../lessons/71-how-to-write-video-ads-part-2.md)
- **X8** **DO** use compliant wording ("could be", not "you have"); be careful with phrases like "root cause" on Meta (Spencer unsure, unverified). — [L27 15:47-16:12](../lessons/27-how-to-write-a-bridge.md) · [L68 34:44](../lessons/68-how-to-write-image-ads-part-2.md)
- **X9** **DON'T** over-claim ("if you're claiming like it's a wonder drug... you're going to run into belief issues"); don't use power words or headline moves that imply claims you can't substantiate *(derived)*. — [L67 20:54](../lessons/67-how-to-write-image-ads-part-1.md) · [L63 page](../lessons/63-power-words.md) · [L64 page](../lessons/64-eugenes-headlines.md)
- **X10** **DO** keep urgency and scarcity real: urgency is why to act now, not "sale ends tonight"; only run sale or sell-out lines that are true. — [L50 07:40-08:35](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md) · [L72 docx Section D](../lessons/72-action-item-write-your-video-ad-briefs.md) · [L29 11:40-12:58](../lessons/29-how-to-pitch-without-rejection.md)
