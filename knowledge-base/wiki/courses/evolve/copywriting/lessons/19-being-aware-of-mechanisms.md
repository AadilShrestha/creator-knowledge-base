# 19. Being aware of mechanisms
> Every claim or angle needs a mechanism that explains how the product delivers it, because the mechanism is "what builds belief" and "your proof"; for each claim ask "what is the mechanism that can prove it?"

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** pairing each angle/claim with proof in a script; auditing a competitor ad ("what is the thing that makes the claim believable?"); deciding what to show on screen.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a claim or angle → its mechanism (a concrete, ideally visual product or brand feature) and a line that shows it.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** attach a mechanism to every claim: "if you're going to make a claim... there has to be some kind of mechanism that is shown to back up the claim." [02:15–03:04]
  - **DO** run this exercise on every claim: "What is the mechanism that can prove it?" [03:45–04:23]
  - **DO** prefer mechanisms that are visual and easy to picture ("it's pretty visual, and people can put two and two together"). [00:00–00:46]
  - **DO** advertise the angle, not the mechanism's name: "I'm not advertising that this is a weighted hoodie. I'm advertising that this is a hoodie that feels like a hug." The mechanism then explains why you believe it. [01:43–02:15]
  - **DON'T** expect every great ad to state its mechanism, but "a lot of the best ads do". [03:04–03:45]
  - **IF** the market is sophisticated about a mechanism **THEN** the mechanism needs refreshing or renaming, which is where new mechanisms come from (see [14](14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md)). [02:15]
  - **IF** a mechanism is about the brand, not the product (e.g. donating to charity), it can still back a brand-level angle. [00:46–01:43]
- **See also:** [14. Why you need to use unique mechanisms](14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md) · [18. Being aware of angles](18-being-aware-of-angles.md) · [20. Being aware of authorities](20-being-aware-of-authorities.md) · [15. Why you need to understand beliefs](15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md)

## Key ideas

### 1. The mechanism delivers the desire (or the angle) [00:00–00:46]
- The lesson's question (page text): "What is the mechanism that delivers the desire?" The instructor narrows it to "what delivers the angle". He says mechanisms are "so crucial... to build belief". [00:00–00:46]
- Grüns: angle "Your child will enjoy taking their greens"; mechanism "It's in a gummy bear format". It is "pretty visual": "my kid loves eating gummy bears and these are just gummy bears that have greens in them." [00:00–00:46]

- The mechanism "is essentially what builds belief and essentially is your proof that what you're saying your product does, it actually does." [01:43–02:15]
- He notes the visibility problem: "this is the problem that so many of you guys are struggling with" (non-visual mechanisms), and says copywriting techniques later address it. [00:46]

### 3. Claim, angle and mechanism travel together [02:15–03:04]
- He uses "claim" and "angle" interchangeably here. Chain for Grüns: avatar = parents whose kids struggle to take greens; desire = "I want my kid to take more greens"; angle = "your child is going to enjoy taking this"; mechanism = gummy, "and kids like gummies". [02:15–03:04]
- Mechanism strength depends on market sophistication; where mechanisms are worn, "new mechanisms are created". [02:15]

### 4. Avis: positioning as mechanism [03:04–03:45]
- Not on the slide: Avis's "We try harder." "Sure you do. But... their mechanism is essentially there, their positioning is essentially there... They said we're number two. And so even that in itself is a mechanism. That builds the belief... it makes sense why they have to try harder because they're not the best company." (He says students saw this in the principles section.) [03:04–03:45]

### 5. The exercise [03:45–04:23]
- "Whenever we make a claim, I always say, what is the mechanism that can prove it? And if you start doing that, you'll start thinking a lot deeper about the ads that you're writing and probably write a lot better ads as well." [03:45–04:23]

## Steps / framework
1. Write the claim/angle as a reason to buy ([18](18-being-aware-of-angles.md)).
2. Ask: what is the mechanism that can prove it? Choose a concrete thing (format, container, ingredient, process, brand action).
3. Test: is it visual? Can the viewer "put two and two together"? If not, find a way to show it.
4. Write the line "[claim], because [mechanism]" for the body; keep the claim in the hook, the mechanism in the proof section.
5. Pair with authority ([20](20-being-aware-of-authorities.md)) if the mechanism alone does not carry belief.

## How to apply
- [ ] For each claim in a draft, write the mechanism next to it; if blank, add proof or drop the claim.
- [ ] In teardowns, record the mechanism for each competitor angle (see [14](14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md) for types: new, unspoken, renamed).
- [ ] Prefer a mechanism the viewer can see in the first seconds (a can, a gummy, a weighted hoodie).
- [ ] Keep the claim/angle in the hook; mechanism in the proof beat.
