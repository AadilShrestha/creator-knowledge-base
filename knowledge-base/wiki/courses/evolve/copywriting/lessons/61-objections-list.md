# 61. Objections list
> A standing research habit: keep a running list of every objection around your product, angles, claims and customers' beliefs, and keep it close while writing.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** researching a brand or competitor, planning an angle, or checking whether a draft has left an obvious doubt unanswered.
- **Inputs → outputs:** reviews, comments, competitor ad comments, customer research → a living "objections list" in four buckets → doubts to handle in the script (see lesson 28's techniques).
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** list the potential objections in four buckets: around the product, around the angles, around the claims, and those rooted in the customer's beliefs. [page]
  - **DO** keep the list close while writing and add to it "whenever researching". [page]
  - **IF** a draft makes a claim or uses an angle **THEN** check the list for its objections and decide how the copy prevents or handles each. (derived; the techniques are in [lesson 28](28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md))
  - **DON'T** only collect objections for the product; angle, claim and belief objections are separate buckets on the page. [page]
- **See also:** [28. How to prevent/handle objections in writing](28-how-to-prevent-handle-objections-in-writing.md) · [15. Why you need to understand beliefs](15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) · [60. Facts list](60-facts-list.md) · [Customer research stack](../../../../frameworks/customer-research-stack.md)

## Key ideas

### What to list [page text]
"Identify" the potential objections around:
- your product
- your angles
- your claims
- the objections rooted from your customers' beliefs

Then: "Keep this list close by when writing." And: "Whenever researching, jot down all of the 'OBJECTIONS'."

## Steps / framework
(derived layout; the buckets themselves are the page's)

| Bucket | What to capture | Pair with (derived) |
|---|---|---|
| Product | doubts about the product itself | facts list, proof, demo |
| Angles | doubts triggered by the angle we choose | reframe or bridge |
| Claims | "I do not believe this will work / is true" | facts, show-don't-tell |
| Customer beliefs | objections that come from what they are already certain of | [lesson 15](15-why-you-need-to-understand-beliefs.md) |

1. Create four headings in the research document.
2. Log each objection in the customer's own wording where possible (derived), with where you saw it.
3. Before finalizing a script, tick every listed objection as prevented, handled or consciously ignored.

## Prompts / templates (verbatim)
Page text, verbatim:

```text
Identify

* The potential objections around your product
* The potential objections around your angles
* The potential objections around your claims
* The objections rooted from your customers beliefs

Keep this list close by when writing

Whenever researching, jot down all of the "OBJECTIONS"
```

## How to apply
- [ ] Open an objections list with four buckets for each brand and each angle.
- [ ] Add objections during research (reviews, ad comments, surveys) with source notes.
- [ ] For every claim in a draft, name the likeliest objection and where the copy answers it.
- [ ] Mark any unanswered objection and revise or accept the risk explicitly.
