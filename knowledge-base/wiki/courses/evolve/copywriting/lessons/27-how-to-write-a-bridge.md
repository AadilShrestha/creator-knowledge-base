# 27. How to write a bridge
> The hook's one job is to stop the scroll; the bridge's one job is to keep the viewer, and it is where people decide to keep watching or drop off. Six bridge patterns (mechanism, escalate the problem, escalate the desire, reposition the problem, allude to new information, set the timeline), plus the "in medias res" move of cutting the hook and starting at the bridge, which produced a $1M+ ad.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a hook exists and the next 3-8 seconds of the script need writing; a hook gets stopped scrolls but poor retention; making a "hookless" variation of a finished script.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a hook + product mechanism + avatar problem/desire + authority (if any) -> one bridge per hook, matched to one of six patterns, written so the viewer sees the escalation; optional hook-cut variation.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** give the bridge one goal: "keep their attention." The hook's goal is to stop the scroll. [00:00-00:31]
  - **DO** customise the first seconds per hook: "We're definitely not just writing a hook and then having everything go to the same bridge." The first ~8 seconds are custom for each hook in the course's 3-2-2 test structure. [23:06-23:52]
  - **IF** you can't bridge a hook (it "doesn't make any sense") **THEN** the hook isn't doing its job; rewrite the hook. [00:31-01:00]
  - **DO** pick the bridge pattern by what the hook sets up: mechanism, escalate problem, escalate desire, reposition problem, allude to new information, or set a timeline. [02:15-04:39]
  - **DO** show the escalation, don't just say it: not "now the problem is worse" but "now my kids don't want to eat their greens at all". [08:34-09:02]
  - **DO** call out the avatar in the bridge ("I thought I was being a good parent... after Y months the problem was still happening"). [08:10-08:34]
  - **DO** stack bridges (e.g. escalate problem, then a timeline: "give me the next 90 seconds"). [07:55]
  - **DO** be careful and compliant with "could be" language on reposition bridges ("It could be something living inside of you", not "you have"). [15:47-16:12]
  - **DO** write the whole ad first, then cut off the hook and play it; if the ad still makes sense, test it as a variation (a fourth hook: 4-2-2 instead of 3-2-2). [20:13-20:59; 23:06-24:05]
  - **DO** make sure the bridge carries context if it will serve as a hook ("ideally your bridge has some kind of context... otherwise this won't be as effective"). [22:51-23:06; page text]
  - **DON'T** open with "in this video I'm going to explain..." Just start in the action. [24:05-24:24]
  - **DO** for product-aware markets, use a strong hook then introduce the mechanism immediately and show it happening. [02:27-03:41]

## Key ideas

### 1. What the bridge does [00:00-02:15]
- Once the hook "accomplishes goal number one, which is grab the person's attention", the bridge's goal is "to keep their attention". [00:00-00:31]
- "Even if you're just grabbing someone's attention doesn't mean that it's going to convert." [00:11-00:31]
- After a hook "most people will stop and read the hook. And then either they keep watching because it leads into something interesting, which is essentially the bridge, or we lose them." The bridge is "how we keep an audience hooked in and engaged" and the point where "the customer decides whether or not they want to keep watching or drop off". [00:31-01:19]
- **Bridges can be hooks (in medias res).** "You basically just start right in the action." Credit for naming the strategy to a colleague whom Whisper writes as "Sarah Levenger" (spelling unverified). Cutting the hook completely "can lead to breakthrough ads". [01:19-01:47]
- **The $1M ad:** "One of the ads that we made, which a single ad spent over a million dollars, was actually just an in medias res hook... a bridge hook, where you just don't have a hook, you just start at the bridge." (instructor claim; the page says "single ad spent $1M"). [01:47-02:15; page text]

### 2. The six bridge patterns [02:15-04:39]
He says it is "not every single way" but the commonalities from reviewing ads and "multiple ad accounts spending a million dollars plus a month". [02:15-02:56]
1. **Introduce the mechanism, then bridge back to the benefit (P-B-P).** "A strategy that I don't see a lot of people doing." If you have a great mechanism, introduce it right away; in product-aware markets, an open loop or strong headline, then the mechanism, shown happening, "is an extremely effective way. And this is the exact strategy that we use to make the million dollar ad." He spent "about an hour and a half, two hours" searching for other brands doing this and "couldn't find a lot of people doing this"; recommends testing it as a bridge and a hook. Works especially for "more visual products". [02:15-03:41]
2. **Escalate the problem** (relatability / emotion). [03:41]
3. **Escalate the desire** (relatability / emotion; "sometimes we use both"). [03:41]
4. **Reposition the problem**: "You don't have this. This is not what you have. It's this instead." "Ideally... with some form of an authority", including "some really interesting AI ads". [03:41-04:10]
5. **Allude to new information**, "ideally from an authority": the authority shares what they found, which repositions or clarifies the problem. [04:10-04:39]
6. **Set the timeline** (how long / what you will explain): "here's what I'm going to explain in this video" or "here's what happens over this period of time". Common in bigger brands; viewers know "what they're about to watch and what they're about to get". [04:39-05:01]

### 3. #1 Introduce the mechanism + tie back to the promise [05:01-07:10]
- Refers to the "PPB framework that we talked about with Eugene" (Whisper writes "PPB"; the page labels it "P-B-P"; the framework itself is not explained in this lesson). [05:01-05:20]
- **Framework A** (from a brand they are "crushing" with; creative can't be shown): Hook "If you have X, I need to show you this." Bridge "The X MECHANISM helped me get DESIRE (CONTROL). And the Y MECHANISM helps with COMMON PROBLEM." The product has two mechanisms working together; the first earns a control-based desire, the second removes a common blocker. "We didn't escalate the desire here"; "this is the bridge that we wrote". [05:01-05:49]
- **Framework B:** Hook "Watch how this [PRODUCT] stops [PROBLEM]". Bridge "First, this [PRODUCT] has a [NEW MECHANISM]. And the [NEW MECHANISM] is what actually stops [PROBLEM] and causes [VISUAL SOLUTION OF PROBLEM]." In their ad "we weren't even showing it. We were just explaining it with words... using words that show and not tell": "you probably noticed this thing happening, and as a result of using our new mechanism this no longer happens" or a more positive behaviour starts. [05:49-06:50]
- No visual examples: "I can't share their creative"; he promises to add some if found. [06:50-07:10]

### 4. #2 Escalate the problem [07:10-11:04]
- **Dandruff:** Hook "What if everything you knew about dandruff was wrong?" (negative hook that opens a loop). Bridge "There's an uncomfortable truth about those embarrassing white flakes on your shoulders, and it's way grosser than you think." Escalates and charges with emotion. The ad also stacks a timeline bridge ("give me the next 90 seconds and I'll explain this"). (Skool still: split screen, a comb scraping a scalp over a man at a microphone, on-screen "What if everything".) [07:10-08:10]
- **Template:** Hook "Watch if you use X". Bridge "I thought I was being a X AVATAR, but after Y MONTHS, PROBLEM WAS STILL HAPPENING, and now PROBLEM IS EVEN WORSE." Calls out the avatar (relatability) and escalates. "Rather than saying this verbatim, explain and show visually": e.g. "I used to struggle to get them to eat greens, now they're not eating it at all." [08:10-09:02]
- **Three red flags of vitamin deficiency in kids:** Bridge "Only wanting chicken nuggets? Red flag. Sugar cravings? Red flag. Low appetite? Red flag." Relatable symptoms are each labelled red flags, so "that's a problem... this is also a problem... Oh my goodness, this is serious." (Skool still: child with mac and cheese, on-screen "3 red flags of vitamin deficiency in kids".) [09:02-09:36]
- **Nonverbal toddler:** Hook "This is how my non-verbal toddler found his voice." Bridge "my three-year-old has always struggled to speak and was recently diagnosed as non-verbal." Viewers with a three-year-old who struggles to speak think "they might be diagnosed as nonverbal"; "much more serious... higher stakes." [09:36-10:32]
- **Dental powder for dogs:** Hook "Dental powder cleans dog teeth fast." Bridge "Are you one of the 96% of dog owners who don't clean their dog's teeth daily? Well, by age 3, you could be setting up your dog for a lifetime of dental issues." First line is relatable and gets acceptance ("yeah, I am one of those people"); then it escalates. "Holy cow, I really need to watch this to figure out what the solution is." (Page script says 96%; the Skool still shows a 90% donut chart and "of dog owners".) [10:32-11:04]

### 5. #3 Escalate the desire [11:04-13:37]
- **Dog nail trimming:** Hook "Here is how to safely trim your dog's nails without fear, stress, or risk of infection" (juxtaposition: nail trimming usually has all of that). Bridge "You're probably dreading tomorrow's nail battle as much as your dog is, but what if I showed you dogs actually falling asleep during trims." Mostly escalating the desire to "the absolute dream outcome... imagine your dog is just sleeping and you can trim its nails"; "dreading" is good emotional word choice. (Skool still: Today's Find Facebook ad for "Silent Groom Pro", created Sep 23, 2025: "Is nail trimming a nightmare for your dog? Switch to the Silent Groom Pro for a safe, quiet, and straightforward experience!", video 3:21, overlay "HERE'S HOW TO SAFELY".) [11:04-12:21; img05]
- **Dr. Squatch:** Hook "Packs of women are going feral for the new Dr. Squatch scent." Bridge "If you smell like this... I'm definitely coming over." The desire escalates through copy and visuals (a woman running toward the man; on-screen "If you smell like THIS... I'm definitely coming over"; disclosure "Those appearing in this video were compensated for their time, testimonial, and use of their image"). [12:21-12:39; img06]
- **Anti-aging example (hook + bridge):** Hook "My husband didn't want me to use this, so I bought more" (juxtaposition). Bridge "Nobody believes I'm 54 anymore. They're convinced I got some expensive treatment" ("third-party recognition", the dream outcome; "we've blown it right out of proportion"). No image on the page. [12:39-13:37]

### 6. #4 Reposition the problem [13:37-16:17]
- **AI stomach ad (weight):** Hook "Stop starving me, you're making things worse..." Bridge "I'm chubby because your cortisol is high, and now you're spiking it even higher because I'm hungry and stressed." The solution is not to starve yourself; the stomach itself is the "authority" talking (an AI animated character; Skool still: 3D cartoon stomach saying "cortisol is high,"). [13:37-14:16; img07]
- **Scratch mittens (baby eczema/itch):** Hook "You don't need scratch mittens. You need him to stop scratching." Bridge "Mittens control the scratching, but what's triggering the itch? Remove what's causing it." Even on an image there is a bridge; the click-through to an advertorial/listicle can carry the rest. "I'm putting a bandaid on a deeper problem." He warns "be careful with saying root cause". (Skool still: Shower Envy ad, "order.ecowaterspa.com", "America's #1 Filtered...", dated Feb 12, 2026.) [14:16-15:17; img08]
- **Ryze (itching at night):** Hook "If your [butt] won't stop itching at night..." (the page uses an emoji; the instructor says the word aloud). Bridge "The problem isn't skin related. Be careful because it could be something living inside of you." He praises the careful, compliant wording ("it could be", not "you have"). He calls Ryze (Whisper "Rise") "the kings of... repositioning problems"; not an authority exactly, but the speech pattern works. [15:17-16:17; img09]

### 7. #5 Allude to new information [16:17-18:22]
- **Compression socks:** Hook "The hollow fiber revolution that's making cardiologists trash their own compression socks happened completely by accident." Bridge "So why do most compression socks feel so uncomfortable? It turns out we've been using the wrong materials." The hook is "a bit long drawn out" but has an authority and many unanswered questions. (Skool still: on-screen "THE HOLLOW FIBER"; also the Hollow Alpaca socks seen in lesson 24.) [16:17-16:56; img10]
- **Four cats:** Hook "I've had four cats in my life, and I'm embarrassed to say I just learned this." Bridge "My cat was trying to teach me something for years, but I ignored him. Every time he scratched my couch instead of his scratcher, he was actually showing me what he needed." Suspense plus new information; "the cat in this way is essentially an authority". (Skool still: woman kissing a white cat, on-screen "IGNORED".) [16:56-17:53; img11]
- **Ticks:** Hook "Ever wonder why tick preventatives don't work anymore?" Bridge "My vet's revelation revealed something that the pet industry doesn't want you to know." (Skool still: a dog with a tick-infested ear, on-screen "exposed a deadly secret".) The instructor notes it is dramatised, but sets up that they will learn something new. [17:53-18:22; img12]

### 8. #6 Set the timeline [18:22-19:44]
- **Laundry:** Hook "It took me 30 years of doing laundry to learn this." Bridge "But I'll teach it to you in about one minute." Authority is established immediately ("30 years"), then the timeline. (Skool still: Earth Breeze laundry sheets ad, video 2:39, on-screen "TO YOU IN ABOUT", ad copy "Skip the heavy jugs and messy pods. Earth Breeze laundry sheets are easy to use, mess-free, and tough on stains.") [18:22-19:06; img13]
- **Mushroom coffee:** Hook "Most people don't realize how fast mushroom coffee works." Bridge "Here's what happens inside your body in the first seven days." Works because mushroom coffee is a big market; also "alluding to new information" in the hook. (Skool still: man drinking from a mug, caption "IN THE FIRST 7 DAYS.") [19:06-19:44; img14]

### 9. In medias res: bridges that are hooks [19:44-24:24]
- Recap: the million-dollar ad "just started it right at the bridge": the new-mechanism bridge ("if you have X, watch this") with the hook cut off: "This X mechanism helped me get this desire and Y mechanism helps me with common problem." "Just not having a hook at all, just going straight to the bridge is what essentially led to this million dollar ad." [19:44-20:39]
- **Pro-tip:** "Don't think about doing this; just write your entire ad, then literally look at the three hooks you wrote for your 3-2-2 and cut the hook off and play it. See if the ad still makes sense and test that as a variation... rather than 3-2-2 you can do 4-2-2." [20:13-20:59; page text]
- **Ryze (hookless):** "He doesn't say anything other than 'drink alcohol if you want to damage your liver, if you want to repair your liver, eat [something like king trumpet mushroom]'." No hook; he is an authority in a white coat (name tag on the Skool still reads "Blane Schilling, M.D."). [20:59-21:28; img15]
- **The Groovd (children's copybooks):** "The unique groove design of these books." Doesn't say "here's the exact book I use to keep my kid focused". Starts "right at the point". (Skool still: ad copy "Get Your Magic Copybooks Now thegroovd.com/magicbook"; overlay "The unique groove design of these books".) [21:28-21:48; img16]
- **Based Supplies (skincare):** "Yes, we make skincare from beef fat. Everyone thought we were crazy until their kid's eczema finally healed." "A little less in medias res" because of the title "We make skincare for eczema". "We're just starting right inside of the action of this girl cutting this beef fat." Post copy (screenshot at 22:00, dated Jan 29): "Most bathroom counters look like a chemistry lab. Dozens of products, zero results. Here's what the beauty industry won't tell you: beef tallow + raw honey can fix what 12 products can't. Tallow is bioidentical to skin's natural oils - instead of sitting on top creating a film, it absorbs completely and repairs from within. For anyone who's exhausted from trying everything with nothing to show for it, the answer might be to try almost nothing. Get your jar today and finally see yourself in the mirror again. 30-day money-back guarantee because we know it works!" [21:48-22:25; img17; frame 22:00]
- Observation: with an ad that has "literally no hook at all", you still have the context of what they're talking about (Ryze liver, Groovd books, Based beef fat). In the mushroom case "I don't have as much context until I see the mushroom come in." [22:25-22:51]
- Close: "Write your ad, cut the hook off and see what happens"; "look to see if there's an action shot, even if it's later in the ad, where you just start it right there" (more on payoffs in a later module). "You don't try to set anything up. You don't be like 'in this video I'm going to explain...'." [23:06-24:24]

## Steps / framework

| # | Bridge type | When the hook... | Formula | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce the mechanism (P-B-P) | names a problem/product ("Watch how this stops X"; "If you have X, I need to show you this") | "First, this [PRODUCT] has a [NEW MECHANISM]. And the [NEW MECHANISM] is what actually stops [PROBLEM] and causes [VISUAL SOLUTION]." / "The X mechanism helped me get DESIRE. The Y mechanism helps with COMMON PROBLEM." | [05:01-07:10] |
| 2 | Escalate the problem | opens a loop about a pain | name the avatar, show the problem worsening, label relatable symptoms as red flags, give a statistic then a consequence | [07:10-11:04] |
| 3 | Escalate the desire | promises a result | take the dream outcome to an extreme (dog asleep during nail trim; "I'm definitely coming over"; "Nobody believes I'm 54") | [11:04-13:37] |
| 4 | Reposition the problem | says "you don't need X" | "The problem isn't [what you think]. It's [real cause]." Use an authority; use "could be" for compliance | [13:37-16:17] |
| 5 | Allude to new information | promises insider knowledge | "[Authority] revealed something [the industry] doesn't want you to know" / "It turns out we've been using the wrong materials" | [16:17-18:22] |
| 6 | Set the timeline | claims a long experience or a fast result | "But I'll teach it to you in about one minute" / "Here's what happens inside your body in the first seven days" | [18:22-19:44] |
| 7 | (Variation) Cut the hook | finished ad | Start at the bridge/action; test as the fourth hook | [19:44-24:24] |

Tests (derived): after a bridge, can the viewer name what comes next? Does the bridge answer "why should I keep watching?" Does it escalate rather than restate?

## How to apply
- [ ] For each hook, write one bridge per applicable pattern (1-6) and label them.
- [ ] For escalation bridges: write the line, then rewrite it as a visual beat (what do we see getting worse?).
- [ ] Add a relatable first line ("Are you one of...", "You're probably dreading...") before escalating, so the viewer nods first.
- [ ] For reposition bridges: name the false belief, name the true cause, add an authority, and use hedged wording ("could be").
- [ ] For new-information bridges: name who learned it and what they learned (unspecified), without leaking the answer.
- [ ] For timeline bridges: state time or a list of what will be covered.
- [ ] After drafting, cut the hook and test the bridge as the opener; ship it as an extra variation if it makes sense.
- [ ] Confirm every bridge carries enough context to stand alone.
