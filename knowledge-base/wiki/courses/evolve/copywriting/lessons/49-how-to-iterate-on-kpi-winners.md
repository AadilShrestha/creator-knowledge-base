# 49. How To Iterate On KPI Winners
> A KPI winner hits your KPI but pulls little spend; it is either a loser that got lucky (treat it exactly like a loser) or a deliberate bottom-of-funnel/offer ad that is doing its job (usually leave it alone), and the instructor does not rate it as an iteration priority.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** an ad hits the KPI (purchases at target ROAS/CPA) but receives a small share of account spend.
- **Inputs → outputs:** ad's share of spend vs account spend, ad intent (was it a deliberate offer/BOF/retargeting ad?), ad type (ideation / iteration / imitation), learnings → one of: leave running, treat as a loser (use [48](48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md) list), or test formats of an offer ad.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** define: "a KPI winner is an ad that doesn't pull significant spend, but this time it does hit KPI". [01:27]
  - **DO** first ask whether it was *meant* to be bottom-of-funnel: "you might've made a bottom of funnel or offer focused or retargeting style ad that was just meant to convert users who have likely already seen some of your other ads." If so, it is doing its job. [00:39-01:27]
  - **IF** the ad's spend share is tiny (instructor: $300-400/day on a $20,000/day account, "like 1%") **THEN** it is probably "a losing ad that got lucky and got some purchases": treat it exactly like a loser. [00:00-00:39]
  - **IF** the ad gets a modest share (instructor: $100-200 of a $2,000/day account = 10%) and hits KPI **THEN** "you just need to leave the ad running in the account and see if Meta starts to shift spend to it later." [00:39]
  - **DON'T** dismiss KPI winners: they "can sometimes hold up the account", improve overall ROAS/CPA and convert users coming from colder prospecting ads. [02:14]
  - **IF** the ad was an offer-specific ideation that does its job **THEN** "mission accomplished"; **IF** it was not offer-specific **THEN** you may try the idea 2-3 more times (with evidence). [03:06]
  - **DON'T** iterate imitations that are KPI winners ("we rarely do iterations on KPI winning imitation ads"). [03:06; page text]
  - **IF** you do iterate a KPI winner **THEN** use the same eight loser iterations, and for BOF/offer ads maybe test different formats. [03:06-03:56]
  - **DO** put your iteration effort elsewhere: "ideas that we felt really good about that we executed wrongly" and breakthroughs/spend winners. [03:56]

## Key ideas

### Problem statement [00:00-00:39]
- Page text: "Problem We're Trying To Solve: The ad isn't getting breakthrough levels of spend." The ad is hitting KPI but not pulling "significant amounts of spend". [00:00]
- The majority of the time a KPI winner is due to one of two things: (1) similar issues to losing ads; (2) a purposely made BOF/offer-focused/retargeting ad. [00:00]

### Two kinds of KPI winner [00:00-02:14]
| Kind | Signal | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Lucky loser | Very low share of spend, a few purchases, "likely just got lucky getting attribution or being forced to very warm buyers" | "Treated either exactly like losers" |
| Intentional BOF / offer / retargeting ad | Hovering at "maybe 10%, 15%, whatever it may be, or if we're at more scale, maybe five" of spend; steady purchases | Keep; "we would still kind of consider that a winner"; "there's a scale of KPI winners" depending on percentage of spend |

- Instructor's numeric examples of spend share (illustrative, not thresholds): 1% (at a $15-30K/day account, $300-400 of spend), 10% ($100-200 of $2,000/day). [00:00-00:39]
- Role in the account example: a spend winner at 1.5x ROAS against a 2x target may be balanced by a KPI winner "that's like help balancing out the account"; the KPI winner is not a 3x ROAS. [02:14] (the numbers are the instructor's illustration)

### Most common reasons (page text, expanded at 02:14) [02:14]
1. Very low spend % with a few purchases: "got lucky" with attribution or serving to very warm buyers.
2. Offer-focused ad that "can't scale past product/most aware" (BOF ad).
3. Angle resonates with a small sector: "too niche".

### Which ads to iterate [03:06-03:56]
- Same as losers: ideation (may retry 2-3 times only if the ad was *not* offer-specific and with evidence of what to change), iteration (identify what went wrong in execution), imitation (rarely). [03:06]
- Offer-specific KPI winners: "if we have a certain focus to just make an offer style ad, we just do the offer style ad and that's it." Maybe iterate to make it "more offer focused". [03:06]
- Iteration list = the eight loser iterations "literally the exact same": hook issue #1, TAM, broad, valence/intensity, hook issue #2 (audience signal), empty claims, bridge, belief, offer. See [48](48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md). The page repeats the same examples (TRT mirror vs needle; Beat the dad bod; chocolate bridge; MILF cream) via Canva links. [03:56]
- Priority: "most of the time, we're not overly excited by KPI winners." [03:56]

## Steps / framework
1. Compute the ad's share of account/campaign spend over the same window.
2. Was it designed as BOF/offer/retargeting? Yes -> leave running; optionally test formats. No -> go to step 3.
3. Treat as a loser: run learnings, pick from the eight loser iterations ([48](48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md)), iterate with evidence only.
4. If imitation: stop.
5. If still low spend but hitting KPI after a while: leave running to see whether Meta shifts spend.

## Structured units
### Rules of thumb
- **DO** tell BOF/offer ads apart from lucky losers before iterating. [00:39-01:27]
- **IF** share of spend is ~1% **THEN** treat as a loser. [00:00-00:39]
- **IF** a BOF ad is doing its job **THEN** leave it. [03:06]
- **DON'T** expect KPI winners to scale the account. [03:56]

## How to apply
- [ ] Compute spend share; tag the ad lucky-loser or BOF/offer.
- [ ] If BOF/offer and efficient, keep it live; log it as an "offer KPI winner".
- [ ] If lucky-loser, run the loser checklist from [48](48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md).
- [ ] Skip iterations on imitation KPI winners.
- [ ] Revisit after a period to see whether Meta moved spend to it.
