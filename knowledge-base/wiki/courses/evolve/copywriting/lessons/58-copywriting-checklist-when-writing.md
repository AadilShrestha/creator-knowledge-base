# 58. Copywriting Checklist (when writing)
> A 13-question pre-flight checklist to run over any hook, script or ad before it ships; each question maps to a principle taught elsewhere in the course.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you have a draft hook, UGC script or static-ad headline and need to QA it, or you are scoring a competitor ad against the course's standard.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a draft (or a competitor ad) + the sub-avatar, beliefs and mechanism notes → an answer per question (yes / no / missing), a list of gaps, and a revised draft.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** run every draft through all 13 questions in order and record an explicit answer for each. [page list]
  - **DO** use the Google exact-match test for question 9: search the headline in quotation marks; if the same line already exists, it is not distinctive. [page, Q9]
  - **DO** treat missing items as the revision list. The instructor's logic in the course overview: "if I'm missing a lot of these things, it's probably not a good script. If I have a lot of these things, it's probably a pretty good script" (quoted from [lesson 01](01-overview-of-copywriting-program.md) at [03:37–03:58]; not on this page).
  - **DON'T** treat it as pass/fail on every line. Lesson 01 says the draft "doesn't have to go through each 13 of these things... it's just to kind of check yourself" ([lesson 01](01-overview-of-copywriting-program.md) [03:37–03:58]).
  - **IF** the copy fails Q7 (visual), Q8 (falsifiable) or Q9 (nobody else can write it) **THEN** rewrite with concrete, specific detail before touching anything else; these are the "3 checks" from [lesson 08](08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md) (derived mapping).

## Steps / framework
1. Write the draft.
2. Answer Q1-Q6 first; they are about inputs (who, why, targeting, hook mechanics, mechanism, authority). If you cannot answer one, go back to research before editing words.
3. Answer Q7-Q10 (visual, falsifiable, unique, communicates something). Rewrite lines that fail.
4. Answer Q11-Q13 (reading level, compression, spoken test) as the final polish.
5. For a UGC script, read the script aloud for Q13 (derived: the creator will speak it).

## Prompts / templates (verbatim)
The checklist exactly as it appears on the lesson page (numbering and wording kept; the hyperlink on Q7 is noted in brackets).

```text
1. Who am I writing this for? (Sub avatar + beliefs)
2. Why am I writing it for them?
3. Am I starting broad or targeted?
4. Does my hook have a gap, trigger emotion, and have high stakes?
5. What is the mechanism?
6. What is the authority?
7. Is the copy visual? (Proofing your writing with AI module)   [hyperlink on the page]
8. Is the copy falsifiable (can somebody say it's true/false?)
9. Can nobody else write this? (Search your headline on Google with " ")
10. What is this copy actually communicating?
11. Is this writing at a 6th grade reading level?
12. Can this be condensed further without losing meaning?
13. Would you say this door-to-door? (spoken not written)
```

## How to apply
- [ ] Copy the 13 questions into the QA step of every script and static-ad brief.
- [ ] For each question write yes / no / not-applicable and one line of evidence (the phrase in the draft that answers it).
- [ ] Run the exact-match Google search on the headline (Q9) and log the result.
- [ ] Paste the draft into a reading-level tool for Q11 (lesson 13 teaches the Hemingway app) and read it aloud for Q13.
- [ ] Rewrite only the lines that failed, then re-run the checklist.
