# 18. Being aware of angles
> An angle is "the main reason you're giving someone to buy" (the selling strategy), every ad needs one, and each ad should focus on exactly one even though a product usually has several.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** extracting the angle from a competitor ad in a teardown, or choosing/labelling the angle of our own hook or script before writing.
- **Inputs → outputs:** product functions + chosen avatar → a list of candidate angles written as "the reason to buy", then one main angle chosen per ad.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** write every angle as a plain reason someone should buy, in the form "Your child will enjoy...", "You won't be...", "It will be easier for you to...". [00:00–03:42]
  - **DO** list several angles per product (the instructor gives two or three per brand) and then pick one per ad. [00:00; 03:42]
  - **DO** let the avatar shape the angle: broad "easiest way to get your daily greens" becomes "your child will enjoy taking their greens" when written for parents of kids. [00:43–01:26]
  - **DO** define the angle even when the concept is a format test: "Even if your concept is 'testing formats,' you still need to define why someone is going to buy from you." [page text]
  - **DON'T** pack several angles into one ad: "if you're trying to cater to all these different people, it's very hard to establish any kind of position... and it's hard to be relatable." [03:42–04:32]
  - **IF** the ad has a headline plus other claims **THEN** keep the main angle in the hook and push secondary angles into sub-headlines or body copy. [03:00–03:42]

## Key ideas

### 1. Definition [00:00–00:43]
- Before writing headlines or copy, ask "what is the reason why someone should buy this product?" This is similar to "why will someone care", but the focus is the **angle = the selling strategy**, "the main reason you're giving someone to buy." [00:00–00:43]
- Page text: "**Angle** ⇒ The selling strategy - how you're choosing to sell the product"; "**12-Year-Old Angle Definition:** The main reason you're giving someone to buy". The instructor says he has updated the angle examples "to make them even more clear" and refers to an earlier "understanding ad definitions" video. [00:00–00:43]
- He gives two or three angles per brand because "your brand likely has multiple angles". [00:00–00:43]

### 3. Connect avatar to angle to headline [03:00–03:42]
- Back to the beginner-chef avatar from [17](17-being-aware-of-avatars.md): desire = impress the family; belief = can't cook chicken properly; headline = "never overcooked chicken again"; **angle = "it will be easier for you to cook"**, "the main reason that someone is buying". The angle might even appear only as a sub-headline or in the copy. [03:00–03:42]
- "It's very hard to write an ad for someone if you don't actually have an angle that you're writing it around." [03:00–03:42]
- "Why do they care" (avatar) overlaps with the angle but "goes a little bit deeper"; the angle is "just what you're focusing that ad on." [03:00–03:42]

### 4. One main angle per ad [03:42–04:40]
- Page text, key insight: "Every ad needs an angle. But you must focus on one main angle per ad. You likely have multiple of them based on the functions your product has." [03:42]
- Reason for single focus: serving many audiences makes it "very hard to establish any kind of position" and "hard to be relatable. And that's very, very important in today's market." [03:42–04:32]

## Steps / framework
1. List the product's functions and benefits.
2. For each, write the reason to buy in a full sentence addressed to the buyer ("You will...", "Your child will...").
3. Narrow broad angles by adding the avatar ("who are we writing this for?").
4. Tag each angle: benefit, health/avoidance, identity, status, ease/convenience (these tags come from the instructor's remarks, [01:26–03:00]).
5. Pick one main angle per ad; keep others for other ads or secondary copy.
6. Write the headline to deliver that angle; put a mechanism behind it (see [19](19-being-aware-of-mechanisms.md)).

## Examples
- Grüns: broad "easiest way to get your daily greens" → narrowed "Your child will enjoy taking their greens". [00:43–01:26]
- Liquid Death: three angles for one product (avoidance of microplastics, environmental identity, status). [01:26–03:00]
- Wagyu chicken: flavour angle vs ease angle; ease is chosen for the beginner chef. [02:14–03:42]

## How to apply
- [ ] State the angle of every ad (ours and competitors') as one sentence "reason to buy" before judging the copy.
- [ ] Keep one main angle per ad; assign secondary angles to separate ads.
- [ ] For each product, keep an angle list tagged by type (benefit, avoidance, identity, status, ease).
- [ ] Narrow broad angles by naming the person they are for.
- [ ] Log angle + avatar + mechanism together (see the `sub-avatarsangles` tab in [22](22-how-to-finalize-your-ad-ideas.md)).
