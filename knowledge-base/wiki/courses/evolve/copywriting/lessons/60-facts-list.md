# 60. Facts list
> A standing research habit: keep a running list of every fact about your brand, your product and your market, and keep it close while writing.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** starting research for a brand or product, or writing any hook, script or static ad that needs a concrete claim to stand on.
- **Inputs → outputs:** brand site, product pages, reviews, competitor ads, market articles → a living "facts list" in three buckets (brand, product, market) → raw material for claims, hooks and proof lines.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** build three lists: all the facts around the brand, around the product, and in the market (only the interesting ones for the market). [page]
  - **DO** jot down every fact as you research, and keep the list close while writing. [page]
  - **DON'T** limit the list to statistics; the course's facts lesson says a fact does not have to be a number ([lesson 10](10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md); not on this page).
  - **IF** a draft line is an opinion ("our best formula yet") **THEN** look for a listed fact that replaces it. (derived from [lesson 10](10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md))
  - **DO** record each fact with its source so a claim can be substantiated. (derived)
- **See also:** [10. Why you need to write facts](10-why-you-need-to-write-facts.md) · [61. Objections list](61-objections-list.md) · [Customer research stack](../../../../frameworks/customer-research-stack.md) · [58. Copywriting checklist](58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md)

## Key ideas

### What to list [page text]
The page's whole teaching, in its own words: "Identify" these three things.
- All the facts around your brand.
- All the facts around your product.
- All the facts in your market (that are interesting).

Then: "Keep this list close by when writing." And: "Whenever researching, jot down all of the 'FACTS'."

## Steps / framework
(derived layout of the page's instruction)

| Bucket | Question to ask | Examples of where to look (derived) |
|---|---|---|
| Brand | What is verifiably true about the company? | about page, founder story, certifications |
| Product | What is verifiably true about the product (what it is made of, does, measures)? | product page, spec sheet, reviews |
| Market | Which facts about the category or customer are interesting? | industry reports, competitor ads, customer research |

1. Create the three buckets in the working research document.
2. Add every fact as you meet it during research, with its source link.
3. Before writing, read the whole list; pull the fact that makes the line specific and true.

## Prompts / templates (verbatim)
Page text, verbatim:

```text
Identify:

* All the facts around your brand
* All the facts around your product
* All the facts in your market (that are interesting)

Keep this list close by when writing

Whenever researching, jot down all of the "FACTS"
```

## How to apply
- [ ] Open a facts list for the brand before drafting; three headings: brand, product, market.
- [ ] Add facts during research, not afterwards; note the source beside each.
- [ ] Before each script or headline, scan the list and choose the fact the line will rest on.
- [ ] Flag any claim in a draft that has no matching fact in the list.
