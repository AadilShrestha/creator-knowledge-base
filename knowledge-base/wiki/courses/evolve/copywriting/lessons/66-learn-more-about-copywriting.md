# 66. Learn more about copywriting
> Two external reference sites for studying copy: Harry Dry's Marketing Examples and the Famous Campaigns archive.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you need real-world marketing and advertising examples to study or to borrow a mechanism from, beyond the course's own ads and swipe files.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a topic, niche or mechanism you want examples of → pages from the two sites that show it → teardown notes and remix ideas ("copy the mechanism, not the asset").
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** use Marketing Examples (Harry Dry) when you want concrete, visual copy examples; Harry Dry is also the credited source of the [visual words prompt](59-visual-words.md) and the "visualize it" check in [lesson 08](08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md). [page text; 59]
  - **DO** use Famous Campaigns to study established, well-known campaigns (the page label is "Famous Campaigns"; the page says nothing else about the site). [page text]
  - **DON'T** assume the course endorses or summarises any specific article on these sites; the page lists only the two home URLs. [page text]
  - **IF** you borrow an example **THEN** reduce it to its mechanism and rewrite it for our product, and run it through the [checklist](58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md); never reuse the original wording. (derived)

## How to apply
- [ ] When a brief needs inspiration outside our category, search both sites for the mechanism (e.g. comparison, pun, demonstration) and log candidate examples with their URLs.
- [ ] For each example, write the mechanism in one line and a rewrite for our product; keep the source link for attribution.
- [ ] Use the examples as evidence for the "why it works" column in teardown notes, not as copy to reuse.
