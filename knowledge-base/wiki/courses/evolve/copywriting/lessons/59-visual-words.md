# 59. Visual words
> An AI prompt (credited to Harry Dry) that flags abstract verbs, nouns, adjectives, adverbs and cliches in a draft, plus the writing rule behind it: keep rewriting until the copy goes from abstract to concrete.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a script, hook or static headline reads vague ("productive routines", "premium quality") and needs to become something the reader can picture; also as the check for Q7 of the [copywriting checklist](58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md).
- **Inputs → outputs:** a draft → every abstract or cliched word or sentence highlighted by category (a-e) → a concrete, visual rewrite of each flagged line.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** run the prompt below over the draft and list every flagged word or sentence under its category. [page]
  - **DO** rewrite each flag from abstract to concrete, repeating until it is concrete: "just keep writing until you go from ABSTRACT to concrete". [page]
  - **DO** swap a general concept for a specific, filmable action: not "productive routines" but "waking up at 6 am to write". [page]
  - **DON'T** accept a rewrite that is still a category word (routine, quality, lifestyle, solution); ask what a camera would show. (derived)
  - **IF** the draft is a UGC video script **THEN** the concrete version should be something the creator can show or do on screen (derived; see [31](31-how-to-create-ads-that-show-dont-tell.md)).

## Key ideas

### Abstract to concrete [page text]
- The goal stated on the page: "Your goal is to go from ABSTRACT to CONCRETE."
- Method: take a sheet of paper and keep writing until the line stops being abstract.
- The page's one example: writing about habits, do not write "productive routines" (abstract); write "waking up at 6 am to write" (concrete/visual).
- Credit on the page: "Credit Harry Dry" (the marketingexamples.com author; see [66. Learn more about copywriting](66-learn-more-about-copywriting.md)). Harry Dry's "visualize it" test is also the first of the three checks in [lesson 08](08-the-3-checks-for-great-copywriting.md).

## Steps / framework
1. Paste the draft after the prompt (below).
2. Take the AI's flagged list as a to-do list, not a rewrite. (derived: [lesson 36](36-what-about-ai.md) says the writer's judgment, not the AI's, decides what is good.)
3. For each flag, write by hand what a person would literally see, hear, do or touch.
4. Repeat until no flags remain, then run the draft through the [checklist](58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md).

## Prompts / templates (verbatim)
AI prompt exactly as on the page (the page labels it "AI Prompt"):

```text
Read the following copy. Can you highlight every word or sentence that contains

a) Abstract verbs
b) Abstract nouns
c) Abstract adjectives
d) Abstract adverbs
e) Cliches
```

Credit line on the page: `^ Credit Harry Dry`

Writing rule on the page, verbatim:

```text
Your goal is to go from ABSTRACT to CONCRETE

Sheet of paper → and just keep writing until you go from ABSTRACT to concrete

For example, say you're writing about habits. Don't talk about

"productive routines."

That's abstract.

Write about

"waking up at 6 am to write" instead.

It's concrete/visual.
```

## How to apply
- [ ] Run the prompt on every draft headline, hook and script before the final edit.
- [ ] Keep a list of the abstract words the AI flags most often for our brand and avoid them in briefs.
- [ ] For each flagged line, produce a concrete rewrite and a one-line note of what the viewer would see.
- [ ] Re-run the prompt on the rewrite to confirm no flags remain.
