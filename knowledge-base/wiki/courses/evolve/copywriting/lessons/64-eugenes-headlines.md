# 64. Eugene's Headlines
> A swipe list of 38 ways to state a claim in a headline, each with one or two classic example headlines (mostly mid-century print ads); use it to turn one product claim into many different headline structures.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you have one strong claim (a fact, a mechanism, a result) and need many distinct ways to say it; when a hook feels stuck in one structure ([angle rut](../../../../concepts/angle-rut.md)); when classifying a competitor headline by its move.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a single claim about our product (plus proof, mechanism, authority) → 38 candidate headline structures → a shortlist of 5-10 rewritten for our avatar and checked against the [checklist](58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md).
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** start from one claim and apply several moves to it; each numbered title on the page is a move on "the claim". [page structure]
  - **DO** copy the move, not the words: the examples are historical ads for other products (Viceroy, Bayer, Rolls Royce, Alka-Seltzer ...). (derived; consistent with "copy the mechanism, not the asset")
  - **DO** make every claim measurable or showable before using moves 1-6, 11 and 30 (they need a real number, speed, comparison or demonstration). (derived)
  - **DON'T** reproduce an example headline for our own product; check uniqueness with the exact-match Google search from [58](58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md) Q9.
  - **DON'T** use a move that implies a claim we cannot substantiate (e.g. 21 "symbolize", 36 "accuse the claim of being too good", 9 "remove limitations") without proof. (derived)
  - **IF** you need a high-curiosity hook **THEN** try moves 8 (paradox), 18 (challenge), 23 (contradict how the mechanism should work), 25 (offer information) or 33 (address people who can't buy). (derived selection from the list)
- **See also:** [63. Power words](63-power-words.md) · [62. Value words](62-value-words.md) · [57. Hook templates](57-100-hook-templates-form-winning-ads-deck.md) · [25. The 2 ways to write great hooks](25-the-2-ways-to-write-great-hooks.md) · [Hooks library](../../../../libraries/hooks.md)

## Key ideas

### One claim, 38 treatments [page text]
- The page is a numbered list of 38 titled moves. Each begins with a verb phrase that acts on "the claim": measure, compare, metaphorize, sensitize, demonstrate, dramatize, state as a paradox, remove limitations, associate, question, tie authority, before-and-after, stress newness, exclusivity, challenge, case-history quotation, condense, symbolize, connect the mechanism, startle, connect need and claim, offer information, name the problem, warn, emphasise the phrasing, show ease, state the difference, surprise, address non-buyers, address the prospect, dramatize difficulty, accuse the claim, challenge beliefs, question and answer.
- Several moves rely on **mechanism** language (22 "Connect the mechanism to the claim", 23 "Startle the reader by contradicting the way he thinks the mechanism should work"), which ties to [lesson 14](14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md).
- Several moves rely on **authority** (10 "Associate the claim with value or people with whom the prospect wishes to be identified", 14 "Tie authority into the claim"), which ties to [lesson 16](16-why-you-need-to-understand-authority.md).
- Move 28 ("Warn the reader about possible pitfalls") and 33 ("Address the people who can't buy your product") are the page's direct-callout and fear-based moves; see [lesson 24](24-the-2-types-of-hooks.md) on targeted hooks. (derived links by theme)

## Steps / framework
1. Write the single claim in one plain sentence: what the product does and for whom.
2. List the facts that back it ([60. Facts list](60-facts-list.md)): numbers, speeds, comparisons, demonstrations.
3. Apply 8-10 moves from the table below that fit the claim; write each as a headline for our avatar.
4. Run each through the [13-question checklist](58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md) and the [power-word scan](63-power-words.md).
5. Keep 5-10; hand them to the format step (UGC hook line or static-ad headline).

## How to apply
- [ ] State one claim and its evidence, then apply at least eight different moves to it.
- [ ] Prefer moves that can be shown on camera (4-7, 15, 21, 32) for UGC video; prefer moves that read in one line (1-3, 8, 12, 31) for statics. (derived)
- [ ] Rewrite each result in plain language for our avatar, not in the 1950s register of the examples.
- [ ] Verify uniqueness and truth of the finished headline before use.
- [ ] Log which move produced the winning ad so later iterations can try the neighbouring moves ([iteration vs variation](../../../../concepts/iteration-vs-variation.md)).
