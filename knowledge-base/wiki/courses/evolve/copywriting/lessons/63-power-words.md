# 63. Power words
> A 33-pair swap list (credited to Davie Fogarty) for replacing flat words with higher-emotion, higher-stakes words: scan your writing and swap where it adds intensity without breaking the truth.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** a hook, script or static headline is accurate but low-energy, or you are raising the stakes in a draft ([lesson 30](30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md)).
- **Inputs → outputs:** a draft line → every plain word that has a power-word swap → candidate rewrites ranked by fit and truthfulness.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** scan the draft for the left-hand words and test the right-hand swap: "see if any of these words can be implemented to enhance the emotion of your writing and add higher stakes or more intensity". [page]
  - **DO** swap only where the new word is still true of the product and the avatar's situation (derived; see [58. checklist](58-copywriting-checklist-when-writing.md) Q8 falsifiable).
  - **DON'T** swap every word; stacked power words read as hype (derived). One or two swaps per line.
  - **DON'T** use a swap that implies a claim the product cannot support (e.g. "Deadly", "Toxic", "Fatal", "Save") without substantiation, and check ad-platform and legal rules before running it (derived).
  - **IF** a swap changes the meaning (e.g. "Help" to "Save") **THEN** confirm the new meaning is intended before keeping it. (derived)
- **See also:** [30. How to raise the stakes](30-how-to-raise-the-stakes.md) · [62. Value words](62-value-words.md) · [64. Eugene's headlines](64-eugenes-headlines.md) · [Intensity](../../../../concepts/intensity.md) · [Emotional zones](../../../../concepts/emotional-zones.md)

## Key ideas

### Swap list [page text]
- The instruction on the page: "Scan your writing and see if any of these words can be implemented to enhance the emotion of your writing and add higher stakes or more intensity."
- The list is written "plain word → **power word**". The first block has 11 pairs; "Even More" adds 22, for 33 pairs in total.
- Credit on the page: "Davie Fogarty" (his name appears as written; the course files do not say more about him).

## Steps / framework
1. Take the draft line and list its nouns, verbs and adjectives.
2. Look each up in the left column below.
3. Try the right-hand word; read the line aloud ([lesson 12](12-why-you-need-to-speak-copy-not-write-it.md)).
4. Keep a swap only if the line is still true, still plain-spoken ([lesson 13](13-why-you-need-to-write-at-a-6th-grade-level.md)) and clearly more intense.

## How to apply
- [ ] After the first draft, search the text for each left-hand word and test its swap.
- [ ] Keep at most one or two swaps per line; read aloud to check it still sounds like something a person would say.
- [ ] Verify each swapped claim is true and acceptable under platform ad policies before approving.
- [ ] Log which swaps survived testing for the brand (derived) so the list can be pruned by results.
