# Nine-component audit
> Break any ad (ours or a competitor's) into the nine components Spencer Pawliw's team plans and checks (awareness, valence + intensity, desire, avatar, angle, mechanism, belief/authority, positioning, urgency), mark each **working / improvable / missing**, and compare what was **planned** with what was **executed** before you decide that an idea failed.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** (a) step 5 of the [ten-step learnings](ten-step-iteration-learnings.md) after a test; (b) breaking down a spend winner or breakthrough before a "components rewrite" ([L52 00:55](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)); (c) tearing down a competitor winner so you can rebuild its components in your own format; (d) pre-launch, to fill the plan you will later check against.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the ad (transcript or on-screen text, hooks verbatim, visuals) + the planned row from the idea tracker (desire, sub-avatar, angle, memo, awareness; see [memo and Growth Guide](mini-breakthrough-memo-and-growth-guide.md)) → a nine-row component card (planned / executed / status / evidence / iteration idea) + a labelled hypothesis.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** pull the planned values from the tracker row *before* reading the ad, then check the ad against them: "This is where a lot of you guys are like throwing out angles or like saying that your idea didn't work when it just wasn't even executed properly." [L46 09:20](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)
  - **DO** mark every component as working, can be optimised, or missing; "it's like building blocks... you can just play Tetris." [L52 00:00–01:37](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
  - **DO** treat the result as a hypothesis: "this is a hypothesis. This is not fact." It becomes a learning only when an iteration built on it "pops". [L46 14:17–15:40](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)
  - **DO** challenge an AI's component reading: in the demo the agent said the hook "reads more unaware" than planned and the instructor agreed, but he also warns "Claude is not always 100% right". [L47 05:12–05:55](../lessons/47-claude-code-10-step-iteration-process.md)
  - **IF** a component is missing (no mechanism, no authority, no urgency) **THEN** the first iteration adds it, "without again, bloating it or making it worse". [L52 01:37–02:19](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
  - **IF** you would add authority or facts **THEN** use only true ones: "always take the facts and tell the truth." [L52 02:19](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)
  - **DON'T** read hook rate or hold rate from tiny spend ("if your ad spent $2 and it says it has a 70% hook rate ... It needs more data"); the page recommends $1k spend after 7 days. [L46 07:18–08:00](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)

## The nine components
The course gives the list in two orders. The lesson-46 step-5 list (video and page are "now identical", [L46 08:00](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)) and the "Breakthrough Ad Winner" DOCX on lesson 52 contain the same nine items; the lesson-47 DOCX has eight (no urgency). In lesson 46 the instructor renamed "authority" to **belief** ("this belief component is a huge piece of why so many ads fail", [L46 10:58](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)); the DOCX keeps "authority building trust". Treat belief = authority + any strategic copy that builds trust.

## Procedure
1. **Load the plan.** Open the tracker row for the batch (sub-avatar, angle, memo, awareness level) [L46 09:20](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md). If no plan exists, write "no plan" in the Planned column; the audit then only describes the ad (and the instructor's point stands: the step "exposes whether you really prepare ads", [L46 08:42](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)).
2. **Read the ad.** Hooks verbatim (on-screen text and voiceover listed separately), bridge, hold, CTA, visuals, audio mood (audio "plays into the valence and intensity factor", [L52 09:46–10:40](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md)).
3. **Fill the card** (template below), one row per component: Executed (what the ad actually does, with the quoted line), Status (working / improvable / missing), Evidence.
4. **Flag mismatches** between Planned and Executed. A mismatch means the idea was not tested as planned; log it as an execution issue, not as proof the angle is bad.
5. **Compare with your winners** for valence/intensity and desire: "if every winner is low valence, low intensity" and this ad is high/high, note it [L46 10:15](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md). But winners exist "in every single zone" [L54 01:18](../lessons/54-iteration-examples-breakdowns-walkthrough.md), so this is a comparison, not a rule.
6. **Write the hypothesis** ("This is what I think", not "this is what is", [L46 14:17–15:40](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)) and 3+ iteration ideas from the decision table.
7. **Route** to the [issue codes](iteration-issue-codes.md) (for losers, KPI winners, spend winners) or to the script/format iterations in the [iteration playbook](iteration-playbook.md) (for spend winners and breakthroughs).

## Decision table: component status → what to do
| Component | Fail signal | Iteration / fix | Evidence in the course |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Planned problem aware, copy reads unaware (or product aware) | Relabel the ad's awareness in the tracker, or rewrite to the planned level; test an awareness-level iteration up or down | "this was a mistake", moved to unaware [L47 05:12–05:55](../lessons/47-claude-code-10-step-iteration-process.md); awareness-level test [L52 13:01–14:30](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md) |
| Valence + intensity | "Saying the right thing, but it isn't converting because it just doesn't feel right" | Flip valence (positive ↔ negative) or move intensity (boring → crank up; very intense → dial down) | [L48 10:49–12:57](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md); emotional hook testing [L52 05:51–07:24](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md) |
| Desire | Outcome promised is not the planned desire, or not intensified | Keep authority and mechanism, shift the core desire (health → status) | Washer: "Desire shifted to Status", authority "still the same (grandpa)" [L52 04:47](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md) |
| Avatar | Hook speaks to everyone when the plan named a sub-avatar, or the sub-avatar is too narrow to scale | Carry the call-out further into the ad; or widen the TAM; or rewrite for a different sub-avatar (rewrite authority and setting too) | Demo: hook "speaks more broadly" than "pet owner" [L47 06:31–07:22](../lessons/47-claude-code-10-step-iteration-process.md); TAM issue [L48 06:43](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md); sub-avatar iteration [L52 17:32–18:32](../lessons/52-the-iteration-playbook-script.md) |
| Angle | No clear reason to buy, or several angles competing | One main angle per ad; secondary angles go to sub-heads or other ads | "Every ad needs an angle. But you must focus on one main angle per ad." [L18 03:42](../lessons/18-being-aware-of-angles.md) |
| Mechanism | Claim with nothing that explains how; or the market has heard the mechanism | Add a mechanism ("what is the mechanism that can prove it?"); refresh/rename a worn one | [L19 03:45–04:23](../lessons/19-being-aware-of-mechanisms.md); [L14 15:36–16:31](../lessons/14-why-you-need-to-use-unique-mechanisms.md) |
| Belief / authority | Empty claim, speaker makes the claim with no reason to trust her, opinions instead of facts | Add authority (signal it before the pitch), facts, or vulnerability ("we can't help iPhone 12 or below") | Korean cream "some MILF" [L48 17:23–18:22](../lessons/48-how-to-iterate-on-losing-ads.md); [L50 07:04–07:40](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md); [L20 00:00–00:34](../lessons/20-being-aware-of-authorities.md) |
| Positioning | Ad says what competitors say; competitor could sign it | Find what only you can say; expect copied ads to die fast | "this breakthrough ad might not last that long because I'm just gonna get copied back" [L46 11:51](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md); [L51 12:19–18:29](../lessons/51-how-to-iterate-on-breakthroughs.md) |
| Urgency | No reason to act now; no consequence of waiting | Add a fact, a time-bound consequence, a "every day you delay" line (not fake scarcity) | Dog dental powder [L50 08:35–10:58](../lessons/50-how-to-iterate-on-spend-winners.md) |

## Output template: component card
```yaml
component_audit:
  ad: "<ad name with Batch#/ITER# naming>"
  outcome: loser | kpi_winner | spend_winner | breakthrough
  source: ours | competitor
  hooks_verbatim:
    - {on_screen: "<text>", voiceover: "<text or same>"}
  components:
    - name: awareness
      planned: "<from tracker or 'no plan'>"
      executed: "<what the copy actually does, quote>"
      status: working | improvable | missing
      evidence: "<line / timestamp>"
      iteration_idea: "<one specific change>"
    # repeat for: valence_intensity, desire, avatar, angle, mechanism, belief, positioning, urgency
  mismatches: ["<component: planned X, executed Y>"]
  hypothesis: "I think <reason>. Not yet a learning."   # becomes a learning only if the iteration pops
  next: issue_codes | script_iterations | format_iterations
```

## Using it on a competitor ad (derived)
- You do not have their plan; leave Planned blank and fill Executed only. The output is the list of components that carry the ad.
- The instructor's own use of this on native ads: crack a winning native ad, then "redo" it as "AI voiceover, AI animated ad, text on screen" hitting the same components [L51 14:45](../lessons/51-how-to-iterate-on-breakthroughs.md). Copy the components (the mechanism of persuasion), never the lines, footage or claims.
- Check positioning and differentiation risk: easy-to-make ads with no product differentiation die fast, and you can be "copied back" [L51 12:19–16:59](../lessons/51-how-to-iterate-on-breakthroughs.md).

## Common mistakes
- Judging the idea instead of the execution (step 4 skipped). [L46 09:20](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)
- Treating the AI's component labels as final; awareness is where models struggle most ("Claude will struggle with awareness levels", [L70 19:26–20:20](../lessons/70-how-to-write-video-ads-part-1.md)).
- Summarising hooks instead of writing them verbatim. [L46 06:33–08:00](../lessons/46-the-10-step-iteration-process.md)
- Fixing several components at once without labelling each change. The course examples change several things at once but always name them in a "Changes Made" line [L54 08:49](../lessons/54-iteration-examples-breakdowns-walkthrough.md).
