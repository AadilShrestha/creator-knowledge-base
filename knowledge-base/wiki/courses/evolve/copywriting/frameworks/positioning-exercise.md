# Positioning exercise
> Decide what position you can **own** before writing angles: map the category and what competitors already own, take either a new (sub)category or the obvious hole, make it concrete with a strategic enemy, a "no" and a visual difference, then fill four tables (who you compete against, what you compete against, product problems, differences → problems they solve). Claim "different", never "better".

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** before a new brand or product's first angles; when every ad sounds like the competitor's ("could a competitor sign it?"); when auditing a competitor's position from its ads; when a breakthrough dies fast because it was copied.
- **Inputs → outputs:** category, competitor list and their ads/pages, competitor reviews (complaints), our real product differences → four filled tables + a one-line position statement (category, contrast, values, enemy, "no", visual difference) that every angle and brief must reinforce.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** ask "What position do you want to own?"; "Own is the key word... Too many brands want to own a position that is impossible to own because someone else already owns it." [L56 00:00–00:34](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
  - **DO** check the customer will accept it: "it's not just about what you want. It's also about what the customer is willing to accept." [L56 01:13](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
  - **DO** fill the tables with "How are you different?", not "Why are you better?" [L56 03:23–04:01](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
  - **DON'T** claim to be better: "you should never, ever say that you're better, because if you were, you'd be number one." [L55 19:00–19:32](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
  - **DO** find product problems in competitor reviews "where people complain", then ask how you avoid or solve each. [L56 04:01–04:46](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
  - **DO** keep "a unique position with an appeal that is not too narrow"; under about $100K/month, "it's better to be more narrow than it is to be more broad". [L56 05:41–06:24](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)
  - **DO** make the difference visible ("seeing is believing"). [L55 13:42; 19:32–20:26](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
  - **DO** track competitors "not so you can copy their ads" but to separate yourself. [L55 07:48](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)
  - **IF** you run the worksheet in a doc or in Claude ("if you want to, you totally can") **THEN** fill it from real competitor, review and product facts. [L56 03:23; 04:46–05:41](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md)

## Steps (L56 page)
| Step | Questions (verbatim) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Map the competitive landscape | "What category are you in? Who do you compete with in that category? Who's the leader in the category and what do they stand for? What positions do competitors already own in this category?" | [L56 02:04](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md) |
| 2A. New (sub)category | "Can you narrow your focus to be FIRST in something?" | [L56 02:35] |
| 2B. The obvious hole | "Can't be first? Redefine who it's for or how to think about the category · What are competitors unwilling to do/say/show? · What inverse position can you take? (timeless vs trendy, one size vs inclusive) · What can you call out that others won't? (Halo Top: eat the whole pint) · What's the obvious, but no so-obivous, problem/gap no one's addressing?" | [L56 02:35] |
| 3. Make it concrete | "Define your strategic enemy(s): Is it a competitor, an approach, or a belief/concept? · Define what you'll say 'no' to: What will you eliminate that competitors won't? · Make it visually different: Different beats better, how can you visually stand out?" | [L56 02:35–03:23] |
| Active exercise | Four tables (below) | [L56 03:23–05:41] |

## The five takeaways as a decision table (L55/L56)
| Move | Ask | Use when | Course examples | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New category | Can you invent a subcategory with no competitors? | You can be first in something narrow but real | Apple (smartphone), Poppi (prebiotic soda), Dude Wipes (wipes for men) | [L55 20:26–23:16](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md) |
| Contrast | Can you do the opposite of the status quo? | You can't be first; reposition the leader | Liquid Death (water in a can), Shopify ("for the merchant"), Stolichnaya ("It is actually Russian."), Scope (no "medicine breath"), Tylenol (for people who can't take aspirin) | [L55 23:16–25:47] |
| Values | Can you align with a belief or cause that attracts a tribe (and alienates others)? | A real value the brand lives | Comfrt (mental health), Black Rifle Coffee (veteran rights) | [L56 page] |
| Strategic enemy | Who or what are you against? | Always; "When you define what you are against, it becomes clear what you are for" | Uber vs taxis, The Farmer's Dog vs kibble, Lalo vs ugly high chairs, Kind Bar vs extrusion | [L55 25:47–28:49] |
| Say no | What will you give up that competitors can't? | A trade-off customers accept | Chick-fil-A (no beef, no Sundays), Uniqlo (no trends), GEM (no pills), Brandy Melville (one size), The Conscious Bar (no added sugar) | [L55 28:49–33:49] |

Find the hole ("creneau"): Milk Duds → "America's long-lasting alternative to the candy bar"; Halo Top → "280 calories per pint" in a huge font, something competitors were unwilling to say [L55 33:49–39:31](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md). Flaw into category: NyQuil turned drowsiness into "nighttime cold remedies" [L56 00:34–01:13](../lessons/56-positioning-exercise.md).

```text
Who I'm Up Against | How Are You Different

What do you compete against?
What I'm Up Against | How Are You Different
Thing 1 |        (features: "if a product has a specific feature and you don't, how are you different?")
Thing 2 | 

Ask yourself, big or small, serious or not, what are all the problems with products out there.
Product problems | How you can solve
Problem 1 |      (from competitor reviews where people complain)
Problem 2 | 

Ask yourself, big or small, meaningless or meaningful. What is different, and how could that solve a problem?
Things that are different | Problem it could solve
Different 1 |    ("Different one solves this problem. Boom, now we're onto something.")
Different 2 | 
```

## Output template: position statement
```yaml
position:
  category: "<existing category or new sub-category we can be first in>"
  path: new_subcategory | obvious_hole
  for_whom: "<who it is for (and, by implication, not for)>"
  contrast: "<what we do opposite the leader/status quo>"
  values: "<belief/cause, only if real>"
  strategic_enemy: {type: competitor | approach | belief, name: "<...>"}
  say_no_to: "<what we eliminate that competitors won't>"
  visual_difference: "<packaging/product/ad device that shows it>"
  proof_of_difference: ["<product fact id>", "<...>"]     # differences must be real
  customer_acceptance: "<review/research evidence they want this>"
  narrowness_check: "<TAM note; narrow first if < ~$100K/month>"
  statement: "We are the <category/contrast> for <who>, not <enemy>; we say no to <x>; you can see it in <visual>."   # (derived) template
  reinforcement_device: "<recurring line or stunt, e.g. Halo Top's 280 label>"
```

Evolve's own filled example (slide "Finding Your Position", [L55 41:56–44:05](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md)): new category = high-ticket coaching for a monthly subscription (first on Skool); contrast = "we do it slowly"; values = "building real skills to win long term"; enemy = gurus teaching copy-paste strategies; say no to = copy-paste strategies, hacks, trends.

## Common mistakes
- Picking a position already owned ("glacier water in a can" would still make people think of Liquid Death) [L55 22:23–23:16](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md).
- "We're better than our competitors" presented as repositioning [L55 23:16].
- The "everybody trap": selling to everyone sells nothing [L55 27:18].
- Going so narrow you cap your TAM when the revenue goal is eight or nine figures [L56 05:41–06:24].
- Using the instructor's valuation and sales figures as facts in copy (they are slide claims; the Comfrt sales chart was AI-generated by the instructor) [L55 limitations](../lessons/55-why-you-need-to-position-your-brand.md).
