# 104. BAD UGC
> How the agency reviews raw creator footage on five axes (technical, performance, script adherence, editability, B-roll) and which red flags force a reshoot; use it as the QA rubric when you receive footage from a UGC creator.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you receive raw creator footage (hooks, talking head, B-roll) and must accept, request a fix, or order a reshoot; also when writing creator instructions so the footage arrives usable.
- **Inputs → outputs:** raw clips + the brief (hooks, talking points, pronunciation notes) → per-axis verdict (OK / flag for next time / reshoot) and a short feedback note to the creator.
- **Rules:**
  - **DO** have the brief open next to the footage and tick off talking points, benefits, hook and pronunciation [03:20–03:53].
  - **DO** expect raw footage, not polished ads: "we're not expecting creators to send us polished, edited ads, that's our job" [00:01].
  - **DO** send pronunciation guidance before the shoot; a mispronounced ingredient after instructions = reshoot [04:31].
  - **DO** ask for multiple takes and varied angles or movement so editors can cut cleanly [05:06].
  - **DON'T** accept a read-aloud delivery: "you can tell she's reading... forced and awkward, and that breaks connection" [02:19].
  - **DON'T** accept distracting background objects that cannot be cut out in edit (a table and a plant pulling focus); flag for next batch, not a reshoot [01:20].
  - **IF** lighting, background, tone or performance are off, or key instructions are ignored **THEN** reshoot; the agency had one creator redo the whole shoot, in a new location, for the next batch [06:45].
  - **IF** the delivery is flat but the multiple takes exist **THEN** it can still be "almost impossible" to edit; performance beats take count [05:06].

## Key ideas
### Axis 1: technical [00:50–02:19]
- Bright enough or too dark / blown out? Natural light usually best because it feels authentic, but depends on the product [00:50].
- Audio clear without background noise; orientation as the brief asked (vertical or horizontal); subject framed, not blurry or awkwardly cropped; footage stable. Handheld can feel natural depending on the script, but "shaky footage that distracts the viewer is going to be a problem" [00:50–01:20].
- Worked example (collagen-powder brief): lighting decent, audio solid, framing off because a table and plant sit in the background and pull focus. Hard for an editor to cut out; "there can be workarounds", so flag for improvement, not reshoot [01:20].

### Axis 2: performance and authenticity [02:19–03:20]
- Energy, alive and believable vs flat and monotone; natural vs stiff body language; most important: "does this look like a real recommendation" or reading off a script [02:19].
- Example: a creator reading a line about doubting the product but curious enough to try; delivery forced, so the line does not land [02:19–03:20].

### Axis 3: content and script adherence [03:20–04:31]
- Talking points hit, product highlighted with benefits, hook delivered strongly in the first seconds, pronunciation right [03:20].
- The reviewer keeps a second monitor with the brief to track what is missing [03:53]. In the example the creator said "I literally spent over a year doing leg workouts thinking..." and then mispronounced the ingredient name (transcribed as "Sod B dimples"); because pronunciation had been supplied, "we're going to have to have you reshoot it" [03:53–04:31].

### Axis 4: editing flexibility [05:06–06:45]
- Multiple takes or one long shot? Natural cut points? Enough angles or movement to keep the ad dynamic? The example creator gave multiple takes but the performance made them unusable [05:06].
- Example talking-head lines shown on screen (from the collagen creator): "not only did it hear my cellulite, but it cleared my skin. And I struggle with adult acne..." and "I used to plan my entire wardrobe around hiding my thighs..." [05:36].

### Axis 5: B-roll [06:45–07:19]
- B-roll reviewed the same way; in the example it had "no issue" and was accepted [06:45].

### Common red flags that force a reshoot [07:19–08:17]
Poor lighting; distracting backgrounds; wrong vibe or tone for the brand; ignoring key instructions; mispronunciations; flat delivery with no energy. "We don't expect polished ads... we expect clean, usable footage with strong delivery, good lighting, and enough variety" [07:19].

## Steps / framework
Reviewer pass (derived from the five axes, in the order taught):
1. Technical: light, audio, orientation, framing, stability, background clutter.
2. Performance: energy, body language, reading vs talking.
3. Script adherence: hook strong in first seconds, talking points, product + benefits, pronunciation (use the brief).
4. Editability: takes, angles, cut points.
5. B-roll: same checks.
6. Decision: accept / flag for next batch / reshoot with specific notes.

## Structured units
### Rubric
| Axis | Pass looks like | Red flag | Action | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Technical | Even light, clean audio, correct orientation, stable | Dark or blown-out, noise, shaky, clutter | Flag or reshoot | [00:50] |
| Performance | Energy, believable, natural body language | Flat, monotone, stiff, reads script | Reshoot | [02:19] |
| Script adherence | Hook, points, product, benefits, pronunciation | Missing points, mispronunciation | Reshoot if instructed already | [03:20] |
| Editability | Multiple takes, variety, cut points | One long shot, no movement | Ask for more | [05:06] |
| B-roll | Usable variety | Missing or poor | Reshoot | [06:45] |

## How to apply
- [ ] Put the brief and the footage side by side; score the five axes.
- [ ] Send pronunciation, outfit and background instructions in the brief before filming.
- [ ] Reshoot only for red flags; flag background and minor framing for next batch.
- [ ] Run the final talking head through the claim audit; do not let a reshoot keep unsupported results claims.
