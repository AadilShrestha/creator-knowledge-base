# Skeptic Format (Recurring Skeptic Creator)
> A UGC format where a recurring creator plays the viewer's disbelief ("I don't believe you, this is an ad"), **tests the brand's claim on camera**, and reluctantly concedes. HexClad's version: Paige vs Gordon Ramsay's claims, "All right, Gordy… go off, Gordy." Its scripted cousin is the **reluctant-convert** testimonial ("I did not want to like this").

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** the product has a claim you can **demonstrate** on camera (non-stick, scratch-proof, sharp, waterproof, "you'll notice in a week"); the category is hype-saturated or buyers are skeptical (supplements, skincare, "tried everything" pet owners); you need an evaluation-stage UGC concept; you're tearing down a competitor ad where a creator "tests" or "debunks" a product.
- **Inputs → outputs:** the product's testable claims + an authority/claim source (founder, expert, celebrity, the brand's own ad) + customer objections from reviews → a series concept (character, through line, catchphrase), 3–5 episode scripts (one claim each), a casting and editing brief, and test notes.
- **Rules:**
  - DO make the skeptic stand in the **viewer's shoes**: she doubts because "she knows you don't believe the other ads". [J 0:35:37–0:36:43]
  - DO structure each episode as **claim → doubt → test on camera → reluctant concession**. One claim per episode (egg flip = non-stick; mashing potatoes in the pot = scratch resistance). [J 0:36:43]
  - DO make it **recurring**: the same character across many ads, with a through line and line ("Don't believe us? Paige doesn't believe us either."). [J 0:35:37–0:36:43]
  - DO cast a performer, not a strategist: HexClad hired an actress/comedian/creator. Comedy by non-comedians "is obvious"; competitors who copied the structure "fell flat". [J 0:35:37; 0:43:04–0:44:08]
  - DO edit for comedic timing (Paige edits her own videos: "snappy"). [J 0:45:08]
  - DO keep it realistic: skeptical, "cautiously at first", imperfect, week-by-week. DON'T claim "it transformed everybody overnight". [M14 0:41:04–0:41:54]
  - DO write skeptic lines as **situational first-person quotes**, not news-style headlines ("Skeptical dog mom becomes believer" → "I didn't think this would work because it looks like every other bowl, but this one… was just different."). [R3 0:34:44]
  - DON'T fake the test or fabricate results, reviews or press. The format only works because the demo is real (wiki rule; see the Module 4 "never fabricate proof" rule).
  - IF you use a person close to the brand (employee) THEN that strengthens the [messenger effect](../concepts/heuristics/messenger-effect.md). [J 0:36:43–0:37:48]

## Definition
[Joanna Wallace](../people/joanna-wallace.md) describes HexClad's **character-driven** humor: "we have a character of a skeptic… I hired this woman Paige specifically because she is an actress and a comedian, like a content creator and a strategist… a little bit of slapstick, but her thing is that we have a through line where she's done numerous ads where she is the skeptic… putting herself in the viewer's shoes of 'I don't believe you, this is an ad'… Paige is going to test it because she knows you don't believe the other ads." [J 0:35:37–0:36:43]

Episodes seen: Gordon says you can flip an egg and it won't stick → Paige: "All right, Gordy…" → flips it → "whoa… go off, Gordy." Scratch resistance → she mashes potatoes in the pot and reluctantly agrees. Also used for knives, "back and forth with an expert", or reacting to a commercial. [J 0:36:43]

## Why it works
| Mechanism | What the course says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| [Messenger effect](../concepts/heuristics/messenger-effect.md) | "Who says the message is actually a little bit more important than what the message is." A doubter who concedes is a more credible messenger than the brand. People close to the brand (employees) are especially credible. | [J 0:36:43–0:37:48] |
| Objection pre-emption | The skeptic says the viewer's objection out loud first ("this is an ad"), so the viewer's guard drops. Same move as the "barrier buster" (call out the friction). | [J 0:35:37; 0:38:48–0:40:56] |
| Demonstration as proof | The claim is shown, not asserted. Pair with the "show, don't tell" rule from Roast #1. | [J 0:36:43]; [R1 0:07:22](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-28-roast-my-ads-1.md) |
| [Social proof](../concepts/heuristics/social-proof.md) via a convert | A former doubter's concession reads as a believable testimonial. The Maelove scripts and the dog-bowl quote use it. | [M14 0:18:29–0:19:45]; [R3 0:34:44] |
| [Halo effect](../concepts/heuristics/authority-borrowing.md) / borrowed authority | HexClad's skeptic pushes back on Gordon Ramsay. Sarah calls borrowing Gordon's voice a halo effect. The authority makes the claim; the skeptic makes it believable. | [J 0:42:01] |
| Fluency (recurring character) | A recurring character is a "fluency mechanism": "when we see X we know it's [brand]". | [J 0:34:35–0:35:37]; [Jn4 0:19:46–0:21:52; 0:33:26] |
| Realism | "Customers know that there's nuance to everything." Skepticism and gradual results are what make story ads believable. | [M14 0:41:04–0:41:54] |

## Inputs
| Input | Notes |
|---|---|
| Testable claims | Each must be demonstrable in one shot (a physical test, a visible before/after, a timer). One claim per episode. |
| Claim source | Who "says" the claim: celebrity partner, expert, founder, the brand's own ad or packaging, a review quote. |
| Objections | The doubts buyers voice in reviews, Reddit and comments ("I've tried everything", "all marketing is fiction"). Use the [customer research stack](customer-research-stack.md) and [review mining](customer-research-stack.md). |
| Character | A performer who can do deadpan / reluctant reactions; ideally recurring and, if possible, close to the brand. |
| Stage | Usually evaluation (choosing between you and alternatives) (derived from R3's evaluate-stage skeptic quote). |

## Procedure
1. **List claims and pick the testable ones.** Keep only claims you can prove on camera honestly. (derived)
2. **Mine the doubt.** Pull the exact objection language from reviews/comments. Write the skeptic's lines in that language, "four levels deep" and situational, not generic ("every other bowl was a failure", not "nothing worked") [R3 0:33:42–0:35:44].
3. **Define the character and through line.** Name, persona, signature reaction, recurring line ("Don't believe us? [Name] doesn't believe us either.") [J 0:36:43]. Pick **one** humor type and commit to it (deadpan or reluctant-sarcastic fits) [J 0:00:00; 0:22:46–0:24:06].
4. **Write episodes: one claim each** (skeleton below). Put the claim source in the first seconds, the doubt immediately after, and the test in the middle.
5. **Cast and brief.** Hire a comedic performer; give them room to riff; use an editor who understands timing (or let the creator edit) [J 0:43:04–0:45:08].
6. **Build the first frame.** The doubt line or the claim goes on screen as the heaviest text in frame 1 ([visual weight hierarchy](visual-weight-hierarchy.md); "message in frame 1" [R3 0:18:56–0:21:32]).
7. **Test as a series.** Launch 3 episodes, keep winners, add more episodes on the winning claim types ([creative testing system](creative-testing-system.md)). Tag each episode: claim, zone, self, TEEP stage. (derived)
8. **Re-use the concession.** The concession line becomes a static quote, a hook for other creators, or a headline variant. (derived)

## UGC script skeleton (derived)
The beat order is reconstructed from Joanna's HexClad description [J 0:35:37–0:36:43] and the Maelove reluctant-convert script [M14 0:18:29–0:19:45]. Timings are an agent default, not from the course.

| Beat | Time (derived) | On camera | Line pattern | Text overlay |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Claim | 0–2 s | Clip/still of the claim source (expert, founder, packaging, the brand's own ad) | "[Source] says you can [claim]." | The claim, in quotes |
| 2. Doubt | 2–5 s | Skeptic, deadpan, side-eye | "All right, [Source]…" / "I've tried [category] before. Expensive ones. Cheap ones." | "[Name] doesn't believe it." |
| 3. Setup the test | 5–8 s | Skeptic sets up the harshest fair test | "Let's see if this survives [harsh real use]." | Test name |
| 4. Test | 8–18 s | The demo, one continuous shot if possible | Minimal talk, reactions only | Optional timer / step labels |
| 5. Reluctant concession | 18–22 s | Surprise, then grudging admission | "…whoa." / "Okay, fine. I'll say it. It works. And I don't say that lightly." | Concession quote |
| 6. Tag / CTA | 22–25 s | Product + recurring line | "Don't believe us? [Name] didn't either." + definitive CTA ("See how it works") | Catchphrase + CTA |

**Reluctant-convert variant (multi-week, derived from the Maelove Hero's Journey script):** Hook "I did not want to like this" → past failures ("expensive ones, cheap ones, 'game-changer' ones") → first sight of the product, doubt ("I've heard that before") → reviews/science nudge ("let's see if this can prove me wrong") → first use, no expectations → week 1 / 2 / 3 check-ins → replaces old products → "Okay, fine. I'll say it." Verbatim script: [May 14 session](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-14-live-with-sarah.md) [0:18:29–0:19:45].

## Decision table: when to use it
| Situation | Use the skeptic format? | Why / source |
|---|---|---|
| Product has a visual, testable claim | Yes, live-test version | HexClad non-stick / scratch tests [J 0:36:43] |
| Claim is invisible or slow (skincare, supplements) | Yes, reluctant-convert or check-in version | Maelove week-by-week script [M14 0:18:29–0:19:45] |
| Buyers "tried everything" and feel defeated | Yes: skeptic quote / testimonial at evaluation | Dog-bowl insight and rewrite [R3 0:31:35–0:35:44] |
| Category buyers are skeptical about claims (ingested products) | Yes | Supplements buyers are "very skeptical about the claims" [M7 0:29:25–0:30:00] |
| Brand has a celebrity/expert making claims | Yes: skeptic vs authority ("go off, Gordy") | [J 0:36:43; 0:42:01] |
| No comedic performer or editor available | Use the reluctant-convert quote/static version instead of a comedy series (derived) | Non-comedian comedy "is obvious" [J 0:43:04] |
| Claim can't survive a fair test | No. Fix the claim, don't fake the test (wiki rule) | Never fabricate proof |
| Humor ad spikes then dies | Attach the humor to the product/character, keep it consistent across the brand | Humor ads die fast "unless the whole brand stays humorous" [Jn4 0:31:21–0:33:26] |

## Output template
```markdown
### Skeptic series brief: <brand> / <product>
- Character: <name>, <persona: deadpan / reluctant / sarcastic> · Recurring line: "Don't believe us? <Name> doesn't believe us either."
- Casting: comedic performer (actor/comedian/creator), not a strategist · Editor: understands comedic timing (or creator self-edits)
- Messenger: <outside creator / employee close to the brand> · Claim source: <celebrity / expert / founder / packaging>
- Humor type (one): <deadpan / sarcasm / slapstick> · Stage: <evaluation> · Zone: <…> · Self: <Actual>
- Episodes (one claim each, all claims true and demonstrable):
  1. Claim "<…>" → doubt "<objection in customer words>" → test <…> → concession "<…>"
  2. …
  3. …
- Frame 1 text: "<claim or doubt line, ≤7 words>"
- CTA: "<definitive CTA>"
- Proof guardrails: real demo, no invented results, reviews or press
- Test plan: launch 3 episodes → keep winners → add episodes on winning claim types
```

## Common mistakes
- **Copying the structure without the comedy skill.** HexClad's imitators "fell flat". "Trying to be funny and failing is not cute." [J 0:43:04–0:44:08]
- **A skeptic who never actually tests.** The demo is the proof; without it the format is just a testimonial. (derived)
- **Overclaiming the conversion** ("it transformed everything overnight"). Keep the nuance and the grudging tone. [M14 0:41:04–0:41:54]
- **News-headline skeptic copy** ("Skeptical dog mom becomes believer") instead of a first-person situational quote. [R3 0:34:44]
- **Straight-laced editing** that kills the timing. [J 0:45:08]
- **Faking results or proof.** Never invent test outcomes, reviews, press or stock counts (wiki rule).

## Contradictions and notes
- **Employee vs hired creator.** Sarah frames the messenger effect around **employees** ("she works for us… knows better than even our top customers") and asks whether Paige was presented as an employee. Joanna says she *hired* Paige as an actress/comedian/creator; the transcript doesn't settle whether ads presented her as staff [J 0:35:37–0:37:48]. Treat "close to the brand" as a bonus, not a requirement.
- **"Halo effect"** is Sarah's label for HexClad borrowing Gordon Ramsay's humor [J 0:42:01]. The session-page wiki note points out this is closer to association/transfer. Use the label loosely.
