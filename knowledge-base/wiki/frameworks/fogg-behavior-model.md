# Fogg Behavior Model (B = MAP) and Ability Crushers
> A behavior happens when **Motivation**, **Ability** and a **Prompt** meet at the same moment. Sarah's point: marketers over-invest in prompts (urgency, discounts, "add to cart") and neglect **ability**, the buyer's "will this work for *me*?". So audit every ad and page for all three, find the single biggest **ability crusher**, and answer it, usually with a 4–5-word qualifier, a "who it's for" list or visual proof over time. [J16 0:02:50–0:06:30; 0:27:14]

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** (a) tearing down a competitor ad + PDP to see what it does for motivation, prompt and ability (and what it misses); (b) writing a UGC script or creator brief that must answer "is this for me / can I do this / will it fit / will it last"; (c) writing proof lines, qualifiers and option labels; (d) diagnosing a page or ad with clicks but no purchases.
- **Inputs → outputs:** ad script or PDP (URL/screenshots) + target customer + product category → a 3-column audit (Motivation · Prompt · Ability), the named **biggest ability crusher**, and fixes: qualifier lines, option micro-labels, "who it's for" lists, time-sequence visuals, typed social proof, dollar savings, message order, plus one **ability beat** for the UGC script (template below).
- **Rules:**
  - DO audit every asset for all three elements. Expect ability to be the weakest: "I really think it's the ability piece we miss constantly as marketers." [J16 0:05:50–0:06:30; 0:10:50]
  - DO ask "What is the biggest ability crusher?" for every product, "constantly", and fix that first. [J16 0:27:14]
  - DO label choices with who they're for, in 4–5 words ("Best for most legs" / "Roomy fit for wide legs": +10% conversion). [J16 0:06:30–0:10:24]
  - DO qualify the buyer ("Designed for women 45+ experiencing hair thinning"). [J16 0:31:23]
  - IF you claim personalization THEN show the buyer types served (hair types), not your ingredients. [J16 0:16:43]
  - IF the claim is durability or speed THEN show a time sequence (after 1 / 2 / 3 months; bowl at 10 / 20 / 40 minutes), ideally on the main product shot. [J16 0:26:13; 0:28:18–0:29:19]
  - DO type your social proof ("350,000 tea drinkers love us", "tired / stressed-out women"), so the buyer recognises herself. [J16 0:39:20; 0:53:50; 0:54:50]
  - DO raise motivation through **word intensity**, not harshness (happy → elated). [J16 0:17:47–0:19:00]
  - DO use [three selves](../concepts/three-selves.md) to decide *whose* ability you're talking to. [J16 0:32:40–0:33:00]
  - DON'T show "0 reviews", stack competing claims ("6 Day Results" + "30 Doses") or reference something before introducing it. These crush ability. [J16 0:22:30; 0:55:36–0:58:45]
  - DON'T add more prompts to fix a conversion problem until ability is solved (wiki synthesis of [J16 0:05:50–0:06:15]).

## Definition
The **Fogg Behavior Model** (BJ Fogg, Stanford Behavior Design Lab; BehaviorModel.org, ©2007) "basically teaches how behavior gets made inside the brain" [J16 0:03:40]. "People have to have **motivation** to do something, the **ability** to do something and then be **prompted** to do something" [J16 0:04:10]. On the chart, Motivation runs high↔low (y-axis), Ability runs "hard to do"↔"easy to do" (x-axis), and a curved **action line** separates prompts that **succeed** (above) from prompts that **fail** (below).

![Fogg Behavior Model chart: Motivation vs Ability, action line, B=MAP](../assets/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-fogg-behavior-model-00515.jpg)
*The chart Sarah used: B = MAP "at the same moment"; prompts succeed above the action line and fail below it. [J16 0:05:15]*

| State | What happens (Sarah) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| High motivation + hard to do + few prompts | "Nothing really is going to happen… it feels too hard, so I just stay where I am" | [J16 0:04:30] |
| Some motivation + prompted + low ability | Still "in this kind of fail spot" | [J16 0:05:14] |
| Target | "Decent ability, high motivation and then some sort of a nudge to move forward" | [J16 0:05:30] |
| Where marketers sit | "A ton of prompting: now is the time, boost this… limited time offer, here's the discount, this will only be here for 10 days, here's a bundle" | [J16 0:05:50–0:06:15] |

**Ability**, in Sarah's words: "Am I capable of getting this right now? Is it easy for me to do?" For SaaS: "Am I going to be able to get inside the product and use it without three hours of training?" For physical products: "When I get into my house, am I going to want to keep it?" [J16 0:25:30–0:26:13]. "All you're doing when you're trying to talk to ability is **soothe**: it's okay, this is for you, we got you." [J16 0:35:20]

**Ability crusher**: "the thing that's going to drop their ability level… to the point where they don't want to buy." [J16 0:27:14]

## Why it matters
- One small ability fix can beat any new prompt: two 4–5-word labels on a compression-sock PDP lifted conversion **~10%**, about **300 extra orders** in a month on a page with ~750,000 peak-season views [J16 0:06:30–0:10:24].
- "We're really good at prompting but we're pretty bad, I think, about making it easy to do." [J16 0:06:15]
- "A lot of these behavioral scientists are giving us the map; we just stopped halfway through it." (on Prose's half-finished personalization) [J16 0:17:47]
- For ad agents: competitor ads usually show you their prompts and motivation openly. Their ability moves (qualifiers, demos, fit proof) are often what actually make them convert, and they are easy to miss in a teardown (wiki synthesis).

## Procedure
1. **Collect** the ad (script/frames) and its landing page or PDP.
2. **Fill the 3-column audit** (template below) using the classification table. Mark each column strong / weak / missing.
3. **Name the biggest ability crusher.** Ask from the target buyer's chair: will it fit me, is it for my type, can I use it, how long will it last, how big is it, can I afford it, is the claim real? Sarah's own crusher on Olivia Burton: "I just don't know how big those chains are… the images are moving so fast." [J16 0:27:14]
4. **Pick the fix** from the ability-crusher table below. Prefer visual proof or a 4–5-word label over paragraphs.
5. **Choose whose ability.** Use the [three selves](../concepts/three-selves.md): "Self-discrepancy, I think, does a better job of choosing whose abilities." Nutrafol speaks to Ideal ("thicker, fuller, stronger hair") and Actual ("a seamless addition to your daily wellness routine") [J16 0:32:40–0:34:40].
6. **Check motivation.** If copy is neutral Zone 1 ("two gentles in one sentence"), raise word intensity without harshness [J16 0:11:28–0:19:00]. See [tone-shifting](tone-shifting.md).
7. **Check prompts last.** Present but not stacked into pop-ups and countdowns that add cognitive load for first-time visitors [J16 0:44:40–0:49:25]. Note: for some buyers (men buying one "forever" Cuban-link chain) heavy prompting can be right [J16 0:48:22–0:51:30].
8. **Check for self-inflicted ability crushers:** 0-review widgets, competing claims, out-of-order references, tiny text, eye-bouncing layouts [J16 0:21:45–0:22:30; 0:48:22; 0:55:36–0:59:47].
9. **Write the UGC ability beat** (section below) and the page fix, then test one at a time (Darian's slow-feeder timing circles: "test it for CRO" [J16 0:28:50]).

## Applying it to UGC scripts and ads
*July 16 audits pages; the session page notes "each PDP fix is also a line or visual a creator can say/show". The script lines below are (derived) from those fixes unless cited.*

| Fogg element | Script beat | Example line / shot | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ability (type call-out) | Creator names their type in the first seconds | "I have thick, coarse hair and nothing ever works on it…" | Derived from [J16 0:16:43] |
| Ability (fit) | Creator shows the option and who it suits | "I have wide calves, so I got the roomy fit — look." | Derived from [J16 0:08:50] |
| Ability (time proof) | Time-lapse or before/after sequence on camera | Bowl at 10 / 20 / 40 min; chew toy at 1 / 2 / 3 months | Derived from [J16 0:26:13; 0:28:18]; Sarah suggests the static version for ads too |
| Ability (specific situation) | Show the evidence of the problem | "There's hair clogging the drain" + shot of the drain | Cited: founder-video fix [J9 0:39:38–0:40:40] |
| Ability (past failure) | Call out what they've tried before | "You've tried 10 diets…" (wiki example on the J2 page) | Derived from gambler's fallacy [J2 0:06:49–0:08:20] |
| Ability (friction) | Name the barrier and defuse it | "Yeah, we're sketched out about having to put in a credit card too" | Cited: barrier buster [Joanna 0:38:48–0:39:50] |
| Ability (not for everyone) | Qualify the viewer | "This hair care product doesn't work for everyone… but it might work for you" | Cited [J9 0:37:31] |
| Motivation | Raise word intensity | "elated", not "happy" | Cited [J16 0:18:49] |
| Motivation (trust) | Typed proof line on screen | "350,000 tea drinkers love us" | Cited [J16 0:54:50] |
| Prompt | Offer / CTA at the end | "Most customers get the 12 pack" + action CTA | See [offer psychology](offer-and-bundle-psychology.md) |

**DO** keep one ability beat per script, and keep it congruent with the landing page (the same qualifier, label or time proof should appear in both). (derived)

## Output template
```markdown
### Fogg audit: <brand / asset> (<ad | PDP | both>)
- Target buyer: <…> · Self(s) addressed: <Actual | Ideal | Ought>

| | Motivation | Prompt | Ability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present | <intensity, trust signals, guarantee> | <selectors, offers, urgency, CTA> | <labels, qualifiers, types, proof over time, $ savings> |
| Missing / weak | | | |

- Biggest ability crusher: "<buyer's doubt in their words>"
- Fix (page): <label / type grid / qualifier / time sequence / typed proof / remove competing claim>
- Fix (UGC script ability beat): "<line>" + shot <…>
- Self-inflicted crushers found: <0 reviews | competing claims | order | tiny text | layout>
- Test: <one change> · metric: CVR
```

## Common mistakes
- Fixing low conversion with more urgency, discounts and pop-ups (more prompts) when ability is the gap [J16 0:05:50–0:06:15].
- Starting personalization and stopping halfway (Prose) [J16 0:17:47].
- Generic proof ("thousands of women", "350,000 sold") that doesn't let the buyer see herself [J16 0:53:50–0:54:50].
- Neutral language mistaken for safe language. "More intense with your language doesn't necessarily mean harsher." [J16 0:18:30]
- Showing results claims that conflict, or zero reviews [J16 0:22:30; 0:55:36].
- Treating ability as only "physical experience". Gabriela asked whether ability = replacing the in-store experience; Sarah broadened it to "am I capable… is it easy for me", which depends on product type [J16 0:25:11–0:26:13].

## Conflicts and nuances
- **Prompts are not always bad.** Sarah says marketers over-prompt [J16 0:05:50], yet for Jaxxon's male buyers "you might not need a whole lot of motivational text… it's actually better for our dudes if we prompt… urgency, scarcity… offers" [J16 0:48:22–0:51:30]. **Reading:** fix ability first, then match prompt intensity to the buyer.
- **Trust signals sit in Motivation in Sarah's classification** [J16 0:13:20], but reviews from people like me are used as **Ability** on Jaxxon [J16 0:50:27]. The same element can serve both; classify by the doubt it answers.
- **Overlap with self-discrepancy:** Sarah sees Fogg and SDT as lining up "pretty clearly", with SDT choosing *whose* ability [J16 0:32:40–0:33:00]. Selves ≠ identities ("a totally different topic") [J16 0:33:00].
- The related July 2 wrap-up ("all of these heuristics come down to make it easier for the brain to say yes" [J2 0:56:36]) predates the Fogg session but expresses the same ability idea for offers.
