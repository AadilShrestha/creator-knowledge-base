# Story Frameworks for Ad Scripts
> Choose one named story structure for the job the ad has to do (Sarah's "Storytelling Loadout"). Give ChatGPT the concept, the avatar, the main character, the CTA and the framework, with its steps pasted in. Spend about two-thirds of the script on setup, then turn the winning script into static headlines and test speed → hook → story → visuals.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing a UGC, influencer, founder, fake-podcast or voiceover script; remixing a competitor's story ad into a *different* structure for our product; generating static headlines from a script; diagnosing a story video that doesn't convert.
- **Inputs → outputs:** product + one core problem/angle + avatar + main character + CTA + funnel job → (Loadout table) chosen framework → scene-by-scene script with directions → deepest emotion + core message → static headlines → test plan (speed → hook → story → visuals; length 2–3 min → 30 s → 15 s).
- **Rules:**
  - DO name the framework in the prompt. IF the output blends frameworks (e.g. "Monomyth: Hero's Journey") or reads bland THEN paste that framework's steps in, delete any "similar to…" line, and say "follow only this one" [May 14 0:15:36; 0:51:31; 0:54:35].
  - DO always tell ChatGPT the **main character** and the **final CTA** [May 14 0:35:40].
  - DO spend **~2/3 of the script on setup** (problem + lived experience). Solution and CTA go only in the last 3–4 scenes. "The punchline is not the point." [May 14 0:19:45]
  - DO pick the framework by job: Batch 1 for transformation, Batch 2 for conversion / bottom of funnel, Batch 3 for short-form UGC [May 14 0:12:27–0:15:15].
  - DON'T default to AIDA/PAS for everything. Save them for bottom-of-funnel / evaluation [May 14 0:04:56; 0:07:57; 0:13:41; 0:40:09].
  - DO add a **realism** beat (skeptical, "cautiously at first", imperfect results). DON'T write "it transformed everybody overnight" [May 14 0:41:04–0:41:54].
  - DO try characters around the customer (vet, trainer, neighbour, the pet) as narrators. Each one is an avatar test [May 14 0:33:17; 0:40:09].
  - DO brief creators to say the script **in their own words**. Use a natural voiceover (ElevenLabs) when there's no creator [May 14 0:22:33–0:24:30].
  - DO write the script first, then ask for the deepest emotion + core message → headlines. Never ask for "Pixar headlines" directly [May 14 0:25:06].
  - IF a story video flops THEN A/B test **speed → hook → story → visuals/creators**, in that order [May 14 0:44:50–0:45:41].
  - DON'T let ChatGPT invent the avatar from nothing. Ground it in surveys, reviews or Reddit [May 14 0:29:57] (see contradictions).
  - DON'T fabricate proof inside the story (reviews, star counts, "vet-approved") unless the claim is true for our product (KB rule from module 4).
- **See also:** [Creative testing system](creative-testing-system.md) · [Skeptic format](skeptic-format.md) · [Psychology application ladder](psychology-application-ladder.md) · [TEEP journey](teep-journey.md) · [Micro-story](../concepts/heuristics/micro-story.md) · [Open loop](../concepts/heuristics/open-loop.md) · [ChatGPT](../tools/chatgpt.md) · [ElevenLabs](../tools/elevenlabs.md)

## Definition
A **story framework** is a fixed sequence of beats (e.g. Pixar's "Once upon a time… Every day… One day… Because of that… Until finally…") that you hand to an LLM so the script follows an emotional progression instead of a flat feature list. Sarah distilled 15 of them from a 2024 study of comedians, stage speakers, TED talks, bestselling authors and top-grossing films (5–10 h a month), then had ChatGPT group them into the **Storytelling Loadout** by job [May 14 0:02:53–0:04:05; 0:12:27].

## Why it matters
- "Humans are incredibly sensitive to stories, but not just any story. They're really sensitive to how stories progress." The framework decides *when* the solution enters, which is the difference between "hitting the brain in the right spot" and a proof dump [May 14 0:19:38].
- A Pixar-model Maelove script "has a whole lot more emotion in it than if [you] just say write me a UGC script" [May 14 0:17:42].
- Default UGC/AIDA scripts "get so bland after a while… like eating oatmeal every day with nothing on it" [May 14 0:40:40].
- Story generation also surfaces **new angles**. The vet story found "chew time long enough to actually scrape tartar" and the emotion "she no longer had to play the bad guy". The ice-cream story found "ice cream that loves your body back" [May 14 0:38:27; 0:42:00; 0:53:34].
- Sarah: format is step four after identity → emotion → behavior → core message, and "this comes down to storytelling" [May 7 0:38:29–0:39:00] → [Psychology application ladder](psychology-application-ladder.md).

## Inputs
| Input | Notes |
|---|---|
| Product + offer | What we sell. For multi-product brands, decide whether the story sells the **acquisition** product (gets them in fastest) or the **profit** product (subscription/upsell) [May 14 0:39:32–0:40:09] |
| Concept / core problem | One angle, e.g. "people have too many bottles on their countertop" [May 14 0:17:42]. Ideally a message that already won in statics [Jun 11 0:32:53] |
| Avatar | Who she is and what she's struggling with. Must come from real data (pictorial/metaphor surveys, reviews, Reddit), not invented by ChatGPT [May 14 0:29:57] → [Customer research stack](customer-research-stack.md), [CIM surveys](core-identity-map-surveys.md) |
| Main character | Customer, founder, or anyone around the customer (vet, trainer, pet sitter, neighbour, the pet) [May 14 0:33:17] |
| CTA | Always stated, e.g. "shop now" [May 14 0:35:40; 0:49:22] |
| Funnel job | Transformation/origin story, conversion/retargeting, or short-form hook → selects the batch |
| Optional modifiers | "I want five characters" · "we want it to be really intense" · or no context [May 14 0:35:40] |

## Procedure
1. **Pick the job → batch → framework** with the Loadout table below. Stuck and nothing is hitting this week? Use **Pixar** [May 14 0:14:43]. Selling a switch? Use **SCQA** [May 14 0:08:16]. Retargeting or a close? Use **In Medias Res**, PAS or AIDA [May 14 0:13:41–0:14:31].
2. **Prompt** with the recipe below: concept + avatar + framework (+ main character + CTA).
3. **Check for blending.** If the output names two frameworks, follows the wrong steps, or reads generic, re-prompt with the framework's steps pasted in. Delete the "similar to Hero's Journey" line and say "follow only this one" [May 14 0:51:31; 0:54:35].
4. **Run the script QA:**
   - Is at least 2/3 of it setup? [0:19:45]
   - Is there a realism beat? [0:41:04]
   - Does the solution arrive only in the last 3–4 scenes?
   - Is the CTA tied to a person's approval or a habit (e.g. "start the routine that even your vet will notice")? [0:42:30]
5. **Produce:** give the creator the script and ask them to say it their way, or render a natural-voice voiceover. Don't over-slow TTS, or it turns robotic [May 14 0:22:33; 0:48:52].
6. **Derive statics:** paste the finished script back in and ask for "the deepest emotion and the core message… then give us headlines for that" [May 14 0:25:06].
7. **Test** (see the decision tables below): speed → hook → story → visuals. Then the length ladder 2–3 min → 30 s → 15 s, keeping avatar tests at a constant length [May 14 0:44:50; 0:45:41–0:46:50].
8. **Scale a winner:** build an **advertorial** landing page from it and keep the character recurring (e.g. Dr. Riley) as a **fluency mechanism** [May 14 0:48:12–0:48:52] → [Fluency mechanism](../concepts/fluency-mechanism.md).
9. **Spin a series:** same product and framework, new narrator (vet → trainer → sitter → the pet). Each one tests a new avatar [May 14 0:40:09].

```text
We sell <product + one-line what it does>.
Concept: <one core problem/angle, e.g. "too many bottles on the countertop">.
Avatar: <who she is, what she's struggling with — from real research>.
Write me a <UGC | influencer | fake-podcast | voiceover> script using the <Framework> story framework.
Our main character is <customer | founder | vet | trainer | the pet…>. The final CTA: <shop now / …>.
For context: <Framework> — FRAMEWORK: <paste steps from the step tables below, nothing else>.
Follow only this framework. Spend about two-thirds of the script on setup (the problem and the lived experience) before the product appears. Include scene directions. Keep it realistic: skepticism, nuance, no "overnight" claims. Do not invent reviews, stats or endorsements.
```

### The "paste the steps" fix
- **Symptom:** ChatGPT labels the output "THE STORY (The Monomyth: Hero's Journey)" and follows the 12 Hero's Journey stages ("Meet Rachel…"). "It's pulling hero's journey and monomyth at the same time… chat is sometimes real dumb." [0:50:44–0:51:22]
- **Cause:** similar frameworks (Monomyth / Hero's Journey / Three-Act; Pixar / Story Spine; Freytag / Dramatic Arc), and an untrained personal ChatGPT [0:09:00; 0:51:31].
- **Fix:** "go back copy the actual framework in here and… basically force it and say I want you to just follow this one specifically". Remove the "similar to the Hero's Journey" sentence from the pasted text [0:51:31; 0:54:35].
- **Also fixes blandness:** "with hero's journey particularly you'll also get like very bland stories sometimes because hero's journey is used a lot" [0:54:35].

### The two-thirds setup rule
"It's very important that we spend more time setting up the actual story than we do presenting the solution… this whole piece is just setting up the story. This last like three or four scenes are the solution and the call to action… [comedians and stage speakers] spend two-thirds of their story just setting up what's happening, talking about the problem and… my experience around the problem and then just like a little little bitty time on the punchline. The punchline is not the point. The point is I've set you up to get invested in the story." [0:19:45–0:20:41]

| Script length | Setup (problem + experience) | Solution + proof | CTA | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 scenes (Maelove Pixar) | Scenes 1–3 (hook, once upon a time, every day) | 4–7 | 8 | The source script only reaches ~3/8 setup. Per the rule, an agent should expand the problem scenes. Wiki observation |
| 15 s / 30 s / 60 s | ~10 s / ~20 s / ~40 s | rest | last 2–3 s | Timings derived from the 2/3 rule |

Proof doesn't have to come after the solution: the Hero's Journey Maelove script "actually backed out of that", returning to "I didn't think this was going to work" instead of a testimonial dump [0:18:54–0:19:38].

## Decision tables: testing a story video
### Fix order when a story video doesn't convert [May 14 0:42:59–0:48:12]
"When it comes to video we have such little control over what converts people… it could literally be like the plants in the background." [0:43:09]

| Test # | Change only this | How | Why (Sarah) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Speed** | Slower voice (creator or voiceover). Pause on the lines you want heard. Expand cuts from 2–3 s to ~4 s | "The faster you go through a story the more difficult it is for people to attach emotionally to it." "It's almost always the speed that's the issue." | [0:43:30; 0:44:06; 0:44:50; 0:47:13–0:48:12] |
| 2 | **Hook** | New opening line/visual, same story | "This is social media and we have to make sure that we understand what's drawing people in" | [0:45:07] |
| 3 | **Story** | New framework or narrator. If a creator isn't hitting it, change the story to what feels natural to them | | [0:45:07] |
| 4 | **Visuals / creators** | New footage, new creator | | [0:45:07] |

Benchmarks: clip-cut standard **2–3 s**, longest she's seen **4 s**. Past 4 s attention can drop "unless the story is strong" (30-minute talking-head YouTube works because "the story is [not] too boring") [0:43:47; 0:47:13]. Over-slowed ElevenLabs voices sound robotic, so find the sweet spot [0:48:52]. → Full testing context: [Creative testing system](creative-testing-system.md).

### Length ladder [May 14 0:45:41–0:46:50]
| Step | Length | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Run as-is at 2–3 min | Longer videos "currently" convert better: more context, story and emotion |
| 2 | "Chat, we need to cut this down to 30 seconds" | Keep avatar tests at the same length |
| 3 | 15 s | "If… my customer type just hates long videos, just have chat cut it down" |

**A. Pixar model → UGC script (verbatim from screen) [0:17:40]**

![Maelove Pixar UGC script](../assets/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-14-maelove-pixar-script-01740.jpg)
*"UGC Script for Maelove – Pixar Model": scene directions in bold, lines in italics. [0:17:40]*

1. **Hook** (Scene: person scrolling on their phone, sighing dramatically): "You ever buy a skincare product just knowing it's not gonna work… but you do it anyway? Yeah. That was me with Maelove." (Pauses, smirks) "Unfortunately, now I have to admit I was wrong."

**B. Hero's Journey → influencer script (verbatim from screen, as far as visible) [0:18:45]**

![Maelove Hero's Journey influencer script](../assets/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-14-maelove-heros-journey-script-01845.jpg)
*"Influencer Script for Maelove – The Hero's Journey Model". [0:18:45]*

Hook (person holding a random skincare bottle, unimpressed): "I'm gonna be honest… I did not want to like this." ## Common mistakes
- "Write me a UGC script" with no framework, which gives flat scripts [0:17:42].
- Letting ChatGPT blend Monomyth / Hero's Journey (or produce bland Hero's Journey) instead of pasting the steps [0:50:44; 0:54:35].
- Rushing to the solution, so the setup is under 2/3 [0:19:45].
- Proof dumps after the solution instead of letting the story carry the proof [0:18:54].
- Hype with no realism ("transformed everybody overnight") [0:41:08].
- Asking for "Pixar headlines" directly instead of script → emotion → headlines [0:25:06].
- Creators reading the script verbatim ("it's the way Sarah would say it") [0:22:44].
- Robot voices, or TTS slowed so much it sounds robotic [0:24:00; 0:48:52].
- Changing visuals first when a story video fails, instead of speed [0:44:50].
- Using AI UGC for trust-dependent brands. Attendees' Creatify videos didn't convert [0:26:56; 0:30:53].
- Building the avatar from ChatGPT's imagination [0:29:57].

## Contradictions and source notes
- **Count/numbering:** the doc says "15 frameworks" but skips #12 (14 named). The Loadout reaches 15 only with the Founder's Post bonus, and numbers Seven Basic Plots 14 where the doc has 13 [0:10:25; 0:12:27].
- **StoryBrand steps:** the doc ("A Character → Has a Problem → And Meets a Guide → Who Gives Them a Plan → And Calls Them to Action…", cut off) and ChatGPT's 7-step list ("…Takes Action → Avoids Failure → Finds Success") differ [0:07:16; 0:14:20].
- **Story Spine:** the doc has 6 lines. ChatGPT's list adds "And ever since then…", and so does Sarah's own description [0:11:35].
- **Freytag vs Dramatic Arc:** identical step lists in the doc. Sarah separates them only by pacing (slow curve vs "crazy quickly") [0:05:10; 0:12:00].
- **Cut length:** 2–3 s is the standard, 4 s the max she's seen, and past 4 s attention drops. Yet longer videos convert better and 30-min talking heads work. She resolves it as "the story is too boring", not attention span [0:43:47; 0:47:13].
- **ChatGPT for avatars:** May 14 says avoid ChatGPT as an ICP generator [0:29:57]. May 7 suggests ChatGPT for a candidate list, then verify on Reddit ([May 7](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md)). Default: use ChatGPT only to organise real research.
- **Direction of derivation:** May 14 goes script → headlines [0:25:06]. June 11 goes winning static message → script [0:32:53]. Both work. Use whichever asset already has signal.
- **Ice cream names:** V1 "Rachel", V2 "Emily" (different generations).
