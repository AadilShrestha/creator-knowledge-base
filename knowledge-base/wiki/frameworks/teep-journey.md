# TEEP Journey (Trigger → Exploration → Evaluation → Purchase)
> Sarah's paid-ads version of a Google consumer-journey study: buyers hit a **trigger event**, then loop between **exploration** and **evaluation** until emotional pressure gets strong enough to tip them into **purchase**. Write ads for all four stages, keep the stages apart in the account, and read which stage takes off to learn how educated your market is.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** (1) tagging a competitor ad's journey stage during a teardown; (2) planning a creative batch so it covers all four stages instead of only trigger and offer ads; (3) writing the campaign / ad-set structure and naming for a test; (4) reading test results to decide what to produce next.
- **Inputs → outputs:** product + 1–3 target emotions (see [mindstate emotion mapping](mindstate-emotion-mapping.md)) + budget level → a TEEP brief (ads per stage per emotion, structure, names) → after the test, a read-out (which stage won, what that says about customer education, what to produce next).
- **Rules:**
  - DO write ads for **all four stages**. Most brands run only trigger-event and purchase (discount/bundle/urgency) ads and skip "two full ranges of behavior". [May 21 0:06:19–0:07:23]
  - DO make **exploration** ads the weirdest, most creative content you have; they pull in people "on the fringes" (acquisition). [May 21 0:07:23–0:08:24]
  - DO make **evaluation** ads about us vs them: ingredients, materials, manufacturing, founder ads, "what we do best", and behaviour call-outs ("Amazon's got knockoffs, but we do this full time"). [May 21 0:08:24–0:09:20]
  - DO keep stages from blending in the data: one TEEP stage per campaign (healthy budget) or per ad set (limited budget), one emotion per ad set. DON'T put the whole TEEP model in one ad set. [Jun 4 0:15:38–0:16:41; 0:39:49–0:40:55]
  - DO test **one learning goal per campaign** (stage, or generation, or emotion/zone). Don't mash every model together. [Jun 11 0:48:40–0:50:47]
  - IF the budget is small THEN stay close to purchase: run exploration, evaluation and purchase ads first. Triggers are endless and "great for big budgets". [Jun 11 0:46:34–0:47:37]
  - DO test the stages with **statics first**, then turn the winning stage's message into GIF / animation / UGC / pro video. [May 21 0:11:31–0:12:35]
  - DO name ads so stage and emotion are readable in Ads Manager ("in this ad I'm testing [X]"). [Jun 4 0:51:32–0:52:34]
  - DO move purchase-stage winning lines up into other stages; they show "which messaging is going to work to push that emotional pressure". [Jul 23 0:12:36–0:14:42]
  - DON'T treat a purchase as "this ad made them buy". The ad "just happened to hit right at the time that their emotional pressure was stronger". [Jun 4 0:12:28]
- **See also:** [Mindstate emotion mapping](mindstate-emotion-mapping.md) · [Creative testing system](creative-testing-system.md) · [Format variation ladder](format-variation-ladder.md) · [Psychology application ladder](psychology-application-ladder.md) · [Valence-intensity diagnostic](valence-intensity-diagnostic.md) · [Offer and bundle psychology](offer-and-bundle-psychology.md) (purchase-stage ads) · [Skeptic format](skeptic-format.md) (evaluation-stage UGC)

## Definition: the four stages
TEEP is "built from actually a Google study" on how people really walk the journey: "there is no step-by-step down the funnel… people kind of morph in and out… this weird looping behavior." Sarah calls the funnel "way, way too simplified for what people actually do". [May 21 0:04:10–0:05:13; Jun 4 0:11:24]

| Stage | What the buyer is doing (Sarah) | Ad job | Ad types Sarah names | Example lines / notes | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T: Trigger event** | Something happened: "you bought a pet, you sold your house, you gained 30 pounds". One-way: "once the trigger phase happens… now you're kind of in the ecosystem." | Talk **only to the event** | Event call-outs. This is where marketers usually stay and where angles come from | "You want to lose 30 pounds? Here's a product for you." "Do you want to get better at your golf swing?" "A family that just bought a house." | May 21 0:06:19; Jun 4 0:11:24; Jun 11 0:45:32; Jul 23 0:13:39 |
| **E: Exploration** | "What's out here, what products exist… which brands offer this?" Low-educated, can stay here a long time, "watch a lot of TikTok, follow a lot of influencers, go on YouTube" | Entertain and introduce; the content itself is the draw | The weirdest, most creative content. Before/afters are "exploration-ish" | "Get water balloons and pop them all over and make them a paint… try and shave a cat." Jun 11 also puts brand introduction here: Liquid Death "everybody else is a plastic water bottle… we actually have skulls and crossbones" | May 21 0:07:23–0:08:24; Jun 4 0:12:28, 0:16:41; Jun 11 0:45:32 |
| **E: Evaluation** | "What's the difference between two brands… does this one have a better price", features, colours | Prove you're the better choice | Us vs them, ingredients, materials, manufacturing, founder ads, behaviour call-outs. Before/afters that call out another brand | "You've already went to Amazon. Amazon sucks, don't go there… Amazon's got knockoffs, but we do this full time." | May 21 0:08:24–0:09:20; Jun 4 0:12:28, 0:16:41 |
| **P: Purchase** | Happens when "the emotional pressure that someone feels gets strong enough that they can't do anything about it anymore" | Remove the last barrier | Offer: discount, bundle, sale, urgency, scarcity | Usually "extremely persuasive… which is not how the brain works". "You're 1 click away from better underwear" won as a purchase-stage line | May 21 0:06:19; Jun 4 0:12:28; Jun 11 0:46:34; Jul 23 0:12:36 |

**Trigger events vs motivators** (for writing trigger-stage ads): triggering events are stable, distinct life moments ("I just moved", "a wedding coming up and I want to lose 30 pounds", "I just bought a puppy"). Motivators are temporary moods that "wax and wane" ("I didn't drink enough coffee this morning"). Write trigger ads to events. [May 21 0:39:40–0:40:44] A trigger event can also connect two identities into an instant buy ("impulse buys don't exist"). [May 21 0:45:03–0:47:06]

## Why it matters
- **Account structure trains the pixel.** "Your account structure is indicative of your account. You're training your pixel." Sarah uses TEEP "because I'm trying to get very, very scientific." [May 21 0:04:10]
- **Conversion optimisation only finds ready buyers.** Optimising for conversions "only lets us dip into mid to bottom of this funnel… you can't get new customers." Stage-specific ads are how you teach the algorithm to go after new people. Optimisation happens at the ad-set level (Jamie Ahmed in chat, Sarah agreed). [May 21 0:12:35–0:13:36]
- **The winning stage diagnoses your market.** It tells you whether to widen the top or nurture. [Jun 4 0:21:52–0:22:56]
- Running all four stages can shorten the time people spend in the loop. [Jun 4 0:12:28]

## Inputs
| Input | Notes |
|---|---|
| Product + offer | The offer is needed for purchase-stage ads |
| 1–3 target emotions | From reviews mapped to the nine mindstate emotions ([mindstate emotion mapping](mindstate-emotion-mapping.md)). Sarah starts with emotions split, not blended [Jun 4 0:14:34–0:15:38] |
| Optional split dimension | Generation (boomers / Gen X / millennials / Gen Z) if that's this month's learning goal [May 21 0:09:28] |
| Budget level | Limited vs healthy decides the structure (table below) |
| Trigger events | List of real life events that start the category journey (puppy, move, wedding, weight gain) |
| Proof assets | Ingredients, materials, manufacturing, founder story, competitor differences for evaluation ads |

## Procedure
1. **Pick this test's learning goal.** TEEP stage? Generation? Emotion / zone? Pick one per campaign. "I usually use them one at a time because I'm trying very carefully not to blend all this data together." [Jun 11 0:48:40–0:49:43]
2. **Pick the emotions** (usually ~3 per industry) and write each one precisely. [Jun 4 0:13:31–0:17:41]
3. **Write the ads per stage per emotion.** Sarah's counts: ~9 ads per emotion ad set [Jun 4 0:15:38]; 10–15 per stack when all four stages share an ad set [May 21 0:09:28]. Use the stage table above. Statics first. [May 21 0:11:31]
4. **Choose the structure** with the budget table below and **name everything** with stage + emotion. [Jun 4 0:39:49–0:40:55; 0:51:32]
5. **Launch and let Meta filter.** Only a few ads will get spend: "that's okay with me", because the winner tells you how fast customers reach the end. [May 21 0:10:30–0:11:31]
6. **Read the winning stage** with the read-out table.
7. **Scale the winning stage into motion:** "take that piece of the funnel and make it UGC, pro video, like podcast… it's a repeat recipe." [May 21 0:12:35]
8. **Cross-pollinate:** paste purchase-stage winning lines into other stages' creatives and test them there. [Jul 23 0:14:42]

## Account structure: two versions taught, and the recommended default

| | **Version A (May 21, 2025)** | **Version B (June 4, 2025)** |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign | One per **generation** (boomers, Gen X, millennials, Gen Z) "so I can understand which generation is actually resonating" | One per **TEEP stage**: "one campaign for trigger… one for exploration, one for evaluative and one for purchase" |
| Ad set | One per **emotion** (e.g. esteem) | One per **emotion** |
| Ads | 10–15 statics, **all four stage types in every ad set**: "I'll have trigger event ads… exploratory ads… evaluative ads and a purchase ad in every single bucket" | ~9 ads, one stage only, "nine different ads that just say esteem in slightly different ways" |
| What it measures | Generation × emotion; stage mix is a read-out ("do we see boomers… really close to purchasing?") | Stage, cleanly: "so that we're not blending" data |
| Sarah's caveat | "One generation at a time, one emotion at a time" | All of TEEP in one ad set gets "very confusing… was this one a trigger ad or an exploration ad?" |
| Source | [May 21 0:09:28–0:11:31](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-21-live-with-sarah.md) | [Jun 4 0:15:38–0:16:41; 0:21:52](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) |

**June 11 tie-breaker:** "I build my campaign based upon what I want to test." Generational data → structure by demographic; customer-cycle data → TEEP; best emotions now → valence-intensity (campaign = emotion, ad sets = zones). [Jun 11 0:21:13–0:22:19; 0:48:40–0:49:43]

**Recommended default for agents (derived):** use **Version B**, the later and more explicit of the two. One campaign per stage, one ad set per emotion, ~9 ads per ad set, all statics. It isolates the stage, which is the whole point of TEEP, and it matches the June 11 one-learning-goal rule. Use **Version A** only when this month's learning goal is generation × emotion. In that case the stage mix inside each ad set is a secondary signal, and every ad must carry its stage in the name. Version A puts three dimensions (generation, emotion, stage) in one test, which is the blending June 4 and June 11 warn against.

## Budget decision table

| Budget situation | Structure | Stages to run | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Healthy | One campaign per stage, ad set per emotion | All four | Jun 4 0:40:55 |
| Limited | One campaign, **one ad set per TEEP stage** ("ad set one is trigger, ad set two is evaluation…"), then build out | All four if possible | Jun 4 0:39:49–0:40:55 |
| Small / new business | Stay "closer to the purchase than the trigger side" | Exploration, evaluation, purchase. Add trigger ads later | Jun 11 0:46:34–0:47:37 |
| Only one testing campaign | Strict naming: "in this ad I'm testing [X]" | Any | Jun 4 0:51:32–0:52:34 |
| Big budget | Add trigger-event ads at volume. Triggers are so specific ("it's uncomfortable to wear a seat belt") "you could never ever run enough ads to hit them all" | All four, heavy on trigger | Jun 11 0:46:34–0:47:37 |

*No dollar thresholds are given in the course. "Limited" vs "healthy" is the operator's call.*

## Read-out decision table: which stage won and what to do

| Observed | What it means (Sarah) | What to do | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evaluation campaign / ads take off | "Pretty highly educated customers in my market" | "Put more effort into widening the top of the funnel": more exploration (and trigger) creative | Jun 4 0:21:52 |
| Trigger-event ads take off | "Very low educated, they just started this journey… haven't even figured out whether they want to do this yet" | "A lot more nurturing": build exploration and evaluation content | Jun 4 0:21:52–0:22:56 |
| Exploration ads (the creative ones) win | The customer type "is really resonating more just with the content than… with the offer" | "As a brand I need to produce more content" | May 21 0:11:31 |
| Purchase ads win in a generation × emotion stack | That emotion brings that generation in "really close to purchasing" | Scale that emotion for that segment. Copy the purchase line up-funnel | May 21 0:10:30; Jul 23 0:14:42 |
| Evaluation (us vs them) wins for one generation | That generation "resonates with that a little bit better" | Make more evaluation creative for that segment | May 21 0:10:30 |
| Only a few ads get spend | Expected | Read the stage of the spenders, don't force spend | May 21 0:10:30–0:11:31 |
| A winning static at any stage | Core message is taking off | Turn it into GIF / animation / UGC / pro video at the same stage ([format variation ladder](format-variation-ladder.md)) | May 21 0:11:31–0:12:35 |

## Teardown use: tagging ads by stage
- Tag every competitor ad with its TEEP stage. "Some of these brands are only running one specific level of the model." The stages a category ignores are open space. [Jun 4 0:39:49]
- Use Darian's tag stack for every concept: **zone · intensity · self · need · TEEP phase**, e.g. "Zone 4, High, Actual, Competence, Evaluate". [Jul 23 0:34:44; 0:36:00]
- Classification cues: event call-out = T; entertaining/weird content or before/after = E1 (exploration); comparison, ingredients, founder, "why not Amazon", before/after naming a competitor = E2 (evaluation); discount, bundle, urgency, scarcity = P. [May 21 0:06:19–0:09:20; Jun 4 0:16:41]

## UGC script skeletons per stage (derived)
The course gives stage definitions and static examples, not UGC scripts. These skeletons apply the stage jobs above to UGC. Treat them as starting points, not Sarah's templates.

| Stage | Hook pattern (derived) | Body | Close |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Creator names the event in the first line: "I just got a puppy and nobody warned me about ___" | What changed in their life and the need it created | Soft: "this is what I ended up trying" |
| Exploration | Pattern-break or weird demo: unexpected visual, experiment, entertaining stunt with the product | Content first, product as the vehicle | Brand name / "follow for more", no hard offer |
| Evaluation | "I compared ___ vs ___ so you don't have to" / "why I stopped buying this on Amazon" / founder: "I started this because ___" | Ingredients, materials, process, side-by-side proof; a [skeptic](skeptic-format.md) test fits here | "Here's why I pay a bit more" |
| Purchase | "If you've been on the fence…" / "1 click away from ___" | The offer, bundle, guarantee, honest scarcity | Specific CTA with the offer ([offer and bundle psychology](offer-and-bundle-psychology.md)) |

## Output templates

**TEEP test brief**
```markdown
### TEEP test: <product> / <month>
- Learning goal (one): stage | generation | emotion
- Structure: Version B (campaign per stage) | limited-budget (one campaign, ad set per stage) | Version A (campaign per generation)
- Emotions: <E1 precise wording>, <E2>, <E3>
- Ads per ad set: ~9 statics (B) | 10–15 mixed-stage (A)
- Stage coverage: T <n> · E1 <n> · E2 <n> · P <n>   (small budget: skip T)
- Naming: <STAGE>_<EMOTION>_<angle>_<format>_v<n>   e.g. EVAL_SECURITY_amazon-knockoffs_static_v1
- Read-out: which campaign/ad set takes spend + converts → education level → next production step
- Next: winning stage's static → GIF/carousel → UGC → pro video
```
*(The naming pattern is derived from Sarah's "be really careful with your naming convention" [Jun 4 0:51:32]. She gives no exact format.)*

**Teardown tag line**
```text
<Brand> | <ad id/link> | Stage: T/E1/E2/P | Zone <1-4> | Intensity <low/high> | Self <A/I/O> | Need <…> | Emotion <…> | Why this stage: <cue>
```

## Common mistakes
- Running only trigger ads and offer ads and skipping exploration and evaluation. [May 21 0:06:19]
- Putting all four stages in one ad set with no naming, so you can't tell which stage won. [Jun 4 0:16:41]
- Testing TEEP × generation × zone × self in one campaign: "there's no freaking way you can tell what's going on." [Jun 11 0:49:43]
- Spreading a small budget over endless trigger events. [Jun 11 0:47:37]
- Making the purchase ad do all the persuading. [May 21 0:06:19]
- Producing video before a static has shown which stage and message work. [May 21 0:11:31]

## Contradictions and open points
- **Structure (May 21 vs June 4):** see the two-version table above. Default: June 4 (campaign per stage). The May 21 page flags the same conflict.
- **Small budget (June 4 vs June 11):** June 4 keeps all four stages as ad sets in one campaign. June 11 drops the trigger stage. These can be combined: one campaign, three ad sets (E1, E2, P), adding T when budget allows (derived).
- **Ads per ad set:** 10–15 (May 21, mixed stages) vs ~9 (June 4, one stage).
- **What exploration ads are:** May 21 = the weirdest, most entertaining content. June 11 = "introducing yourself… here's the reason why we're better than everybody else", which sounds like May 21's evaluation (us vs them). Before/afters sit between the two (June 4). Tag by the dominant job: entertaining/introducing = exploration; explicit comparison = evaluation.
- **Where the TEEP graph lives:** Sarah says it's in the Heuristics 101 bonus playbook [May 21 0:54:02]. The extracted playbook and e-book text don't mention it (possibly in PDF images; unverified). A TEEP course ("July or August") and a TEEP swipe file were promised [Jun 4 0:10:19; 0:39:49]. Neither is in this course dump.

## Outside context (not from the course)
Sarah never names the study. It is almost certainly Google / The Behavioural Architects, *Decoding Decisions: Making Sense of the Messy Middle* (2020). That study describes triggers, then a loop between exploration and evaluation (the "messy middle"), then purchase, with exposure to the category happening throughout. Use it for background only. The stage definitions, ad types and account structure above are Sarah's.
