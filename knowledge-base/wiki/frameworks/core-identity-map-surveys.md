# Core Identity Map (CIM) Surveys and Survey-Question Design
> Sarah's **Core Identity Map** replaces text multiple-choice surveys with **picture-based and metaphorical questions** ("If this brand was a superhero, which one would it be?"), so customers answer from emotion and association instead of logic. Pair it with three wording rules for any survey or interview: ask how people **feel**, never what they **think**; open with an **NPS-style 1–10** then ask **"what would get you to a 10?"**; and **never lead** or name the feeling for them.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** designing a customer survey, post-purchase survey, quiz or interview script to feed ad angles; auditing a brand's existing survey before trusting its data; turning survey answers into identities, behaviors and hooks (layer 3 of the [customer research stack](customer-research-stack.md)).
- **Inputs → outputs:** product + research goal (identity, behavior, objections, retention, consumption rate) + customer list → a survey draft (picture questions with image briefs, metaphor questions, NPS + follow-up, open "how did you feel" items) + a read-out table (identity, behavior, outliers, objections → angles/hooks).
- **Rules:**
  - DO build questions around **pictures** "so that people can have an emotional response", or **metaphors** that "help people make an association without having to logic their way". [Jun 4 0:07:10–0:08:11]
  - DO use about **four images** per question and choose them carefully: images "could weight answers very easily". [May 7 0:26:48–0:27:20]
  - DON'T label images with names that signal status or price (the cashmere flop). [May 7 0:27:50–0:29:00]
  - DON'T offer forced either/or choices ("yellow or blue?" when the answer is purple). [May 7 0:27:20–0:27:50]
  - DON'T use the word **think**: "it causes the brain to go into thinking mode." Ask "how do you **feel**". [Jun 4 0:55:41–0:56:43]
  - DO open with NPS: "On a scale of one to ten, how do you feel about this?" Then: "What would get you up to a ten?" That surfaces objections and what's missing. [Jun 4 0:55:41–0:56:43]
  - DON'T lead ("was it fun?", "was it XYZ?"): "they're going to answer the way they think you want them to answer." Keep it open-ended. [Jun 4 0:56:43–0:57:44]
  - DON'T name the feeling: "extract the emotion" and let them choose it. [Jun 4 0:57:44]
  - DO ask about **behavior**, not just opinion: where the product is stored, who uses it, how often they think they use it vs actually use it. [May 7 0:18:40–0:19:22]
  - DO look for **outliers**, not averages. [Jun 4 0:23:59; May 7 0:19:22]

## Definition
"I use something called a CIM, it's a core identity map… they're picture-based surveys, so all of the questions that we have are run to pictures so that people can have an emotional response to the questions." [Jun 4 0:07:10] She switched all her surveys "about a year ago over to picture-based or metaphorical questions. The metaphorical questions would be things like: if this brand was a superhero, which one would it be." [Jun 4 0:08:11] On July 16 she describes the service as "picture-based surveys to help people pull out all of these emotional motivators, and specifically behavioral ones". [Jul 16 0:07:18] On May 7 she calls the same thing **pictorial** or **metaphorical surveys** and compares them to "which Harry Potter house are you in" quizzes: you click images and get bucketed. [May 7 0:16:11; 0:26:31–0:26:48]

Sarah never showed a full CIM (they are brand-specific) [May 7 0:26:31] and offered to teach members how to run one [Jun 4 0:07:10]. The question bank and image-brief method below are therefore partly **(derived)**: built from her rules, not copied from her instrument.

*(Wiki note: the July 16 page suggests a resemblance to ZMET, Zaltman's picture-based metaphor method. Unconfirmed; Sarah never names it.)*

## Why it matters
- **Text surveys need reading comprehension** ("in the US right now I think we're reading at about a ninth-grade reading level", as she says) and invite logical, polite answers. [Jun 4 0:07:10–0:08:11]
- **Pictures get feelings.** "Pictures often elicit a better emotional response than logical surveys." She has "my own beef with post-purchase surveys". [May 7 0:27:20]
- **Demographics don't explain purchase.** "She's 29, lives in Chicago with two cats… That's just a snapshot… doesn't tell me anything about why she would buy from me." Avatar work "needs to start with identity, but then behavior is pretty important." [May 7 0:16:25–0:17:51]
- **Badly worded surveys lie, and cost money.** The cashmere sweater nobody bought. [May 7 0:27:50–0:29:00]
- **Reviews can't tell you behavior.** Review NLP shows "what they were thinking at that time and what they were feeling. It doesn't necessarily tell you behaviorally why they purchased." [Jun 4 0:50:27–0:51:32] CIM is the layer that does.

## Inputs
| Input | Notes |
|---|---|
| Research goal | Identity (who they are), behavior (what happens to the product), objections, retention ("what makes you feel special"), consumption rate (for bundles) |
| Audience | Buyers (post-purchase), email list (warm), or cold panel. Sarah's ~40-brand study compared cold traffic with newsletter subscribers [Jun 4 0:08:11] |
| Image pool | Photos for each picture question, unlabelled, ~4 per question |
| Channel | Survey/quiz tool, post-purchase page, in-person conversation (party business example) [Jun 4 0:54:36] |

## Procedure
1. **Pick one goal per survey block** (identity, behavior, objections, retention, consumption). *(derived)*
2. **Write metaphor questions** for identity: "If this brand was a superhero, which one would it be?" [Jun 4 0:08:11] More in the same style are in the question bank (derived).
3. **Write picture questions.** For each: ~4 images, no captions or names, no status or price cues, comparable photo quality so no image "wins" by looking nicer. [May 7 0:26:48–0:29:00] *(Equal photo quality is (derived) from "images could weight answers very easily".)*
4. **Write behavior questions.** Storage, who uses it, perceived vs actual frequency, what they did before, how many they own. [May 7 0:18:40–0:19:22]
5. **Add the NPS pair**: "On a scale of one to ten, how do you feel about <experience/product>?" + "What would get you up to a ten?" [Jun 4 0:55:41–0:56:43]
6. **Add 1–2 open feeling questions**: "How did you feel about this?" / "What was your experience like?" Use **how** for a range, **what** to make them pick a specific emotion. [Jun 4 0:57:44–0:58:46]
7. **Lint every question** with the decision table below. Remove "think", leading adjectives, named feelings, forced binaries, labels.
8. **Run it**, then read for **outliers and behavioral patterns**, not majority vote. [Jun 4 0:23:59]
9. **Translate** findings into identity + behavior messages (the "40 bottles" → "last time you'll ever switch" move), and feed them into the [three selves](three-selves-diagnostic.md) and [valence-intensity](valence-intensity-diagnostic.md) passes. [May 7 0:17:51–0:20:05]
10. **Optional:** paste the real answers into ChatGPT: "Here's what we found from their lifestyle… give us a good ICP." Only with real answers, never as a substitute. [May 7 0:20:58–0:21:30; May 14 0:29:57–0:30:53]

### Decision table: lint a survey question
| If the question… | Problem | Rewrite to… | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uses "think" ("What do you think about…?") | Triggers logical "thinking mode" | "How do you feel about…?" | [Jun 4 0:55:41](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-06-04-live-with-sarah.md) |
| Names the expected feeling ("Was it fun?", "Did you love it?") | Leading; they answer what you want | "How did you feel about this?" / "What was your experience like?" | [Jun 4 0:56:43–0:57:44] |
| Asks about a negative ("Did you find anything frustrating?") | "Kind of leading", though it surfaces objections | Prefer NPS + "what would get you to a 10?"; keep the frustration question only for objection mining | [Jun 4 0:57:44] |
| Offers two options only | Forced choice hides the real answer (purple) | ~4 options or open-ended | [May 7 0:27:20–0:27:50] |
| Labels images with names/materials/prices | Turns it into a status vote; they answer as their [ideal self](../concepts/ideal-self.md) | Unlabelled images | [May 7 0:27:50–0:29:00](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-07-live-with-sarah.md) |
| Asks "how often do you use it?" only | Perceived use ≠ actual use | Ask both "how often do you think…" and a concrete behavior (what's on your counter right now?) | [May 7 0:18:40–0:19:22] |
| Asks about the product only (retention) | Misses what makes them feel special | "What makes you feel special, just as a person, not as a customer?" | [Jun 4 0:10:19] |
| Asks demographics only | No purchase motive | Identity, hobbies, who they are outside the product, friends, family time | [May 7 0:16:25; 0:20:58] |

### Decision table: finding → ad action
| Survey finding | Ad action | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Behavioral outlier (owns 15–50 bottles, won't throw them out: sunk cost) | Identity + behavior message: "this is the last time you're ever going to switch" | [May 7 0:17:51–0:20:05] |
| NPS 7 + "what gets you to 10" answers | Objection list → objection-handling hooks and landing-page copy *(hook use is (derived))* | [Jun 4 0:55:41–0:56:43] |
| Picks the aspirational option in a labelled survey | Distrust it for product decisions; test both actual- and ideal-self messages | [May 7 0:27:50–0:29:00] |
| Retention: what makes them feel special (dates, flowers…) | Surprise-and-delight touches in the brand, not loyalty points | [Jun 4 0:09:15–0:10:19] |
| Consumption rate (e.g. ~2 packs a month) | Bundle sizes in buckets that fit ("two, four, eight", not "three, six, twelve"); "Most people go through this bag in three months, so you'll want the four-month pack" | [Jul 2 0:35:38–0:36:39; 0:47:10–0:49:11](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-02-live-with-sarah.md) |
| Cold traffic responds to identity, warm doesn't need it | Acquisition ads: strong emotion + identity ("He's not just part of the family, he is the family"); retention: make them feel special | [Jun 4 0:08:11–0:09:15] |

## Question bank
Items marked (S) are Sarah's own wording; the rest are **(derived)** from her rules.

| Block | Question | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Identity (metaphor) | "If this brand was a superhero, which one would it be?" (S) [Jun 4 0:08:11] | Picture choice (4 unlabelled characters) or open |
| Identity (metaphor) | If <product> was a place / a song / a person at a party, which would it be? | Picture choice, 4 options |
| Identity (picture) | Which of these mornings looks most like yours? | 4 unlabelled photos |
| Identity (life) | What do you do for fun? Who are you outside of <category>? (from "hobbies, who they are outside the product, friends, family time") [May 7 0:20:58] | Open |
| Behavior | Where does <product> live in your home? Who uses it? (S, paraphrased) [May 7 0:18:40] | Picture choice (bathroom counter / drawer / bag / …) or open |
| Behavior | How often do you think you use it? And how many did you use last week? (from "how often do you think you use it compared to how often do you actually use it") | Number |
| Behavior | How many other <category> products do you have right now? | Number (this is the question type that found "40 bottles") |
| Consumption | How fast does your family go through a bag? (S) [Jul 2 0:47:10] | Number / picture of quantities |
| NPS | "On a scale of one to ten, how do you feel about <this>?" (S) [Jun 4 0:55:41] | 1–10 |
| NPS follow-up | "What would get you up to a ten?" (S) [Jun 4 0:55:41] | Open |
| Experience | "How did you feel about this? What was your experience like?" (S) [Jun 4 0:57:44] | Open |
| Retention | "What makes you feel special, just as a person, not as a customer?" (S) [Jun 4 0:10:19] | Open or picture choice (flowers, dinner out, handwritten note…) |

## Output template: survey and read-out
```markdown
### CIM survey: <brand> / <goal> / <audience> (<date>)
**Q1 Identity (metaphor):** "If <brand> was a superhero, which one would it be?"
  Images: [A] <brief, no label> [B] <…> [C] <…> [D] <…>
**Q2 Identity (picture):** "<question>" Images: [A]…[D] (no labels, no price/status cues, equal photo quality)
**Q3 Behavior:** "Where does <product> live in your home?" · "How many other <category> products do you have right now?"
**Q4 NPS:** "On a scale of one to ten, how do you feel about <product/experience>?"
**Q5:** "What would get you up to a ten?"
**Q6:** "How did you feel about <experience>?"
Lint: [ ] no "think" [ ] no named feelings [ ] no either/or [ ] no image labels [ ] ~4 images per picture question

### Read-out
| Finding | Majority / outlier | Evidence (answer text or image share) | Identity / self | Emotion (specific) | Behavior | Ad angle / hook candidate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| <…> | outlier | "I have 40 bottles on my counter" | holistic, Actual | relief / security | hoards unused products | "The last <product> switch you'll ever make" |
| NPS avg <x>; "to get to 10" themes | – | <top 3 themes> | – | – | – | objection hooks: <…> |
```

## Common mistakes
- Labelled images (status vote). [May 7 0:27:50]
- Too many images, or images of unequal appeal. It took Sarah "months" (May 7) or "a good year" (June 4) to learn image choice. [May 7 0:26:48; Jun 4 0:23:59]
- "What do you think of…" questions. [Jun 4 0:55:41]
- Leading closed questions ("was it fun?"): "they're always going to say ten." [Jun 4 0:56:43]
- Treating bland post-purchase survey answers as the customer's real emotional state. Reddit can show a 10/10 emotion that the surveys miss. [Jun 4 0:28:12]
- Using survey data from one season all year. [May 7 0:10:15–0:10:51]
- Building the ICP from ChatGPT instead of survey data. [May 14 0:29:57–0:30:53]

## Conflicts and open questions
- **Sarah breaks her own rule on air.** Right after "never use the word think", she phrases NPS as "on a scale of one to ten, what do you think about this", then corrects herself to "how do you feel". Use "feel". [Jun 4 0:55:41–0:56:43]
- **Post-purchase surveys.** May 7: "my own beef with post-purchase surveys" [0:27:20]. June 4: NPS is the first thing she'd ask, "which sounds really weird because Sarah loves… emotional questions and… picture-based questions" [0:55:41]. Reconciled: keep NPS + "what gets you to 10" as the plain-text anchor; use pictures and metaphors for identity and emotion.
- **"How" vs "what".** "How did you feel" gives a range (happy/sad); "what did you feel" makes them pick a specific emotion. She offers both without choosing. [Jun 4 0:57:44–0:58:46]
- **Learning time**: "months" [May 7 0:26:48] vs "a good year" [Jun 4 0:23:59].
