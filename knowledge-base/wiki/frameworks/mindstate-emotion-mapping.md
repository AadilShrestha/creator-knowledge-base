# Mindstate Emotion Mapping
> Sarah's procedure for choosing which emotions an ad sells. Have ChatGPT map your reviews to Will Leach's nine *Marketing to Mindstates* purchase emotions and take the top three (usually two industry-wide plus one brand-specific outlier). Write three nuanced ads per emotion, name each emotion as specifically as you can (specific = cheap), test one emotion per ad set, then **blend the winners** into one headline + subhead.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** before writing any batch of hooks or UGC scripts, to decide *which feeling* each ad sells; when tearing down a competitor's account to see which emotions it owns; when two separately winning ads should become one.
- **Inputs → outputs:** reviews (plus post-purchase surveys and quizzes) → top 3 emotions with evidence quotes → 3 nuanced sub-emotions each (9 ads) → one ad set per emotion → winners → a blended headline + subhead.
- **Rules:**
  - DO start from the nine *Marketing to Mindstates* emotions, but any emotion is testable "as long as you're very careful with how you're saying it so that you know which one is actually testing". [Jun 4 0:16:41–0:17:41]
  - DO expect ~3 emotions per industry (4 for very generic products like a phone case): two consistent across the industry, one outlier specific to the brand. [Jun 4 0:13:31–0:14:34]
  - DO write **3 nuanced ads per emotion**: esteem → confidence / control / appeal. And go further: "boss babe" and "cheeky" are two types of confidence. [Jun 4 0:36:36–0:37:39]
  - DO name the **specific** emotion: "It's not fear… it's disappointment. It's not anxiety… it's depression." "The only reason ads are expensive is because we're using a generalized term." [Jun 4 0:38:43]
  - DO start split (one ad set per emotion), then blend. Blending all emotions at once is "a difficult way to do this… you have to know where do I put each emotion". [Jun 4 0:14:34–0:15:38]
  - IF two emotion ad sets both crush THEN "squish them into one ad": take each winner's headline and subhead and merge them, then run a new blended-emotion campaign. [Jun 4 0:46:18–0:48:21]
  - DO hand the blend to ChatGPT: "don't try and do all the stuff yourself… this will hurt your brain." [Jun 4 0:50:27]
  - DON'T put two *untested*, unrelated emotional headlines in one ad. One core emotion per ad; supporting elements only back it up. [May 28 0:41:46–0:42:48]
  - DON'T read reviews word for word. Ask ChatGPT to "read between the lines" for context. [Jun 11 0:27:40–0:28:44]
  - REMEMBER review NLP tells you "what they were thinking at that time and what they were feeling. It doesn't necessarily tell you behaviorally why they purchased." Pair it with behaviour research ([CIM surveys](core-identity-map-surveys.md)). [Jun 4 0:50:27–0:51:32]

## Definition
- **Source model:** *Marketing to Mindstates* by **Will Leach**, a behavioural scientist "doing it for about 25 years", who "found nine different emotions that people usually go through while they're purchasing". Sarah calls it "one of the best books I've ever read" and her #1 book pick. [Jun 4 0:13:31; Jul 9 0:13:23]
- **Origin of the method:** Sarah's first NLP panel ("like 2020… no such thing as Chat") categorised reviews by hand into Leach's emotions. Now ChatGPT does the mapping. [Jun 4 0:13:31; 0:36:36]
- **The nine emotions:** Sarah names *security, belonging, achievement, esteem* in the calls. *(Outside context, not verified in the course: Leach's list is usually given as achievement, autonomy, belonging, competence, empowerment, engagement, esteem, nurturance, security. Darian's concept tag "Competence" [Jul 23 0:36:00] fits that list.)*
- **Core emotion = mindstate:** asked whether her brand-level "core emotion" equals mindstates, Sarah said "Yes… you can use the mindstates to get to these." [May 7 0:43:43]

## Why it matters
- **Specific emotions are cheap to buy.** "Everybody experiences nervousness, but only a few people experience depression, and that customer type is very cheap to get in the door." [Jun 4 0:38:43]
- **Blended winners last.** The Maelove blended static ran **8 months straight**. [Jun 4 0:47:21]
- **Emotional range protects the account.** One emotion and one zone everywhere leads to [emotional stagnation](../concepts/emotional-stagnation.md). [Jun 11 0:08:23]

## Procedure
1. **Collect** your reviews (plus surveys and quizzes).
2. **Map:** give ChatGPT the reviews and the nine emotions. Ask for the top three, with quotes. Sarah: "here's the nine emotions I want you to look for… it would spit out the top three." [Jun 4 0:36:36]
3. **Read between the lines:** ask for the context behind each statement, not just the literal words. [Jun 11 0:27:40]
4. **Find the outlier:** of the three, one is usually specific to your brand (e.g. Maelove's "40 bottles on the counter" → achievement). That outlier is your differentiator. [Jun 4 0:14:34; 0:48:21]
5. **Drill down with valence-intensity:** "is it actually esteem that they want, or do they want to be more appealing? Is it belonging, or do they just want to feel noticed?" [Jun 4 0:37:39–0:38:43] ([valence-intensity diagnostic](valence-intensity-diagnostic.md))
6. **Write 3 nuanced ads per emotion** (≈9 ads): e.g. esteem → confidence, control, appeal. Name each one precisely. [Jun 4 0:37:39]
7. **Structure:** one campaign, one ad set per emotion, ~9 ads each. Combine with TEEP if testing stages ([TEEP journey](teep-journey.md)). Alternatively campaign = emotion, ad sets = zones, if you're testing zones. [Jun 4 0:46:18; Jun 11 0:21:13]
8. **Read:** which emotion ad sets win.
9. **Blend:** if two emotions both win, merge each winner's headline and subhead into one ad. Use the blend prompt below. Run a new blended-emotion campaign. [Jun 4 0:46:18–0:50:27]
10. **Keep the brand core:** the winning emotion(s) feed the brand's core message, which you then "never stop saying". [May 7 0:48:09]

## Decision table
| Situation | Do | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Top-3 output is generic ("confidence", "fear") | Push to a sub-emotion (cheeky confidence, disappointment) before writing | Jun 4 0:37:39–0:38:43 |
| Very generic product (phone case) | Expect 4 emotions | Jun 4 0:13:31 |
| One emotion ad set crushes, others flat | Scale it. Write more nuances of it. Keep testing the others in other zones | Jun 4 0:46:18; Jun 11 0:22:19 |
| Two emotion ad sets crush | Blend: headline from one, subhead from the other | Jun 4 0:46:18–0:48:21 |
| You have two buyer types with different feelings | Name both emotions; test the stronger first (Gavin: "try nostalgia first"), then blend via ChatGPT | Jun 4 0:49:22–0:50:27 |
| Ad carries two untested emotional headlines | Cut to one headline, one CTA | May 28 0:41:46 |
| Reviews say what but not why | Add behaviour research (CIM / pictorial surveys) | Jun 4 0:50:27–0:51:32 |

## Prompts
**Mapping prompt (derived; reconstructed from Sarah's description at [Jun 4 0:36:36], not verbatim):**
```text
Here are <N> customer reviews for <product>. Here are the nine purchase emotions from Will Leach's
Marketing to Mindstates: <list>. Map each review to the emotion(s) it expresses. Read between the
lines: give the context behind what they say, not just the literal words. Return the top 3 emotions
with share of reviews and 3 verbatim quotes each, and flag any outlier emotion that seems unique to us.
```

**Blend prompt (near-verbatim from [Jun 4 0:50:27]):** "Tell chat: I have two emotions that my customers feel: nostalgia, and they also want freedom. They feel nostalgia currently but they want freedom. And have it generate a headline for you." General form: "Chat, blend me, make me a headline" from the emotions you discovered.

## Common mistakes
- Writing to umbrella emotions ("confidence", "fear") that Meta can't target cheaply. [Jun 4 0:38:43]
- Blending from day one, before you know which emotion is doing the work. [Jun 4 0:14:34–0:15:38]
- Treating review NLP as the whole truth about *why* people buy. [Jun 4 0:50:27]
- Building new-customer ads from reviews alone. That "rinses and repeats" the customers you already have ([customer research stack](customer-research-stack.md)). [Jun 4 0:23:59–0:25:00]
- Every ad in the same emotion and zone ([emotional stagnation](../concepts/emotional-stagnation.md)). [Jun 11 0:08:23]

## Contradictions and open points
- **One emotion vs blended:** Roast #1 (May 28) says one core emotion per ad. June 4 says "more blended emotion actually does really, really well". Reconciled: blend only emotions that have each **won on their own**, merged into one coherent headline + subhead. FireClub's ad stacked two unproven, unrelated emotional headlines.
- **One core emotion per brand (May 7) vs ~3 emotions per industry/brand (June 4):** the brand's core message is the long-term line ("never stop saying it"). The three mindstate emotions are what you test in ad sets. The core message can be a blend of winners (Maelove).
- **Date of Sarah's first NLP panel:** "about two years ago" and "like 2020" in the same breath [Jun 4 0:13:31].
- The exact list of nine emotions is never read out in the course.
