# Customer Research Stack (Sarah's 4 Layers + Review Mining)
> Before writing a single hook, run four research layers: **market** (is the category growing, how educated is the buyer), **consumer** (NLP on reviews for *outliers* and hidden motivators), **behavior/emotion** ([Core Identity Map](core-identity-map-surveys.md) picture surveys), and **social trends** (TikTok Creator Search Insights). Mine reviews for language and hooks, but **never build new-customer ads from reviews alone**: that only re-recruits the buyers you already have.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** before a brief or script batch for a brand/product (new or stalled); before tearing down competitors (to know what "opposite" means); when an account keeps fatiguing and you suspect the ads talk to the same middle-of-funnel buyer; when you need hook candidates in the customer's own words.
- **Inputs → outputs:** brand + product + top-level category keyword + reviews (≥50, ideally ~80 paragraph-length) + any surveys/quiz data/comments + (optional) competitor ad library → a **research brief**: category health, buyer education level (→ TEEP stage), customer types validated on Reddit/TikTok, 3 core emotions with nuances, hidden motivators with quotes, outlier behaviors, under-served search terms, and a hook candidate list.
- **Rules:**
  - DO run the layers in order: market → consumer (reviews) → behavior/emotion (CIM) → social trends. [Jun 4 0:25:00–0:28:12]
  - DO re-run market research **monthly** and the other layers about **weekly**; **update, don't accumulate**. Drop stale data (Black Friday behavior is useless in May). [Jun 4 0:28:12; May 7 0:10:15–0:10:51]
  - DON'T build acquisition ads from reviews + post-purchase data alone: "you are going to be pulling in the exact same person who already purchased from you". Use reviews to find **outliers** and things you haven't run yet. [Jun 4 0:23:59–0:27:07]
  - DO ask ChatGPT for the **hidden motivators** in the reviews and tell it to **read between the lines**: it reads literally by default. [Roast #1 0:46:04–0:47:07; Jun 11 0:27:40–0:28:44]
  - DO use ~**80 paragraph-length reviews** (50 good ones can work); quality over quantity. [Roast #1 0:47:07–0:48:07]
  - DO lift headlines and quotes straight from reviews and comments, in the customer's words ("pots and pans", not "cookware"). [Heuristics 101 L3 01:27; Joanna 0:07:12–0:08:17]
  - DON'T use ChatGPT as the *source* of customer types or ICPs. At most let it brainstorm a candidate list, then **verify every type on Reddit (thread count), then TikTok**. See the conflict note below. [May 7 0:30:31–0:31:34; May 14 0:29:57–0:30:53]
  - DO name the **specific** emotion (disappointment, not fear). Generic emotions make expensive ads. [Jun 4 0:38:43–0:39:49]
  - IF you are the target customer yourself THEN check your assumptions against reviews first (founder bias). [Jun 11 0:28:44–0:29:47]
  - DON'T copy competitors' messaging. Tag their ads by emotion, intensity, self and core message, then go the opposite way. [Jun 11 0:40:14–0:44:31]

## Definition
Sarah's research routine, described in full on June 4 when a member asked how to "do more core identity work and emotional research": "I'll go through market research first then… consumer research second then… behavior and emotional research third and then the last one… large scale social media trends." [Jun 4 0:25:00] Its purpose is to find **people who haven't found you yet**: "I don't need Chat to tell me who my best customers are… I need customers who haven't found us yet, who don't know we exist." [Jun 4 0:27:07]

## Why it matters
- Most accounts recycle one buyer. Reviews, post-purchase surveys and quizzes all describe people who already bought, so ads built only from them "rinse and repeat basically the middle of this funnel". [Jun 4 0:23:59–0:25:00] Sarah's matching diagnosis on May 7: "If you're struggling to get top of funnel customers in, it's predominantly because you're making ads to a middle of the funnel customer." [May 7 0:20:05–0:20:58]
- Market health tells you how hard the job is before you blame the creative. Several brands told her "this has been the hardest year"; a flat Google Trends line for the category explains it. [Jun 4 0:26:05]
- Hidden motivators produce the message. FireClub's buyers might be mourning a childhood they never had ("I wasn't allowed to as a kid") or just wanting to "be free for a minute". Each needs a different message, and the reviews tell you which. [Roast #1 0:44:59–0:46:04]
- Specific emotions are cheaper to buy: "The only reason ads are expensive is because we're using a generalized term, so Facebook can't find anybody." [Jun 4 0:38:43–0:39:49]

## The 4 layers
| # | Layer | Cadence | Tools | Question it answers | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Market research** | Monthly | [Google Trends](../tools/README.md) (keyword over 5 years), Google Keyword Planner (volume / "keyword health"), Pinterest Trends ("what are people pinning"), Reddit, [AnswerThePublic](../tools/answerthepublic.md) | "How hard is my job going to be?" | 2 | **Consumer research** | ~Weekly | [ChatGPT](../tools/chatgpt.md) NLP on all reviews and current-customer data | "Is there any outlier data in here… anything we haven't run before?" | Hidden motivators, top 3 emotions (+ nuances), outliers, customer phrases |
| 3 | **Behavior / emotional research** | ~Weekly (Sarah); see note | [Core Identity Map](core-identity-map-surveys.md): picture-based "panels" and metaphor questions | Why they *behaviorally* buy, which reviews can't tell you | Identities, behaviors (storage, usage), emotional motives |
| 4 | **Social trends** | ~Weekly | [TikTok Creator Search Insights](../tools/tiktok-creator-search-insights.md) (phone app) | Which searched terms "don't have enough content to serve" them? | Under-served search terms → video topics |

## Procedure
1. **Category health (layer 1).** Type the top-level keyword into Google Trends, 5-year view. Rising → normal job. Flat or dying → expect a harder year; tell the client before blaming creative. Check volume in Keyword Planner. [Jun 4 0:25:00–0:26:05]
2. **Education level (layer 1).** Paste the keyword into AnswerThePublic and read *all* the questions. Classify with the decision table below. This sets how much education the ads must carry and which [TEEP stage](teep-journey.md) to lead with. [Jun 4 0:52:34–0:54:36]
3. **Raw emotion (layer 1).** Browse Pinterest Trends and Reddit for the category. Reddit often carries far more emotion than surveys (r/FemaleHairLoss is "like an 11, a 10" on valence while post-purchase surveys are "very simple, nice, easy"). [Jun 4 0:28:12] Attendee tip: [Gigabrain](../tools/README.md) (thegigabrain.com) for Reddit research (not discussed by Sarah). [May 7 ≈0:32:15]
4. **Review NLP (layer 2).** Collect ~80 paragraph-length reviews (50 minimum). Run the prompts in [Review mining](#review-mining-for-hidden-motivators) below: hidden motivators, read-between-the-lines context, the nine mindstate emotions → top 3 → three nuances each. Flag **outliers** ("40 bottles on the counter") above majority patterns. [Roast #1 0:46:04–0:48:07; Jun 4 0:27:07; 0:36:36–0:38:43]
5. **Customer types (only if no customer access).** Ask ChatGPT for a list of everyone who could use the product, then keep only types with real Reddit threads; double-check on TikTok; test the ones people actually talk about. [May 7 0:30:31–0:32:06] Look for *small viable subgroups*, not just the obvious identity (70–80-year-olds taking protein to keep muscle mass vs bodybuilders). [May 7 0:30:00–0:30:31]
6. **Behavior and identity (layer 3).** Run a [Core Identity Map survey](core-identity-map-surveys.md): picture/metaphor questions about who they are and what happens to the product at home. [Jun 4 0:07:10–0:08:11; May 7 0:17:51–0:20:05]
7. **Social trends (layer 4).** In TikTok Creator Search Insights, list the most-searched terms with too little content. Use them as video topics and hook seeds. [Jun 4 0:27:07–0:28:12]
8. **Competitor pass (optional, Jun 11).** Paste 10–20 competitor ads into ChatGPT, tag each by emotion, intensity, zone, self and core message. If one zone dominates, write in the zones they never use, and go opposite on message, color, size and tone. Sarah's on-screen prompt: **"Analyze this ad for valence + intensity. Which zone is this ad in?"** [Jun 11 0:03:10–0:08:23; 0:40:14–0:44:31] *(Sarah does not list this as one of the four layers; it is a separate habit.)*
9. **Synthesize** into the research brief (template below). Pick **one** type of person + **one** emotion per first test batch (~10 statics). [Jun 18 0:59:15–1:01:19]
10. **Prune.** Next month, replace stale findings rather than appending. [May 7 0:10:15–0:10:51]

| Finding | Do this | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Category keyword flat or declining | Set expectations; widen top of funnel; don't over-diagnose creative | [Jun 4 0:26:05] |

## Review mining for hidden motivators
The consumer layer, taught in Roast #1, the Joanna Wallace guest session, June 4 and June 11.

### Steps
1. **Gather** ~80 reviews that are at least a paragraph each. "Usually after about 80, as long as there is quality, I just keep seeing the same… results… over and over." 50 good ones can work; 15 is too few (implied by the question). [Roast #1 0:47:07–0:48:07]
2. **Add** other first-party text if you have it: post-purchase surveys, quizzes, emails, ad comments, call notes. [Jun 11 0:27:40; May 21 0:01:42; Joanna 0:07:12]
3. **Run the prompts** (below) in ChatGPT.
4. **Listen for keywords** the way Sarah does on customer calls: "I've never done this before… I wasn't allowed to as a kid" = deprivation → "you were deprived of this experience as a child, here's the experience for you". "I just got to be free for a minute" → "be free". [Roast #1 0:46:04]
5. **Sort** findings into: majority patterns (current buyer, retention and conversion ads), **outliers** (new angles), customer language (headlines), jokes customers already make (humor ads). [Jun 4 0:27:07; Joanna 0:07:12–0:09:21]
6. **Drill each emotion down** with [valence and intensity](valence-intensity-diagnostic.md): "is it actually esteem that they want, or do they want to be more appealing? Is it belonging, or do they just want to feel noticed?" [Jun 4 0:37:39–0:38:43]
7. **Lift hooks.** Pull lived scenes and exact phrases as headline candidates (see examples). Label real quotes as real ("real user quote"). [Heuristics 101 L3 01:27–01:36; Joanna 0:07:12–0:08:17]
8. **Hand off** outliers to the acquisition batch; hand majority patterns to retargeting/retention. Don't let the acquisition batch be majority-pattern only. [Jun 4 0:23:59–0:25:00] *(The routing split is (derived) from Sarah's warning.)*

Agent-ready combined prompt *(derived; assembled from the prompts above, not spoken by Sarah)*:
```text
Below are <N> customer reviews for <product>. Read between the lines: look for the context
behind what people say, not only the literal words.
1. What are the hidden motivators inside all of these reviews? Quote 2–3 reviews as evidence for each.
2. Map the reviews to these nine emotions: achievement, autonomy, belonging, competence,
   empowerment, engagement, esteem, nurturance, security. Return the top three, then three
   more specific sub-emotions for each.
3. List outliers: behaviors, situations or phrases that appear in only a few reviews but are
   vivid or unexpected.
4. List exact phrases customers use for the product and the problem.
Do not invent quotes or statistics.
```
*(The list of nine emotions is outside context for Leach's mindstates; Sarah named only security, belonging, achievement and esteem on the call. Verify against [Marketing to Mindstates](../people/will-leach.md).)*

## Common mistakes
- Dumping reviews + post-purchase data + quiz into ChatGPT, asking "what should we run", and briefing acquisition ads from the answer. [Jun 4 0:23:59]
- Letting ChatGPT invent the ICP: "chat is training you… it's going to take your internal bias… and spit out something that seems like it's pretty close to your customer." [May 14 0:29:57–0:30:53]
- Demographic avatars ("She's Sarah, she's 29, she lives in Chicago with two cats"): "doesn't tell me anything about why she would buy from me." [May 7 0:16:25–0:17:05]
- Keeping every research file forever; seasonal data contaminates the current picture. [May 7 0:10:15–0:10:51]
- Using 15 short reviews and trusting the output. [Roast #1 0:47:07]
- Over-testing instead of listening: marketers over-test "because they don't know and they don't want to take the time to know". [Roast #1 0:45:30]
- Copying a competitor's message because it runs long. "We have no idea how much they're spending or how much they're making." [Jun 11 0:41:18]
- Fabricating proof from research gaps (AI copy that invented "Featured in Outside and Men's Health"). Never invent quotes or press. See [M4 L7](../courses/tether-lab/04-self-targeting-system/07-how-to-rewrite-winning-ads-for-new-selves.md) (wiki context note, not said in the course).

## Conflicts and open questions
- **ChatGPT for ICPs.** May 7: use ChatGPT to list possible customer types, then verify on Reddit and TikTok [0:30:31–0:31:34], and paste real lifestyle findings in to get an ICP [0:20:58–0:21:30]. May 14: "Avoid chat as a generator for ICPs" [0:29:57–0:30:53]. **Recommended default:** ChatGPT may brainstorm candidates or summarize *real* customer data; it must never be the source. Every type needs outside evidence (Reddit threads, TikTok content, survey answers).
- **Reviews as a message source.** Roast #1, Joanna and June 11 build messages and hooks directly from reviews, while June 4 warns against building new-customer ads from reviews alone [0:23:59]. Not a real conflict: use reviews for language, outliers and retention; pair them with market, CIM and social layers for acquisition.
- **Learning curve.** Picture surveys took Sarah "months" to learn (May 7 [0:26:48]) vs "a good year" (June 4 [0:23:59]).
- **Dates.** June 4: her first NLP panel was "two years ago" but also "like 2020" [0:13:31] (internal mismatch, kept as spoken).
