# Heuristics 101 e-book

> An 18-page PDF by [Sarah Levinger](../people/sarah-levinger.md) (Tether Consumer Insights). It's the cheat sheet for [Module 2: Heuristics 101](../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/README.md): nine psychological shortcuts, each with a definition, "use it by" list, pro tip and mock static ad, plus how to combine them and mistakes to avoid.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you need the compact definition, pro tip or example line for a heuristic, or a pre-launch checklist (p.17).
- **Inputs → outputs:** a heuristic name → its one-liner, tactics and pro tip. A draft ad → a pass or fail against "Mistakes to avoid."
- **Rules:** treat each "Pro Tip" as a DO rule, and run every draft through the four mistakes on p.17.
- **See also:** [Heuristics hub](../concepts/heuristics.md), [Heuristic stacking](../concepts/heuristic-stacking.md), [Playbook page](../courses/tether-lab/02-heuristics-101/07-bonus-heuristics-101-playbook.md).

Note: the PDF's printed page numbers run one lower than the PDF page index (the cover is unnumbered). Below, **p.N = PDF page N** (matching the PNG file `pNN.png`), with the printed number in brackets.

## Page-by-page summary

### p.1 — Cover

*"HEURISTICS 101 by Sarah Levinger," with nine icons for the nine shortcuts: hourglass, people, award, heart, check, clock, warning, target, frame.*

### p.2 [1] — "Welcome to the only marketing shortcut that matters."
- Addressed to "the brand owner, the ad tinkerer, the 'I'm-just-trying-to-sell-stuff' human."
- "If you've been struggling to get people to actually care about your ads, it's not because your product sucks. It's because **you've been talking to the wrong brain.**"
- "Nine psychological shortcuts — proven moves your customer's brain is already wired to respond to. These aren't tactics. They're human instincts."

### p.3 [2] — How your customer's brain actually works
![Brain page](../../raw/assets/tether-lab/pdf-pages/7-Heuristics-101-e-book/p03.png)
*Two systems with an iceberg image: Conscious Mind (tip), Subconscious Mind (below water).*
- "Your customer isn't sitting there rationally weighing options like a mini CFO. They're shortcutting — fast, automatic, emotional decisions based on ancient brain wiring."
- **Subconscious:** fast, emotional, reactive, **11 million bits/second**. **Conscious:** slow, logical, deliberate, "struggles through **40 bits/second**." See [Subconscious vs conscious mind](../concepts/subconscious-vs-conscious-mind.md).

### p.4 [3] — The pizza
- "Guess which one makes most buying decisions? (Yep. Subconscious, every time.)" The 3PM pizza craving: the subconscious noticed you skipped breakfast, saw a pizza ad, and "made the decision before your conscious mind even logged on."
- "**Good ads talk to the boss. The subconscious.**"

### p.5 [4] — Why we use heuristics (mental shortcuts)
![Why heuristics](../../raw/assets/tether-lab/pdf-pages/7-Heuristics-101-e-book/p05.png)
*The brain's one job and the three questions every heuristic answers.*
- "The brain has one job: keep you alive." Debating every ad "would take too long. Burn too much energy."
- Heuristics answer: **Does this look safe? Is this something people like me do? Will this solve my immediate need?**
- "You don't need to 'convince' people. You just need to press the right button."

### p.6 [5] — Section divider: "The 9 psychological shortcuts that sell for you"

### pp.7–15 [6–14] — The nine shortcuts

| PDF p. | # | Heuristic | One-liner | Why | Use it by | Pro tip | Mock ad |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 1 | [Scarcity](../concepts/heuristics/scarcity-heuristic.md) | If it's about to be gone, we want it more. | Survival instinct: act fast or miss out | Limited stock; time-sensitive offers ("Only 24 hours left!"); purchase limits ("Max 2 per customer") | Quantity limits often outperform time limits. People trust a number they can see. | "TikTok's Most Viral Moisturizer" + "Only 8 units left!" |
| 8 | 2 | [Social proof](../concepts/heuristics/social-proof.md) | If others like it, it must be good. | Pack animals: following the crowd meant survival | Customer counts ("10,000+ customers served"); testimonials, reviews, UGC; "Top Seller" badges | Don't just show 5 stars. Show the people behind them ("10,000 coffee lovers trust us.") | Sneaker: "Like walking on a cloud (trust me)" + "4,842+ Sneaker Heads Love Us" |
| 9 | 3 | [Authority bias](../concepts/heuristics/authority-bias.md) | We trust perceived experts. | Not PhDs necessarily, anyone who seems to know more | Expert recommendations; technical terms ("plant-based biopolymer" = big authority energy); guarantees ("Dermatologist-recommended," "Taste guarantee") | Choose your authority carefully. Not all audiences trust traditional experts. | "This Pill Works When Others Don't." + "Clinically Proven" |
| 10 | 4 | [Effect (affect) heuristic](../concepts/heuristics/affect-heuristic.md) | If it feels good, it must be good. | Emotional snap judgments | Paint the desired experience ("Put up the tent, brought the flask."); sell the feeling, not the feature | The faster you make them feel something, the faster they'll buy. | Water bottle: "Feels Like Home, Even Miles Away…" |
| 11 | 5 | [Processing fluency](../concepts/heuristics/processing-fluency.md) | If it's easy to understand, it feels more trustworthy. | Simple = safe. Complicated = risky. | Clean layouts; short, clear headlines; avoid clutter and over-explaining | If a 7-year-old can't understand your ad in 3 seconds, simplify it. | Sunglasses: "You Know the Look." + "1,872+ Rated 5 Stars Review" |
| 12 | 6 | [Temporal discounting](../concepts/heuristics/temporal-discounting.md) | The brain craves instant rewards. | Later feels uncertain. Now feels good. | Immediate results ("Feel better today."); fast-action language ("Start now," "First use.") | Remind them what's happening **right now**, not "someday." | Lounger: "If You've Been Waiting for Cozy, Sit Happier Now" |
| 13 | 7 | [Salience bias](../concepts/heuristics/salience-bias.md) | Whatever stands out gets the brain's attention. | Novelty and contrast force a pause | Bright, bold colours against muted backgrounds; unexpected visuals; clear focal points | In a sea of beige, be the red balloon. | Headphones: "When the BASS drops" + grimacing model + "Only 8 units left!" |
| 14 | 8 | [Goal gradient](../concepts/heuristics/goal-gradient-effect.md) | The closer we feel to the finish line, the faster we move. | Humans are junkies for progress | Progress bars ("You're 75% there!"); step-by-step visuals ("Pick size > Upload photo > Confirm order") | Always show how close they are to "done." | Treadmill: "You've Burned 312 Calories. Let's Make It 500." + "1,342+ Reached Their Ideal Weight" |
| 15 | 9 | [Framing effect](../concepts/heuristics/framing-effect.md) | How you present info changes how it's received. | Same facts, different frames = different decisions | Break big numbers into daily actions ("3 sprays/day = 1 year of fragrance"); cost per use, not just sticker price | Frame it in terms that make the customer feel smart for buying. | Gummies: "Tastes Like Candy. Works Like a Supplement." + "5,342+ Like This Gummies" |

All nine mock ads share one template: a cream background, purple headline, a single product cut-out, a round badge, and a purple CTA button ("Shop now," "Get yours now," "Get yours today"). The template is itself a [processing fluency](../concepts/heuristics/processing-fluency.md) demonstration.

### p.16 [15] — How to combine these shortcuts (and why you should)
![Combine page](../../raw/assets/tether-lab/pdf-pages/7-Heuristics-101-e-book/p16.png)
*Three Pela ads ("Your Phone Doesn't Need a Case…unless you drop things," "4X more material costs for us… less worry," "…and so do we").*
- "Your customer's brain isn't running one shortcut at a time. It's stacking them, fast. So should your ads."
- Example from Pela: **Processing fluency** = clear, simple design; **Effect heuristic** = "Less worry for you"; **Authority bias** = "Plant-based biopolymer."
- "The ad isn't shouting. It's whispering all the right things to the subconscious." "Stack shortcuts = faster, easier decisions." See [Heuristic stacking](../concepts/heuristic-stacking.md).

### p.17 [16] — Mistakes to avoid (verbatim checklist)
![Mistakes page](../../raw/assets/tether-lab/pdf-pages/7-Heuristics-101-e-book/p17.png)
*The pre-launch checklist.*

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Fix |
|---|---|
| Overcomplicating it. | Simpler wins. |
| Trying to manipulate. | Customers aren't dumb. Make it easier, not sneakier. |
| Using only one shortcut. | Layer at least two or three. |
| Talking only to the conscious mind. | No long rational debates. Emotional, visual, fast. |

### p.18 [17] — Final pep talk (verbatim)
> You're not here to convince. You're here to make it ridiculously easy for your customer's brain to say "yes." These nine shortcuts are the cheat codes. Use them. Test them. Layer them. Make smarter ads. Lower your costs. Grow your brand. Let's go make the subconscious your best closer.

## Differences between the e-book and the videos
- The e-book uses invented mock ads. The videos use real client ads (Pela, Crazy Compression, Maelove, LEGENDS, Ankhway, flask brands, fragrance brands, a T-shirt quilt).
- The e-book spells it "Effect Heuristic." The video slides say "Affect Heuristic."
- The e-book adds a few things the videos don't have: the "7-year-old / 3 seconds" test, "In a sea of beige, be the red balloon," the three "heuristic questions" (p.5), and the explicit mistakes list.
