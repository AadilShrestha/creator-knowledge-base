# Books: Sarah's Reading List
> Every book [Sarah Levinger](../people/sarah-levinger.md) recommends in Tether Lab: the 11 titles in her "Rare & Underrated Books on Behavioral Science and Psychology" PDF (the [Secret Book Stash](../courses/tether-lab/06-ad-lab-toolkit/05-sarahs-secret-book-stash.md) in the Ad Lab Toolkit), plus the books she named on live calls.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you need deeper theory behind a lever (emotion, motivation, choice architecture, habit, belief change) to justify an angle, or you're looking for research-backed mechanisms beyond the course. Agents can't read these books from the KB. Use this page to know **which source would back which kind of claim** and to cite it in a brief.
- **Inputs → outputs:** a topic (emotion vocabulary, choice architecture, subconscious buying, habits) → the book(s) to cite or research.
- **Rules:** DO cite these as background, not as proof for product claims. DON'T quote statistics "from" a book unless the source text is in hand. Sarah herself couldn't remember the Frito-Lay stat. DO note the *Predictably Irrational* data controversy Sarah raised before you lean on Ariely studies.
- **See also:** [Heuristics hub](../concepts/heuristics.md) · [Subconscious vs conscious mind](../concepts/subconscious-vs-conscious-mind.md) · [Valence](../concepts/valence.md) · [Ad Lab Toolkit](../courses/tether-lab/06-ad-lab-toolkit/README.md)

## Rare & Underrated Books (the PDF: 11 titles)
Toolkit blurb: "Unlock a deeper level of strategy with 11 rare and underrated books that reveal how consumers *really* think, feel, and buy. Perfect for marketers, creatives, and brand owners who want an edge most of their competitors will never even discover." In the July 9 live session Sarah calls it a "very hidden" list of less-known psychology books, some of which she rereads every year ([0:16:33]).

The "Sarah's description" column is verbatim from the PDF. The "Use for ad work" column is *wiki-derived*.

| # | Title | Author(s) (as listed) | Sarah's description | Use for ad work *(derived)* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Behavioral Science: Tales of Inspiration, Discovery, and Service* | Stephen F. Ledoux (Ed.) | "A collection of personal narratives from behavioral scientists, highlighting real-world applications of behavior principles." | Real-world behaviour-change cases. Story material for founder/expert content |
| 2 | *Secrets of the Moneylab* | Kay-Yut Chen & Marina Krakovsky | "Explores how behavioral economics experiments influence business decisions and uncover hidden patterns in consumer behavior." | Pricing, incentives and offer design (see [#9 Gamification + Time-Sensitive Offer](../courses/tether-lab/07-prompt-library/09-gamification-and-time-sensitive-offer.md)) |
| 3 | *Consumerology* | Philip Graves | "Challenges traditional market research and emphasizes how subconscious influences shape purchasing decisions." | Why surveys mislead. Backs the course's [subconscious-first](../concepts/subconscious-vs-conscious-mind.md) stance |
| 4 | *Consumer Behavior* (3rd Edition, 1978) | James F. Engel, Roger D. Blackwell, David T. Kollat | "A foundational but hard-to-find text in consumer behavior studies, offering deep historical insights." | Classic decision-process models (background) |
| 5 | *Consumer Behavior and Marketing Strategy* (6th Edition) | J. Paul Peter & Jerry C. Olson | "Integrates consumer psychology with actionable marketing strategies, suitable for practitioners and students alike." | Linking psychology to strategy and funnel stages |
| 6 | *Handbook of Research Methods in Consumer Psychology* | Frank R. Kardes, Paul M. Herr, & Norbert Schwarz (Eds.) | "Comprehensive methodologies for consumer research, aimed at academics and insight-driven marketers." | Designing cleaner creative tests and reading results |
| 7 | *The Psychology of Emotion* | David J. Lieberman | "Breaks down how emotions function and how individuals can manage emotional reactions more effectively." | Emotion mechanics behind [valence](../concepts/valence.md) / [intensity](../concepts/intensity.md) |
| 8 | *Understanding Motivation and Emotion* (7th Edition) | Johnmarshall Reeve | "Provides deep dives into motivational theory and emotion science, including classroom and workplace applications." | Motivation theory behind [the three selves](../concepts/three-selves.md) and goal-driven angles |
| 9 | *Strange Emotional Psychology: Do Your Own Emotions Bystander* | Fang Xiang Ping | "A unique Chinese work that explores emotional detachment and the subtle layers of emotional awareness." | Subtle, low-intensity emotional states (Zone 1/3 nuance) |
| 10 | *The Dictionary of Obscure Sorrows* | John Koenig | "Invents poetic words for unnamed human emotions, expanding emotional vocabulary and self-understanding." | Precise emotion words for hooks ([emotional trigger](../concepts/heuristics/emotional-trigger.md), [specificity](../concepts/heuristics/specificity.md)) |
| 11 | *Behavioral Economics for Leaders* | Matthias Sutter | "Applies behavioral economic principles to leadership, focusing on irrationality and decision-making in the workplace." | Irrational decision-making. B2B buyer psychology |

## Mentioned in live sessions

| Book | Author | What Sarah said | Session |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Marketing to Mindstates* | Will Leach | **Sarah's #1 pick.** "It… solidified for me how deep the emotional connection goes… and how sensitive we are to the weirdest things." Will is a friend who works with Pepsi, Coca-Cola and Frito-Lay. Story: sequenced fairy lights down a convenience store's chip aisle reportedly "tripled the amount of chips that they sold for Frito-Lay" to lunch-break construction workers (she couldn't remember the exact stat). Lesson: tiny cues steer behaviour ([salience](../concepts/heuristics/salience-bias.md)). | [July 9, 2025](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:13:23–0:15:04] |
| *Predictably Irrational* | Dan Ariely | Pick #2, "kind of controversial" because of a fabricated-data scandal ("Don't fabricate data"). The premise as she frames it: from your childhood, parents and school experience "we can pretty much tell you what kind of person you're gonna be." | [July 9, 2025](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:15:04–0:15:58] |
| *The Elements of Choice* | Eric J. Johnson | Pick #3, on **choice architecture**: "how you present options to people is going to drastically change what they choose and how much of it they consume", including the decoy effect and pre-selected options. | [July 9, 2025](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:15:58–0:16:33] |
| *The Biology of Belief* | not named in the session (*context, not from course: Bruce H. Lipton*) | A "fourth book" on "how beliefs get injected into humans and how deep they go". Mentioned alongside her point that marketing changes the subconscious by making people "second-guess" beliefs. | [July 9, 2025](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:55:24–0:56:27] |
| *Atomic Habits* | James Clear | "If you haven't read Atomic Habits, really really good one… talks a lot about… how the brain makes habits and how to change your habits… very applicable to marketers." She introduces it as influenced by BJ Fogg. | [July 16, 2025](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) [0:02:03–0:03:09] |
| *Tiny Habits* | BJ Fogg | The July 16 session teaches **Fogg's Behavior Model** (B = MAP). The book title itself isn't spoken in the transcript; it's listed here as the model's source text (*context, not from course*). | [July 16, 2025](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-16-live-with-sarah.md) |

Sarah also pointed members to the CRO agency **Conversion Wise** as the closest "daily" application of psychology (attendee tip, [July 9](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-07-09-live-with-sarah.md) [0:16:33–0:17:56]).
