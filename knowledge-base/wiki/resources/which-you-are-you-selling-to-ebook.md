# Which 'You' Are You Selling To? (e-book)
> Sarah Levinger's 11-page (10 numbered) playbook, the bonus for [Module 4](../courses/tether-lab/04-self-targeting-system/README.md): "How to write ads that speak to identity, not just intent." It covers Self-Discrepancy Theory, worksheets to spot and score selves, a rewrite formula, one-line prompts, a self-to-funnel guide and a "brand self signature" audit.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** you need the compact rules (cue lexicon, 0–10 scoring, the rewrite formula, the funnel mapping) while tearing down ads, mining reviews, or writing self variants.
- **Inputs → outputs:** ads or customer text (reviews, surveys, DMs) → self tags and scores. Target self + funnel stage → copy direction and a 4-part copy block.
- **Rules:**
  - DO mine reviews, survey responses and DMs for self language (p. 5).
  - DO score each self 0–10 (0 = not present, 10 = dominant focus) and note tone, language, emotional weight (p. 6).
  - DO write variants as Headline (4–7 words) + Subhead (1–2 lines) + Social Proof (if needed) + Bullets or Badge (p. 7).
  - DO map self to funnel: Awareness = Ideal/Ought · Consideration = Actual/Ideal · Conversion = Actual/Ought (p. 9).
  - DO audit 10–20 recent ads to find the brand's default "self signature" (p. 10).
- **See also:** [Three selves diagnostic](../frameworks/three-selves-diagnostic.md) · [Rewriting ads for new selves](../frameworks/rewriting-ads-for-new-selves.md) · [Three selves prompts](three-selves-prompts.md)

## Page 2: Introduction: Stop selling benefits. Start selling selves.
- "You know the drill: 'Sell the benefit, not the feature.' But in today's marketing landscape, that's not enough. **People aren't buying benefits. They're buying a version of themselves.** And there isn't just one version."
- They have: **The Actual Self** (who they believe they are) · **The Ideal Self** (who they want to become) · **The Ought Self** (who they think they should be).
- "Most ads only speak to one. The best ads? They know which one they're targeting — and they do it with surgical precision."
- The guide teaches that precision using **Self-Discrepancy Theory (SDT)**.

## Page 3: Chapter 1: The psychology behind Self-Discrepancy Theory
- "Self-Discrepancy Theory, introduced by psychologist **E. Tory Higgins**, reveals that our emotions and behaviors are deeply influenced by the gap between different versions of ourselves."

| Self | Tagline | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actual | *Who I am right now.* | Seeks validation, consistency, and identity alignment. Ads that reinforce this self feel comforting and familiar. | "Finally, cookware that gets *you*." |
| Ideal | *Who I want to be.* | Chases growth, pride, and emotional reward. Ads here feel aspirational and exciting. | "Cook like the version of you who doesn't cut corners." |

![E-book page: Chapter 1, Actual Self and Ideal Self definition boxes](../../raw/assets/tether-lab/pdf-pages/10-eBook-Which-You-Are-You-Selling-To-2/p03.png)
*Chapter 1: the Actual and Ideal definitions with cookware examples.*

## Page 4: Chapter 1 (cont.): Ought Self
| Self | Tagline | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ought | *Who I think I should be.* | Driven by duty, pressure, or social expectation. These ads act like responsible reminders. | "You know you should be taking better care of yourself." |

- "Each self creates a different kind of emotional pull. **When your ad doesn't match the customer's dominant self, it falls flat. When it does? It converts.**"

## Page 5: Chapter 2: How to spot each self in the wild
"Each self-type shows up differently in the way people talk, think, and behave." **Self-Spotting Worksheet:**

| Self-Type | Language | Emotion | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actual | "This is so me." "Exactly what I needed." | Comfort, pride | Seeking validation or alignment |
| Ideal | "One day I'll…" "Finally becoming…" | Hope, inspiration | Comparing themselves to who they want to be |
| Ought | "I should really…" "It's about time I…" | Guilt, pressure | Social norms or inner expectations |

**Pro tip:** "Use reviews, survey responses, and DMs to gather these clues."

## Page 6: Chapter 3: Score your ads (and reveal your default self)
**Ad Self-Score Worksheet**
1. Read your ad.
2. Score each self from 0 to 10: **0** = Not present · **10** = Dominant focus.
3. Add notes on tone, language, and emotional weight.

**Example:** *"No more skipping workouts. Your future self will thank you."*
- Actual: **1** (doesn't affirm who they are)
- Ideal: **7** (motivational future pacing)
- Ought: **9** (guilt-driven pressure)

Tracking table columns: `Ad Description | Actual Score | Ideal Score | Ought Score | Notes`

**What to look for:** Are you relying too heavily on one self? · Are your top-performing ads balanced? · Are low-converting ads emotionally mismatched?

![E-book page: Chapter 3 Ad Self-Score Worksheet with worked example and blank tracking table](../../raw/assets/tether-lab/pdf-pages/10-eBook-Which-You-Are-You-Selling-To-2/p06.png)
*Ad Self-Score Worksheet. Note the worked example: "future self" copy scores highest on Ought, not Ideal.*

## Page 7: Chapter 4: How to rewrite ads for each self
"Let's say you wrote this **Ideal Self** ad: *'The skincare routine your future self deserves.'* Here's how to shift it for the other selves:"

| Rewrite | Headline | Subhead |
|---|---|---|
| Actual Self | Skin that feels like you again. | A routine that fits your life, not the other way around |
| Ought Self | You know you should be doing this. | Dermatologist-approved, zero guilt, full coverage. |

**Use this formula:**
- **Headline (4–7 words):** Direct, emotional, specific.
- **Subhead (1–2 lines):** Clarifies the motivation.
- **Social Proof (if needed):** Stars, reviews, certifications.
- **Bullets or Badge:** Key value pops or icon-based credibility.

## Page 8: Chapter 5: Ad prompts by self-type
"Use these plug-and-play prompts to create emotionally aligned ads:"
- **ACTUAL SELF PROMPT:** "Write an ad that validates who the customer already is. Emphasize identity, comfort, and ease."
- **IDEAL SELF PROMPT:** "Write an ad that speaks to who the customer wants to become. Emphasize transformation, possibility, and pride."
- **OUGHT SELF PROMPT:** "Write an ad that reminds the customer of what they should be doing. Emphasize responsibility, social proof, and consequences."

(Also reproduced on [Three selves prompts](three-selves-prompts.md#e-book-one-line-prompts-chapter-5).)

## Page 9: Chapter 6: Match self-type to funnel stage
**Self-to-Funnel Match Guide**
| Funnel stage | Use | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Ideal or Ought | Tap into emotion or urgency |
| Consideration | Actual or Ideal | Build trust or show transformation |
| Conversion | Actual or Ought | Anchor in comfort or push with guilt |

"Think of it like this": **Ideal**: pull them in with possibility. **Actual**: calm their nerves with familiarity. **Ought**: nudge them off the fence with pressure or logic.

![E-book page: Chapter 6 Self-to-Funnel Match Guide with a funnel graphic](../../raw/assets/tether-lab/pdf-pages/10-eBook-Which-You-Are-You-Selling-To-2/p09.png)
*Self-to-funnel mapping, an e-book-only addition useful for placing self variants in prospecting vs retargeting.*

## Page 10: Chapter 7: Discover your brand's self signature
- "Every brand leans into one self-type more than others. It happens subconsciously. But when you spot it, you can balance it."
- Use the Ad Self-Score Worksheet across **10–20 recent ads**. Look for your **highest average score**.
- Then ask: Are we defaulting to Ideal and ignoring Actual? · Are we overusing guilt or pressure? · Do our top performers align with our target self-type?
- "Use the data to calibrate your creative direction."

## Page 11: Closing: This isn't just copy. It's consumer therapy.
- "You're not just writing copy. You're helping people resolve emotional tension. You're offering alignment between how they feel and who they want to be."
- "When your messaging mirrors the right self, your customer doesn't just buy. They say, 'That's so me.' Or 'That's who I want to be.' That's **identity resonance**. And that's what makes brands unforgettable."
