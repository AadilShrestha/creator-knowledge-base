## Chapter 2: Understanding Valence & Intensity
*(pp4–5; video: [lesson 2](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/02-understanding-valence-and-intensity.md))*

![Chapter 2 page](../../raw/assets/tether-lab/pdf-pages/8-Diagnose-Why-Your-Ads-Arent-Working/p04.png)
*Chapter 2 with an unlabeled 2×2 grid. ⚠️ **Here intensity is the vertical axis (low → high) and valence the horizontal (positive → negative).** That's the opposite orientation from the video map, where valence is vertical with positive at the top.*

- "This entire framework hinges on two levers:"
  - **Valence** = "What emotion are we trying to evoke? (Good vs. bad)"
  - **Intensity** = "How strongly are we hitting it? (Whisper vs. scream)"
- **Valence examples:** Positive = joy, pride, comfort, hope. Negative = fear, FOMO, frustration, anxiety.
- **Intensity examples (p5):** Low = soft, reflective, reassuring. High = urgent, bold, aggressive.
- "You map every ad into one of **four emotional zones** based on these axes."

## Chapter 3: The 4 Emotional Zones (a.k.a. Your Target Map)
*(pp6–7; video: [lesson 3](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/03-the-4-emotional-zones.md))*

![Chapter 3 page with mislabeled quadrant graphic](../../raw/assets/tether-lab/pdf-pages/8-Diagnose-Why-Your-Ads-Arent-Working/p06.png)
*⚠️ **Source problem:** the Chapter 3 graphic labels its quadrants (intensity vertical, valence positive → negative horizontal) as *Frustration* (positive/high), *Inspiration* (negative/high), *Indifference* (positive/low), *Satisfaction* (negative/low). That contradicts the text on the same page (e.g., frustration is negative valence). It looks like a generic stock graphic. **Ignore its labels.** Use the [video map](../concepts/emotional-zones.md).*

| Zone | E-book text (verbatim key phrases) | Good for | "Think:" (visual/copy cues) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zone 1: High Valence + Low Intensity** | "*The feel-good zone.* Calm, cozy, supportive." | Wellness · Belonging · Safety | "morning light, soft pastels, gentle copy." |
| **Zone 2: High Valence + High Intensity** | "*The hype zone.* Pride, thrill, anticipation." | Product launches · Performance gear · Celebratory moments | "bold type, energy visuals, verbs that punch." |
| **Zone 3: Low Valence + Low Intensity** | "The pain-point whisper. Frustration, fatigue, unmet expectations." | "Good for soft problem awareness" · "Best in industries with thoughtful buyers" | "sighs, subtle sarcasm, the tension before the solution." |
| **Zone 4: Low Valence + High Intensity** | "*The danger zone.* Fear, scarcity, urgency." | "Great for high-stakes industries (safety, financial risk)" · "Use with care. This one can backfire." | "harsh lighting, high contrast, jarring visuals." |

![Zones 2–4 page](../../raw/assets/tether-lab/pdf-pages/8-Diagnose-Why-Your-Ads-Arent-Working/p07.png)
*p7: Zones 2–4 with their "Think:" cues.*

> Difference from video: the video says Zone 3 should be avoided in **saturated industries**. The e-book says it's best with **thoughtful buyers**. These are compatible, but only the video gives the saturation warning.

## Chapter 4: Diagnosing Emotional Misfires
*(p8; video: [lesson 4](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/04-diagnosing-emotional-misfires.md))*

![Chapter 4 Diagnostic Checklist Table](../../raw/assets/tether-lab/pdf-pages/8-Diagnose-Why-Your-Ads-Arent-Working/p08.png)
*"Diagnostic Checklist Table" graphic. It shows **five** truncated rows ("1. Is the emotion…? 2. Is it high energy or…? 3. What is the…? 4. What do I want…? 5. What do they want…?"), but the text lists four questions. The fifth ("What do they want…?") is never spelled out.*

"If your ad isn't hitting, ask:"
1. Is the **emotion** it communicates positive or negative?
2. Is it **high energy** or **low energy**?
3. What state is my customer in when they see this?
4. What do I want them to feel instead?

"Example: A calm, cozy ad (Zone 1) shown to a stressed-out customer hunting for fast solutions? It'll get skipped."

## Chapter 5: Fixing the Tone Without Rebuilding the Ad
*(p9; video: [lesson 5](../courses/tether-lab/03-valence-intensity-model/05-fixing-the-tone-without-rebuilding-the-ad.md))*

![Chapter 5 with skincare before/after](../../raw/assets/tether-lab/pdf-pages/8-Diagnose-Why-Your-Ads-Arent-Working/p09.png)
*Before → After skincare example. **Before:** "Nourish your skin while you sleep." (lilac, woman asleep: Zone 1, calm). **After:** "Wake up to younger looking skin (overnight)." (bright turquoise, smiling woman, sparkles: higher intensity, more of a result promise).*

"You don't need to scrap your ad. You need to tune it."
- **Step 1: Shift Your Verbs.** "Headlines are tone-setters. Words like **'find' vs. 'seize'** trigger very different intensities."
- **Step 2: Shift Your Visuals.** "**Zoom in = more intensity. Bold colors = more urgency. Morning light = calm. Desaturated tones = trust.**"
- **Step 3: Shift the Emotional Promise.** "Ask: What emotional payoff are we selling? **Indulgence? Safety? Control?** Change that, and the whole ad pivots."

→ [Tone-shifting](../frameworks/tone-shifting.md) · [Emotional promise](../concepts/emotional-promise.md)

## Structured units

### Rewrites (before → after)
| Before | After | What changed (verb / visual / promise / self / intensity) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Nourish your skin while you sleep." (lilac background, woman asleep) | "Wake up to younger looking skin (overnight)." (bright turquoise, smiling woman, sparkles) | Verb ("nourish" → "wake up to"), visual (pastel → saturated, asleep → smiling), promise (care → visible result). Intensity ↑. | e-book p9 |
| "find" | "seize" | Verb: same meaning, much higher intensity | e-book p9 |

### Rules of thumb
- **DO** use "Zoom in = more intensity. Bold colors = more urgency. Morning light = calm. Desaturated tones = trust." (p9)
- **DO** brief Zone 1 visuals as "morning light, soft pastels, gentle copy". Zone 2: "bold type, energy visuals, verbs that punch". Zone 3: "sighs, subtle sarcasm, the tension before the solution". Zone 4: "harsh lighting, high contrast, jarring visuals". (pp6–7)
- **IF** buyers are thoughtful **THEN** Zone 3 (soft problem awareness) fits. (p7)
- **DO** treat Zone 4 with care. "This one can backfire." (p7)
- **IF** a calm Zone 1 ad is shown to a stressed customer hunting for fast solutions **THEN** "It'll get skipped." (p8)
- **DO** match visuals to the rewritten zone. "Calm lighting vs. jarring contrast matters more than you think." (p10)
- **DON'T** manipulate. Map to resonate. (p11)
