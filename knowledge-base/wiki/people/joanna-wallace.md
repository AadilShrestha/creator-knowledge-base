# Joanna Wallace
> Paid-media creative leader with a comedy background. She ran **HexClad's** paid creative and was the guest on **Tether Lab Guest Session 01 (May 23, 2025)**, an open Q&A on **using humor in ads**. Sarah calls her "the creative director for HexClad… she uses humor everywhere". Her core moves: humor built on the persona's insecurities, the humor register of whoever the audience already follows (Gordon Ramsay), jokes mined from customer comments, puns that carry real product information, recurring characters such as a skeptic, and comedians and comedy-literate editors on the team.

## 🤖 Agent use
- **Use when:** writing, briefing or tearing down humorous ads and UGC; choosing a brand's comedic voice; turning customer comments into ad copy; designing a recurring creator character; writing "call out the friction" objection copy.
- **Inputs → outputs:** persona, reviews/comments, the audience's favourite figures, product traits → one humor type, customer-sourced joke lines, a character/messenger plan, and channel-appropriate wording (acquisition vs retention).
- **Rules (hers, distilled):** know the persona's insecurities and joke from inside; borrow the voice they already love and check the data before backing off; let customers tell you what's funny; periods for deadpan; every acquisition pun needs "meat information"; dip your toes, then make humor part of the brand once it gets momentum; call out what went wrong; hire comedians and an editor with timing. Full detail and timestamps: [the session page](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md).

## Key ideas (with links back)
| Idea | One-line summary | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Humor from insecurity, not insult** | Embody the persona; be self-deprecating "but not so far" that people feel bad; "I'm not the only one" lowers the guard. | [0:01:01–0:03:04](../courses/tether-lab/05-live-recordings/2025-05-23-guest-session-01-joanna-wallace.md) |
| **Borrow the audience's favourite voice** | HexClad's cocky-confident core + Gordon Ramsay's brash cursing → "the best [expletive] cookware on the planet", replacing the legally threatened "Rolls-Royce of pans". Check demographics data before backing off after complaints. | [0:03:04–0:06:10] |
| **Customers write the jokes** | Read reviews and ad comments; use their words ("pots and pans"); turn comments into "real user quote" statics ("Can be used as a weapon…"). | [0:07:12–0:08:17] |
| **Mom humor** | Show the unseen side of motherhood; kids saying brutally honest things. | [0:09:21–0:11:27] |
| **Golden Turd Award** | Team ritual for "polishing a turd": laugh through frustration instead of resentment. | [0:12:29–0:15:37] |
| **Get ugly** | Self-deprecating content can't be filtered or beautified. | [0:17:42–0:18:43] |
| **Periods = straight face** | Deadpan and sarcasm read better with periods than exclamation points; keep the joke in disclaimers. | [0:19:44–0:21:56] |
| **Commit, with a brand spin** | Weirdness shows confidence; jump on comedy trends only with a distinctive brand spin. | [0:23:02; 0:27:13–0:28:17] |
| **Meat information in puns** | "To the point" says nothing; "sharp as f*ck" says *sharp*. Cute puns belong in email and retention. Each channel has its own voice. | [0:31:27–0:34:35] |
| **Recurring skeptic character** | Paige, an actress-comedian, voices the viewer's disbelief and tests Gordon's claims ("go off, Gordy"). | [0:35:37–0:36:43] |
| **Call it out** | If a joke misfires or there's friction (Lingopie's credit card for a free trial), name it: "we're sketched out about the credit card too." | [0:38:48–0:40:56] |
| **Hire comedians and comedy editors** | Comedy by non-comedians "is obvious"; competitors copied HexClad's structures and fell flat; timing lives in the edit. | [0:43:04–0:45:08] |

## Caveats an agent should know
- Cursing and shock lines worked for **HexClad's** Gordon-adjacent audience. Don't port them to brands without that voice; she rejected even an on-trend crude joke as off-brand. [0:08:17]
- The recording starts mid-session, so her intro and first answers are missing.
- Sarah's "halo effect" and "messenger effect" framings in the session are Sarah's, not Joanna's.
